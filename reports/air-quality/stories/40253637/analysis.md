# [People with gas and propane stoves breathe more unhealthy nitrogen dioxide](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40253637)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion centers on indoor nitrogen dioxide and other pollutants from gas stoves, and on how much ventilation can reduce exposure in real homes. Participants compare range hood performance, installation and makeup-air constraints, and the costs and cooking characteristics of gas, resistance electric, and induction appliances. Practical concerns include outages, electrical upgrades, and the affordability of stove replacement.
Tags: indoor air quality, gas stoves, nitrogen dioxide, ventilation, range hoods, induction cooking

## Relevance

Discusses NO2 and other combustion pollutants from gas stoves, firsthand CO2 readings, range-hood capture and ventilation limits, and tradeoffs of switching to induction. Comments disagree about study reliability and how much cooking emissions come from the food itself. ## Arguments

- Gas adds a combustion source independently of food preparation. kergonath [[./thread#^c40256019|kergonath]] and throw0101b [[./thread#^c40256759|throw0101b]] argue that emissions from frying or browning do not eliminate this additional exposure. kergonath [[./thread#^c40256098|kergonath]] also identifies CO, CO2, and particles alongside NOx. These are qualitative arguments; the comments do not quantify each source's contribution.
- The clearest numerical NO2 comparison is reported research, not a commenter's household experiment. throw0101b [[./thread#^c40266809|throw0101b]] describes a 2014 intervention involving 78 Baltimore homes: replacing gas stoves with electric reportedly reduced NO2 by 50\%, providing carbon-filter air purifiers reduced it by 22\%, and installing range hoods produced no statistically significant difference. Absolute concentrations and detailed methods are not supplied.
- Ventilation performance can fall short of complete capture. throw0101b [[./thread#^c40266809|throw0101b]] cites separate studies reporting capture figures of 55\% and 30\%, and research finding better capture over rear burners than front burners. These figures describe particular studies, not a universal efficiency range for every hood.
- Hood design and operation matter for particles. throw0101c [[./thread#^c40280030|throw0101c]] quotes research finding that ultrafine-particle reduction generally improved with higher exhaust flow and rear-burner use, while smaller particles were less effectively reduced at the same flow. The quoted research also identifies energy costs as a tradeoff. This is UFP evidence, not a firsthand PM2.5 measurement.
- Monitoring may reveal conditions that occupants have not otherwise recognized. ydnaclementine [[./thread#^c40258435|ydnaclementine]] reports unexpectedly high household CO2, especially while cooking, while throw0101c [[./thread#^c40280030|throw0101c]] recommends measurement instead of relying on perceived air cleanliness. Neither comment supplies a validated instrument, numerical readings, or a measurement protocol.

## Counterarguments

- WirelessGigabit [[./thread#^c40255086|WirelessGigabit]] argues that smoky food preparation makes a proper outdoor-exhausting hood essential regardless of stove fuel. pandaman [[./thread#^c40261287|pandaman]] interprets that argument as food heating producing more emissions than the flame, and peteradio [[./thread#^c40257060|peteradio]] emphasizes ordinary frying and browning. No supplied measurements establish that food emissions dominate combustion emissions for NO2 or for every cooking method.
- peteradio [[./thread#^c40281020|peteradio]] specifically disputes the reported finding of no difference after hood installation, rather than merely demanding perfect capture. Their skepticism rests on the strong odor improvement they experience with a hood, described in peteradio [[./thread#^c40279773|peteradio]]. This challenges the plausibility of the study summary to that commenter but does not provide an alternative NO2 measurement.
- Switching to induction does not resolve every cooking-air problem. peteradio [[./thread#^c40257097|peteradio]] prefers their gas kitchen with a hood to their parents' induction kitchen without one, describing the latter as a mess. This supports the importance of extraction for cooking emissions, but changing both stove technology and ventilation prevents the comparison from isolating either factor.
- lupusreal [[./thread#^c40255962|lupusreal]] proposes meeting air-quality standards through whichever equipment works, including gas with extraction, instead of assuming appliance replacement is always necessary. This is a conditional alternative: the discussion does not demonstrate that a particular affordable gas-and-hood installation achieves equivalent exposure reductions.
- Induction's practical advantages are contested independently of its combustion-emission advantage. zfxfr [[./thread#^c40255897|zfxfr]] prefers gas responsiveness; woeh [[./thread#^c40255859|woeh]] reports delayed, water-triggered touch controls; and searealist [[./thread#^c40278259|searealist]] claims induction can warp carbon-steel pans. These concern usability or equipment performance, and the warping claim is not accompanied by a firsthand failure account or comparative test.

## Assumptions

- The evidence boundary is story 40253637, part 0. smitty1110 [[./thread#^c40254710|smitty1110]] supplies a link to the underlying paper without reproducing its findings. Quantitative research claims here principally come from throw0101b [[./thread#^c40266809|throw0101b]] and the excerpt supplied by throw0101c [[./thread#^c40280030|throw0101c]]; they should remain distinct from firsthand observations and independently verified results.
- The claim that emissions all go outside assumes adequate capture, installation, and actual use. peteradio [[./thread#^c40257060|peteradio]] makes that assumption, whereas throw0101b [[./thread#^c40256759|throw0101b]] points to absent, ineffective, or unused hoods, including avoidance because of noise. An ideal ventilation arrangement therefore cannot stand in for every household's operating conditions.
- Neither intuitive acceptance nor intuitive disbelief quantifies health risk. chrisbrandow [[./thread#^c40255231|chrisbrandow]] explicitly distinguishes finding the result unintuitive from saying the science is wrong. brnt [[./thread#^c40255456|brnt]] argues that combustion makes pollution unsurprising, while Ninn [[./thread#^c40254785|Ninn]] emphasizes repeated everyday exposure rather than only accidents. None supplies an exposure–response estimate or individual health outcome.
- Claims of nearly instant heating and lower running costs assume relevant comparisons of cookware, burner power, and energy transfer. switch007 [[./thread#^c40255891|switch007]] requests wattage and challenges CuriousCosmic's account; kergonath [[./thread#^c40256075|kergonath]] offers an explanation involving direct pan heating, mass, and heat losses. No controlled heating or energy-consumption measurements settle those comparisons.
- Preference and professional adoption are not representative evidence. kukkamario [[./thread#^c40255870|kukkamario]] explicitly guesses that at least 95\% would prefer induction with equal costs; el\_duderino\_ [[./thread#^c40255044|el\_duderino\_]] describes their own professional circle. Even the linked adoption example from knifie\_spoonie [[./thread#^c40254930|knifie\_spoonie]] is qualified by smegsicle [[./thread#^c40255144|smegsicle]] as a switch from an already electric setup. These accounts establish neither population-wide preference nor measured exposure benefits.

## Conditions

- Housing control and ventilation infrastructure constrain improvements. jemmyw [[./thread#^c40254763|jemmyw]] only obtained a choice of stove when renovating their first owned home. throw0101b [[./thread#^c40256759|throw0101b]] identifies rentals without suitable extraction. amluto [[./thread#^c40261828|amluto]] argues that proper exhaust also requires makeup air to avoid depressurization and cites a roughly 400-cfm code threshold, without establishing which jurisdiction's requirements apply to any particular home.
- Purchase and conversion costs are geographically and technically specific. kergonath [[./thread#^c40256114|kergonath]] reports a local €150 three-zone induction cooktop, while lupusreal [[./thread#^c40256676|lupusreal]] discusses US stove-and-oven replacements starting around \$1,000 and potentially requiring additional wiring. bifrost [[./thread#^c40278417|bifrost]] reports a \$20,000 installation cost for their situation. These are different appliance formats and installation circumstances, not interchangeable price estimates.
- The cheaper intervention depends on existing infrastructure and whether work concerns a retrofit or new construction. lupusreal [[./thread#^c40255962|lupusreal]] calls extraction cheaper; tredre3 [[./thread#^c40278375|tredre3]] counters that a roughly \$400 electric oven could cost less than routing ducts through walls and ceilings. amanaplanacanal [[./thread#^c40256022|amanaplanacanal]] and orwin [[./thread#^c40256828|orwin]] also challenge the assumption that existing appliances would have to be replaced. No specific applicable requirement is established.
- Operating-cost claims depend on tariffs, efficiency, season, and local generation. com2kid [[./thread#^c40255496|com2kid]] claims induction transfers roughly twice as much input energy into cookware, while CaptainMarvel [[./thread#^c40255745|CaptainMarvel]] reports UK gas costing roughly one-quarter as much as electricity. Spartan-S63 [[./thread#^c40255243|Spartan-S63]] raises rooftop solar. com2kid also argues that gas adds summer cooling load; imiric [[./thread#^c40256012|imiric]] notes the possible winter heating benefit. These are unmeasured household-cost arguments.
- Electrical reliability cannot be assumed uniform across locations. lupusreal [[./thread#^c40258068|lupusreal]] proposes rural exposure and harsh winters as explanations for the New York–London contrast, but the original New York account does not establish its precise location. themaninthedark [[./thread#^c40278904|themaninthedark]] discusses overhead lines, trees, and weather; prirun [[./thread#^c40257978|prirun]] reports that even their neighborhood's buried lines depend on overhead supply.
- Electric cooking during outages introduces capacity, price, and maintenance questions. amluto [[./thread#^c40261927|amluto]] proposes an approximately \$600 portable power system providing half an hour of full-power induction cooking, with solar replenishment dependent on sunshine. bifrost [[./thread#^c40278449|bifrost]] contrasts a claimed \$6,000, 3-kWh battery stove with a \$400 gas stove. bell-cot [[./thread#^c40255976|bell-cot]] and bell-cot [[./thread#^c40272898|bell-cot]] emphasize connection requirements and the work of maintaining backup equipment. These proposals are not equivalent in cooking capacity or duration.
- Commercial conversion has different constraints from replacing a domestic hob. Former chef el\_duderino\_ [[./thread#^c40255044|el\_duderino\_]] cites electrical capacity, already-paid-for gas equipment, thin margins, and uncertainty about durability. bell-cot [[./thread#^c40256027|bell-cot]] relays southeast Michigan business-news accounts of extremely costly or unavailable service upgrades; vel0city [[./thread#^c40255016|vel0city]] explicitly labels their infrastructure explanation an outsider's hypothesis. mistercow [[./thread#^c40258433|mistercow]] likewise assumes higher induction maintenance costs without providing failure-rate data.

## Lived experiences

- ydnaclementine [[./thread#^c40258435|ydnaclementine]] reports discovering high CO2 during normal household activity and especially cooking. They recommend a monitor to people waking with persistent headaches, but provide no concentrations, instrument details, confirmed personal headache diagnosis, or evidence that changing ventilation improved symptoms.
- peteradio [[./thread#^c40279773|peteradio]] says cooking bacon without a hood would make the main floor smell and require opening windows and doors, whereas using the hood leaves no noticeable smell. This is a household sensory account; it is not a logged comparison of NO2, CO2, or particle concentrations, nor a measured window-opening intervention.
- liotier [[./thread#^c40255838|liotier]] reports a 15-year-old gas installation with a substantial extractor, remote motor, and rooftop exhaust, and says a future upgrade would be induction. The account documents an existing mitigation arrangement and preference, but gives no before-and-after exposure measurements.
- CuriousCosmic [[./thread#^c40254761|CuriousCosmic]] reports faster pan heating and boiling, easier cleaning, and occasional buzzing after moving from gas to induction. frodo8sam [[./thread#^c40255932|frodo8sam]] also finds induction more responsive than gas, contrary to zfxfr's gas preference [[./thread#^c40255897|zfxfr]]. Ekaros [[./thread#^c40255947|Ekaros]] experiences noticeable noise mainly at high power and says cookware affects it. These are varied performance and comfort experiences, not measured air-quality outcomes.
- Controls affect satisfaction with a switch. jemmyw [[./thread#^c40254763|jemmyw]] chose induction with physical knobs and later identified an expensive Smeg unit [[./thread#^c40260419|jemmyw]]. woeh [[./thread#^c40255859|woeh]] reports frustration with touch-control delays and water activation. brianglick [[./thread#^c40281301|brianglick]] reports satisfactory knob-controlled Frigidaire and LG models at prices they considered reasonable. These accounts show that cost and usability depend on the chosen equipment.
- Outage experiences differ substantially. StanislavPetrov [[./thread#^c40255299|StanislavPetrov]] reports several New York power failures per year, sometimes lasting days, but no gas interruption over five decades. bifrost [[./thread#^c40278417|bifrost]] reports three or four annual outages in San Francisco and successful gas cooking during an outage. kergonath [[./thread#^c40256091|kergonath]] reports only three outages in ten years, mostly in London, none exceeding three hours; prirun [[./thread#^c40257978|prirun]] reports two week-long Midwest outages.
- el\_duderino\_ [[./thread#^c40255044|el\_duderino\_]], identifying as a former chef, enjoyed portable induction for banquet omelettes and small cooking tasks and preferred it to the butane burners previously used. Their account of friends liking induction at home is limited to that circle. scott\_w [[./thread#^c40255084|scott\_w]] gives a secondhand contrasting account: their mother had always used gas in catering and apparently had not considered switching.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Why did the reported hood-installation intervention show no statistically significant NO2 change? peteradio [[./thread#^c40281020|peteradio]] treats that result as implausible, while throw0101b [[./thread#^c40266809|throw0101b]] uses it to challenge hood-only mitigation. The supplied account does not establish installation quality, actual use, statistical power, or other exposure sources. No significant difference is not proof of literally zero effect, and the separate UFP study quoted by throw0101c [[./thread#^c40280030|throw0101c]] does not directly resolve that NO2 result.
- Health, cognition, and sleep effects remain largely unmeasured in this unit. ydnaclementine [[./thread#^c40258435|ydnaclementine]] connects a recommendation to monitor CO2 with waking headaches but supplies no causal test or measured sleep outcome. kergonath [[./thread#^c40256098|kergonath]] identifies additional pollutants without reporting individual illness. There are no supplied cognitive tests, quantified respiratory outcomes, or demonstrated symptom improvements after an intervention.
- Sensor reliability cannot be assessed. ydnaclementine [[./thread#^c40258435|ydnaclementine]] gives no device, calibration, placement, units, concentrations, or time series. throw0101c [[./thread#^c40280030|throw0101c]] recommends an IAQ meter without specifying its pollutant capabilities. Consequently, the discussion contains no documented firsthand numerical NO2, CO2, or PM2.5 comparison and no validation of a particular sensor.
- PM2.5 and filtration conclusions have narrow support. throw0101c [[./thread#^c40280030|throw0101c]] supplies UFP research, which should not be relabeled as a PM2.5 mass measurement. throw0101b [[./thread#^c40266809|throw0101b]] reports a carbon-filter intervention's NO2 reduction, but provides no basis for extending that result to CO2 removal, PM2.5 performance, other purifier types, or long-term filter effectiveness.
- When opening windows improves total exposure remains unresolved. peteradio [[./thread#^c40279773|peteradio]] describes windows and doors as a remedy for cooking odor, without pollutant measurements. throw0101b [[./thread#^c40266809|throw0101b]] relays an attribution of residual NO2 to traffic and other outdoor sources. Together these motivate comparing indoor and outdoor conditions, but do not demonstrate a window-opening benefit or penalty for NO2, CO2, or particles. No firsthand outdoor-monitoring or wildfire-smoke account is supplied.
- Fuel-specific and off-state exposures are unclear. zakki [[./thread#^c40256120|zakki]] asks whether the gas under discussion is LPG and receives no visible answer. throw0101b [[./thread#^c40266809|throw0101b]] claims stoves can leak pollution while turned off, but does not identify that pollution in the comment. The unit therefore cannot establish separate propane versus natural-gas exposure estimates or treat the unspecified leakage as measured NO2.
- The least costly way to achieve verified good air quality is not settled. lupusreal [[./thread#^c40255962|lupusreal]] favors extraction where it can meet standards, while throw0101b [[./thread#^c40266809|throw0101b]] favors preventing combustion emissions and allowing more tolerance for imperfect ventilation. No supplied account compares complete installation costs with measured exposure in the same home. Likewise, outage cooking reports from StanislavPetrov [[./thread#^c40255299|StanislavPetrov]] and bifrost [[./thread#^c40278417|bifrost]] establish cooking availability, not ventilation performance or air quality during those outages.
