# [Clever coating turns lampshades into indoor air purifiers](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=37211365)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

The thread questions whether a thermocatalytic lampshade can meaningfully reduce indoor pollutants, especially given limited airflow and uncertainty about room-scale effectiveness. Participants discuss the need for heat or light to activate the coating and whether versions could work with LEDs. Much of the conversation compares passive purification claims with active filters, including DIY box-fan and duct-fan designs, their airflow, noise, cost, energy use, filter efficiency, and performance against particles versus VOCs. Some commenters share detailed builds and discuss using filtration for household odors.
Tags: air purification, VOC, photocatalysis, thermocatalysis, LEDs, DIY filters, HEPA

## Relevance

The thread compares a proposed passive indoor air purifier with active filtration and questions whether it can clean a room effectively. A detailed firsthand account reports PM readings during cooking and wildfire season, purifier performance, airflow, noise, and filter costs; commenters also distinguish particle filtration from VOC removal. ## Arguments

- prometheus76 [[./thread#^c37221730|prometheus76]] advocates a box fan with a furnace filter for inexpensive particle filtration and high airflow. nucleardog [[./thread#^c37223106|nucleardog]] supplies the discussion's strongest supporting evidence: household particulate readings that fell substantially after activating a homemade filter.
- Several commenters propose combining treatments: sooheon [[./thread#^c37219944|sooheon]] suggests the coating alongside HEPA and charcoal, especially near cooking; criley2 [[./thread#^c37220774|criley2]] proposes putting the catalyst inside an active purifier; plagiarist [[./thread#^c37223084|plagiarist]] considers a larger coated surface potentially useful. These are design suggestions without reported performance tests.
- justinlloyd [[./thread#^c37225534|justinlloyd]] [[./thread#^c37227206|justinlloyd]] describes filtering around litter boxes to control a local source of dust and odor. In reply, nucleardog [[./thread#^c37226559|nucleardog]] proposes adapting an existing litter-box enclosure with a small fan and filters; that proposed retrofit is not a completed intervention.
- nucleardog [[./thread#^c37223106|nucleardog]] reports that opening several windows helps cooking-related particle concentrations decline when filtration is absent. This supports ventilation for that particular indoor-source situation, without establishing its effectiveness under different outdoor conditions.
- ambientenv [[./thread#^c37228887|ambientenv]] argues for addressing pollution sources rather than continually adding cleanup technology. The comment supplies a prevention-oriented position but no specific intervention, exposure measurement, or demonstrated health benefit.

## Counterarguments

- rajnathani [[./thread#^c37223379|rajnathani]], replying to the fan-and-filter recommendation, stresses that the lampshade proposal concerns VOCs and claims carbon, HEPA, and MERV approaches barely reduce them. This is an unmeasured, broad claim about varied compounds, but it identifies a real gap in the discussion's evidence: reported particle reductions do not demonstrate VOC removal.
- pxx [[./thread#^c37223142|pxx]] disputes the claim that inexpensive box-fan systems are necessarily efficient, citing an analysis of electricity consumption, particularly with 1-inch filters, and emphasizing labor and noise. Ajedi32 [[./thread#^c37223970|Ajedi32]] challenges that comparison because, in their reading, operating costs omit replacement filters and use electricity substantially more expensive than their own.
- rob74 [[./thread#^c37219888|rob74]] questions how a lampshade processes enough room air through incidental circulation or convection. criley2 [[./thread#^c37220774|criley2]] makes the same throughput objection. Their concern is practical capacity; criley2's suggested 0.5\% contribution is speculation, not a measured effectiveness figure.
- The salt-lamp comparison from getwiththeprog [[./thread#^c37220565|getwiththeprog]], willcipriano's skeptical response [[./thread#^c37221270|willcipriano]], rob74's placebo joke [[./thread#^c37222305|rob74]], and VoodooJuJu's accusation of misleading marketing [[./thread#^c37222893|VoodooJuJu]] express disbelief. None supplies measurements establishing that the coating works or fails.
- jbm [[./thread#^c37219563|jbm]] questions the energy implications of requiring heat from incandescent or halogen lighting and notes that their home uses LEDs. frereubu [[./thread#^c37219802|frereubu]] likewise questions compatibility, while reporting that LED adaptation is being developed. These objections concern deployment and energy costs, not measured air-cleaning performance.
- francisofascii [[./thread#^c37222449|francisofascii]] worries that strong airflow could redistribute allergens and suggests running the system while the room is unoccupied. iambateman [[./thread#^c37222735|iambateman]] replies that filtered intake and repeated room-air circulation should remove airborne material. Neither provides a test of allergen resuspension versus capture.

## Assumptions

- nucleardog's AirQ readings [[./thread#^c37223106|nucleardog]] are instrument reports, not proof of literally particle-free air. The comment groups PM1, PM2.5, and PM10 together without separate traces, calibration information, placement details, or a reference instrument. A displayed zero cannot establish absence below the instrument's reporting capability or absence of gaseous pollutants.
- nucleardog's coverage and air-change estimates [[./thread#^c37223106|nucleardog]] assume the system delivers its expected airflow through the filters. Their construction account [[./thread#^c37224612|nucleardog]] describes seals and a tissue-based leak check while acknowledging possible leakage; it does not provide independent airflow, filtration-efficiency, or whole-room mixing measurements.
- justinlloyd's reported odor control [[./thread#^c37227206|justinlloyd]] does not establish comprehensive VOC removal or a health benefit. Their explanation that the thick MERV filter slows airflow enough to improve charcoal contact is explicitly a personal hypothesis; the reported lack of benefit from additional charcoal layers was also anecdotal.
- Related DIY designs should not be treated as one tested configuration. dillydogg [[./thread#^c37221813|dillydogg]] recalls Corsi–Rosenthal boxes, and haneefmubarak [[./thread#^c37224295|haneefmubarak]] tentatively identifies nucleardog's build that way. nucleardog [[./thread#^c37224612|nucleardog]] [[./thread#^c37224655|nucleardog]] specifically describes an inline duct fan drawing through two filters at opposite ends of a wooden enclosure.

## Conditions

- nucleardog [[./thread#^c37223106|nucleardog]] reports approximately \$200 for construction and hardware plus \$75 for two filters, rounded to \$300 overall. Their comparison was against selected commercial products advertising about 600 square feet at two air changes per hour, versus their own estimated 3,000 square feet at that rate. These are a builder's estimates and contemporaneous shopping comparisons, not independently matched performance tests.
- Ajedi32 [[./thread#^c37223970|Ajedi32]] reports access to standard MERV13 filters for \$10 each, claims some proprietary replacements cost hundreds, and says the cited analysis uses an electricity price nearly twice their own. The exchange with pxx [[./thread#^c37223142|pxx]] therefore leaves the cheapest option dependent on local power prices, replacement-filter costs, and operating time; no location is specified.
- Labor and maintenance affect the economics. pxx [[./thread#^c37223142|pxx]] argues that valuing construction time can favor commercial purifiers. nucleardog [[./thread#^c37223106|nucleardog]] reports that similar deep filters last over a year in their furnace, but does not establish the same replacement interval in the homemade purifier under cooking or wildfire loads.
- salad-tycoon [[./thread#^c37222200|salad-tycoon]] says the hydroxyl generators they researched cost considerably more than ozone generators. They also raise irritation and rubber/silicone deterioration as concerns with ozone. No purchase price, measured exposure, or comparative cleanup result is supplied.

## Lived experiences

- nucleardog [[./thread#^c37223106|nucleardog]] reports AirQ particulate readings around 20 µg/m³ without filtration and 300–400 µg/m³ while frying. After letting readings rise and then running the filter at about a 60\% setting, they report falling below 100 within 15 minutes and reaching a displayed or rounded zero roughly another half-hour later. This is a household before-and-after account, without a controlled comparison or separate PM2.5 time series.
- nucleardog [[./thread#^c37223106|nucleardog]] [[./thread#^c37224612|nucleardog]] built the purifier after becoming frustrated with wildfire smoke. Its two deep MERV12 filters have charcoal-sheet and screen prefilters, with the screen intended to catch pet hair. High airflow was noisy, so they normally use about a 20\% setting and increase it for smoky cooking or wildfire season. This documents a comfort-versus-throughput tradeoff, without reporting sleep outcomes.
- justinlloyd [[./thread#^c37225534|justinlloyd]] [[./thread#^c37227206|justinlloyd]] describes plywood litter-box enclosures using low-speed box fans, 4-inch MERV12 filters, and replaceable charcoal sheets, plus similar filters elsewhere in the home. They report strong dust and odor reduction, with especially strong odors sometimes escaping, and say a charcoal sheet maintains odor control for over a year. Their claim about removing allergens is not accompanied by allergen measurements.
- pfdietz [[./thread#^c37221997|pfdietz]] recounts a coworker whose reactions to nearby organic vapors, including an opened dry-erase marker, reportedly required medical attention. The coworker's UV/TiO2 purifier apparently did not help enough; pfdietz is uncertain whether insufficient removal or reaction to degradation products explained this. This is an account about another person's health, without exposure concentrations or clinical documentation.
- salad-tycoon [[./thread#^c37222200|salad-tycoon]], replying to pfdietz's purifier account, describes researching hydroxyl versus ozone generators after buying an old smoke-filled, malodorous house. They tentatively identify the earlier technology as a hydroxyl generator, but do not report operating either device or measuring subsequent air quality.
- woadwarrior01 [[./thread#^c37220615|woadwarrior01]] reports Philips Hue LED bases reaching 75–80°C after several hours while the bulbs remain relatively cool. baq [[./thread#^c37220427|baq]] says their LEDs use a UV-absorbing coating that emits visible light. These are observations about lighting hardware, not demonstrations of catalyst activation or pollutant removal.
- criley2 [[./thread#^c37220774|criley2]] says their existing purifier needs about half an hour at full speed for one room-air turnover; this is a reported turnover estimate rather than a concentration-decay measurement. walleeee [[./thread#^c37226002|walleeee]] favors a duct-fan 'Cuboid' design as quiet, easy, and effective, but supplies no sound, power, or air-quality measurements.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Can greater airflow compensate sufficiently for lower particle-capture efficiency? Ajedi32 [[./thread#^c37223618|Ajedi32]] explicitly asks whether repeated passes through a lower-rated filter can substitute for higher-rated filtration and says they never obtained a satisfying answer. The supplied discussion does not establish equivalence across particle sizes, room conditions, or devices.
- Does DIY filtration remain cheaper after electricity, replacement filters, construction time, and acceptable noise are included? prometheus76 [[./thread#^c37221730|prometheus76]], pxx [[./thread#^c37223142|pxx]], and Ajedi32 [[./thread#^c37223970|Ajedi32]] emphasize different components of efficiency and cost. Their exchange does not yield a common comparison demonstrating a universal winner.
- How much VOC reduction is achievable, and are catalytic reaction products tolerable? raj nathani's broad skepticism about carbon filtration, expressed by rajnathani [[./thread#^c37223379|rajnathani]], and justinlloyd's favorable odor account [[./thread#^c37227206|justinlloyd]] concern different endpoints and cannot directly resolve one another. pfdietz's uncertain recollection of a failed photocatalytic treatment [[./thread#^c37221997|pfdietz]] adds a possible byproduct concern, but no chemical measurements identify what happened.
- Can the lampshade approach operate effectively with household LEDs or other available heat sources? rantallion [[./thread#^c37219815|rantallion]] quotes proposed near-UV photocatalysts and conversion of visible light into heat; woadwarrior01 [[./thread#^c37220615|woadwarrior01]] reports hot LED bases, while baq [[./thread#^c37220427|baq]] raises UV absorption. mnemotronic [[./thread#^c37238476|mnemotronic]] asks about refrigerator heat. None demonstrates suitable catalyst conditions, room-scale cleaning, or favorable energy use.
- When should windows be opened during smoke events? nucleardog [[./thread#^c37223106|nucleardog]] reports a benefit from opening windows after indoor cooking and separately reports using filtration during wildfire season. No geographic location, paired indoor/outdoor readings, or wildfire window-opening comparison is supplied. Whether opening windows would instead admit more outdoor pollution remains untested in this account.
- The approved question is only partly covered by this source unit. jbm [[./thread#^c37219563|jbm]] expresses a desire to reduce CO2 but gives no measurements or successful treatment. There are no supplied NO2 measurements, traffic-exposure measurements, or measured cognition or sleep outcomes. pfdietz's vapor-sensitivity account and remembered smog-removing radiator proposal [[./thread#^c37221997|pfdietz]] do not establish those effects; nucleardog's particle reductions [[./thread#^c37223106|nucleardog]] likewise are not measured health improvements.
