# [NYC Subway Riders See 'Exceptionally High' Air Pollution](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41202059)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion explores possible sources of high subway particle concentrations, including wheel and brake wear and particles stirred up by train movement. Participants question how much each source contributes and whether regenerative braking or better motor control could reduce mechanical brake use, while also mentioning the distinctive subway smell, mask use, and station climate control.
Tags: air pollution, public transit, particulate matter, subways, braking systems

## Relevance

The comments discuss subway particulate pollution, possible sources such as braking and resuspended dust, and firsthand sensory experiences. They also mention a protective mask, while some explanations remain hypotheses rather than measured source apportionment. ## Arguments

- arbuge [[./thread#^c41203190|arbuge]] quotes researchers attributing airborne metal particles to wheel/brake–rail rubbing. They reportedly observed concentrations rising when trains arrived and beginning to decline about 15–20 seconds after departure, interpreting this as evidence that trains churn up settled tunnel particles. This is a secondhand account of research observations.
- amluto [[./thread#^c41202519|amluto]] proposes cleaning tracks and stations to reduce particles resuspended by train-induced airflow. They also suggest reducing friction-brake use through improved controls; neither intervention has a reported trial or measured benefit here.
- antisthenes [[./thread#^c41204608|antisthenes]] suggests continuing to wear an N95. This is a personal-protection recommendation without an accompanying account of use, fit, comfort or measured exposure reduction.

## Counterarguments

- amluto [[./thread#^c41202519|amluto]] questions how much pollution comes from braking because NYC trains appear already to have regenerative and resistor brakes, which they describe as non-rubbing. They tentatively expect wheel–rail wear to contribute relatively little, while acknowledging that expectation may be naive.
- Responding to the research excerpt supplied by arbuge [[./thread#^c41203190|arbuge]], amluto [[./thread#^c41203913|amluto]] argues that listing possible sources does not establish their relative contributions or explain why friction brakes are applied. The reported arrival-associated spikes leave those objections unanswered.

## Assumptions

- amluto [[./thread#^c41203913|amluto]] assumes experience with EV motor braking transfers sufficiently to subway trains that software or motor-control improvements could achieve a complete stop with minimal hardware changes. The thread does not establish train-specific feasibility, operating requirements or resulting particle reductions.
- jmclnx [[./thread#^c41202435|jmclnx]] treats an odor detectable despite their poor sense of smell as evidence of particularly bad air. In reply, reverius42 [[./thread#^c41205691|reverius42]] expresses concern about carcinogenicity. Neither comment establishes a relationship between odor intensity, particle concentration and health risk.

## Conditions

- The geographic and exposure setting is NYC subway stations and tunnels. The observations quoted by arbuge [[./thread#^c41203190|arbuge]] suggest concentrations vary with train movements, but supply no station comparisons, exposure durations or outdoor baseline from which to estimate riders’ cumulative exposure.
- stemlord [[./thread#^c41202262|stemlord]] describes some stations as sweltering and hopes attention to pollution will encourage climate control. This adds thermal comfort to the practical concerns, but the comment provides no cooling or ventilation design, costs, or evidence of an air-quality benefit.

## Lived experiences

- jmclnx [[./thread#^c41202435|jmclnx]] reports that NYC subway air consistently smelled odd during their visits despite a self-described poor sense of smell. Their firsthand evidence concerns sensory experience; they report no instrument readings or physical symptoms.
- Replying to jmclnx [[./thread#^c41202435|jmclnx]], reverius42 [[./thread#^c41205691|reverius42]] describes a distinctive subway smell unlike anything encountered elsewhere and vividly remembered years later. Their cancer concern is apprehension, with no reported diagnosis or demonstrated health effect.

Their accounts allow several mechanisms to coexist.
- The research excerpt supplied by arbuge [[./thread#^c41203190|arbuge]] includes no absolute concentrations, particle-size breakdown, instrument details or calibration evidence. Consequently, this unit cannot establish PM2.5 levels or sensor reliability.
