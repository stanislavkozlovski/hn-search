# [I measured the pollution from my gas stove](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=34316613)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants debate measurements and health claims about pollution from gas stoves, especially whether consumer monitors can accurately measure nitrogen dioxide and how much weight to give individual readings. They discuss ventilation, range hoods, leaks, building airtightness, and the distinction between stove emissions and particles produced by cooking. Many comments compare gas, resistive electric, and induction cooking in terms of performance, cost, safety, and installation, while others challenge the evidence, question the article’s framing, or discuss personal experiences with symptoms and air-quality monitors. The thread also touches on regulation, personal choice, and broader arguments about fossil fuels.
Tags: gas stoves, indoor air pollution, NO₂, air-quality sensors, induction cooking, ventilation

## Relevance

The discussion directly addresses pollution from gas cooking, including NO2, CO2, PM2.5, and VOC readings; disagreement over consumer sensor accuracy; and ventilation, range hoods, windows, and induction as alternatives. It includes firsthand measurements and experiences alongside claims about health effects and uncertainty. ## Arguments

- Combustion quality and NO2 exposure are different questions. ericbarrett [[./thread#^c34317585|ericbarrett]] argues that sufficiently hot combustion creates nitrogen oxides from nitrogen and oxygen in air, so cleaner fuel does not eliminate this source. kragen [[./thread#^c34317602|kragen]] cautions that complete combustion can increase NO2 while oxygen-starved combustion introduces dangerous carbon monoxide. hedora [[./thread#^c34317454|hedora]] adds that incorrect fuel/regulator combinations can make emissions worse.
- The discussion contains a health-risk claim, but its evidence is secondhand here: kragen [[./thread#^c34317528|kragen]] summarizes the article as reporting 291 ppb NO2 associated with a gas stove and furnace, and a 42\% relative increase in childhood asthma risk. kragen explicitly questions the unspecified comparison baseline and notes that the article omits particles generated by cooking food.
- Several commenters advocate capturing emissions and supplying replacement air. musicale [[./thread#^c34317179|musicale]] recommends opening windows and running an externally vented hood before cooking and afterward. danans [[./thread#^c34320377|danans]] explains that an extractor needs replacement air: otherwise an airtight home becomes negatively pressurized and extraction becomes less effective. These are recommendations and mechanisms, not quantified intervention results.
- Airtightness makes deliberate ventilation more important. danans [[./thread#^c34317021|danans]] argues that newer, less leaky homes can accumulate combustion pollutants and advocates continuous mechanical ventilation with heat/humidity recovery. danans [[./thread#^c34320118|danans]] also identifies filtration and more consistent ventilation throughout the home as benefits of HRV/ERV systems, while disputing that adequate ventilation must be prohibitively expensive.
- Monitoring and maintenance address failures that normal appliance operation may conceal. asveikau [[./thread#^c34316983|asveikau]] warns that an apparently functioning stove can develop problems unnoticed. sliken [[./thread#^c34317960|sliken]] recommends an independent CO alarm alongside broader air-quality monitoring after a damaged water-heater exhaust coincided with serious symptoms. DoingIsLearning [[./thread#^c34325125|DoingIsLearning]] similarly treats CO protection as separate from optional sensor analytics.
- Cooking ventilation remains relevant after switching fuels. Reason077 [[./thread#^c34317694|Reason077]] reports large particulate increases during frying and argues that electric or induction cooking still needs ventilation. mattpallissard [[./thread#^c34327512|mattpallissard]], drawing on industrial-kitchen work, criticizes residential hoods and describes smoke from high-temperature cooking; their suggestion that burning oil may be worse than gas combustion is an opinion, not a measured comparison.
- Automatic extraction could reduce dependence on remembering to use a hood. nomel [[./thread#^c34317391|nomel]] connected a smart gas range to start the fan whenever burners operate and keep it running afterward, later confirming that it exhausts outside [[./thread#^c34331162|nomel]]. This addresses danans's observation that people they know generally use extraction for food smells rather than combustion byproducts [[./thread#^c34320020|danans]].

## Counterarguments

- Low readings challenge a universal claim that every installed gas stove produces excessive household exposure. lukev [[./thread#^c34316898|lukev]] reports acceptable readings without an overhead vent, and totoglazer [[./thread#^c34317350|totoglazer]] reports low NO2 despite cooking-related CO2 increases. Both used uHoo devices, whose accuracy is disputed elsewhere in the same branch; these accounts establish differing reported readings, not independently verified safety.
- The most detailed sensor criticism concerns absolute NO2 values. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c34317891|ahaucnx]] identifies the uHoo sensor as a low-cost MICS-2714 metal-oxide device and describes interference from other gases, humidity, temperature, manufacturing variation, ageing, and calibration. They argue that displayed concentrations can be substantially wrong and prefer an index for detecting changes. This challenges threshold comparisons based on either reassuring or alarming readings.
- The hardware explanation itself is disputed. \_benedict [[./thread#^c34317431|\_benedict]] initially suggests that consumer NO2 readings may be inferred from other pollutants and says the referenced uHoo assessment did not test NO2. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c34317891|ahaucnx]] instead identifies a physical NO2-sensitive component with substantial limitations. lukev [[./thread#^c34317507|lukev]] [[./thread#^c34320333|lukev]] argues that the same device responding to other people's stoves must detect something relevant and that criticism also affects the article's alarming results.
- Cooking versus not cooking does not isolate the benefit of replacing gas. 99\_00 [[./thread#^c34317914|99\_00]] calls for comparing gas and electric cooking under comparable conditions. nemo44x [[./thread#^c34317785|nemo44x]] points to emissions from hot oil and browning food, while Reason077 [[./thread#^c34317694|Reason077]] supplies a household particulate account consistent with that concern. None supplies a controlled comparison of equivalent meals, temperatures, and ventilation.
- Measured emissions do not automatically establish exposure in an average home. maxerickson [[./thread#^c34319734|maxerickson]] questions how isolating air around a stove translates to household concentrations. This directly limits danans's blanket assertion that all gas stoves produce unsafe indoor levels [[./thread#^c34317393|danans]]; danans later emphasizes the particular problem of using gas without extraction and open windows [[./thread#^c34320020|danans]].
- Methane leakage is not interchangeable with NO2-related health risk. lukev [[./thread#^c34317578|lukev]] argues that the linked methane discussion does not establish significant health effects at the volumes involved. StillBored [[./thread#^c34317880|StillBored]] initially attributes much of the estimated leakage to ignition, but schiffern [[./thread#^c34320240|schiffern]] corrects that reading: the study reportedly assigned more than three-quarters to appliances being off. Neither the leakage estimate nor its correction establishes an individual household's NO2 dose.
- The asthma discussion resists a simple single-cause explanation. barney54 [[./thread#^c34317173|barney54]] cites a reported 54\% decline in US outdoor NO2 since 1990 alongside rising asthma rates. ericbarrett [[./thread#^c34317585|ericbarrett]] challenges the accompanying assumption that cleaner fuel necessarily means less NO2. National outdoor trends, household exposure, and asthma incidence are different measures; the exchange does not establish either the absence of a gas-stove contribution or its magnitude.
- Some commenters argue that ventilation deserves more consideration before replacement is treated as necessary. legitster [[./thread#^c34317574|legitster]] criticizes dismissing potentially fixable installation problems, and lazide [[./thread#^c34317529|lazide]] asserts that a proper hood makes indoor emissions unproblematic. That assertion remains unquantified and conflicts with charlie0's report of a purifier reacting during cooking despite an externally ducted hood running high [[./thread#^c34317467|charlie0]] [[./thread#^c34329278|charlie0]].

## Assumptions

- The evidence unit is the supplied story 34316613, part 0, rather than the linked article, linked studies, or Hacker News generally. kragen's numerical article summary [[./thread#^c34317528|kragen]] is secondhand evidence; dang's list of related discussions [[./thread#^c34317468|dang]] does not supply those discussions' contents. The accounts are self-selected and do not establish population-wide agreement or prevalence.
- Descriptions such as acceptable, safe, or low depend on a threshold, averaging period, location, and instrument accuracy that are usually unspecified. lukev [[./thread#^c34316898|lukev]] supplies no numerical exposure series, while totoglazer [[./thread#^c34317350|totoglazer]] supplies 5–15 ppb NO2 without calibration or duration details. Neither description alone establishes long-term safety.
- Different pollutant channels cannot substitute for one another. sliken [[./thread#^c34317808|sliken]] reports increased CO2 without increased PM2.5 and does not measure NO2. Likewise, DoingIsLearning's recommendation for a CO alarm [[./thread#^c34325125|DoingIsLearning]] should not be interpreted as evidence that such an alarm verifies NO2, PM2.5, or CO2 levels; that commenter also makes an unverified inference about absent CO implying little particulate leakage.
- CO2 as an infection-risk proxy requires assumptions about its source. lukev [[./thread#^c34317416|lukev]] uses their monitor at home and work partly for this purpose, but sliken [[./thread#^c34317808|sliken]] attributes substantial CO2 increases to stove use. The thread supplies no validation translating those combustion-related readings into infection risk, cognitive impairment, or a general air-quality score.
- Flame appearance is an imperfect diagnostic. SoftTalker [[./thread#^c34317178|SoftTalker]] suggests blue rather than yellow as a rough indicator of complete combustion, but kragen [[./thread#^c34317602|kragen]] explains why that does not imply low NO2. halfnormalform [[./thread#^c34319006|halfnormalform]] describes orange flames associated with sodium from a humidifier setup, offering a separate reason color might change; their assurance that this is harmless is not accompanied by exposure measurements.
- Correct exhaust separation is an operating assumption, not a verified condition in every home. lazide [[./thread#^c34317139|lazide]] and koolba [[./thread#^c34317118|koolba]] describe furnace exhaust and indoor air remaining separated by a heat exchanger. nrclark [[./thread#^c34318103|nrclark]] makes a broader claim about US furnaces having automatic CO shutdowns, but the supplied discussion does not substantiate that universal safeguard.
- Uncertain measurements need not imply identical decisions after high and low readings. 8note [[./thread#^c34322241|8note]] notes that false-positive and false-negative rates can differ, and \_benedict [[./thread#^c34322671|\_benedict]] argues that possible harm and prior knowledge of combustion can justify precaution. These are decision arguments; the thread provides no measured error rates for the devices in question.
- Instrument credibility varies by the kind of evidence offered. wcoenen [[./thread#^c34317026|wcoenen]] reports personally encountering a device producing fake readings, whereas byyll [[./thread#^c34340511|byyll]] repeats reviews describing disagreement between two uHoo units. ahelwer [[./thread#^c34316969|ahelwer]] offers a general price/calibration judgment. These are distinct from independent, pollutant-specific validation.

## Conditions

- Opening a window may help when it supplies replacement air and dilutes indoor emissions, as danans [[./thread#^c34320377|danans]] explains. However, danans [[./thread#^c34339611|danans]] also notes that exhaust-driven air replacement can introduce outdoor air that is hot, cold, or dirty. This supports a conditional tradeoff rather than an unconditional window-opening rule; the supplied accounts do not measure simultaneous indoor and outdoor pollutant concentrations.
- Temperature and humidity affect whether occupants tolerate ventilation. bgentry [[./thread#^c34317452|bgentry]] describes an Austin home where direct outdoor-air intake lost heating, cooling, and humidity conditioning, motivating an approximately \$2,000 ERV installation. asdff [[./thread#^c34317900|asdff]] instead opens a window because cooking overheats a small kitchen. The same action can therefore improve immediate comfort in one setting and undermine it in another.
- Ventilation equipment and building infrastructure are geographically contingent. bgentry [[./thread#^c34317992|bgentry]] found an ERV easy to connect to existing central air. alliao [[./thread#^c34317844|alliao]] [[./thread#^c34344098|alliao]] describes Auckland homes with little central air, humidity sometimes above 85\%, and concerns about incoming moisture. danans [[./thread#^c34320309|danans]] proposes HRVs for cool humid climates, but the exchange does not establish which system would work in alliao's house.
- Ventilation requirements differ by locality and building age. jaclaz [[./thread#^c34324885|jaclaz]] describes an Italian rule requiring low intake and high exhaust connections, approximately 100 cm² each, and argues that tighter replacement windows make these openings more consequential. nomel [[./thread#^c34331162|nomel]] describes a local requirement for actual exhaust from the breathing space without identifying the jurisdiction. These are commenters' descriptions, not verified universal code requirements.
- Renters may have limited control over the principal appliance and face incentives that discourage substitution. chadlavi [[./thread#^c34317124|chadlavi]] pays for electricity but not gas and nevertheless uses a portable induction burner. jeffbee [[./thread#^c34317193|jeffbee]] argues that landlord decisions limit individual choice; 0x457 [[./thread#^c34318466|0x457]] doubts existing properties will be forced to retrofit, while thinkcontext [[./thread#^c34325721|thinkcontext]] notes that some requirements apply during major renovations.
- The appliance-price discussion compares different markets and product formats. lotsofpulp [[./thread#^c34317534|lotsofpulp]] reports a \$1,150 minimum induction range at Costco; yamtaddle [[./thread#^c34317925|yamtaddle]] reports \$1,493 induction versus \$529 gas ranges and approximately \$70 portable induction units. interactivecode [[./thread#^c34317687|interactivecode]] links a €242 Dutch cooktop, while miguelazo [[./thread#^c34328623|miguelazo]] cites a \$900 range sale. These January 2023 reports do not establish a single replacement price or equivalent products.
- Installation and cookware can materially increase switching costs. lotsofpulp [[./thread#^c34317534|lotsofpulp]] estimates another \$500–\$1,000 or more for electrical work, and shaklee3 [[./thread#^c34321194|shaklee3]] reports spending \$500 on wire alone during a DIY conversion. maxerickson [[./thread#^c34319835|maxerickson]] distinguishes US 240 V installed appliances from cheaper 120 V countertop units. reddog [[./thread#^c34317788|reddog]] also had to replace non-ferrous pans when moving to induction.
- Operating-cost claims are location- and time-dependent. beebmam [[./thread#^c34317274|beebmam]] describes natural gas as very cheap in much of the US. weaksauce [[./thread#^c34318013|weaksauce]], discussing Southern California, reports bills rising from \$27 to \$75 and anticipates more than \$200 for similar usage. The last figure is a forecast, and the contrast undermines treating cheap gas as a universal, stable condition.
- Household cooking requirements affect whether a cheaper alternative is equivalent. midoridensha [[./thread#^c34320907|midoridensha]] describes smaller Japanese cooktops and ovens, while shaklee3 [[./thread#^c34327007|shaklee3]] and yamtaddle [[./thread#^c34328121|yamtaddle]] describe needing substantial oven capacity for larger meals. bob1029 [[./thread#^c34317197|bob1029]] identifies limitations for moving a wok with fine temperature control; Quiza12 [[./thread#^c34317853|Quiza12]] misses flame-charring vegetables, and ryantgtg [[./thread#^c34348067|ryantgtg]] asks an unanswered question about directly heating tortillas.
- screye [[./thread#^c34317429|screye]] argues that tropical settings with routinely open windows, cylinder gas, curved cookware, flame-dependent cooking traditions, and lower purchasing power change the replacement tradeoff. Their stronger conclusion that pollution benefits do not apply is not supported by measurements in the comment: an open-window habit or absence of piped gas does not itself quantify exposure.
- Noise and ease of use affect whether available equipment is actually used. Reason077 [[./thread#^c34317694|Reason077]] says occupants leave a loud, ineffective kitchen fan off. chadlavi [[./thread#^c34317124|chadlavi]] prefers induction's performance but wants physical knobs and more durable surfaces. bob1029 [[./thread#^c34317775|bob1029]] warns that induction's rapid heating can also overheat oil quickly. These practical constraints matter alongside nominal pollutant reduction.

## Lived experiences

- lukev [[./thread#^c34316898|lukev]] bought a roughly \$300 uHoo after reading about stove pollution and reports acceptable readings despite having no overhead vent; no concentrations are supplied. totoglazer [[./thread#^c34317350|totoglazer]], also using uHoo, reports cooking-related CO2 spikes but NO2 around 5–15 ppb, with the furnace several floors away. Both accounts remain subject to the sensor dispute.
- sliken [[./thread#^c34317808|sliken]] reports CO2 readings rising from a baseline of 550–650 to approximately 1,500 during substantial gas-stove use, with no observed PM2.5 change. Sensors in the basement and bedroom followed the first-floor increase quickly despite a large house with some 20-foot ceilings. Units, calibration, hood operation, and clearance time are not supplied; sliken explicitly does not monitor NO2.
- ladberg [[./thread#^c34316965|ladberg]] reports unfavorable CO2, PM2.5, and PM10 readings during gas cooking using a Qingping Air Monitor Lite. They describe viewing trends through HomeKit and remotely running an air filter when particles rise, reporting a return to normal before arriving home. No concentrations or filter specifications are supplied, and the comment does not establish that filtration lowers CO2 or NO2.
- binkHN [[./thread#^c34317695|binkHN]] placed multiple Awair monitors around the house and reports that gas-cooktop use caused large TVOC increases that spread through the HVAC system. This is a firsthand observation of sensor responses and distribution, without calibrated concentrations or separation of fuel emissions from food, oil, or other cooking-related sources.
- Reason077 [[./thread#^c34317694|Reason077]] reports bedroom PM2.5 often reaching hundreds of µg/m³ during frying in a London flat, even though the bedroom is upstairs across the house. Occupants do not use the kitchen fan because it is very loud and works poorly. The account illustrates substantial particle transport beyond the kitchen, without establishing the monitor's calibration or isolating the fuel contribution.
- charlie0 [[./thread#^c34317467|charlie0]] reports an air purifier about eight feet from the kitchen activating when burners run high, even with the stove fan on high. Asked whether the hood merely recirculates, charlie0 confirms a large pipe through the ceiling [[./thread#^c34329278|charlie0]]. The activation indicates a detected change but supplies neither pollutant identification nor concentration, and ducting alone does not document capture performance.
- StillBored [[./thread#^c34317880|StillBored]] reports that an egg-shaped monitor's readings spike after a shower is turned on, then decline over the rest of the day. Observing the effect when their wife was absent weakened an initial perfume explanation. They suspect humidity or mist interference; the observation identifies a repeatable trigger but does not establish what the device measures.
- wcoenen [[./thread#^c34317026|wcoenen]] reports purchasing an AliExpress device that produced fake air-quality measurements, while clarifying that it did not claim to measure NO2. Their additional distrust of uHoo rests on its apparent inability to be calibrated, rather than a personal uHoo validation experiment.
- bgentry [[./thread#^c34317452|bgentry]] replaced a basic outdoor-air intake in a 2020 Austin house with an ERV rated up to 280 CFM. It runs continuously at low speed, with 10–15 minutes of hourly boost. They report no observed CO2 or VOC buildup and no perceived air-quality problems from gas cooking when using the hood. The 4,300-square-foot home, system configuration, and qualitative rather than numerical results limit generalization.
- virtual\_void [[./thread#^c34316819|virtual\_void]] reports that replacing a furnace coincided with worsening asthma for both themself and their youngest son. No pollutant measurements or confirmed mechanism are provided. koolba [[./thread#^c34317118|koolba]] responds that properly installed exhaust should remain outside and suggests checking humidity, so the reply ancestry preserves an alternative explanation rather than confirming combustion exposure.
- sliken [[./thread#^c34317960|sliken]] describes a rental where hail crushed the water-heater exhaust cap. Their wife's handwriting changed and both recall little of the affected period; after moving out following eight months, handwriting and memory improved. This is a serious firsthand temporal account, but no measured pollutant, dose, or medical diagnosis is supplied. It cannot establish that ordinary CO2 levels caused the cognitive symptoms.
- chadlavi [[./thread#^c34317124|chadlavi]] reports that their wife starts coughing when cooking begins, even from another room without knowing the appliance was turned on. They increasingly use a portable induction burner despite paying for electricity but not gas. They report preferring induction's speed and responsiveness, but do not supply pollutant measurements or explicitly document whether switching eliminated the cough.
- RHSeeger [[./thread#^c34318130|RHSeeger]] reports substantially better comfort with an inline humidifier at home and painful dry eyes during an evening in a Washington, DC hotel with forced-air heating. They also disclose pre-existing chronic dry-eye problems. This is a humidity and comfort account, not evidence of a particular combustion pollutant.
- DrFunke [[./thread#^c34317088|DrFunke]] bought an induction stove and moved the kitchen to a covered outdoor patio to avoid breathing cooking-oil particles. No before/after exposure or health measurements are supplied. The account demonstrates an implemented source-separation choice, rather than proving a cancer-risk reduction.
- Induction experiences conflict. reddog [[./thread#^c34317788|reddog]] says persistent hash-brown problems on a cycling halogen stove disappeared after switching to induction and replacing pans. bob1029 [[./thread#^c34317197|bob1029]] reports much faster heating with a 5 kW element and easier cleaning, with wok limitations. jeffbee [[./thread#^c34317409|jeffbee]] reports an induction appliance scorching a small ring while leaving other areas unheated. llsf [[./thread#^c34317595|llsf]] offers a secondhand counterexample: their retired professional-cook father prefers a midrange induction unit and emphasizes cookware quality.
- Conventional electric cooking also receives conflicting assessments. yellow\_postit [[./thread#^c34317413|yellow\_postit]] reports no significant cooking problems after moving from gas to glass-top electric. wonnage [[./thread#^c34317824|wonnage]] finds good heat output and distribution but slower response, scratching concerns, and difficult cleanup. bodangly [[./thread#^c34317448|bodangly]] reports a high-end radiant Miele working well after changing to a flat-bottomed wok. These accounts qualify claims that all non-induction electric cooking is inadequate.
- Gordonjcp [[./thread#^c34318102|Gordonjcp]] reports measurements on propane-converted vehicles showing little CO but appreciable NOx before the catalytic converter, and no measurable NOx afterward. This is a firsthand exhaust-measurement anecdote consistent with distinguishing CO from NOx, but it concerns engines and after-treatment, not household exposure or measured air beside traffic.

## Unresolved disagreements

- How much does replacing a gas stove reduce exposure during otherwise equivalent cooking? 99\_00 [[./thread#^c34317914|99\_00]] requests the relevant comparison, while sliken [[./thread#^c34317808|sliken]], ladberg [[./thread#^c34316965|ladberg]], and Reason077 [[./thread#^c34317694|Reason077]] report different pollutant patterns under different conditions. The supplied discussion does not separate combustion, cooking aerosols, ventilation, room size, and cooking technique well enough to quantify the incremental benefit.
- A practical, affordable, validated method for measuring absolute household NO2 remains unresolved. TedDoesntTalk [[./thread#^c34320466|TedDoesntTalk]] asks directly; \_benedict [[./thread#^c34321915|\_benedict]] suggests independently tested equipment but says they abandoned NO2 measurement because of the hassle. scottlamb [[./thread#^c34317161|scottlamb]] recommends AirGradient kits they own while acknowledging that the SGP41 output is an index, not threshold-comparable concentration. a\_t48 [[./thread#^c34317142|a\_t48]] mentions some Dyson devices without supplying validation.
- How reliable is a particular hood, and how long do emissions persist? charlie0 [[./thread#^c34317467|charlie0]] [[./thread#^c34329278|charlie0]] reports a response despite high extraction and exterior ducting, while bgentry [[./thread#^c34317452|bgentry]] reports satisfactory conditions with a hood plus whole-house ventilation. ed25519FUUU [[./thread#^c34361396|ed25519FUUU]] asks sliken about hood use and CO2 dissipation, but the supplied export contains no answer. Capture effectiveness and exposure duration therefore remain material unknowns.
- The asthma mechanism and effect size are not resolved by these comments. kragen [[./thread#^c34317528|kragen]] relays a relative-risk figure, while LatteLazy [[./thread#^c34323168|LatteLazy]] objects that the linked study is correlational. conqueso [[./thread#^c34368788|conqueso]], carlivar [[./thread#^c34321439|carlivar]], and ntimcan [[./thread#^c34323405|ntimcan]] introduce hygiene, C-section, and breastfeeding hypotheses or correlations. None supplies an analysis in the prompt that separates these explanations or quantifies an individual household's risk.
- The prevalence and causes of off-state gas leakage remain contested. danans [[./thread#^c34320787|danans]] attributes it to imperfect valves; midoridensha [[./thread#^c34320873|midoridensha]] questions why sufficiently tight sealing would be impossible. StillBored [[./thread#^c34344601|StillBored]] quotes a finding that five stoves—9\% of the sample—accounted for 49\% of off-state emissions and asks whether faults, installation problems, or classification explain the outliers. The discussion does not resolve those mechanisms.
- Shower-related sensor increases could reflect interference, mist, or actual particles. StillBored [[./thread#^c34317880|StillBored]] favors a humidity/mist explanation. schiffern [[./thread#^c34320240|schiffern]] proposes residual dissolved-solid aerosols and argues that their persistence fits the reported slow decay, citing outside studies. Brian\_K\_White [[./thread#^c34322486|Brian\_K\_White]] asks whether those particles are harmful and how they compare with stove emissions. No instrument identification, independent particle measurement, or health comparison settles the exchange.
- Adequate ventilation rates and moisture control remain disputed. alliao [[./thread#^c34317825|alliao]] frames good air quality as requiring more than six air changes per hour and doubts affordability; danans [[./thread#^c34320118|danans]] says three is sufficient and reports approaching that with a \$300 fan. danans [[./thread#^c34320356|danans]] reports humidity reduction with an HRV in wet northern California, whereas alliao [[./thread#^c34344098|alliao]] remains concerned about Auckland conditions and asks for a model. Neither a common exposure target nor a transferable equipment specification emerges.
- Outdoor traffic and wildfire smoke are evidence gaps in this source unit. alar44 [[./thread#^c34317714|alar44]] asserts that road or city exposure outweighs stove exposure, and alfor [[./thread#^c34376408|alfor]] asks for comparisons with traffic, toast, carpets, and dust; neither provides measurements. danans [[./thread#^c34316905|danans]] points to earlier wildfire-era sensor discussions, while scottlamb [[./thread#^c34317161|scottlamb]] notes that NO2 was not their focus. These comments do not establish when opening windows during smoke or traffic pollution would improve or worsen total exposure.
- CO2-specific cognition effects and sleep effects cannot be determined here. sliken's cooking-related CO2 measurements [[./thread#^c34317808|sliken]] contain no associated cognitive or sleep outcomes, while sliken's separate memory account [[./thread#^c34317960|sliken]] involves a damaged combustion exhaust without pollutant identification. The supplied part contains no sleep measurements, sleep-improvement account, or controlled cognitive test linking an outcome to a measured CO2 level.
- There is no agreed threshold for accepting, mitigating, or mandating removal of the exposure. none\_to\_remain [[./thread#^c34317389|none\_to\_remain]] explicitly accepts the risk; StanislavPetrov [[./thread#^c34317722|StanislavPetrov]] favors disclosure and personal choice; jeffbee [[./thread#^c34317193|jeffbee]] argues that renters cannot choose their appliances. These are differing judgments about control and acceptable risk, not evidence that the health effects are either negligible or uniformly severe.
- Source credibility remains disputed separately from the technical evidence. alfor [[./thread#^c34316961|alfor]] [[./thread#^c34333435|alfor]] worries that an advocacy-oriented publisher selectively presents information. virtual\_void [[./thread#^c34317147|virtual\_void]] and ethanbond [[./thread#^c34317774|ethanbond]] argue that the measurements, sources, and reasoning should be examined directly. The exchange does not resolve the sensor problems or exposure comparisons, and neither perceived bias nor the presence of citations independently validates the numerical claims.
