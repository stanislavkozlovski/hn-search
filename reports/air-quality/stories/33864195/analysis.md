# [Ask HN: Do you measure and/or mitigate CO2 in your living space?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=33864195)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants share whether and how they monitor indoor CO₂, what they learned about buildup in bedrooms, offices, and crowded rooms, and how they respond with windows, fans, or mechanical ventilation. Experiences raise trade-offs among fresh air, heating and cooling costs, humidity, outdoor pollution, and home energy efficiency; some discuss HRV and ERV systems. The question about gas stoves leads to debate over ventilation, cooking emissions, switching to electric or induction, and the reliability of consumer air-quality monitors.
Tags: indoor CO₂, ventilation, air-quality monitoring, HRV and ERV, gas stoves, cooking emissions, home energy

## Relevance

The discussion contains extensive firsthand CO2, PM2.5, and VOC monitoring, reports of effects on sleep, alertness, comfort, and cooking-related readings, and comparisons of gas and electric stoves. It covers window ventilation, exhaust hoods, ERV/HRV systems, and tradeoffs involving cold, humidity, energy use, and outdoor air. Sensor reliability and conflicting experiences are also discussed. ## Arguments

- Measurement can expose ventilation problems that otherwise go unnoticed. r1ch [[./thread#^c33865202|r1ch]] traced an office reading of 2000 ppm to a failed fresh-air motor. preinheimer [[./thread#^c33866133|preinheimer]] reported CO2 above 2200 ppm after replacing windows, followed by improvement after installing an air exchanger. These are concrete diagnostic accounts, rather than evidence that every home needs the same intervention.
- Heat-recovery ventilation offers a way to combine an airtight building with deliberate fresh-air exchange. throw0101c [[./thread#^c33865085|throw0101c]] advocates filtered, tempered incoming air and distinguishes temperature recovery through an HRV from temperature-plus-moisture recovery through an ERV [[./thread#^c33865120|throw0101c]]. physicalscience [[./thread#^c33865133|physicalscience]] supplies an installation account supporting this approach, while also reporting continued winter humidifier use.
- Window ventilation can lower measured CO2, especially when airflow crosses the dwelling or fans assist it. Syzygies [[./thread#^c33868765|Syzygies]] describes exhausting through windows in one room while admitting air through another; spiffytech [[./thread#^c33865603|spiffytech]] reports an outward-facing window fan lowering readings to the sensor's minimum. These observations establish improvement in their settings, without establishing a universal opening duration.
- Cooking mitigation includes source changes and consistent extraction. scld [[./thread#^c33866114|scld]] runs an externally exhausted hood whenever a burner operates; adql [[./thread#^c33865231|adql]] points out that people may otherwise use extraction only when smoke is visible. henearkr [[./thread#^c33873686|henearkr]] switched to all-electric cooking, and micromacrofoot [[./thread#^c33866353|micromacrofoot]] replaced gas with induction, although neither provides pollutant measurements before and after the change.
- Monitoring several pollutants helps reveal competing effects of ventilation. AirGradient-affiliated ahaucnx [[./thread#^c33866575|ahaucnx]] explicitly warns that opening windows can lower CO2 while increasing particulate exposure in polluted locations, and proposes filtered fresh-air intake. Maxburn [[./thread#^c33865251|Maxburn]] recommends logging multiple parameters across seasons rather than judging a building from isolated readings.
- Commenters invoke research to motivate better ventilation, but the research claims remain distinct from their measurements. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c33866575|ahaucnx]] cites a study linking higher CO2 with impaired cognition; Syzygies [[./thread#^c33864990|Syzygies]] claims sufficient air exchange can nearly eliminate respiratory disease transmission. The supplied comments do not reproduce study methods or results sufficient to assess those claims, particularly the strength of the latter assertion.

## Counterarguments

- The priority given to CO2 is disputed. clairity [[./thread#^c33870913|clairity]] argues that particulate pollution deserves greater attention than ordinary indoor CO2, while 1letterunixname [[./thread#^c33865684|1letterunixname]] emphasizes carbon monoxide and dismisses CO2 as unlikely to exceed 1000–1400 ppm. That numerical dismissal receives a direct counterexample: graycrow [[./thread#^c33865801|graycrow]] reports bedroom readings around 2500 ppm even with interior doors open. The thread supplies no comparative health-risk calculation establishing either commenter's proposed hierarchy.
- The heating penalty from opening windows is contested. throw0101c [[./thread#^c33869163|throw0101c]] objects to discarding heated air; jnxx [[./thread#^c33871322|jnxx]] and heliodor [[./thread#^c33884199|heliodor]] argue that brief exchanges retain much of the heat stored in walls and furnishings. throw0101c [[./thread#^c33874402|throw0101c]] replies that this does not address humidity or incoming pollen. No participant supplies a controlled energy-cost comparison resolving the disagreement.
- Defenses of gas cooking depend heavily on assumed ventilation. emrvb [[./thread#^c33864684|emrvb]] trusts a proper hood started before ignition, but subsequently clarifies that kitchen-specific pollutant measurement is still planned [[./thread#^c33865199|emrvb]]. dotancohen [[./thread#^c33865061|dotancohen]] therefore urges measurement despite sharing the intuition. Markoff [[./thread#^c33866005|Markoff]] claims studies largely concern poorly ventilated kitchens in developing countries, but provides no study details supporting that geographic limitation.
- Electric cooking does not make cooking emissions disappear. neutronicus [[./thread#^c33866311|neutronicus]] reports an induction setting powerful enough to char meat and trigger a smoke detector. neilv [[./thread#^c33864712|neilv]] still observes small particle increases from an electric air-fryer toaster oven. These accounts distinguish eliminating a gas burner from eliminating particles generated during food preparation.
- Plants are challenged as a practical substitute for ventilation. jonahbenton [[./thread#^c33864523|jonahbenton]], an AlgenAir owner, estimates that roughly 100 devices, equivalent to more than 2000 plants, would be needed to match human CO2 production in a closed space. OJFord [[./thread#^c33866115|OJFord]] similarly considers ordinary houseplant quantities negligible. These are commenters' calculations and judgments, without a supplied experimental validation.
- Continuous monitoring is not uniformly valued. Jaxan [[./thread#^c33864599|Jaxan]] says a borrowed monitor quickly taught the relationship between occupancy and ventilation, after which graphs added little; their home never reached levels they considered unhealthy. dewey [[./thread#^c33864494|dewey]] stopped monitoring after selling an Awair and does not miss it because airing remains routine. These accounts limit claims that everyone benefits equally from permanent instrumentation.
- The assertion that residential ventilation requirements do not exist is corrected within its reply chain. insaneirish [[./thread#^c33867105|insaneirish]] points to IRC requirements and alleges inconsistent enforcement. Maxburn [[./thread#^c33869299|Maxburn]] then narrows the original statement to installations and engineering sequences personally encountered, acknowledging limited regulatory knowledge. This should be read as a qualification of professional experience, not an unresolved claim that residential rules are absent.

## Assumptions

- The evidence here is confined to story 33864195, part 0, and is self-selected. qubex [[./thread#^c33864716|qubex]] explicitly contributes a non-monitoring answer to reduce sample bias; marssaxman [[./thread#^c33866836|marssaxman]] had not previously known people monitored household CO2, and willnonya [[./thread#^c33864963|willnonya]] had never considered it. The discussion cannot establish population-wide practices or an HN consensus.
- Reported numbers are commenter-reported instrument readings, not independently verified exposure measurements. anfractuosity [[./thread#^c33865399|anfractuosity]] observes disagreement between two CO2 sensors without a reference instrument. kylerush [[./thread#^c33864768|kylerush]] and nsbk [[./thread#^c33866329|nsbk]] describe substantial particulate spikes but supply no PM2.5 mass concentrations, calibration results, or standardized sampling protocol.
- Using CO2 to infer ventilation or rebreathed air requires attention to sources and background levels. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c33866575|ahaucnx]] presents CO2 as a ventilation and infection-risk proxy, while muxamilian [[./thread#^c33864751|muxamilian]] observes increases during gas cooking and Syzygies [[./thread#^c33864990|Syzygies]] attributes some apartment fluctuations to nearby traffic. A CO2 reading alone does not identify the source or establish exposure to other pollutants.
- Feeling stuffy does not specifically identify elevated CO2. dilippkumar [[./thread#^c33869232|dilippkumar]] associates that sensation with readings described as 'VOx'; bokohut [[./thread#^c33875638|bokohut]] reports an unspecified air-quality rating accompanying family discomfort. lzaaz [[./thread#^c33864821|lzaaz]] opens windows when air feels thin, but provides no oxygen measurement. These experiences support perceived comfort changes without diagnosing the responsible substance.
- Improved readings during HVAC circulation do not by themselves establish fresh-air replacement. binkHN [[./thread#^c33865173|binkHN]] reports less buildup when the system circulates air every 30 minutes, but does not describe its outdoor-air supply. lloydpick [[./thread#^c33868180|lloydpick]] hoped an Awair–Ecobee connection would introduce fresh air and was unsure it worked. Redistribution between rooms and actual outdoor exchange are not distinguished in those accounts.
- There are no quantitative NO2 measurements in this source unit. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c33866575|ahaucnx]] describes an SGP41-based NOx sensing capability as a possible indicator of stove fumes; that is a product-capability claim, not a reported NO2 concentration or a measured reduction after mitigation. The source's CO2 and qualitative PM observations cannot fill that evidence gap.

## Conditions

- Climate strongly changes the practicality of windows. VoodooJuJu [[./thread#^c33865564|VoodooJuJu]] reports using five-minute openings at −10°C, whereas germinalphrase [[./thread#^c33865473|germinalphrase]] finds winter airing difficult below 20°F. In Florida, lloydpick [[./thread#^c33868180|lloydpick]] says spring-to-autumn openings increase heat, humidity, and air-conditioning demand; cooler months permit more ventilation.
- Energy prices and installation space can prevent sustained mitigation even after monitoring reveals buildup. graycrow [[./thread#^c33865573|graycrow]] reports cold weather, high energy prices, and no space for heat-recovery ventilation. j1elo [[./thread#^c33865814|j1elo]] eventually ignores elevated readings because repeated airing seems too costly. By contrast, crazygringo [[./thread#^c33865509|crazygringo]] notices no obvious electricity-bill increase from a carefully adjusted window opening; none supplies a controlled cost comparison.
- Tenure and building restrictions constrain equipment choices. crazygringo [[./thread#^c33865509|crazygringo]] retains a gas stove because the dwelling is rented. gxt [[./thread#^c33867061|gxt]] asks for a portable solution where permanent modifications and window units are prohibited, with operation from −30°C to +50°C; no solution is supplied in the export.
- Retrofit costs vary substantially by intervention. runjake [[./thread#^c33868002|runjake]] reports spending about \$150 on a screen door to improve airflow. kylerush [[./thread#^c33866271|kylerush]] received estimates of \$2000 for a fireplace insert plus \$2000 for installation, delaying the change. digitalsushi [[./thread#^c33865870|digitalsushi]] found enlarging a kitchen exhaust duct from four to six inches logistically difficult and beyond budget. These are historical, project-specific costs.
- Contractor capability is another constraint on mechanical improvements. Maxburn [[./thread#^c33869156|Maxburn]] reports that three residential contractors dismissed an undersized, whistling return grille and excessive filter suction. Syzygies [[./thread#^c33868933|Syzygies]] can afford a California retrofit but anticipates difficulty finding a contractor willing to exceed ordinary standards. The anecdotes support checking design competence without establishing that all residential contractors are inadequate.
- Building fabric and local practice shape ventilation needs. jnxx [[./thread#^c33871275|jnxx]] contrasts rapid buildup in a reasonably insulated German flat with a drafty, 140-year-old Edinburgh home. nicbou [[./thread#^c33865388|nicbou]] and hnbad [[./thread#^c33865725|hnbad]] describe German lease expectations around airing to prevent moisture problems. altairprime [[./thread#^c33868947|altairprime]] recalls not learning deliberate ventilation because the childhood home was drafty. These are situated accounts, not universal national building characteristics.
- Monitor ownership entails cost, integration, and support tradeoffs. graycrow [[./thread#^c33865573|graycrow]] reports a €68 meter adapted for Home Assistant; smcleod [[./thread#^c33865114|smcleod]] considers the Aranet4 expensive but worthwhile. fuzzy2 [[./thread#^c33865784|fuzzy2]] encountered DIY sensor-interface problems. lloydpick [[./thread#^c33868180|lloydpick]] and Karawebnetwork [[./thread#^c33866343|Karawebnetwork]] report abandoned older Awair devices, with the latter also reporting false readings. Hardware affordability does not guarantee reliable long-term operation.
- Comfort and cooking preferences can outweigh an exposure concern for individual users. OJFord [[./thread#^c33865648|OJFord]] values localized fireplace warmth and estimates competitive heating costs against gas priced at 6p/kWh at the time. emrvb [[./thread#^c33865199|emrvb]] explicitly intends to retain gas and charcoal cooking even if measurements show harm, and dislikes induction controls [[./thread#^c33867290|emrvb]]. These are personal tradeoffs rather than evidence of safety.

## Lived experiences

- kylerush [[./thread#^c33864768|kylerush]] reports that sealing drafts increased CO2, two adults watching a movie produced surprisingly rapid buildup, and opening windows throughout the house for 15–30 minutes improved several displayed parameters. The same monitor showed sharp CO2 and particulate increases during fires. A later reply confirms an open fireplace, with an insert still only under consideration [[./thread#^c33866271|kylerush]]; the account should not be presented as a measurement of a sealed wood stove.
- physicalscience [[./thread#^c33865133|physicalscience]] reports occupied rooms around 600–800 ppm and empty rooms returning to roughly 400 ppm with a Panasonic ERV running continuously. Temperature and humidity became more consistent than with repeated window opening, although winter humidifiers remained necessary. preinheimer [[./thread#^c33866133|preinheimer]] likewise reports acceptable conditions after installing a continuously running air exchanger following the rise above 2200 ppm after window replacement, but gives no post-installation concentration.
- Manual airing produced measurable but sometimes short-lived reductions. graycrow [[./thread#^c33865573|graycrow]] reports a desk reading falling below 600 ppm with a window open, then quickly exceeding 800 after closure. j1elo [[./thread#^c33865814|j1elo]] reports a living room above 1500 ppm falling to 700–800 after five minutes of airing and exceeding 1000 again within an hour. dilippkumar [[./thread#^c33869232|dilippkumar]] found that approaching outdoor levels required about ten times longer than intuition suggested.
- Overnight buildup varies substantially by setting. graycrow [[./thread#^c33865573|graycrow]] reports about 2500 ppm with two sleepers; jnxx [[./thread#^c33871275|jnxx]] reports around 3000 in a smaller bedroom. sireat [[./thread#^c33871339|sireat]] reports winter morning readings of 3000–4000, summer readings below 900 with an open window, and drops below 1000 after opening a balcony at night. The last workaround requires interrupting sleep; none of these accounts establishes a clinical outcome from those concentrations.
- Opening interior doors helps some households. postscapes1 [[./thread#^c33866257|postscapes1]] found children's closed bedrooms reaching about 1400 ppm and began leaving doors open. eemil [[./thread#^c33865250|eemil]] reports needing an open bedroom door to stay below 1000 ppm, plus stronger apartment ventilation when four or more people gather. These successes coexist with graycrow's report of 2500 ppm despite open interior doors [[./thread#^c33865801|graycrow]].
- Room volume and location matter in nsbk's account [[./thread#^c33866329|nsbk]]: a mostly single-occupant duplex stays below 1000 ppm through the day and reaches about 500 after morning airing. Upstairs is 3–5°C warmer and about 150 ppm higher in CO2, while PM2.5 is higher downstairs. Soldering also causes a large particulate spike. The suggestion that particles settle downstairs is posed as a question, not demonstrated.
- Different activities and occupancies produce different buildup patterns. AirGradient-affiliated ahaucnx [[./thread#^c33866880|ahaucnx]] reports classroom measurements exceeding 4500 ppm within two or three hours; this is an organizational measurement account, not an independently supplied dataset. arnejenssen [[./thread#^c33987982|arnejenssen]] reports apartment readings above 1000 ppm during stationary-bike exercise and responds by increasing ventilation.
- Reported cognitive thresholds differ. crazygringo [[./thread#^c33865509|crazygringo]] associates readings above 1200 ppm with markedly slower thinking, 700–1200 with partial improvement, and roughly 600 or less with high energy. smcleod [[./thread#^c33865114|smcleod]] notices diminished cognition or wakefulness around 900 and ventilates earlier; orloffm [[./thread#^c33867470|orloffm]] associates readings above 800 with poor productivity. tlb [[./thread#^c33869165|tlb]] calls a 1000-ppm office alarm a productivity investment. These are perceived associations, without objective cognitive testing.
- Sleep and fatigue accounts are similarly observational. lloydpick [[./thread#^c33868180|lloydpick]] reports better sleep with bedroom windows or the door open and afternoon sleepiness with a closed office door. o\_m [[./thread#^c33866298|o\_m]] reports morning grogginess when bedroom CO2 exceeds an unspecified recommendation. switch007 [[./thread#^c33864937|switch007]] reports severe sleep difficulty in hotel rooms without opening windows, without measurements. dewey [[./thread#^c33864739|dewey]] attributes afternoon office exhaustion to air quality and describes coworkers discouraging airing.
- bokohut [[./thread#^c33875638|bokohut]] describes hours of an almost-severe air-quality indication alongside a stuffy sensation reported by family members. Opening exterior doors for about five minutes in a breeze rapidly improved the displayed rating, with comfort and readings remaining better afterward. The pollutant, concentration, instrument validity, and longer-term health implications are unspecified.
- Gas cooking repeatedly appears as a measured indoor source, although emissions are not fully characterized. muxamilian [[./thread#^c33864751|muxamilian]] reports Netatmo CO2 increases whenever the gas stove operates. crazygringo [[./thread#^c33865509|crazygringo]] reports both CO2 and PM2.5 rising quickly and runs the hood plus multiple open windows until readings return to normal. Neither supplies NO2 measurements or a quantified capture efficiency for the hood.
- Syzygies [[./thread#^c33864990|Syzygies]] attributes apartment CO2 patterns above the Henry Hudson Parkway to traffic timing. With substantial fan-assisted cross-ventilation, the apartment reaches about 450 ppm; after cooking lunch with windows closed it reads 728 [[./thread#^c33868765|Syzygies]]. kylerush [[./thread#^c33866407|kylerush]], in a rural town north of New York City, reports a minimum of 659 after only 30 minutes of airing. These are differently ventilated indoor measurements, not a controlled urban–rural outdoor-air comparison.
- altairprime [[./thread#^c33869013|altairprime]] reports bathroom CO2 reaching about 2000 ppm after a 15-minute shower despite the installed exhaust fan, then declining over 12–15 hours and remaining above 1200 while someone was home. The account does not identify the CO2 source or establish that shower water itself caused the increase.
- Vehicle recirculation illustrates a competing exposure concern. wintermutestwin [[./thread#^c33865780|wintermutestwin]] uses recirculation to avoid exhaust fumes. In reply, 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c33866057|5e92cb50239222b]] reports two occupants quickly reaching about 2000 ppm and the sensor's 10000-ppm ceiling after roughly half an hour, while explicitly questioning accuracy above 2000 and reporting little subjective sensitivity. bsenftner [[./thread#^c33864724|bsenftner]] also carries an Aranet into cars and opens windows when readings rise, without reporting concentrations.
- Filtration and filtered supply have favorable comfort accounts but little quantified outcome evidence here. altairprime [[./thread#^c33868986|altairprime]] says positive-pressurizing an apartment with an IQAir during the 2017–2020 wildfires was worthwhile, without PM measurements. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c33866789|ahaucnx]] describes noticeably cleaner-feeling air after installing demand-controlled supply with HEPA and carbon filters. Syzygies [[./thread#^c33868933|Syzygies]] recalls unusually pleasant air in a positively pressurized, highly filtered brewery. None establishes a measured health benefit.
- Sensor comparison yields disagreement rather than a demonstrated reference value. anfractuosity [[./thread#^c33865399|anfractuosity]] logs two NDIR sensors and consistently sees higher CO2 from the SCD30 than the MZ-14A, trusting the former's calibration approach. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c33866723|ahaucnx]] considers those sensor families and the Senseair S8 sufficiently accurate in practice, while warning against TVOC-derived estimated CO2. The latter is a practitioner's assessment, not a supplied benchmark.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The causal contribution of CO2 to cognition, fatigue, and sleep remains unresolved. Strong perceived thresholds from crazygringo [[./thread#^c33865509|crazygringo]] and smcleod [[./thread#^c33865114|smcleod]] coexist with little perceived effect in 5e92cb50239222b's account [[./thread#^c33866057|5e92cb50239222b]], whose highest readings are also uncertain. No controlled intervention separates CO2 from temperature, other pollutants, airflow, or expectations. jnxx's cited 1000-ppm workplace recommendation and assertion that 3000 ppm is not dangerous [[./thread#^c33871275|jnxx]] do not establish a universal clinical threshold within this evidence.
- Awair reliability is disputed without a shared test. 1letterunixname [[./thread#^c33865626|1letterunixname]] returned a unit as inaccurate; crazygringo [[./thread#^c33865680|crazygringo]] describes weekly automatic calibration requiring exposure to outdoor-quality air and says that condition works in their home. d23 [[./thread#^c33865874|d23]] requests evidence, but 1letterunixname [[./thread#^c33905634|1letterunixname]] declines to document the failure. Defective hardware, calibration conditions, and broader product limitations are therefore not distinguished.
- Low and high baseline readings cannot securely rank locations. badpun [[./thread#^c33866648|badpun]] suggests longer ventilation and checking automatic baseline calibration in response to kylerush's rural reading [[./thread#^c33866407|kylerush]]. fabioyy [[./thread#^c33865188|fabioyy]] reports 300–400 ppm in a Brazilian beach setting with electric cooking and showering, while danuker [[./thread#^c33865111|danuker]] cites a background reading above 420 ppm elsewhere. Without comparable instruments, exposure conditions, and calibration checks, these observations neither establish exceptionally clean local air nor prove a particular device faulty.
- The gas-stove and histamine connection remains anecdotal and contested. neilv [[./thread#^c33864712|neilv]] stopped using gas after perceived symptoms; shlant [[./thread#^c33864959|shlant]] proposes investigating a similar association but reports no test. emrvb [[./thread#^c33865288|emrvb]] experienced symptoms near electric stoves too and hypothesizes overheated oil. neilv [[./thread#^c33866243|neilv]] counters that symptoms occurred while boiling pasta and vegetables. That reply limits the oil explanation for this account, but does not establish gas combustion as the cause.
- Unexplained VOC readings remain unidentified. samuraixp [[./thread#^c33873521|samuraixp]] reports nighttime and weekend increases across uHoo, Airthings, and Dyson devices, with possible links to occupancy, furniture, neighboring cooking, air conditioning, or ventilation. Agreement between devices does not identify the compound or source. walterbell [[./thread#^c33876513|walterbell]] suggests isolating airflow zones and comparing logs; Maxburn [[./thread#^c33878241|Maxburn]] supplies a separate personal-care-product explanation from office experience. No diagnostic result follows.
- Bedroom-door advice contains an unresolved safety and privacy tradeoff. postscapes1 [[./thread#^c33866257|postscapes1]] anticipates older children wanting closed doors despite CO2 improvement from opening them. giraffe\_lady [[./thread#^c33866437|giraffe\_lady]] raises the remembered fire-safety benefit of closed bedroom doors and explicitly lacks enough information to weigh the risks. The thread does not resolve that tradeoff or demonstrate a suitable ventilation retrofit for those rooms.
- There is no established universal airing schedule or affordable solution for every building. VoodooJuJu [[./thread#^c33865564|VoodooJuJu]] finds five-minute cold-weather intervals workable; zsolt224 [[./thread#^c33867452|zsolt224]] asserts that longer, frequent openings are necessary; jnxx [[./thread#^c33871275|jnxx]] reports hourly airing for two people in a 30-square-meter room. Differences in occupancy, airflow, and building leakage prevent choosing one rule from these accounts. gxt's restricted-installation request [[./thread#^c33867061|gxt]] and postscapes1's request for specific filtered-supply equipment [[./thread#^c33939931|postscapes1]] remain unanswered.
- Pressure balance and actual air paths require building-specific investigation. digitalsushi [[./thread#^c33865870|digitalsushi]] reports exhaust fans interfering with wood-stove operation in damp New Hampshire weather and discusses the importance of makeup air. Maxburn [[./thread#^c33869222|Maxburn]] advocates positive pressure in a humid climate, then suggests that altairprime's hallway intake might receive dedicated outdoor air [[./thread#^c33878262|Maxburn]]. That proposed explanation is not confirmed, and these comments do not establish one universally appropriate pressure arrangement.
