# [Launch HN: Electric Air (YC W23) – Heat pump sold directly to homeowners](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=35138319)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion of a proposed direct-to-homeowner heat pump system combining ducted heating and cooling, a smart thermostat, air-quality controls, and contractor software. Participants debate its price and advantages over established equipment and DIY options; whether contractors would install, support, and stock parts for a startup product; and concerns about reliability, repairability, long-term parts availability, proprietary controls, cloud dependence, and open local integrations. Other topics include system sizing and duct compatibility, cold-weather capacity and backup heat, regional differences in heat-pump adoption and installation costs, home ventilation and filtration, refrigerants, noise, zoning, installation labor, and the pace and infrastructure needs of home electrification. Several people share installation experiences, compare alternatives, and question the preorder and product-development timeline.
Tags: heat-pumps, HVAC, home-electrification, direct-to-consumer, pricing, contractors, repairability, open-interfaces, smart-home, indoor-air-quality, installation, cold-climates

## Relevance

The discussion directly addresses indoor PM2.5 and CO2 monitoring, filtration, fresh-air ventilation, humidity, and comfort. It includes firsthand accounts of CO2-controlled ventilation improving bedroom comfort, while also raising tradeoffs around filtration pressure, ventilation energy, and outdoor pollution. Comments discuss keeping windows open for fresh air and mold control, and using a whole-house fan overnight; evidence about exposure effects and sensor accuracy is limited. ## Arguments

- Ventilation, particle filtration, and humidity control serve different purposes. Responding to solived’s warning that HEPA alone is insufficient [[./thread#^c35143768|solived]], cmui describes managing PM2.5 through filtration, CO2 through fresh-air intake, and humidity separately [[./thread#^c35143950|cmui]]. The proposed thermostat would coordinate these functions using PM2.5 and CO2 sensors [[./thread#^c35139068|cmui]]; this is a product proposal, not demonstrated exposure reduction.
- Central air distribution can support several air-quality functions together. abakker wants to retain central air specifically for filtration, fresh-air intake, humidification, and mixing despite installing a mini-split for a poorly served area [[./thread#^c35156488|abakker]]. skellington similarly argues that tightly built homes need outdoor-air ventilation, intake filtration, and whole-house air management beyond room-level recirculation [[./thread#^c35140637|skellington]].
- Fresh-air intake location matters. turtlebits warns that older furnaces may draw combustion air from garages or crawl spaces [[./thread#^c35139180|turtlebits]]. cmui agrees that this is unsuitable as the ventilation source and specifies a separate, small outdoor-air duct [[./thread#^c35140513|cmui]]. The exchange distinguishes deliberate outdoor ventilation from merely reusing existing furnace arrangements.
- Heat recovery is presented as a way to obtain fresh air with less heating or cooling penalty. toast0 recommends heat-recovery ventilation when occupants want fresh air during conditioning [[./thread#^c35139411|toast0]], while danans argues that integrated heat recovery could avoid installing a separate HRV/ERV [[./thread#^c35145243|danans]]. Neither supplies measured recovery efficiency or operating savings.
- Filtration intensity could respond to measured need rather than remain permanently maximal. cmui proposes reducing filtration when PM2.5 is considered reasonable and allowing a homeowner-selected high-filtration mode [[./thread#^c35142052|cmui]]. The potential benefit is balancing particle removal against airflow resistance, but thresholds, delivered airflow, and exposure outcomes are unspecified.
- Accessible replacement filters are part of a practical improvement. Beached says they require HEPA and want independently sourced, standard filters [[./thread#^c35141411|Beached]]. amluto favors deeper filter formats [[./thread#^c35141654|amluto]], and tguvot notes that 4-inch filters are available but many existing cabinets cannot accommodate them [[./thread#^c35144269|tguvot]]. These comments emphasize continuing affordability and compatibility alongside filter ratings.
- Temperature alone does not describe comfort. cmui proposes evaporative humidification plus coil-temperature control and reheat to adjust dehumidification [[./thread#^c35142052|cmui]]. zhoujianfu’s desired combination of 72°F and 50\% relative humidity illustrates the demand for controlling both variables [[./thread#^c35139713|zhoujianfu]], although the discussion establishes limits rather than guaranteeing those conditions everywhere.

## Counterarguments

- Ducted equipment does not automatically solve CO2 accumulation. kayson acknowledges limited mini-split filtration but points out that a central system also recirculates indoor air unless it has an outdoor-air supply [[./thread#^c35138983|kayson]]. This qualifies skellington’s criticism of mini-splits: the relevant distinction is which ventilation and filtration functions are actually installed [[./thread#^c35140637|skellington]].
- DIY installation does not imply poor air quality. nerdface challenges cmui’s suggestion that a cheaper DIY route suits people unconcerned about air quality, arguing that an independent HRV can supply ventilation [[./thread#^c35140965|nerdface]]. After sokoloff interprets the original distinction as bundled HEPA equipment [[./thread#^c35144705|sokoloff]], cmui agrees that separate components can be combined and reframes integration as convenience [[./thread#^c35145418|cmui]].
- HEPA’s incremental value is disputed. amluto argues that MERV 16 would avoid HEPA’s additional cost and pressure loss and considers HEPA unnecessary outside healthcare [[./thread#^c35141281|amluto]]. Beached explicitly says they personally require HEPA [[./thread#^c35141411|Beached]]. These are differing judgments and requirements; the thread supplies no comparative exposure or health results resolving them.
- Outdoor air is not assumed clean by everyone. HEPA\_As\_Service challenges the premise that outdoor air can address indoor pollution [[./thread#^c35140128|HEPA\_As\_Service]], while acjohnson55 explicitly wants both indoor-pollution exhaust and outdoor-pollution filtration [[./thread#^c35163664|acjohnson55]]. Their concerns make window opening conditional on outdoor conditions, although this part contains no measured episode of windows importing traffic pollution or wildfire smoke.
- The integrated package may be an expensive way to obtain benefits available separately. lancesells already owns a purifier and thermostat and rejects the claimed roughly \$8,000 premium [[./thread#^c35139916|lancesells]]; alex\_dev considers the air-quality module additional complexity and potential failure points [[./thread#^c35143756|alex\_dev]]. However, kayson’s distinction between filtration and CO2 management means an existing purifier alone is not evidence of equivalent ventilation [[./thread#^c35138983|kayson]].
- A humidity intervention can introduce particles. HVAC engineer tjmc reports working on data centers that reject ultrasonic humidifiers because hard water produces dust [[./thread#^c35146167|tjmc]]. This is professional experience with a particular humidification method, not evidence against Electric Air’s proposed evaporative-media system, which cmui describes elsewhere [[./thread#^c35142052|cmui]].
- Emergency heating can conflict with combustion-air safety. In response to izolate’s Buffalo-blizzard argument for retaining a gas stove as backup heat [[./thread#^c35139627|izolate]], hunter2\_ raises CO and other combustion byproducts and the heat lost through extraction [[./thread#^c35141436|hunter2\_]]. izolate acknowledges the hazard but considers it potentially acceptable against life-threatening cold [[./thread#^c35142758|izolate]]. No exposure measurements or safe operating conditions are established.
- Additional purification technology is not automatically considered worthwhile. jasonmarks\_ suggests UV disinfection near the furnace filter [[./thread#^c35142389|jasonmarks\_]], but cmui regards it as likely excessive for residential use given HEPA filtration [[./thread#^c35146211|cmui]]. Neither provides comparative infection, particle, or health measurements.

## Assumptions

- The integrated air-quality benefits remain prospective in this source unit. Animats quotes the admission that there are no physical prototypes [[./thread#^c35139067|Animats]], and cmui describes future engineering validation and home pilots [[./thread#^c35141779|cmui]]. Sensor-controlled ventilation, filtration, and humidity claims therefore cannot be treated as field-tested performance.
- Feeling better after managing CO2 does not isolate CO2 as the cause. agmetcalf reports more comfortable bedrooms with CO2-controlled intake [[./thread#^c35148015|agmetcalf]], and carom reports improved quality of life from managing household CO2 [[./thread#^c35148855|carom]]. Neither provides controls separating CO2 from accompanying changes in temperature, humidity, odors, or other indoor contaminants.
- Odor and distance from wildfires are being used as informal indicators rather than measurements. efields considers air quality unproblematic because the home is away from seasonal wildfires and smells neutral after recirculation [[./thread#^c35139360|efields]]. That account does not establish PM2.5, NO2, or CO2 concentrations or rule out unrecognized indoor sources.
- Some health language is unsupported by specific evidence. bythreads calls very dry indoor air unhealthy and advocates moisture-transferring heat exchangers, but supplies no humidity readings, diagnosed effects, or study results [[./thread#^c35142595|bythreads]]. ar9av also lists poor air quality among several HVAC frustrations without identifying pollutants or documenting associated symptoms [[./thread#^c35147027|ar9av]].
- An enthusiast’s interpretation is not confirmation of a product feature. nixonpjoshua1 understands the offering as an HRV integrated with a heat pump [[./thread#^c35147864|nixonpjoshua1]], whereas cmui says integral HRV/ERV functionality is still being considered according to application and total cost [[./thread#^c35142052|cmui]].
- The vehicle CO2 statement is broader than the documented personal comparison. robomartin claims that a couple of hours of driving means exposure above 1,400 ppm, but provides no trip readings, occupancy, ventilation settings, or measurement procedure in the supplied text [[./thread#^c35147657|robomartin]]. It should not be generalized to all cars or interpreted as a measurement of traffic-derived pollution.

## Conditions

- Building layout determines retrofit cost and feasibility. cmui initially targets North American homes [[./thread#^c35138528|cmui]]. mdasen, whose air handler is in an attic, anticipates difficulty supplying water for humidification and routing a fresh-air inlet, and asks how those functions would work across multiple mini-split heads [[./thread#^c35139323|mdasen]]. The proposed benefits are not equally straightforward in every house.
- Winter ventilation carries a comfort and energy cost. carom identifies incoming cold air as the main problem with controlling household CO2 [[./thread#^c35148855|carom]]. toast0 argues that continuously open windows require additional heating capacity and energy, with milder climates offering more latitude, and recommends heat recovery [[./thread#^c35139411|toast0]] [[./thread#^c35139818|toast0]]. This is a sizing and operating tradeoff, not evidence that heat pumps categorically forbid window opening.
- Night ventilation depends on local outdoor temperatures. tguvot reports that a whole-house fan operating overnight in the Bay Area reduces subsequent cooling needs [[./thread#^c35144112|tguvot]]. In the same reply chain, criddell explains that central Texas summer nights remain in the low 90s°F, requiring continuous air conditioning [[./thread#^c35144526|criddell]]. Neither account evaluates the outdoor air’s pollutant content.
- Very humid locations may favor separate dehumidification. faromanc reports local relative humidity reaching 99\% daily and a need for multiple or central dehumidifiers, without specifying measurement details [[./thread#^c35148341|faromanc]]. cmui recommends dedicated equipment when substantial cooling is unnecessary because using the proposed system’s resistance heaters for reheat would be expensive [[./thread#^c35148441|cmui]].
- Winter humidity targets depend on climate and construction. Responding to the request for 72°F and 50\% humidity [[./thread#^c35139713|zhoujianfu]], cmui says allowable winter humidity may need to be lower to prevent condensation within the structure [[./thread#^c35146113|cmui]]. mrDmrTmrJ’s interest in an ERV/HRV for parents in moist New England climate zone 5A further illustrates that ventilation and moisture requirements vary geographically [[./thread#^c35146981|mrDmrTmrJ]].
- The financial comparison is between different packages, not a measured price per unit of cleaner air. mdasen compares a quoted \$14,000 system with roughly \$4,000 equipment [[./thread#^c35139323|mdasen]]; cmui says the premium includes air-quality features and different cold-weather capacity ratings [[./thread#^c35138964|cmui]]. robomartin argues that effective standalone filtration can cost under \$100 [[./thread#^c35141784|robomartin]], but that comparison does not include equivalent fresh-air or humidity management.

## Lived experiences

- robomartin reports approximately a 1,000 ppm office CO2 difference between having windows open for about an hour and a typical day with windows closed [[./thread#^c35147657|robomartin]]. This is the clearest quantitative personal pollutant comparison in the supplied part. The text does not give absolute readings, sensor identification, calibration, occupancy controls, or a matched experimental procedure.
- robomartin also reports building a couple of Corsi–Rosenthal boxes using 20-by-30-inch filters, describing measurable filtration results and low power consumption [[./thread#^c35141784|robomartin]]. No particle concentrations, reduction percentages, wattages, sensor details, or measurement protocol accompany that report.
- agmetcalf built prototypes providing CO2-controlled fresh-air intake and found the bedrooms much more comfortable, emphasizing the experience of sleeping with the system for a week [[./thread#^c35148015|agmetcalf]]. The account contains no CO2 series or quantified sleep outcomes. For a future house, they favor hydronic heating with separate ventilation and filtration.
- carom says managing household CO2 greatly improved quality of life and expresses interest in HRV/ERV systems because winter ventilation introduces cold air [[./thread#^c35148855|carom]]. The improvement is firsthand but unspecified: no concentrations, particular symptoms, or cognitive measurements are supplied.
- kayson reports paying \$11,000 for a four-unit mini-split installation, conditioning living areas during the day and bedrooms at night, and using quiet standalone HEPA cleaners [[./thread#^c35138983|kayson]]. Their assessment that air quality is good is subjective; they explicitly leave CO2 accumulation as a potential unresolved problem.
- efields uses ducted Carrier heat pumps with electrostatic filters and a whole-house fan for periodic fresh-air exchange [[./thread#^c35139360|efields]]. They appreciate the neutral indoor smell and fan operation, but report no pollutant measurements. Their absence of nearby seasonal wildfires limits the account’s usefulness for smoke conditions.
- fauigerzigerk clarifies that their London home uses a gas boiler and keeps windows open in summer and much of spring and autumn to maintain freshness and discourage mold [[./thread#^c35140014|fauigerzigerk]]. This clarification follows concern that closed-window operation would rule out a heat pump [[./thread#^c35139664|fauigerzigerk]]; it should not be recast as an account of leaving windows open throughout winter.
- pgporada reports troublesome humidity after spray-foaming the house and wishes the work could be undone [[./thread#^c35164001|pgporada]]. They ask about ERV/HRV integration and moisture removal, but provide no humidity readings or established explanation of the problem. The account is consistent with toast0’s warning that changes to formerly drafty buildings can retain moisture if poorly managed [[./thread#^c35139411|toast0]].
- After extensive research for a new house, acjohnson55 chose a conventional gas furnace and air conditioner with an HRV [[./thread#^c35163664|acjohnson55]]. They wanted indoor-pollution exhaust, outdoor-pollution filtration, humidity control, and maintainability, but encountered fragmented professional advice and a contractor unfamiliar with HRVs. This records a design decision, not measured post-occupancy benefits.
- alkonaut describes personally using a combined exhaust-air heat pump that recovers heat from outgoing ventilation air for space heating and domestic hot water [[./thread#^c35149605|alkonaut]]. The account offers an existing alternative integration model, but no CO2, particle, humidity, or ventilation-rate results.
- criddell reports that the central Texas home struggles to remain at 80°F during outdoor temperatures above 100°F and that oven use can leave it hotter for days [[./thread#^c35139550|criddell]]. This is a cooking-related thermal-comfort account; it does not identify the oven’s fuel or measure cooking particles, NO2, or other emissions.
- Equipment noise provides a separate explanation for sleep-related discomfort. euph0ria reports compressor vibration near a bedroom and about 60 dB from a NIBE F370, without measurement conditions [[./thread#^c35138723|euph0ria]] [[./thread#^c35149841|euph0ria]]; installer konigschnell says this differs from customer feedback they usually hear [[./thread#^c35142078|konigschnell]]. j928734 reports a neighbor 15–20 feet from a Bosch unit being awakened, prompting greater use of backup heat [[./thread#^c35154262|j928734]]. These are mechanical-noise accounts, not evidence of pollutant-induced sleep disruption.
- aidenn0 reports a licensed contractor installing a furnace exhaust run 25 times longer than the manufacturer specified [[./thread#^c35153455|aidenn0]]. An inspector found the problem, and obtaining a subsequent quote including permits required contacting four HVAC contractors [[./thread#^c35174935|aidenn0]]. This illustrates installation-quality concerns relevant to combustion exhaust, without establishing actual exposure or health consequences.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Sensor accuracy and representativeness remain unknown. cmui proposes thermostat-mounted PM2.5 and CO2 sensing [[./thread#^c35139068|cmui]], but gives no models, calibration procedures, accuracy specifications, placement validation, or control thresholds. robomartin’s office comparison likewise lacks instrument validation [[./thread#^c35147657|robomartin]]. The part supports interest in measurement much more strongly than it supports conclusions about sensor reliability.
- The appropriate filtration design is unsettled. amluto asks whether HEPA filters incoming or recirculated air and challenges its pressure loss relative to MERV 16 [[./thread#^c35141281|amluto]]. cmui says pressure loss can be managed and filtration reduced when appropriate [[./thread#^c35142052|cmui]], but the requested system diagram is not supplied in the discussion [[./thread#^c35142589|amluto]]. Delivered particle removal, airflow, energy use, and replacement costs cannot be compared.
- HRV/ERV integration and its economic value remain open. zhyder wants energy-efficient ventilation specifically because CO2 is being discussed [[./thread#^c35141902|zhyder]], and fridder asks about compatibility with an existing ERV [[./thread#^c35140814|fridder]]. cmui treats integral recovery as application-dependent and undecided [[./thread#^c35142052|cmui]]. No complete ventilation design or comparative cost calculation resolves those requests.
- The net exposure effect of opening windows is not established. robomartin supplies a CO2 comparison [[./thread#^c35147657|robomartin]], while HEPA\_As\_Service questions reliance on outdoor air [[./thread#^c35140128|HEPA\_As\_Service]]. This part contains no corresponding indoor/outdoor PM2.5 or NO2 measurements, traffic-pollution comparison, or wildfire-smoke intervention test; efields specifically lacks nearby seasonal wildfire exposure [[./thread#^c35139360|efields]]. It therefore cannot establish when a CO2 benefit would be outweighed by imported pollutants.
- Health and cognition conclusions remain limited. agmetcalf’s bedroom-comfort report [[./thread#^c35148015|agmetcalf]] and carom’s quality-of-life improvement [[./thread#^c35148855|carom]] are positive firsthand accounts, but neither measures cognition, clinical outcomes, or sleep quality objectively. bythreads’ claim about unhealthy dryness is also unsupported by a study or measured outcome in the supplied text [[./thread#^c35142595|bythreads]].
- Participants disagree about the value of integration rather than expressing a common purchasing preference. borland broadly predicts little willingness to pay for air-quality features [[./thread#^c35149379|borland]], and jdlshore says indoor air quality was not a factor in their own purchase [[./thread#^c35145635|jdlshore]]. agmetcalf considers controlled fresh-air intake the most appealing feature [[./thread#^c35148015|agmetcalf]], while acjohnson55 actively seeks comprehensive air management [[./thread#^c35163664|acjohnson55]]. None establishes population-wide demand or consensus.
- The humidity consequences of larger cooling systems remain contested. brudgers warns that oversizing produces short cycles and inadequate moisture removal [[./thread#^c35140562|brudgers]]; criddell argues that continuously variable equipment can avoid that problem [[./thread#^c35143388|criddell]] [[./thread#^c35145727|criddell]]. adanto6840 regards variable-capacity systems as less susceptible, while acknowledging climate differences [[./thread#^c35140616|adanto6840]] [[./thread#^c35145068|adanto6840]]. No shared temperature-and-humidity measurements settle the disagreement.
