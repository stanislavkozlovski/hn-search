# [Monitoring my home's air quality with AirGradient's DIY sensor](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=28457135)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants share readings from home air monitors and discuss indoor pollution from cooking, gas appliances, candles, and wildfire smoke, including questions about which particles are harmful. Much of the discussion compares DIY and commercial sensors, their calibration, accuracy, longevity, and integration with home automation or monitoring systems. Practical topics include box-fan filters, HVAC circulation, ventilation design, CO2 buildup, and differences in housing and building codes across regions.
Tags: indoor air quality, air quality sensors, DIY electronics, cooking, ventilation, air filtration, CO2

## Relevance

This discussion offers firsthand indoor PM2.5 and CO2 measurements, including cooking and wildfire smoke, and compares ventilation and filtration approaches. It also discusses sensor calibration, drift, false CO2 estimates, and uncertainty about how harmful different particulate sources are. ## Arguments

- Household monitoring can reveal actionable sources and show whether mitigation changes readings. macNchz [[./thread#^c28459143|macNchz]] connected pollution spikes to cooking and reductions to filtration; Imagenuity [[./thread#^c28474573|Imagenuity]] reports discovering fumes leaking from an attached garage. These are household observations, not controlled health studies.
- Filtration and ventilation address different problems in these accounts. macNchz [[./thread#^c28459143|macNchz]] reports reducing particulate levels with a box fan and furnace filter, while karmelapple [[./thread#^c28459963|karmelapple]] reports lowering CO2 by opening windows. Neither account establishes that particulate filtration removes CO2.
- Controlled outdoor-air ventilation offers an alternative to relying on building leakage. nsporillo [[./thread#^c28459662|nsporillo]] favors supplying filtered outdoor air intentionally, and davemp [[./thread#^c28459663|davemp]] recommends energy recovery ventilation for airtight homes. metafunctor [[./thread#^c28467462|metafunctor]] describes a Finnish system combining filtered supply air, continuous exhaust, and heat recovery.
- Air must reach the occupied room. Scene\_Cast2 [[./thread#^c28459403|Scene\_Cast2]] reports that opening a window did not lower hallway CO2 without air movement. nsporillo [[./thread#^c28459769|nsporillo]] proposes door undercuts, transfer grilles, or separate ventilation; geerlingguy [[./thread#^c28460844|geerlingguy]] warns that draft blockers can obstruct an existing return-air path. These circulation measures do not themselves establish the amount of outdoor air supplied.
- Gas cooking adds concerns beyond particles generated by food and cookware. velosol [[./thread#^c28464979|velosol]] distinguishes gas combustion's CO2 and other byproducts from induction cooking's remaining food-and-pan emissions. nyghtly [[./thread#^c28460269|nyghtly]] quotes external sources about carbon monoxide, formaldehyde, and other pollutants; those are cited claims, not gas measurements performed in this discussion.
- Cooking technique may reduce pollution without abandoning cooking altogether. graeme [[./thread#^c28465873|graeme]] reports lower AQI readings when avoiding burning residue, cleaning between successive foods, and finishing with residual heat. In reply to a clarification, graeme [[./thread#^c28475921|graeme]] confirms that this means switching the heat off before cooking is finished.
- For quantitative CO2 monitoring, commenters favor sensors that actually measure CO2. tpmx [[./thread#^c28459670|tpmx]] recommends checking for NDIR sensing; numpad0 [[./thread#^c28463227|numpad0]] warns that VOC-derived eCO2 can drift and correlate poorly with actual CO2. s0rce [[./thread#^c28460842|s0rce]] reports satisfactory operation of a photoacoustic SCD41, without presenting reference-calibration results.
- Window decisions can use several measurements together. acidburnNSA [[./thread#^c28466701|acidburnNSA]] describes balancing CO2 against particulates during smoke season. macNchz [[./thread#^c28466288|macNchz]] uses VOC, CO2, and PM trends to decide when to open or close windows, while largely ignoring brief fluctuations.
- Reducing indoor combustion is another proposed intervention. jvanderbot [[./thread#^c28458730|jvanderbot]] warns against incense after monitoring their home and reports using electric candles for relaxation [[./thread#^c28460133|jvanderbot]]. This is a practical substitution account; it does not quantify the health benefit of replacing candles or incense.

## Counterarguments

- The claim that cooking particles are necessarily extremely harmful is disputed. goodpoint [[./thread#^c28459436|goodpoint]] argues that unburnt cooking oil and water-related readings should not be equated with combustion pollution. macNchz [[./thread#^c28460817|macNchz]] responds with claims about research finding harmful cooking particles while acknowledging uncertainty about their toxicity relative to vehicle exhaust. The thread establishes neither harmlessness nor equal toxicity.
- A blanket explanation of cooking spikes as water vapor conflicts with two reported tests. macNchz [[./thread#^c28460817|macNchz]] observed no PM increase from an ultrasonic humidifier using distilled water, but an increase with tap water. 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c28459538|5e92cb50239222b]] reports unchanged PMS5003 readings during very high humidity but large increases during frying. eutectic [[./thread#^c28459627|eutectic]] separately asks about suspended water droplets; that distinction remains unanswered.
- Electric cooking does not eliminate cooking particles. jozzy-james [[./thread#^c28465685|jozzy-james]] reports visible oil and smoke while searing on an electric stove with only a small window. beenBoutIT [[./thread#^c28463632|beenBoutIT]] emphasizes emissions from heated food and cookware and recommends an effective hood with enough replacement air. This qualifies, rather than disproves, the argument that avoiding gas removes an additional combustion source.
- A running range hood may not exhaust outdoors. sneak [[./thread#^c28460061|sneak]] reports repeatedly discovering that apparently functioning hoods recirculated air. klondike\_klive [[./thread#^c28461333|klondike\_klive]] found a hood with neither an outside outlet nor a filter, producing a ceiling stain. Conversely, macNchz [[./thread#^c28459143|macNchz]] explicitly reports cooking spikes despite an outside-venting hood, so recirculation cannot explain every account.
- More outdoor air is not automatically better. Tenoke [[./thread#^c28460866|Tenoke]] questions drawing air from a street-facing side rather than another side plus a purifier. hadlock [[./thread#^c28463053|hadlock]] observed a worse particulate indicator after opening a window. In the opposite direction, newbamboo [[./thread#^c28459639|newbamboo]] chose ventilation even during wildfire smoke because headaches improved; that symptom-led choice does not establish that smoke exposure was harmless.
- The claim that replacement windows necessarily make ventilation inadequate is challenged. geerlingguy [[./thread#^c28460844|geerlingguy]] warns about tightening a house without adding heat recovery ventilation. turtlebits [[./thread#^c28466078|turtlebits]] distinguishes thermal efficiency from air leakage and recommends a blower-door test. nsporillo [[./thread#^c28461082|nsporillo]] also expresses uncertainty about whether renovations would reduce leakage sufficiently to require mechanical ventilation.
- DIY box-fan success does not settle which filter arrangement works best under sustained smoke. wintermutestwin [[./thread#^c28460398|wintermutestwin]] considered a box fan inadequate and preferred an inline fan with a grow-shop carbon filter. Exuma [[./thread#^c28466008|Exuma]] reports dissatisfaction with an existing grow filter and seeks something better. Neither supplies a controlled comparison or enough filter specifications to resolve the conflict.
- Warnings that a filter will necessarily create a box-fan fire hazard are countered by quoted testing. throwawaysea [[./thread#^c28460570|throwawaysea]] recommends a multi-filter design partly for safety; rovr138 [[./thread#^c28463565|rovr138]] quotes a UL report describing five tested fans remaining operational without observed hazard or damage during seven-hour tests, including severe obstruction. That evidence concerns the tested configurations, not every fan or indefinite operation.
- More monitoring can also mean more anxiety and distracting alerts. tclancy [[./thread#^c28464472|tclancy]] questions the benefit of constant notifications after establishing generally good air quality. macNchz [[./thread#^c28466288|macNchz]] initially worried about VOC spikes before associating many with flatulence and changing how they interpreted transient readings. wiether [[./thread#^c28469961|wiether]] asks about monitoring-related stress, but no comparative health evidence is supplied.

## Assumptions

- This analysis concerns only story 28457135, part 0. The accounts are self-selected and differ substantially: macNchz [[./thread#^c28459143|macNchz]] describes a drafty apartment with limited occupant-related CO2 buildup, while Scene\_Cast2 [[./thread#^c28459403|Scene\_Cast2]] describes four-digit bedroom readings in a modern home. Their differences cannot establish population-wide prevalence or Hacker News consensus.
- Agreement between instruments supports consistency, not necessarily absolute accuracy. danans [[./thread#^c28459289|danans]] argues that even two matching devices could share an error and that regional outdoor AQI is not a controlled point-reference measurement. geerlingguy [[./thread#^c28460896|geerlingguy]] supplies useful cross-device comparisons, but no traceable calibration experiment.
- The reported quantities are not interchangeable. crazygringo [[./thread#^c28459577|crazygringo]] gives large numerical PM2.5 changes without units; graeme [[./thread#^c28465873|graeme]] describes AQI categories; teekert [[./thread#^c28468737|teekert]] describes a purifier's colored indicator and fan response. These observations support source detection but cannot all be converted into comparable mass concentrations, exposure doses, or health risks.
- Symptoms and sensor readings occurring together do not isolate a cause. newbamboo [[./thread#^c28462880|newbamboo]] strongly associates VOC readings with migraines, while nsporillo [[./thread#^c28461082|nsporillo]] associates poor indoor air with rhinitis. Neither presents controlled exposure testing. Likewise, macNchz's phrase about CO2 reaching drowsy territory [[./thread#^c28459143|macNchz]] does not establish a measured cognitive threshold.
- A device marketed for air quality does not necessarily measure every relevant pollutant. Someone1234 [[./thread#^c28459079|Someone1234]] notes that the Wave Plus lacks PM2.5 despite measuring other things; philips [[./thread#^c28459459|philips]] notes missing CO2 capability in PurpleAir. newbamboo's speculation about an unidentified substance, including radon [[./thread#^c28462880|newbamboo]], is not a measurement identifying that substance.
- Linked studies and evaluations remain commenter-reported evidence here. pyk [[./thread#^c28465139|pyk]] summarizes a study about differing particulate toxicity; fotta [[./thread#^c28460014|fotta]] claims some inexpensive sensors outperform expensive instruments; rovr138 [[./thread#^c28463565|rovr138]] reproduces excerpts of fan testing. Their supplied descriptions can be analyzed, but the linked materials were not independently examined.

## Conditions

- Building volume, occupancy, leakage, and internal airflow condition the results. macNchz [[./thread#^c28459143|macNchz]] describes two people in a roughly 700-square-foot, drafty apartment; karmelapple [[./thread#^c28459963|karmelapple]] describes two people in a roughly 1,200-square-foot condo with substantial CO2 buildup. nsporillo [[./thread#^c28459769|nsporillo]] describes a bedroom supplied by HVAC but dependent on a hallway return. Floor area or building age alone does not explain ventilation.
- Ventilation arrangements vary geographically and by construction date. rootusrootus [[./thread#^c28462201|rootusrootus]] describes one return per floor as common in the Pacific Northwest; windexh8er [[./thread#^c28463025|windexh8er]] describes returns in every room in upper-Midwest homes. metafunctor [[./thread#^c28467462|metafunctor]] describes Finnish supply air flowing toward wet-room exhausts through door gaps. twothamendment [[./thread#^c28466447|twothamendment]] reports local return requirements changing and later being reversed; herbst [[./thread#^c28467635|herbst]] says some central-European homes have no such returns.
- Window ventilation depends on temperature differences and air movement. 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c28459573|5e92cb50239222b]] reports dramatically faster CO2 removal with a winter indoor–outdoor difference of at least 50°C than in summer. crazygringo [[./thread#^c28460897|crazygringo]] reports difficult ventilation during mild New York weather. These extreme-cold and seasonal examples should not be treated as a universal window-opening schedule.
- Outdoor conditions differ sharply even within the discussion. macNchz [[./thread#^c28461008|macNchz]] describes normally low particulate pollution on a leafy Brooklyn street and questions the need for a major retrofit. wintermutestwin [[./thread#^c28460398|wintermutestwin]] describes roughly two months of severe western smoke. 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c28459538|5e92cb50239222b]] keeps windows closed because of severe outdoor pollution, while zeku [[./thread#^c28463110|zeku]] identifies seasonal pollen and heat as major Tennessee comfort constraints.
- Housing and financial constraints limit source control. jozzy-james [[./thread#^c28465703|jozzy-james]] attributes the lack of ventilation to a century-old house and lack of money. klondike\_klive [[./thread#^c28461333|klondike\_klive]] describes a poorly equipped rental kitchenette. Cooking preferences also matter: toastal [[./thread#^c28470658|toastal]] values browning and fond, while graeme [[./thread#^c28475941|graeme]] reports that browning can sometimes occur without a large AQI spike but can quickly become polluting.
- The 2021 prices illustrate a time-versus-money tradeoff rather than current shopping guidance. hadlock [[./thread#^c28463053|hadlock]] compares quoted DIY components costing \$60–80 with a \$200 ready-made monitor and values the saved time. tpmx [[./thread#^c28459670|tpmx]] lists CO2 sensor components at \$48–96; 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c28459454|5e92cb50239222b]] reports prices above \$100 each for some gas sensors. dhritzkiv [[./thread#^c28462096|dhritzkiv]] adds Canadian shipping costs, fragmented sourcing, and a preference for a CAD 200–300 kit or assembled device.
- Noise, airflow, availability, and ongoing power consumption affect usability. wintermutestwin [[./thread#^c28460398|wintermutestwin]] accepts noise from a \$150 inline-fan setup. Scene\_Cast2 [[./thread#^c28459498|Scene\_Cast2]] proposes quieter high-static-pressure fans, but gives no completed performance results. withinrafael [[./thread#^c28460893|withinrafael]] questions the cost of a continuously running bathroom fan; velosol's approximately 15 W estimate [[./thread#^c28464953|velosol]] is conditional, not a measurement of that apartment's fan.
- Longitudinal monitoring also depends on data access and support. secabeen [[./thread#^c28459288|secabeen]] reports a working MQTT-based DIY monitor. Havoc [[./thread#^c28482270|Havoc]] describes local access complicated by tokens and potentially blocked by firmware updates; reacharavindh [[./thread#^c28485864|reacharavindh]] wants an isolated local network. macNchz [[./thread#^c28466167|macNchz]] is uncertain about future app support, and sliken [[./thread#^c28466081|sliken]] objects to an Airthings app's persistent tracking prompt.
- Thermal comfort and ventilation costs are climate-specific. asciimov [[./thread#^c28459214|asciimov]] describes tolerating approximately 78–82°F in South Texas and the cost of much colder indoor settings. reaperducer [[./thread#^c28459315|reaperducer]] found 82°F comfortable in a dry desert climate while their wife preferred 78°F. dexwiz [[./thread#^c28464187|dexwiz]] emphasizes that relative humidity needs temperature context, limiting comparisons based on a humidity percentage alone.
- Some users knowingly prioritize sensory comfort. sneak [[./thread#^c28461253|sneak]] prefers real flames and dripping wax; eulers\_secret [[./thread#^c28463383|eulers\_secret]] describes candle ambiance as a personal risk tradeoff. These preferences explain resistance to electric substitutes but do not quantify or rebut particle-related health risks.

## Lived experiences

- macNchz [[./thread#^c28459143|macNchz]], later identifying the monitor as an Awair Element [[./thread#^c28459870|macNchz]], reports medium-to-high-heat cooking producing PM2.5 readings exceeding local hazy-day levels and remaining elevated for hours with windows closed. During NYC wildfire haze with outdoor PM2.5 of 40–70 µg/m³, a box fan with a furnace filter reportedly brought the measured level to around 5 within minutes. Gas-oven use was the main activity producing their concerning CO2 readings.
- Scene\_Cast2 [[./thread#^c28459403|Scene\_Cast2]] reports four-digit CO2 readings when sleeping behind a closed door in a modern house, without reporting a sleep-quality outcome. karmelapple [[./thread#^c28459963|karmelapple]] reports CO2 exceeding 1,400 ppm with gas cooking in a former condo, reaching 800–900 ppm with two people working at home, and dropping below 600 ppm after windows were opened.
- crazygringo [[./thread#^c28459577|crazygringo]] reports a toaster oven raising PM2.5 readings from 50 to 1,500 in ten minutes even without food inside, and gas cooking raising kitchen CO2 readings from about 500 to 1,300 in twenty minutes with the hood running; units are not explicitly stated in that comment. In response to a suggestion to clean the toaster, crazygringo [[./thread#^c28461099|crazygringo]] says crumbs were removed but polymerized oil was difficult to clean. The particles were not identified.
- 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c28459573|5e92cb50239222b]] reports winter CO2 falling from above 4,000 ppm to about 450 within minutes of opening one window, compared with hours to reach 600–700 in summer. crazygringo [[./thread#^c28460897|crazygringo]] reports approximately 850 with a cracked window during mild weather and difficulty getting below 650 even with open windows and a bathroom fan, despite satisfactory readings during winter and hot summer weather.
- dghughes [[./thread#^c28465018|dghughes]] reports a Netatmo reading above 1,000 ppm during a six-person Christmas gathering in a 1970s house without HVAC. A cross-draft reportedly reduced it to 300 ppm, and the room felt substantially less stuffy. The reported endpoint has no accompanying calibration check.
- 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c28459538|5e92cb50239222b]] reports PMS5003 readings above 100 µg/m³ when frying eggs and above 300 µg/m³ after slightly burning meat. In the same closed-window home, the commenter reports humidity reaching 100\% after mopping without changing PM readings. This is a single-device observation, not validation of humidity immunity across sensors.
- hadlock [[./thread#^c28463053|hadlock]] reports five months of satisfactory PurpleAir operation: opening a condo window changed its indicator from green to yellow, while closing the window and running a filter restored green. teekert [[./thread#^c28468737|teekert]] observes a parents' Philips purifier changing from blue to red and increasing fan activity during cooking, air-fryer use, and outdoor barbecue lighting. Neither supplies pollutant concentrations for those indicator changes.
- newbamboo [[./thread#^c28459639|newbamboo]] reports indoor TVOC readings around 0.70–1.0 mg/m³, falling to roughly 0.6 with all windows open and fans running. They attribute migraines and brain fog to indoor VOCs and report headache relief even when ventilation admits wildfire smoke. In follow-up, newbamboo [[./thread#^c28462880|newbamboo]] identifies an \$80 Temtop, reports near-zero outdoor TVOC and agreement with nearby PurpleAir particulate readings, but doubts the formaldehyde channel and cannot identify the VOC source.
- Other health accounts are less quantified. nsporillo [[./thread#^c28461082|nsporillo]] reports rhinitis alongside perceived poor indoor air and is considering controlled ventilation during renovations. personjerry [[./thread#^c28463210|personjerry]] reports breathing difficulties and wants to investigate possible humidity, CO2, or PM2.5 contributions; no causal finding or completed monitoring result is supplied.
- jvanderbot [[./thread#^c28458730|jvanderbot]] reports generally satisfactory indoor air using an SPS30-based system and HEPA filtration in the A/C airflow, including when outdoor air was moderately poor. blacksmith\_tb [[./thread#^c28458864|blacksmith\_tb]] qualifies that experience by noting substantial cooking spikes when the monitor is in the kitchen. bfirsh [[./thread#^c28464241|bfirsh]] reports a furnace fan and filter quickly clearing wildfire and cooking smoke, without numerical results in the supplied comment.
- geerlingguy [[./thread#^c28460896|geerlingguy]] reports two identical boards and a TemTop unit agreeing within usually less than 1\% for CO2 and differing approximately 5–10\% for PM2.5. Temperature was the largest discrepancy. geerlingguy [[./thread#^c28460855|geerlingguy]] proposes moving the temperature sensor away from warmed electronics; the apparent 82°F reading should be read alongside the stated actual summer thermostat range of 78–80°F and humidity below 50\% [[./thread#^c28460937|geerlingguy]].
- Awair reliability accounts include both sustained use and recoverable faults. richardfontana [[./thread#^c28466105|richardfontana]] reports an anomalously high PM reading after about sixteen months that returned to normal after blowing on the sensor, suggesting an obstruction. macNchz [[./thread#^c28466167|macNchz]] reports no apparent drift after a year but occasional crashes fixed by power cycling. Scene\_Cast2 [[./thread#^c28459775|Scene\_Cast2]] reports an older dust-sensor clogging problem and replacing a newer Honeywell sensor for \$40 after a fall damaged its fan.
- karmelapple [[./thread#^c28460013|karmelapple]] reports eight months of Aranet4 operation without recharging and appreciates its display. s0rce [[./thread#^c28460842|s0rce]] reports a working SCD41, display, and microcontroller setup; the follow-up notes integrated temperature and humidity sensing [[./thread#^c28462947|s0rce]]. These are usability accounts, not evidence establishing either device's absolute accuracy.
- macNchz [[./thread#^c28466288|macNchz]] reports using temperature and humidity charts to recognize that working above approximately 81°F left them irritable and unproductive. reaperducer [[./thread#^c28459315|reaperducer]] describes comfort at 82°F in a desert climate, while asciimov [[./thread#^c28459214|asciimov]] describes acclimatization and the importance of moving air. The accounts support individual and contextual differences, not a universal comfort or cognitive cutoff.

## Unresolved disagreements

- How much does particulate composition change health risk at the same reading? jeffwass [[./thread#^c28463265|jeffwass]] explicitly asks about toast versus diesel exhaust. pyk [[./thread#^c28465139|pyk]] says composition matters and cites a study, while pirate787 [[./thread#^c28463606|pirate787]] believes toxic payloads increase danger but both sources remain harmful. These claims do not resolve goodpoint's broader assertion that some cooking particles are harmless [[./thread#^c28459436|goodpoint]], or establish source-specific dose–response relationships.
- Do the reported CO2 levels impair cognition or sleep in these homes? Scene\_Cast2 [[./thread#^c28459403|Scene\_Cast2]] reports bedroom accumulation but no measured sleep change; macNchz [[./thread#^c28459143|macNchz]] uses a drowsiness-related label without presenting cognitive testing. newbamboo's brain-fog attribution concerns VOC readings instead [[./thread#^c28459639|newbamboo]]. This unit contains no controlled cognitive assessment, sleep tracking comparison, or demonstrated CO2-specific symptom threshold.
- NO2 evidence is particularly limited. 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c28459454|5e92cb50239222b]] describes extreme local NO2 and SO2 and expensive sensors, but supplies no concentrations, measurement method, indoor comparison, or intervention results. Tenoke's street-facing ventilation concern [[./thread#^c28460866|Tenoke]] is unresolved, and nsporillo's claims about traffic ultrafines and HEPA difficulty [[./thread#^c28461217|nsporillo]] are not supported by in-thread measurements. PM or CO2 improvements cannot be treated as demonstrated NO2 improvements.
- How much calibration does a finished DIY monitor need? danans [[./thread#^c28459597|danans]] argues for testing the assembled device against known conditions; ashtonkem [[./thread#^c28480360|ashtonkem]] sees little reason to recalibrate a self-contained PM module with its own fan. jiveturkey [[./thread#^c28461233|jiveturkey]] stresses uncertainty near AQI category boundaries. Robotbeat's request for affordable CO2 monitoring independent of an outdoor-air calibration assumption [[./thread#^c28460811|Robotbeat]] receives no answer in the supplied discussion.
- Which smoke-filtration setup delivers the best actual particulate removal? wintermutestwin [[./thread#^c28478168|wintermutestwin]] claims approximately 600 CFM and more filtration from an inline setup but does not know its MERV rating. rsync [[./thread#^c28467133|rsync]] questions the implied comparison with a box fan, and Exuma [[./thread#^c28466108|Exuma]] questions whether a grow filter's particulate rating is adequate. Filter thickness, nominal airflow, and favorable impressions do not provide a common measured performance comparison.
- The continuously running bathroom fan's purpose and effectiveness remain unverified. withinrafael [[./thread#^c28461165|withinrafael]] corrects the initial description: air is leaving the apartment. That correction undermines applying hourislate's positive-pressure intake explanation [[./thread#^c28461073|hourislate]] to this installation. velosol [[./thread#^c28464953|velosol]] proposes whole-building ventilation compliance, while asdff [[./thread#^c28461011|asdff]] suggests mold prevention; no airflow, power, or before-and-after air-quality measurements settle the question.
- Whether the electronic air cleaner emits ozone is unanswered. clipradiowallet [[./thread#^c28459697|clipradiowallet]] suggests detecting it by smell; Johnny555 [[./thread#^c28459773|Johnny555]] wants a sensor because the duct-delivered air is diluted, and explicitly clarifies that ozone production is unconfirmed and unwanted [[./thread#^c28460507|Johnny555]]. dperfect [[./thread#^c28459592|dperfect]] reports using an inexpensive MQ131 but acknowledges uncertain accuracy and environmental sensitivity. No completed emissions test is supplied.
- The net health tradeoff between ventilating polluted indoor air and admitting wildfire smoke is unknown. newbamboo [[./thread#^c28459639|newbamboo]] reports immediate headache relief, while macNchz [[./thread#^c28459143|macNchz]] demonstrates a particulate-reduction strategy during smoke. Neither establishes the long-term comparative risk of the alternative exposures. Similarly, wiether's question about monitoring-related stress versus pollution harm [[./thread#^c28469961|wiether]] has no evidentiary resolution.
- Some proposed alternatives carry untested claims. JoeDaDude [[./thread#^c28462424|JoeDaDude]] raises induction-cooktop electromagnetic exposure, but sagarm [[./thread#^c28465758|sagarm]] asks for evidence and mentions only a possible pacemaker concern; no comparative health data follows. sneak [[./thread#^c28460088|sneak]] expects beeswax candles to be better than paraffin and plans to measure them, but supplies no results. Neither claim should be treated as an established exposure tradeoff.
