# [Better Business Bureau rejects Molekule’s air purifier claims](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=22457326)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants discuss whether Molekule’s purifier marketing overstated the performance of its PECO technology, particularly after independent testing found weak particle removal and the company disputed the findings. The debate distinguishes laboratory tests of a filter technology from the performance of a complete device, and compares it with conventional HEPA purifiers and DIY fan-filter builds. Other threads question the BBB’s role and credibility, consumer-protection enforcement, influencer marketing, and how to evaluate air purifiers and air-quality monitors. People also share experiences with wildfire smoke, allergies, product failures, filter costs, and claims about viruses.
Tags: Molekule, air purifiers, PECO technology, HEPA filters, product testing, false advertising, consumer protection, BBB, influencer marketing, wildfire smoke

## Relevance

The discussion compares purifier performance and filtration, including firsthand experiences during wildfire smoke, PM2.5 measurements, and the tradeoff between filtering particles and ventilating to manage overnight CO2. It also questions sensor accuracy and claims about purifier effectiveness. ## Arguments

- Useful household filtration can mean reducing particle concentrations without creating a clean room. naravara [[./thread#^c22457626|naravara]] makes that distinction, while wongarsu [[./thread#^c22457774|wongarsu]] argues that continuous airflow through HEPA and activated-carbon filters can substantially reduce room particle counts within half an hour. The latter is a general claim without accompanying measurements; the thread also contains instrument-backed personal accounts.
- Evaluate the finished purifier rather than assuming promising filter chemistry guarantees effective room cleaning. PragmaticPulp [[./thread#^c22457553|PragmaticPulp]] reports poor Molekule results from Wirecutter and Consumer Reports while allowing that PECO might complement HEPA. jjeaff [[./thread#^c22457531|jjeaff]] quotes reporting that some advertised claims rested on prototypes or the PECO filter alone. These are accounts of external testing and marketing, not the commenters' own experiments.
- Measurements make purifier endorsements more assessable. heliodor [[./thread#^c22458967|heliodor]] challenges tbrock's unsupported statement that their Winix units work great [[./thread#^c22457940|tbrock]]. In that reply branch, shock [[./thread#^c22459373|shock]] supplies measurements from their own, unidentified purifiers. Those measurements should not be attributed to tbrock's Winix devices.
- Particle control and ventilation emerge as separate household concerns. clairity [[./thread#^c22458412|clairity]] reports satisfactory particle control despite some building leakage and welcomes air exchange because of overnight CO2 accumulation. stordoff [[./thread#^c22459965|stordoff]] reports substantially lower overnight CO2 after opening an internal door farther. Neither account establishes that the purifier itself removes CO2.
- Several commenters favor inexpensive fan-and-filter arrangements. antisthenes [[./thread#^c22458343|antisthenes]] proposes two MERV-13 furnace filters arranged in a V on a box fan; wasdfff [[./thread#^c22458408|wasdfff]] proposes attaching a HEPA filter to a fan. These are practical alternatives offered by commenters, but their sweeping claims of outperforming nearly all commercial products are not accompanied by comparative tests.
- Sensor selection and calibration deserve scrutiny alongside purifier performance. Youden [[./thread#^c22458414|Youden]] describes a study comparing Foobot with research-grade instruments that reportedly found underestimated temperature and tVOC, overestimated PM2.5, and poorly correlated CO2 output. Youden criticizes inferred CO2 and inadequate calibration, favoring suitable calibrated sensors, including NDIR for CO2. This is a report about a linked study, not firsthand validation of every consumer monitor.

## Counterarguments

- fortran77 [[./thread#^c22457597|fortran77]] initially argues that small room purifiers accomplish little without frequent whole-room filtration and protection against outside-air entry. Later, fortran77 [[./thread#^c22459508|fortran77]] narrows the objection to Molekule's claims about viruses and destruction rather than capture. BubRoss [[./thread#^c22458851|BubRoss]] points out that this narrower objection does not answer the replies challenging the original broad dismissal of portable filtration.
- eternalny1 [[./thread#^c22459162|eternalny1]] questions whether small devices process enough air and argues that human evolution makes filtration largely unnecessary. shock [[./thread#^c22459275|shock]] counters with claims about historically high particulate exposure and remembered papers connecting pollution with cognition and health. Neither supplies the relevant historical measurements or study results in this exchange; these arguments should not be treated as demonstrated physiological conclusions.
- iudqnolq [[./thread#^c22457891|iudqnolq]] reports a particularly unfavorable Wirecutter smoke test: roughly 6\% particle removal on Molekule's low setting versus approximately 15\% disappearance through natural settling, and approximately 60\% removal on high versus approximately 90\% for \$100 filters. The low-setting comparison concerns particle persistence under that reported test, not proof that the device generated additional particles.
- The reply chain distinguishes particle capture from proven sterilization. jonahhorowitz [[./thread#^c22459762|jonahhorowitz]] says suitable filters capture very small particles and UV can sterilize air. OJFord [[./thread#^c22460080|OJFord]] qualifies the latter using the linked source's distinction between difficult-to-prove sterilization and measurable disinfection or log reduction. No pathogen-removal or infection-outcome experiment is supplied here.
- Not everyone regards a separate purifier as worthwhile for every household. prostoalex [[./thread#^c22458451|prostoalex]] considers asthma and allergy susceptibility strong reasons to buy one but suggests routine central HVAC filter replacement may otherwise suffice. Marsymars [[./thread#^c22459038|Marsymars]] bought one for wildfire smoke and pollen allergies and would not bother without visible symptoms. These are purchasing criteria, not evidence that symptom-free exposure is harmless or that HVAC filtration is universally adequate.
- Review credibility is contested. ilamont [[./thread#^c22457648|ilamont]] emphasizes competitor-funded NAD proceedings, and Jam-B [[./thread#^c22458416|Jam-B]] alleges bias in both BBB and Consumer Reports without providing specific evidence in that comment. 1123581321 [[./thread#^c22457590|1123581321]] distinguishes NAD arbitration from BBB accreditation sales, while lxm [[./thread#^c22458378|lxm]] reports a separate Consumer Reports assessment advising avoidance. These institutional disagreements do not themselves resolve the purifier's measured performance.

## Assumptions

- Interpreting a sensor reading as personal exposure assumes suitable calibration, placement, and sampling. shock's near-zero purifier-outlet readings [[./thread#^c22459373|shock]] do not establish near-zero concentrations throughout a room. hedora's claimed household PM2.5 reduction [[./thread#^c22457644|hedora]] lacks a specified measurement procedure, and betterworldb's question about the measuring instrument [[./thread#^c22461686|betterworldb]] receives no supplied answer.
- Attributing symptom changes to filtration assumes the intervention caused the change. clairity [[./thread#^c22458412|clairity]] reports less coughing and sneezing, and cmroanirgo [[./thread#^c22468645|cmroanirgo]] associates smoky conditions with lung symptoms and impaired cognitive ability. These are meaningful personal observations, but the comments do not control other exposures, quantify cognition, or establish clinical outcomes.
- Pollutant categories cannot be treated as interchangeable on this evidence. borkt [[./thread#^c22458121|borkt]] speculates that Molekule addresses VOCs better than large particles, while jjeaff [[./thread#^c22457531|jjeaff]] questions whether underlying PECO tests establish retail-product VOC performance. Neither supplies gas measurements; shock's particulate readings [[./thread#^c22459373|shock]] cannot establish VOC, NO2, CO2, or pathogen removal.
- Judgments about acceptable exposure differ and remain assumptions rather than demonstrated thresholds. stordoff [[./thread#^c22459965|stordoff]] describes 1600–1800 ppm CO2 as not necessarily a problem but undesirable, while clairity [[./thread#^c22458412|clairity]] worries about unacceptable overnight accumulation. eternalny1 [[./thread#^c22476697|eternalny1]] predicts they would tolerate the same smoky room despite an autoimmune disorder; that hypothetical comparison does not establish another person's susceptibility.

## Conditions

- Geography and pollution episodes shape the reported value of filtration. peferron [[./thread#^c22458449|peferron]] describes operating purifiers in Chinese smog and California wildfires; cmroanirgo [[./thread#^c22459746|cmroanirgo]] describes months of Australian bushfire smoke and dust. Spooky23 [[./thread#^c22457729|Spooky23]] instead describes a city home exposed to tree pollen, dog dander, and diesel soot from a nearby main road. These settings should not be treated as equivalent exposure conditions.
- The window tradeoff depends on the incoming air. peferron's reported reductions during smog and wildfire exposure require closed windows and doors [[./thread#^c22458449|peferron]], whereas clairity [[./thread#^c22458412|clairity]] welcomes some outdoor-air exchange to limit CO2 accumulation. Together, these suggest that opening windows may help ventilation when incoming air is suitable but undermine particle control during smoke or smog. This is a conditional inference; the supplied accounts do not include a controlled exterior-window experiment measuring both pollutants.
- Upfront prices span inexpensive DIY equipment and premium products. NoblePublius [[./thread#^c22457915|NoblePublius]] suggests a \$15 fan plus a \$3 filter, without performance measurements or a specified filter grade. peferron [[./thread#^c22458449|peferron]] describes DIY or sub-\$200 HEPA options, and hedora [[./thread#^c22457644|hedora]] names a \$200 Honeywell. naner [[./thread#^c22458576|naner]] considers Molekule substantially more expensive than effective HEPA systems. These are prices and comparisons reported in March 2020, not current quotations.
- Replacement costs depend on pollution loading. clairity [[./thread#^c22458412|clairity]] reports replacing Blueair filters twice yearly for about \$100 annually. During Australian smoke and dust, cmroanirgo [[./thread#^c22459746|cmroanirgo]] reports replacement after 1.5 months despite an expected 12-month life, describing clogging and apparent dust/smoke output. That account suggests unusually severe conditions can change the maintenance budget; it does not establish a universal replacement schedule or failure mechanism.
- Purchase price does not capture reliability or return difficulties. hedora [[./thread#^c22457644|hedora]] reports frequent discounts exceeding 50\% on the Bissell but also unwanted shutdowns and unsuitable automatic fan behavior. hkeide [[./thread#^c22458308|hkeide]] reports difficulty obtaining a Molekule return. Conversely, borkt [[./thread#^c22469124|borkt]] confirms the municipal-office unit was donated, correcting their earlier uncertainty about whether public money had purchased it.

## Lived experiences

- peferron [[./thread#^c22458449|peferron]] says they own a PM2.5 counter and have used purifiers in Chinese smog and California wildfire conditions. They report that a consumer HEPA unit in a bedroom commonly reduces PM2.5 concentrations tenfold within a few hours with windows and doors closed. No starting concentrations, instrument model, calibration, or time series accompany this firsthand summary.
- shock [[./thread#^c22459373|shock]] reports using a Sensirion particulate sensor at two purifier outlets: one produced all-zero readings and the other readings much lower than ambient. Their bedroom output lists PM1.0 at 0.88 and PM2.5, PM4.0, and PM10.0 each at 0.93. Units are not supplied, and the outlet measurements must be distinguished from the bedroom reading.
- stordoff [[./thread#^c22459965|stordoff]] reports overnight CO2 of 1600–1800 ppm with an internal door open approximately 5 cm. Opening it about 15 cm farther reportedly brought typical readings to approximately 600 ppm, rarely above 700 ppm. With the door shut, the same room reportedly rose from 800 to 1600 ppm in well under an hour. Sensor type, occupancy, and calibration are unspecified; no accompanying sleep or cognition outcome is reported.
- clairity [[./thread#^c22458412|clairity]] reports an immediate and sustained reduction in coughing and sneezing after buying a Blueair 211+, described as having HEPA and activated carbon. An unspecified air-quality sensor suggested leakage through the old building's doors and windows did not overwhelm the purifier. No numerical pollutant readings or clinical assessment are supplied.
- cmroanirgo [[./thread#^c22459746|cmroanirgo]] describes severe breathing discomfort during Australian bushfire smoke and dust, feeling close to needing hospitalization, and finding the filtered space the only comfortable place to breathe. A ceiling fan reportedly helped serve two rooms totaling about 25 square metres. In a later clarification, cmroanirgo [[./thread#^c22468645|cmroanirgo]] says saved referred to relief from harmful conditions, not a claim of otherwise dying; they also report cognitive effects, limited relief from air conditioning, outdoor use of carbon N95/P2 masks, and an unspecified air-quality readout. They doubt the purifier's advertised 70-square-metre coverage.
- Spooky23 [[./thread#^c22457729|Spooky23]] reports a noticeable benefit from a purifier in the front room during peak pollen and dog-shedding seasons, in a home near nut trees and a road contributing diesel soot. This is a subjective account of mixed exposures; it includes neither a PM2.5 measurement nor a separation of traffic particles from gases.
- hedora [[./thread#^c22457644|hedora]] reports leaving a Honeywell HPA300 running and adding a Bissell Air400 for cooking smoke, allergies, or wildfires. Together, the devices reportedly lower PM2.5 by more than 90\%. The Honeywell seems sufficient for their three-bedroom house despite a 465-square-foot rating. The Bissell is quieter and includes a PM2.5 meter, but reportedly shuts itself off and selects fan speeds too low for the house. The exact instrument and method behind the percentage remain unspecified.
- borkt [[./thread#^c22458121|borkt]] reports no noticeable air-quality improvement from a Molekule supplied to city hall during a wildfire emergency. Their suggestion that the technology was better suited to VOCs than particles is an explanation they propose, not a measured finding. borkt [[./thread#^c22469124|borkt]] subsequently confirms the unit was free.
- johnzim [[./thread#^c22457668|johnzim]] bought a Molekule after trusting testimonials from podcasters with allergies but reports no significant improvement over a regular HEPA filter. They describe limited airflow and a HEPA filter at the base. This is a comparative personal impression, not a measured finding of zero filtration.
- hkeide [[./thread#^c22458308|hkeide]] expresses similar disappointment, reports being ignored while seeking a return, and describes objectionable offgassing. The commenter does not identify or measure the emitted substances, so the account establishes perceived emissions and discomfort rather than a particular toxic exposure.
- gregd [[./thread#^c22457890|gregd]] reports that their bedroom Winix automatically extinguishes most blue lights in darkness and that tape covers the remaining indicator. They quote a review warning that covering Molekule's glow would obstruct airflow; that warning is not their own Molekule test. sn\_master [[./thread#^c22458080|sn\_master]] reports a Honeywell button that turns off its blue LEDs. These are concrete bedroom-comfort considerations, without measured sleep outcomes.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The dispute over whether portable filtration can meaningfully protect a person remains unsettled between the participants. eternalny1 [[./thread#^c22465147|eternalny1]] demands scientific airflow and particulate numbers and continues interpreting the Australian account as a claim about preventing death [[./thread#^c22476697|eternalny1]]. cmroanirgo [[./thread#^c22468645|cmroanirgo]] explicitly describes relief from lung and cognitive effects instead. Part of the disagreement concerns the meaning of saved, and the parties never establish a shared quantitative criterion for benefit.
- Health improvements are reported but their magnitude and causes remain uncertain. clairity's reduced coughing and sneezing [[./thread#^c22458412|clairity]] and cmroanirgo's lung and cognitive account [[./thread#^c22468645|cmroanirgo]] do not establish which pollutants caused the symptoms or their long-term consequences. stordoff's overnight CO2 readings [[./thread#^c22459965|stordoff]] contain no cognitive or sleep testing, and gregd's sleep concern [[./thread#^c22457890|gregd]] concerns device lighting. This unit does not establish a CO2 threshold for impaired cognition or sleep.
- NO2 is not directly assessed in this source unit. Spooky23 [[./thread#^c22457729|Spooky23]] mentions traffic-related diesel soot, and hedora [[./thread#^c22457644|hedora]] mentions cooking smoke, but neither measures NO2 or separates gaseous exposure from particles. The comments provide no comparison of cooking fuels, kitchen extraction strategies, or NO2 sensor reliability.
- Molekule's broader performance remains disputed rather than demonstrated here. gonehome [[./thread#^c22457910|gonehome]] quotes the company's defense of aggregate superiority despite poor particulate results, while alephnan [[./thread#^c22457675|alephnan]] reports advertisements challenging Wirecutter's technical competence. The supplied discussion does not contain a resolved methodological rebuttal or retail-device measurements establishing VOC or virus destruction. Xcelerate's request for evidence about Mila [[./thread#^c22459111|Xcelerate]] also receives no supplied answer.
- There is no tested operating rule for balancing closed-window particle protection against overnight ventilation needs. peferron [[./thread#^c22458449|peferron]] reports particle reductions with rooms closed, clairity [[./thread#^c22458412|clairity]] tolerates leakage to address CO2, and stordoff [[./thread#^c22459965|stordoff]] changes an internal door opening. Simultaneous indoor/outdoor measurements, exterior-window trials, and resulting health or comfort comparisons would be needed to resolve that tradeoff for a particular home.
- Comparative performance and adverse emissions remain incompletely characterized. antisthenes [[./thread#^c22458343|antisthenes]] and wasdfff [[./thread#^c22458408|wasdfff]] offer broad DIY superiority claims without comparative measurements, while hedora's household reduction [[./thread#^c22457644|hedora]] lacks a specified protocol. hkeide's perceived offgassing [[./thread#^c22458308|hkeide]] remains chemically unidentified. fortran77's historical claim about a Sharper Image purifier producing dangerous ozone [[./thread#^c22457597|fortran77]] is not an ozone measurement of Molekule or evidence that all purifier technologies share that risk.
