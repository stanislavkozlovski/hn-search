# [Air Quality Monitors Compared: AirGradient vs. Purple Air](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40362848)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants discuss a comparison of AirGradient and PurpleAir monitors, weighing sensor performance alongside data access, ownership, repairability, cost, and cloud dependence. The thread expands into questions about reliable CO₂ monitors, NDIR sensors, automatic baseline calibration, and the limits of inexpensive or imitation devices. Participants compare consumer products and DIY builds, discuss monitoring VOCs and radon, and question how well low-cost sensors can be validated without reference instruments. Personal experiences with bedroom CO₂ readings lead to debate about ventilation, health claims, and whether monitoring is useful even when exact readings are uncertain.
Tags: air-quality monitors, AirGradient, PurpleAir, CO₂ sensors, calibration, data ownership

## Relevance

The comments compare particulate, CO2, VOC, and NOx sensors and discuss calibration, accuracy, sensor drift, and whether readings are actionable. Firsthand accounts describe high bedroom CO2, cooking-related PM2.5, wildfire decisions, and using readings to ventilate or adjust filtration; commenters disagree about the significance of elevated CO2 and the precision needed for practical action. ## Arguments

- CO2 monitoring can reveal inadequate ventilation even when its direct health effects remain disputed. After retracting his description of 'suffocating,' flacebo [[./thread#^c40365134|flacebo]] argued that CO2 remains a useful ventilation indicator; his critic thaumasiotes [[./thread#^c40370482|thaumasiotes]] explicitly agreed with that narrower claim.
- Some participants argue that elevated CO2 deserves attention below levels associated with suffocation. sooheon [[./thread#^c40365055|sooheon]] linked a study as evidence that complex cognitive tasks suffer above 1000 ppm. flacebo [[./thread#^c40365134|flacebo]] attributed poorer sleep, cognition, and headaches above that level to articles he had read. These are claims about research, not firsthand measurements of those outcomes.
- For an enclosed room, ahaucnx [[./thread#^c40363506|ahaucnx]] recommended a genuine NDIR CO2 sensor and configurable automatic baseline calibration: extend the interval to 30 or 90 days, or disable it and calibrate manually. He favored SenseAir while considering several other manufacturers adequate. defrost [[./thread#^c40363928|defrost]] additionally emphasized maintenance, contamination, and following calibration instructions.
- Monitor usefulness includes access to measurements and long-term maintainability. AirGradient-affiliated commenter ahaucnx [[./thread#^c40363194|ahaucnx]] argued that many monitors share similar PM modules, making data ownership, API access, cost, and repairability significant differentiators. His assertion that accuracy often differs little was not supported by comparative measurements in the supplied comments.
- For VOC sensing, ahaucnx [[./thread#^c40363237|ahaucnx]] favored the SGP41, quoting a study reporting better response to nine of ten tested solvents and an additional NOx signal. juntloop [[./thread#^c40363361|juntloop]] agreed about sensitivity, response speed, and stability, while distinguishing the SGP41's external humidity requirement and open algorithm from the BME688's integrated humidity sensor and closed software. Neither supplied calibrated NO2 measurements.

## Counterarguments

- The strongest CO2 alarm language was challenged and withdrawn. thaumasiotes [[./thread#^c40364960|thaumasiotes]] invoked the body's perception of suffocation; flacebo [[./thread#^c40365134|flacebo]] acknowledged that his measured levels were far below those causing suffocation. infecto [[./thread#^c40364983|infecto]] considered some literature overblown, but later allowed that demanding cognitive tasks could be affected while rejecting the suffocation interpretation [[./thread#^c40365817|infecto]]. gureddio [[./thread#^c40365047|gureddio]] expressed a broader opinion that the concern was unnecessarily fashionable.
- thaumasiotes [[./thread#^c40370482|thaumasiotes]] challenged the blanket assertion that CO2 above 1000 ppm causes headaches, while acknowledging that changes in sleep and cognitive performance can be difficult to perceive. This disputes an asserted symptom threshold; it does not establish that elevated CO2 or poor ventilation has no effects.
- A precise monitor may be unnecessary when the practical objective is simply taking ventilation breaks. IanCal [[./thread#^c40364717|IanCal]] proposed scheduling breaks using occupancy, activity, and room volume, and said he personally abandoned monitor shopping in favor of regularly opening a window. His people-minute examples were suggestions, not validated exposure limits.
- Imperfect absolute accuracy need not defeat a coarse ventilation alarm. D13Fd [[./thread#^c40489818|D13Fd]] considered a hypothetical error of about ±200 ppm acceptable for his exercise-room alarm, while BrianHenryIE [[./thread#^c40363503|BrianHenryIE]] recommended interpreting readings comparatively after observing disagreement between devices. Neither account establishes that any magnitude of calibration error is harmless.
- Additional gas-sensing features are not automatically useful. dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c40364014|dzhiurgis]] reported largely ignoring his device's VOC measurement, whereas poulpy123 [[./thread#^c40366440|poulpy123]] considered it useful when there is a particular unhealthy source to monitor. juntloop [[./thread#^c40363361|juntloop]] also judged the BME688's theoretically programmable gas profiles practically unhelpful.

## Assumptions

- Automatic baseline correction depends on an exposure assumption. pstrateman [[./thread#^c40363802|pstrateman]] said meters assume periodic exposure to atmospheric CO2 and therefore under-read when continuously indoors. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c40363506|ahaucnx]] described configurable alternatives. The supplied evidence does not establish pstrateman's broader claim that every indoor-only meter becomes miscalibrated almost immediately.
- The requested music-room solution assumes that occupancy and ventilation can be managed together. squarefoot [[./thread#^c40363472|squarefoot]] described an almost tightly closed room with potentially several musicians; IanCal's [[./thread#^c40364717|IanCal]] timer alternative would require reasonable estimates of occupancy, exertion, room volume, and air exchange that were not supplied.
- Sensor authenticity is a purchasing concern rather than a demonstrated failure in this account. squarefoot [[./thread#^c40363472|squarefoot]] reported finding complaints about alcohol sensors sold as CO2 monitors and counterfeit MH-Z19 modules. He did not report personally testing one of those suspect products.
- The comparison's provenance matters: ahaucnx [[./thread#^c40363506|ahaucnx]] referred to sensors used in 'our monitors' and discussed AirGradient's review plans [[./thread#^c40363169|ahaucnx]]. rcdemski [[./thread#^c40363055|rcdemski]] perceived the comparison as honest, but that favorable impression does not make it independent testing. The linked comparison itself is not included in this source unit.
- Product endorsements are not interchangeable with accuracy evidence. fifteenforty [[./thread#^c40364646|fifteenforty]] listed Aranet4, AirThings View Plus, a Sensirion gadget, and the Kickstarter AirSpot without measurements; xnx [[./thread#^c40363106|xnx]] expressed satisfaction with View Plus. These establish recommendations or satisfaction, not comparative performance.
- Coverage is uneven: the concrete exposure measurements concern bedroom and exercise-room CO2, reported by flacebo [[./thread#^c40364843|flacebo]] and D13Fd [[./thread#^c40489818|D13Fd]]. The unit provides no quantitative PM2.5 exposure series, calibrated NO2 readings, filtration outcomes, or cooking-, traffic-, or wildfire-specific exposure accounts. ahaucnx's [[./thread#^c40363237|ahaucnx]] NOx-sensor discussion cannot fill those gaps.

## Conditions

- Affordability and feature requirements constrain the choice. squarefoot [[./thread#^c40363472|squarefoot]] wanted inexpensive CO2-only monitoring; joecool1029 [[./thread#^c40364483|joecool1029]] described an older low-cost NDIR unit as useful without being laboratory-grade. scionthefly [[./thread#^c40369538|scionthefly]] recalled paying substantially more for datalogging than for a simple display about ten years earlier, a historical rather than current price comparison.
- Continuous ventilation carries an energy tradeoff. In a temperate European country, flacebo [[./thread#^c40364843|flacebo]] considered humidity generally acceptable and therefore favored HRV over ERV, but struggled to find Home Assistant integration. His interim arrangement of permanently tilted windows controlled CO2 while, in his assessment, being poor for energy efficiency. No installed HRV or ERV results were reported.
- Opening windows depends on what is outside. squarefoot [[./thread#^c40364528|squarefoot]] believed his new tree-rich surroundings offered clean air for calibration, without supplying outdoor measurements. jumploops [[./thread#^c40364266|jumploops]] instead attributed exposure to roof off-gassing entering through windows opened to remove indoor flooring smells. Neither account supports a universal recommendation to open or close windows.
- Local access can change ownership costs and usefulness. jhot [[./thread#^c40363665|jhot]] disliked Purple Air's paid API changes but configured direct uploads to his own endpoint. nucleardog [[./thread#^c40364180|nucleardog]] valued AirQ's offline operation, local history, and replaceable sensors, while finding it expensive. poulpy123 [[./thread#^c40364889|poulpy123]] valued AirGradient's open hardware and software but wanted SD-card recording.
- Radon introduces a separate location and budget question. KRAKRISMOTT [[./thread#^c40363299|KRAKRISMOTT]] considered its absence a major omission and claimed only Airthings offered it; poulpy123 [[./thread#^c40364877|poulpy123]] doubted dedicated sensing would fit a €150–200 monitor. defrost [[./thread#^c40364959|defrost]] identified enclosed spaces in granite-rich areas as settings of concern. These comments contain no household radon measurements or verified market comparison.

## Lived experiences

- flacebo [[./thread#^c40364843|flacebo]] reported a DIY monitor reading 3600 ppm after eight hours of sleep and exceeding 1000 ppm within an hour with two people in the bedroom. Keeping two windows tilted open in another room, with connecting doors open, reportedly kept CO2 below 1000 ppm. He reported no measured improvement in sleep, cognition, or headaches after the change.
- D13Fd [[./thread#^c40489818|D13Fd]] reported that heavy exercise behind a closed door repeatedly triggered a 1500 ppm alarm. He found the alarm useful for identifying stale air; his suggested ±200 ppm error was a tolerance judgment, not an independently measured sensor error.
- BrianHenryIE [[./thread#^c40363503|BrianHenryIE]] bought three Aranet4 units and found that the two given to siblings consistently read about 100 apart when adjacent. xvector [[./thread#^c40364296|xvector]] separately praised his device's screen and months of battery life. The latter observations concern usability, not measurement accuracy.
- In the Qingping discussion, pasc1878 [[./thread#^c40364070|pasc1878]] reported temperature readings 3–4°C below sensors from three or four other manufacturers. dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c40365282|dzhiurgis]] observed a similar problem and an ineffective offset adjustment, while lacking a reference for CO2 or PM beyond outdoors. pasc1878 [[./thread#^c40392695|pasc1878]] later reported agreement with other CO2 monitors and matching low/high timing with another air-quality device; this was not a quantitative PM calibration. dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c40364007|dzhiurgis]] also used threshold notifications.
- poulpy123 [[./thread#^c40370636|poulpy123]] reported occasional CO2 readings below outside-air levels and considered a 20–50 ppm error inconsequential. The identical statement also appears as poulpy123 [[./thread#^c40370635|poulpy123]]; these are one repeated account, not two independent observations. Neither post establishes the actual error magnitude.
- jumploops [[./thread#^c40364266|jumploops]] reported headaches in a recently remodeled building and credited VOC monitoring with helping identify an air-quality problem. He attributed it to roof fumes entering through windows opened for flooring odors. His Kaiterra Laser Egg and an off-brand handheld device mostly agreed, according to jumploops [[./thread#^c40363071|jumploops]], but no concentrations, identified chemicals, or symptom-resolution results were supplied.
- retSava [[./thread#^c40365519|retSava]] reported disappointing BME688 results for methane and frustration with the experimentation required to obtain useful detection. He was moving to NDIR after an anticipated twentyfold component-cost reduction proved unrealistic for his application. This is experience with a particular sensing task, not a comparative indoor-health study.
- nucleardog [[./thread#^c40364180|nucleardog]] described AirQ working without an account or internet connection, retaining local history, exposing data through its IP address, supporting Home Assistant, and allowing sensor replacement. He explicitly lacked a reference for assessing its accuracy; his separate purchase of a humidity reference did not validate its pollutant sensors.
- rcdemski [[./thread#^c40363055|rcdemski]] reported satisfactory Purple Air ownership while wanting better data access. jhot [[./thread#^c40363665|jhot]] described an implemented workaround that parses the monitor's uploads and sends PM2.5 and temperature to Home Assistant. This demonstrates data integration, without reporting exposure reductions or reference accuracy.
- poulpy123 [[./thread#^c40364889|poulpy123]] was happy with his AirGradient monitor but could not compare it with a reference. He cited a favorable assessment by an organization monitoring Paris air quality, which is secondhand validation in this record. His proposed outdoor DIY build remained a future project.
- joecool1029 [[./thread#^c40364483|joecool1029]] described owning a basic CO2meter RAD-0301 for roughly six years; his favorable expectations for its successor were not firsthand testing. scionthefly [[./thread#^c40369538|scionthefly]] called an approximately ten-year-old Extech CO210 reliable and useful for logging. alfozan [[./thread#^c40363809|alfozan]] reported over five years of satisfactory Awair use and a custom dashboard. These are durability and usability accounts without reference measurements.
- Connectivity influenced satisfaction: sofixa [[./thread#^c40365211|sofixa]] praised Apollo AIR/MSR integration with Home Assistant; nov21b [[./thread#^c40370781|nov21b]] chose an Airthings Wave Plus partly for Bluetooth access to readings; gnosek [[./thread#^c40365228|gnosek]] praised a TFA Dostmann monitor with USB measurement access. None provided pollutant-response tests.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The health implications of the reported CO2 levels remain unsettled within this discussion. sooheon [[./thread#^c40365055|sooheon]] claimed evidence of cognitive effects above 1000 ppm, flacebo [[./thread#^c40365134|flacebo]] extended concern to sleep and headaches, and infecto [[./thread#^c40365817|infecto]] allowed possible cognitive effects while rejecting suffocation language. The supplied comments do not resolve effect sizes, exposure duration, individual susceptibility, or direct CO2 effects versus other consequences of poor ventilation.
- Calibration-error magnitude is directly disputed. poulpy123 [[./thread#^c40370636|poulpy123]] treated below-outdoor readings as potentially only 20–50 ppm off; pstrateman [[./thread#^c40371179|pstrateman]] insisted the error must be much larger. poulpy123's request for an explanation [[./thread#^c40377366|poulpy123]] received no supplied answer. No contemporaneous reference readings settle the disagreement.
- Whether a dual-beam sensor substantially improves this calibration problem remains unanswered. summm [[./thread#^c40371332|summm]] proposed the SCD30 in reply to ahaucnx's [[./thread#^c40363506|ahaucnx]] NDIR and calibration advice, but the supplied thread contains no comparison or response.
- Absolute PM and gas-sensor performance remains uncertain. pasc1878 [[./thread#^c40392695|pasc1878]] supplied cross-device agreement rather than reference calibration, nucleardog [[./thread#^c40364180|nucleardog]] explicitly lacked an accuracy reference, and ahaucnx [[./thread#^c40363237|ahaucnx]] supplied a study-based VOC/NOx comparison. These do not establish PM2.5 accuracy across aerosol types or reliable, quantitative NO2 exposure measurement.
- The ventilation accounts do not establish one best operating strategy. IanCal [[./thread#^c40364717|IanCal]] found routine window opening sufficient for his purposes, while flacebo [[./thread#^c40364843|flacebo]] found intermittent airing inadequate for sustained bedroom CO2 control. jumploops [[./thread#^c40364266|jumploops]] described outdoor fumes entering through open windows. The unit does not compare these approaches under matched occupancy, weather, outdoor pollution, or energy conditions.
- The proposed inexpensive radon shortcut remains unvalidated. defrost [[./thread#^c40364959|defrost]], citing decades of geophysical surveying, proposed comparing generic Geiger-counter readings indoors and outdoors. poulpy123 [[./thread#^c40365391|poulpy123]] challenged the gamma-based reasoning because radon emits alpha particles; defrost [[./thread#^c40365510|defrost]] responded by invoking gamma emissions and decay products. No measurements validate the shortcut or the accompanying comparison with pack-a-day smoking risk.
