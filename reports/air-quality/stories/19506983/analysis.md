# [Build an Air Quality Monitor with InfluxDB, Grafana and Docker on a Raspberry Pi](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=19506983)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants discuss building a Raspberry Pi air-quality monitoring and dashboard system, focusing on sensor choice, data accuracy, and what different sensors actually measure. They compare particulate, CO₂, VOC, ozone, and humidity sensing, calibration, field reliability, and suitable reference measurements. Another major thread debates whether InfluxDB, Grafana, Docker, and fleet-management tooling are appropriate for a hobby sensor project, balancing simplicity against reproducibility, deployment, updates, and learning value. Users also share alternative dashboards, storage systems, and DIY designs.
Tags: air quality monitor, Raspberry Pi, InfluxDB, Grafana, Docker, sensor accuracy, DIY electronics, time-series data

## Relevance

The thread covers air-monitor sensor types and accuracy, including the distinction between VOC estimates and actual CO2 or particulate measurements. It also includes firsthand indoor CO2 readings, ventilation responses, and questions about sleep, closed windows, traffic particles, and household sources. ## Arguments

- Air quality requires several distinct measurements. Animats says the featured sensor measures VOCs, temperature, pressure, and humidity, but not CO, CO2, or particles [[./thread#^c19510162|Animats]]. retSava proposes separately monitoring PM2.5/PM10, CO/NOx, CO2, VOCs, and humidity [[./thread#^c19510953|retSava]]. These comments argue against treating one sensor's generic air-quality output as a complete exposure assessment.
- Monitoring trends can inform ventilation decisions even when absolute accuracy is uncertain. Replying to calibration concerns, tpxl argues that a baseline can reveal deterioration, qualified as 'for some definition of worse' [[./thread#^c19520456|tpxl]]. msisk6 supplies an anecdotal application: elevated displayed CO2 accompanied feeling unwell, prompting changes to ventilation [[./thread#^c19512230|msisk6]].
- Particle monitoring is relevant to combustion and traffic exposure. hadlock identifies wood smoke and car exhaust as major PM2.5 sources [[./thread#^c19510526|hadlock]]; retSava additionally identifies wheel–road interaction as a particle source and traffic exhaust as a source of CO and NOx [[./thread#^c19510953|retSava]]. These are source-attribution claims, not measurements of these commenters' personal exposure.
- Off-gassing requires attention to gases as well as particles. hadlock recommends formaldehyde and other gas sensors for concerns about furnishings and materials [[./thread#^c19510526|hadlock]]. retSava tentatively associates VOCs with cancer and other harms, explicitly relying on recollection; the comment provides no study or exposure measurements supporting that health claim [[./thread#^c19510953|retSava]].
- Several commenters argue that accessible DIY particle monitoring is practical. zensavona recommends adding a Plantower PMS5003 based on building similar devices [[./thread#^c19510567|zensavona]], while wuyishan points to Luftdaten as a crowdsourced project using inexpensive sensors [[./thread#^c19509880|wuyishan]]. These establish available approaches and reported experience, rather than demonstrated measurement accuracy.

## Counterarguments

- Opening a window can involve competing exposures. msisk6 reports using an open door or window among measures that relieved the closed-office problem, but also observes PM2.5 rising when the office window is open [[./thread#^c19512230|msisk6]]. This account supports evaluating ventilation and particles together; it supplies neither outdoor reference readings nor a quantified comparison of the two exposures.
- VOC-based outputs should not automatically be interpreted as measured CO2. joshvm explicitly distinguishes 'equivalent CO2' from actual CO2 measurement [[./thread#^c19510559|joshvm]]. This qualifies gpm's suggestion that VOCs can be useful proxies for CO2 and air circulation [[./thread#^c19508951|gpm]], without establishing that every proxy is useless.
- A sensor responding to an event does not establish its accuracy. TeMPOraL observes higher SDS011 readings when an indoor humidifier runs, but lacks a reference instrument and questions calibration [[./thread#^c19511161|TeMPOraL]]. The supplied account cannot distinguish a meaningful particle change from a measurement problem, much less establish a corresponding health effect.
- The reported association between CO2 and feeling unwell does not establish causation or a universal threshold. msisk6 attributes discomfort to readings above 1500 ppm but acknowledges uncertainty about the instrument's accuracy [[./thread#^c19512230|msisk6]]. No controlled comparison separates CO2 from other conditions in the closed office.
- Commercial convenience has its own constraints. msisk6 likes Awair partly because it avoids adding substantial maintenance after work [[./thread#^c19512230|msisk6]]. In a separate reply to an Awair recommendation, zantana says the requirement for Wi-Fi connectivity was a deal breaker [[./thread#^c19512193|zantana]]. These are different user priorities, rather than agreement on one best monitor.

## Assumptions

- TheSpiceIsLife suspects overnight CO2 accumulation in a small bedroom shared with two 16 kg dogs, especially after installing double glazing and bedroom heat pumps [[./thread#^c19510287|TheSpiceIsLife]]. This is an occupancy-and-ventilation hypothesis: the commenter reports neither bedroom CO2 measurements nor an observed sleep effect.
- TheSpiceIsLife wonders whether a 56-year-old house, furnishings or finishes several years old, and little fresh paint imply limited off-gassing [[./thread#^c19510342|TheSpiceIsLife]]. hadlock redirects attention toward furniture and electronics but does not establish how emissions change with age [[./thread#^c19510526|hadlock]]. The presumed protection from older materials remains unverified.
- Trend-based interpretation assumes that changes in the output meaningfully track changes of interest. tpxl's baseline argument [[./thread#^c19520456|tpxl]] and msisk6's reliance on CO2 deltas despite uncertain absolute accuracy [[./thread#^c19512230|msisk6]] depend on that assumption. Similarly, gpm's proposed VOC proxy assumes a useful relationship with CO2 or ventilation that is not demonstrated here [[./thread#^c19508951|gpm]].
- TheSpiceIsLife's proposed supply of HEPA-filtered outdoor air assumes that an appropriate airflow and operating schedule would improve bedroom conditions while preserving acceptable warmth [[./thread#^c19510287|TheSpiceIsLife]]. The suggested small continuous fan or larger timed fan are proposals, not tested interventions; no filtration performance or required ventilation rate is established.

## Conditions

- The March 2019 component prices suggest different costs for different measurements: Animats quotes \$12.80 for the temperature/pressure/humidity/VOC sensor [[./thread#^c19510162|Animats]], and zensavona quotes approximately \$20 for a PMS5003 particle sensor [[./thread#^c19510567|zensavona]]. These are component-price claims, not prices for complete, independently validated monitors.
- Reported CO2 sensor prices vary substantially. joshvm estimates about \$100 for a good infrared-absorption sensor [[./thread#^c19510598|joshvm]]; Nanite suggests NDIR modules without automatic calibration for under \$20 in China [[./thread#^c19510849|Nanite]]; gsich names MH-Z19 and MH-Z14 modules at about €20 [[./thread#^c19511579|gsich]]. No comparative accuracy results establish whether these differently priced options satisfy the same requirements.
- Geography affects which particles deserve attention. After pookieinc reports moving to the Middle East and struggling to find a demonstrably accurate monitor [[./thread#^c19510441|pookieinc]], mike\_h suggests that some locations there may require PM10 dust monitoring rather than the PM2.5-only devices in the linked comparison [[./thread#^c19510933|mike\_h]]. The advice is explicitly location-dependent, not a characterization of the whole region.
- Housing and weather constrain ventilation choices. msisk6 describes a roughly year-old, well-insulated Texas house [[./thread#^c19512230|msisk6]]. TheSpiceIsLife wants heat retention after window upgrades but also fresh air, and reports airing the house even when it is '-5 outside' [[./thread#^c19510287|TheSpiceIsLife]] [[./thread#^c19510342|TheSpiceIsLife]]. Neither account quantifies heating costs or identifies an optimal ventilation–comfort balance.
- Product availability was also disputed in this 2019 discussion: borumpilot says Awair ships only to the US and Canada [[./thread#^c19511123|borumpilot]], while Luc responds with a UK storefront [[./thread#^c19512042|Luc]]. The exchange does not establish actual delivery coverage elsewhere or current availability.

## Lived experiences

- msisk6 reports placing an Awair on a desk after feeling unwell while working with the office door and windows closed. Discomfort repeatedly coincided with displayed CO2 above 1500 ppm. Reported remedies included opening the door or window, running the central-air fan, and increasing the system's outside-air 'bleed' adjustment. The commenter explicitly doubts absolute measurement accuracy; no before-and-after values or isolated intervention results are supplied [[./thread#^c19512230|msisk6]].
- In the same home, msisk6 observes PM2.5 rising with the office window open and a 'chemicals' reading fluctuating at a low level [[./thread#^c19512230|msisk6]]. The commenter is surprised that the new house does not show problems on those outputs but does not know what 'chemicals' means. This is a device-specific observation, not evidence that the home lacks every relevant gaseous pollutant.
- TheSpiceIsLife already practices intermittent ventilation without reporting monitor data: opening doors or windows, using the kitchen exhaust fan to draw air through the house, and sometimes opening several entrances for about ten minutes [[./thread#^c19510342|TheSpiceIsLife]]. The account describes behavior and a wish to avoid device maintenance, with no measured air replacement, pollutant reduction, or health outcome.
- TeMPOraL operates a Luftdaten module with an SDS011 particle sensor and reports higher readings during indoor humidifier use [[./thread#^c19511161|TeMPOraL]]. They also question the calibration of their DHT11/DHT22 temperature and humidity devices. The experience illustrates the difficulty of interpreting inexpensive instruments without a nearby reference, rather than proving that their readings are wrong.
- zensavona reports building several ESP-based devices with laser dust sensors and recommends the PMS5003, without supplying numerical validation results [[./thread#^c19510567|zensavona]]. luma reports building 20 units using PMS5003 particle sensors, ozone sensors, GPS, and ESP32 controllers, with six deployed in the field and testing at an EPA monitoring station [[./thread#^c19511038|luma]]. Deployment and testing are firsthand claims; comparison results are absent.
- retSava reports home relative humidity of 10–15\%, considers 30–50\% desirable, and plans a monitor including humidity and temperature for sleeping conditions [[./thread#^c19510953|retSava]]. The commenter also notes that some sensors' heating elements complicate battery operation. No completed intervention or resulting improvement in sleep or comfort is reported.

## Unresolved disagreements

- How accurate are the recommended inexpensive sensors? gpm asks directly about accuracy and alternatives [[./thread#^c19508720|gpm]]. mike\_h links a laboratory comparison but supplies no findings [[./thread#^c19510933|mike\_h]]; gpm links a detailed calibration analysis involving a related sensor rather than the featured one [[./thread#^c19508847|gpm]]; luma mentions EPA-site testing without results [[./thread#^c19511038|luma]]. The supplied text therefore cannot establish calibration error, agreement with reference instruments, or a best-performing monitor.
- Is relative change sufficient when calibration is unknown? TeMPOraL questions unverified DIY readings and criticizes a rental-monitor company for undisclosed sensors and missing error bars [[./thread#^c19511161|TeMPOraL]]. tpxl directly responds that a baseline can still reveal worsening [[./thread#^c19520456|tpxl]], while msisk6 accepts trends for personal decisions [[./thread#^c19512230|msisk6]]. The discussion does not resolve when those trends are reliable enough or when absolute accuracy is essential.
- Which ventilation or filtration strategy works best remains unanswered. TheSpiceIsLife asks about filtered outdoor supply, continuous versus timed fans, and whether the proposed effort matters [[./thread#^c19510287|TheSpiceIsLife]]. msisk6 reports several simultaneous adjustments, including an outside-air inlet, so that account does not isolate the contribution of the central fan, window, door, or inlet setting [[./thread#^c19512230|msisk6]]. No successful HEPA installation or quantified heat-retention tradeoff is reported.
- The health and cognition evidence is limited to an uncontrolled discomfort account. msisk6 reports feeling unwell alongside elevated displayed CO2 [[./thread#^c19512230|msisk6]]; TheSpiceIsLife raises an unmeasured overnight concern [[./thread#^c19510287|TheSpiceIsLife]]; retSava gives preferred sleeping conditions without sleep outcomes [[./thread#^c19510953|retSava]]. These comments provide no cognitive testing, measured sleep changes, or study results establishing effects at the reported concentrations.
- NO2, cooking emissions, and wildfire smoke remain evidence gaps in this source unit. retSava names traffic-related NOx without reporting NO2 measurements [[./thread#^c19510953|retSava]]. hadlock mentions wood smoke and car exhaust generally, without a wildfire account [[./thread#^c19510526|hadlock]]. TheSpiceIsLife's kitchen-exhaust use is described as household ventilation, not a measured cooking-emissions experiment [[./thread#^c19510342|TheSpiceIsLife]]. These mentions cannot support exposure-specific conclusions.
- The interpretation and persistence of indoor chemical emissions remain uncertain. TheSpiceIsLife asks whether materials continue off-gassing throughout their lives [[./thread#^c19510342|TheSpiceIsLife]], hadlock identifies possible sources without answering that duration question [[./thread#^c19510526|hadlock]], and msisk6 does not know what the monitor's 'chemicals' output represents [[./thread#^c19512230|msisk6]]. Neither building age nor a low generic reading settles the question.
- Spatial representativeness is unresolved. bhargav asks whether one Awair was used throughout an entire house and receives no answer in the supplied replies [[./thread#^c19509322|bhargav]]. msisk6's observations concern a monitor on an office desk [[./thread#^c19512230|msisk6]]; they do not establish bedroom conditions or whole-house exposure.
