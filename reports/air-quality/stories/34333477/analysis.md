# [A US federal agency is considering a ban on gas stoves](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=34333477)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion reacts to news that a US federal safety agency is considering action related to gas-stove emissions, with many participants clarifying whether an outright ban is actually proposed. Participants debate evidence linking gas cooking to indoor pollution and childhood asthma, the role of ventilation and range hoods, and the quality of the underlying studies. Practical concerns include rural power reliability, emergency cooking, household costs, landlord retrofits, and electrical capacity. The thread compares gas, coil electric, and induction cooking, including performance and user experience, and broadens into arguments about regulation, mandates, grid resilience, and consumer choice.
Tags: gas stoves, indoor air, health research, induction cooking, ventilation, regulation

## Relevance

The discussion directly covers indoor pollution from gas stoves, possible asthma and cognitive effects, ventilation and hood limitations, and alternatives such as induction. It includes differing experiences and practical tradeoffs for renters, rural households, and people facing power outages. ## Arguments

- mikeyouse [[./thread#^c34333754|mikeyouse]] argues that indoor gas cooking introduces combustion pollution and contrasts it with appliances that isolate combustion air and exhaust outdoors. This supports separating combustion from occupied air, although the comment supplies no pollutant measurements. seanp2k2 [[./thread#^c34344107|seanp2k2]] proposes a heat-exchanger cooking vessel with separately vented combustion exhaust; that remains an untested design suggestion.
- techwizrd [[./thread#^c34333954|techwizrd]] quotes a study associating indoor gas-stove use with childhood asthma and attributing almost 13\% of current US childhood asthma to it. That statistic concerns the attributed share of existing cases, not the percentage of exposed children who develop asthma. mikeyouse [[./thread#^c34333816|mikeyouse]] supplies the study link and mentions other research, but the underlying studies and their data are not supplied in this unit.
- giardia [[./thread#^c34333797|giardia]], jmyeet [[./thread#^c34333934|jmyeet]], and parshimers [[./thread#^c34333827|parshimers]] advocate better kitchen ventilation as a way to reduce exposure while retaining gas cooking. giardia also proposes education about gas hazards. Their argument is that exposure control could address the health concern without sacrificing outage resilience; none reports a measured reduction achieved by a particular hood.
- throw0101c [[./thread#^c34334057|throw0101c]] argues that induction directs more heating into the vessel while gas releases substantial heat around it into the kitchen, suggesting a comfort advantage alongside eliminating the burner flame. The supporting numbers concern appliance power ratings, not measured room temperatures or air pollution.
- Marsymars [[./thread#^c34333859|Marsymars]] proposes generator-powered induction and separate backup cooking equipment as alternatives to keeping an indoor gas range for outages. miguelazo [[./thread#^c34333867|miguelazo]] argues for improving grid reliability. These proposals could separate everyday cooking choices from emergency provision, but the thread does not demonstrate their comparative cost, reliability, or exposure.

## Counterarguments

- techwizrd [[./thread#^c34333954|techwizrd]] says the asthma paper does not appear obviously flawed, while describing its data as limited to 2019 and its reporting as insufficient to reproduce the analysis. They want broader, preferably longitudinal evidence. giantg2 [[./thread#^c34334039|giantg2]] asks whether asthma rates followed declining gas use over time; no historical analysis is supplied, so this remains a proposed check rather than contrary evidence.
- throwaway9191aa [[./thread#^c34333765|throwaway9191aa]] reports cooking with gas for roughly 20 minutes daily and questions how that exposure compares with professional cooks' much longer use. jmyeet [[./thread#^c34333934|jmyeet]] raises the same occupational comparison. Both acknowledge commercial ventilation as a possible explanation. Neither supplies chef health statistics, so the presumed absence of widespread illness among cooks cannot establish safety.
- mikeyouse [[./thread#^c34334207|mikeyouse]] [[./thread#^c34334963|mikeyouse]] disputes the idea that requiring a hood necessarily provides effective protection: they argue that household hoods are often unused and that capture depends on coverage, proximity, and burner position. Their comparison with commercial kitchens emphasizes large hoods and continuous operation. These are design and usage arguments; household capture efficiencies and usage frequencies are not measured in the supplied comments.
- trefoiled [[./thread#^c34334376|trefoiled]] argues that reliable replacement infrastructure should precede restrictions, while mfer [[./thread#^c34333930|mfer]] emphasizes the expense and years needed for grid improvements. These objections concern whether an exposure-reduction strategy remains workable during outages, rather than demonstrating that gas emissions are harmless.
- rootusrootus [[./thread#^c34334123|rootusrootus]], giantg2 [[./thread#^c34333969|giantg2]], and sibartlett [[./thread#^c34334127|sibartlett]] challenge willio58's description of their furnace problem as simply a gas leak. They distinguish a CO alarm from natural-gas detection and suggest faulty combustion, ignition, or exhaust as explanations for the symptoms. These are plausible interpretations offered in replies, not findings from an inspection or medical diagnosis.
- goalieca [[./thread#^c34333869|goalieca]] reports that induction's boost setting can make their pan smoke, and Johnny555 [[./thread#^c34333940|Johnny555]] says their induction range can char meat black. These accounts limit any inference that changing the heat source guarantees clean cooking air. They contain no measurements identifying particle sizes, concentrations, or the emissions difference between gas and induction.

## Assumptions

- willio58 [[./thread#^c34333985|willio58]] describes physical and mental developmental harm from gas stoves as established and extrapolates to unrecognized adult fogginess. The supplied comment provides no supporting study details or adult cognitive measurements. Their separate furnace account [[./thread#^c34333893|willio58]] concerns a suspected malfunction and cannot establish cognitive effects from ordinary stove use.
- nanidin [[./thread#^c34333874|nanidin]] suggests children may be more vulnerable because they are smaller, more sensitive, or closer to emissions, then explicitly invokes breathing height [[./thread#^c34334253|nanidin]]. JimtheCoder [[./thread#^c34334259|JimtheCoder]] questions that spatial explanation because combustion products might rise. The thread supplies neither airflow observations nor age-specific exposure measurements to resolve these proposed mechanisms.
- In a reply about developmental harm, giantg2 [[./thread#^c34334077|giantg2]] asserts that masks increase CO2 concentrations, especially for children and in classrooms with already elevated CO2. No concentrations, measurement methods, study details, or health outcomes accompany the claim. It cannot establish room CO2 exposure or explain gas-stove health effects.
- Several arguments assume a particular form of prohibition, but the discussion corrects that premise. giardia [[./thread#^c34333797|giardia]] retracts the claim that Oregon had already acted; miguelazo [[./thread#^c34342840|miguelazo]] describes restrictions on new construction. Later, cpleppert [[./thread#^c34343804|cpleppert]] quotes the CPSC chair saying there was no proceeding to ban gas stoves and describing emissions research and voluntary safety work. These comments do not establish a federal requirement to remove existing appliances.

## Conditions

- Energy prices and rural equipment vary. Overtonwindow [[./thread#^c34334913|Overtonwindow]] reports expensive electricity and cheap gas locally, making conversion a household-budget concern, but gives no location or prices. rootusrootus [[./thread#^c34342997|rootusrootus]] describes older and less wealthy rural homes they know as commonly using electric ranges, electric heaters, and wood stoves. The accounts do not support treating rural households as having one uniform fuel arrangement.
- Rental housing separates technical feasibility from purchasing authority. Entinel [[./thread#^c34333810|Entinel]] argues that tenants often face low-quality coil ranges and limited access to induction. Johnny555 [[./thread#^c34333913|Johnny555]] responds that an apartment already wired for an electric range can also accommodate induction, while acknowledging that the landlord may decline its purchase premium. Entinel's broad prevalence claims are not backed by housing data.
- Gas-to-electric conversion can require electrical work. parshimers [[./thread#^c34333899|parshimers]] mentions the possible need for a 40-amp supply, and Johnny555 [[./thread#^c34333913|Johnny555]] discusses new range circuits and service upgrades in older buildings. rootusrootus [[./thread#^c34334141|rootusrootus]] explains that US full-size ranges already use 240 V, addressing senectus1's US-versus-Australia voltage hypothesis [[./thread#^c34334005|senectus1]]. Existing wiring and service capacity, rather than the country-level voltage shorthand, are the relevant constraints raised here.
- Lower-cost or partial alternatives appear, with limits. pwinnski [[./thread#^c34334106|pwinnski]] reports a \$70 IKEA induction unit used for hotpot; verbify [[./thread#^c34333845|verbify]] notes the counter space a portable unit occupies. incomingpain [[./thread#^c34338038|incomingpain]] quotes rebates of up to \$840 for an appliance and \$500 for conversion, without establishing eligibility. numtel [[./thread#^c34334877|numtel]] links a battery-assisted range proposal, but supplies no firsthand installation cost or operating results.
- Rural backup options depend on terrain, season, fuel storage, and local services. giardia [[./thread#^c34334132|giardia]] says local solar is less useful during winter in a mountain valley. kyleblarson [[./thread#^c34333928|kyleblarson]] reports expensive generators and difficulty obtaining an electrician. trefoiled [[./thread#^c34344003|trefoiled]] describes truck-filled bulk gas tanks where utility gas is unavailable. giardia [[./thread#^c34333897|giardia]] also says local communities cannot afford underground electrical lines and other governments will not fund them.
- Ventilation has building and cooking tradeoffs. mikeyouse [[./thread#^c34334207|mikeyouse]] argues that strong exhaust in tightly sealed homes requires make-up air and can backdraft other gas appliances, complicating retrofits. rootusrootus [[./thread#^c34334101|rootusrootus]] says downdraft airflow noticeably reduces heat reaching the pan, while rootusrootus [[./thread#^c34342958|rootusrootus]] raises head-clearance problems with low, projecting hoods. These comments offer practical concerns without measured airflow, pressure, or heat-loss results.

## Lived experiences

- willio58 [[./thread#^c34333893|willio58]] reports gas odor during the first hour of furnace operation, a nearby CO alarm activating, and months of grogginess and cloudy thinking after moving into a rental. After switching to a small electric space heater, they report feeling substantially better within two weeks and strongly attribute the change to stopping furnace use. This is a firsthand symptom-and-intervention account, with no concentration readings, confirmed fault, clinical diagnosis, or controlled comparison.
- giardia [[./thread#^c34333797|giardia]] recounts being snowbound for at least two weeks during a 2019 storm, with electricity unavailable, frozen pipes, and prolonged gas-stove use to melt drinking water. The reported burn duration is contextual: after sixstringtheory [[./thread#^c34333921|sixstringtheory]] describes snow-melting techniques from personal experience, giardia [[./thread#^c34333978|giardia]] acknowledges possibly inefficient technique. No indoor exposure was measured during this unusually intensive use.
- rootusrootus [[./thread#^c34333902|rootusrootus]] reports that natural gas continued working during hard freezes while electricity and internet were unavailable for most of a week. kyleblarson [[./thread#^c34333928|kyleblarson]] describes relying on a propane range in a rural mountain area and using a small generator for well pumps. These accounts establish particular households' resilience needs, without comparing their air exposure with alternative arrangements.
- Entinel [[./thread#^c34333810|Entinel]] reports living in at least three apartments whose stoves had no hoods. miguelazo [[./thread#^c34343740|miguelazo]] says proper ventilation has been rare in their experience. These observations support concern about actual installed protection and everyday use, but provide neither representative prevalence estimates nor measured air-quality outcomes.
- Johnny555 [[./thread#^c34333940|Johnny555]] reports that their induction range uses the same 50-amp circuit as its electric predecessor and heats faster with more immediate control. goalieca [[./thread#^c34333869|goalieca]] describes heating a cast-iron pan to smoking within seconds on boost and generally avoiding that setting; after using high-output gas appliances at their in-laws', goalieca [[./thread#^c34334017|goalieca]] prefers their induction's heat delivery. These are cooking experiences, not calibrated temperature or emissions comparisons.
- Induction experiences also conflict. skrbjc [[./thread#^c34333819|skrbjc]] reports uneven cooking, coarse temperature control, and a poor interface on a particular holiday-use cooktop, while acknowledging fast water boiling. riknos314 [[./thread#^c34334198|riknos314]] prefers the adjustment feel of coil controls to the stepped induction controls they have encountered. bayyyls [[./thread#^c34333946|bayyyls]] reports household satisfaction with a combined gas-and-induction setup. None reports a corresponding change in health or measured air quality.
- Some commenters had prepared to switch without yet reporting results: rootusrootus [[./thread#^c34333952|rootusrootus]] had installed a 240 V cooktop circuit, and gdubs [[./thread#^c34333970|gdubs]] had upgraded an electrical panel anticipating replacement of a gas range. These are firsthand retrofit preparations, not completed demonstrations of reduced exposure.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The asthma effect's magnitude, causality, and dependence on ventilation remain unresolved within this export. techwizrd [[./thread#^c34333954|techwizrd]] questions data depth and reproducibility, while throwaway9191aa [[./thread#^c34333765|throwaway9191aa]] asks about ordinary daily cooking duration. No commenter supplies personal asthma outcomes after changing stoves, exposure-response measurements, or an analysis resolving the professional-versus-domestic kitchen comparison.
- Whether a practical residential hood can sufficiently control gas-stove exposure remains contested. mikeyouse [[./thread#^c34334963|mikeyouse]] emphasizes capture geometry and commercial-kitchen differences; giantg2 [[./thread#^c34342186|giantg2]] describes larger residential hoods and explicitly requests air-quality test data instead of visual design comparisons. No such results follow, leaving hood configuration, operating setting, capture effectiveness, and residual exposure undetermined.
- Emergency camping-stove substitution raises an unresolved acute-versus-cumulative exposure dispute. jcadam [[./thread#^c34343177|jcadam]] warns that people may operate camping stoves indoors without proper ventilation and considers a household range safer. miguelazo [[./thread#^c34343740|miguelazo]] argues that a few emergency days are a smaller concern than years of gas-range use. Neither supplies exposure or health-risk comparisons that establish the relative safety of those scenarios.
- The suitability of induction for every cooking task is disputed independently of air quality. pclmulqdq [[./thread#^c34333905|pclmulqdq]] argues that wok cooking effectively requires gas, whereas goalieca [[./thread#^c34334017|goalieca]] reports stronger heating from induction than from gas appliances they have used. obpe [[./thread#^c34334711|obpe]] prioritizes continuous, low-output control, and qbrass [[./thread#^c34336933|qbrass]] emphasizes heat distribution. hyperbovine's explanation that skrbjc's uneven heating reflects a cooktop or pan problem [[./thread#^c34333936|hyperbovine]] remains untested. These disagreements concern particular equipment and tasks, with no accompanying exposure comparisons.
- This unit cannot resolve opening-window, filtration, outdoor-pollution, or sleep questions. miguelazo [[./thread#^c34333867|miguelazo]] mentions wildfire safety in connection with electrical infrastructure, while giardia [[./thread#^c34333797|giardia]] describes winter outages; neither reports wildfire-smoke exposure. There are no supplied firsthand comparisons involving traffic pollution, indoor-versus-outdoor concentrations, opening windows, air cleaners, or sleep outcomes. Consequently, the thread provides no conditions under which window opening was demonstrated to improve or worsen personal exposure.
