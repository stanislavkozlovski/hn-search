# [Low Cost CO2 Sensors Comparison: Photo-Acoustic vs. NDIR](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41620955)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion compares photo-acoustic and NDIR CO₂ sensors, including accuracy, calibration drift, sensor lifespan, counterfeit concerns, and affordable DIY builds. Participants weigh consumer monitors such as Aranet, AirGradient, and Qingping, and discuss using CO₂ as a ventilation and respiratory-virus-risk proxy, its limits when filtration is present, and how home ventilation choices affect readings. Other threads touch on indoor air quality, oxygen sensing, and whether monitoring is useful when rooms are regularly aired.
Tags: CO₂ sensors, NDIR, photo-acoustic sensing, calibration, indoor air quality, ventilation, consumer monitors, DIY electronics

## Relevance

The discussion compares CO2 sensor accuracy and calibration, and describes using CO2 to assess ventilation, cognition, and respiratory risk. Firsthand reports cover indoor readings, ventilation changes, bedroom comfort, and tradeoffs between fresh air and outdoor particles. ## Arguments

- CO2 monitoring can reveal ventilation problems that ordinary airing routines miss. matznerd [[./thread#^c41621685|matznerd]] uses it to assess ventilation relative to occupancy; llm\_trw [[./thread#^c41621399|llm\_trw]] argues that a small occupied study can become unpleasant within an hour; rallison [[./thread#^c41621498|rallison]] describes seeing rapid increases in particular rooms even when the building seems generally well ventilated.
- Some commenters argue for monitoring CO2 because of cognition, independently of infection risk. scosman [[./thread#^c41625597|scosman]] asserts that CO2 reduces cognitive capacity, and zik [[./thread#^c41622701|zik]] links a Harvard article while claiming measurable effects even from small increases. These are claims about research, not firsthand cognitive measurements or study results reproduced in this unit.
- Ventilation and filtration answer different questions. matznerd [[./thread#^c41621685|matznerd]] notes that CO2 does not reveal whether air passes through sufficiently effective filters. rallison [[./thread#^c41621517|rallison]] nevertheless finds it useful as an imperfect infection-risk proxy because inexpensive portable instruments do not directly measure airborne viral concentrations.
- Broader monitoring can help distinguish pollutants rather than treating CO2 as a complete air-quality score. SparkyMcUnicorn [[./thread#^c41622182|SparkyMcUnicorn]] values AirGradient's separate CO2, PM2.5, PM10, temperature, and humidity channels; telcal [[./thread#^c41632612|telcal]] also emphasizes VOC and NOx sensing. Neither supplies numerical PM2.5 or NO2 exposure measurements.
- For deciding when to ventilate, usability may matter more than small accuracy differences. caseyy [[./thread#^c41622386|caseyy]] recommends actual CO2 measurement rather than estimated eCO2, considers distinguishing roughly 500 ppm from 1000–2000 ppm sufficient for their purpose, and favors alarms. Their acceptance of errors approaching 200 ppm is a practical preference, not a validated health standard.
- Measurements can guide changes to cooking, ventilation, and bedroom habits. twothamendment [[./thread#^c41621579|twothamendment]] describes changing several household practices after monitoring different locations; danw1979 [[./thread#^c41625297|danw1979]] uses a desk monitor's traffic-light display to decide when to open windows. These accounts demonstrate actionable feedback, although they provide no numerical before-and-after exposure reductions.

## Counterarguments

- aulin [[./thread#^c41622597|aulin]] argues that bodily sensations already indicate when home ventilation is needed, later allowing a role for automation in aulin [[./thread#^c41622824|aulin]]. Aachen [[./thread#^c41628382|Aachen]] directly challenges this with a monitor reading around 3000 ppm without a recognizable CO2 warning, apart from warmth. This contradicts reliable subjective detection in that person's setting; it does not establish absence of physiological effects.
- Replying to CO2-based infection assessment, left-struck [[./thread#^c41621851|left-struck]] proposes particle measurements, or both measurements, because CO2 cannot detect filtration. lucubratory [[./thread#^c41622169|lucubratory]] counters that particle counts include nonhuman sources and are therefore not automatically better indicators of viral exposure. The disagreement concerns which imperfect proxy is useful, not a demonstrated ability of either instrument to measure infection risk.
- Replies disputing whether 700 ppm is concerning must be read in their calibration context. dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c41621588|dzhiurgis]] calls 700 ppm good, and ctenb [[./thread#^c41622828|ctenb]] calls 400–1000 ppm good quality. Their parent, fotta [[./thread#^c41621186|fotta]], was discussing an apartment that did not reach outdoor levels and a subsequent improvement in quality of life, rather than demonstrating that 700 ppm itself caused illness.
- kayson [[./thread#^c41621170|kayson]] relays Sensirion engineers' claim that even closed environments reach outdoor CO2 levels at least weekly, supporting automatic calibration. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c41621826|ahaucnx]] challenges that generalization with basements, low-energy homes, and schools that reportedly remained above ambient throughout an unoccupied weekend. Calibration suitability therefore remains dependent on the deployment environment.
- atoav [[./thread#^c41622837|atoav]] describes forcing a 700 ppm baseline to 400 ppm as an accumulating daily error. bialpio [[./thread#^c41622989|bialpio]] corrects that calculation: repeatedly treating a true 700 ppm minimum as 400 ppm implies an approximately 300 ppm bias, not an additional 300 ppm of drift every day.
- Opening windows is not an unqualified air-quality improvement. kupfer [[./thread#^c41623438|kupfer]] reports needing more frequent or longer airing than expected, while warning that it can introduce outdoor particulate matter. Lower indoor CO2 can therefore coexist with increased particle exposure; this comment supplies no concentrations or health comparison quantifying that tradeoff.
- monkburger [[./thread#^c41624844|monkburger]] recommends fresh-air systems and particularly ERVs. Abekkus [[./thread#^c41625471|Abekkus]] disputes their value in humid climates, favoring a ventilation fan and dehumidifier and questioning passive moisture-transfer efficiency. The proposed limits on ERV performance are the commenter's reasoning, not measurements presented in the discussion.
- mapt [[./thread#^c41624985|mapt]] doubts strong airplane-filtration claims on grounds of equipment size, noise, cost, and airline incentives. avianlyric [[./thread#^c41625641|avianlyric]] responds that newer air-management systems can reduce aircraft operating costs. mapt [[./thread#^c41627003|mapt]] then clarifies that the objection concerns internal filtration effectiveness, which the explanation about fresh-air management does not directly establish.

## Assumptions

- Using CO2 for infection assessment assumes a useful relationship between occupants' exhaled air and insufficient ventilation. lucubratory [[./thread#^c41622169|lucubratory]] and rallison [[./thread#^c41622295|rallison]] acknowledge that effective filtration changes that relationship, but go further by calling CO2 an upper bound on risk. No paired CO2, viral-concentration, or infection measurements here establish a guaranteed upper bound.
- Outdoor calibration assumes that the reference air is known and appropriate. fotta [[./thread#^c41621153|fotta]] describes automatic calibration assigning the observed minimum to 400 ppm, whereas pixelfarmer [[./thread#^c41622842|pixelfarmer]] reports outdoor readings of 416–432 ppm and higher readings when neighboring apartments ventilate. An outdoor check is consequently not equivalent to validation against a certified reference.
- Quoted ppm categories serve different purposes. matznerd [[./thread#^c41621685|matznerd]] describes approximately 800 ppm as good indoor ventilation; ctenb [[./thread#^c41622828|ctenb]] gives a 400–1000 ppm range; caseyy [[./thread#^c41622386|caseyy]] uses broader bands for deciding when to act. None establishes that a room meeting its CO2 target also has low PM2.5, NO2, or infectious aerosol exposure.
- The claim that an inexpensive DIY monitor matches an Aranet4 rests partly on component specifications. scosman [[./thread#^c41625597|scosman]] cites comparable Senseair accuracy ratings, but aftbit [[./thread#^c41626204|aftbit]] reports a DIY sensor becoming stuck at 500 ppm. Matching nominal specifications does not demonstrate equivalent calibration, reliability, or complete-device performance.
- Some favorable sensor reports come from a supplier perspective. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c41621785|ahaucnx]] identifies himself as AirGradient's founder, providing context for ahaucnx's [[./thread#^c41621770|ahaucnx]] observations about sensor noise and lifespan. That affiliation does not invalidate the observations, but a ten-year warranty and reported field stability are different forms of evidence.
- Physiological sensitivity in one breathing environment does not validate everyday room detection. brok3nmachine [[./thread#^c41623139|brok3nmachine]] discusses CO2-related breathing and warning signs in technical diving with rebreathers. That account does not establish the household detection capability asserted by aulin [[./thread#^c41622597|aulin]], especially given Aachen's [[./thread#^c41628382|Aachen]] contrasting indoor experience.

## Conditions

- The commenters' 2024 prices span substantially different budgets and formats. scosman [[./thread#^c41625597|scosman]] estimates about \$20 for a Senseair S8 and under \$30 for a DIY monitor; lucubratory [[./thread#^c41622120|lucubratory]] recalls paying \$100–200 for an Aranet4; tomaskafka [[./thread#^c41634389|tomaskafka]] recommends an approximately \$70 Qingping monitor after more than a year of ownership. These are historical purchase reports or estimates, not current quotations.
- Additional channels and convenience affect cost comparisons. SparkyMcUnicorn [[./thread#^c41622182|SparkyMcUnicorn]] says AirGradient One costs somewhat more than Aranet4 but offers more sensors and replaceable components. MetaWhirledPeas [[./thread#^c41621627|MetaWhirledPeas]] uses a \$300 Airthings device, while danw1979 [[./thread#^c41625297|danw1979]] describes an approximately £85 USB-powered TFA monitor. Different sensing capabilities prevent a simple comparison by purchase price alone.
- Low-cost sourcing has unresolved authenticity risks. imjonse [[./thread#^c41622460|imjonse]] worries about AliExpress sellers outside the manufacturer's distribution network; ius [[./thread#^c41623171|ius]] argues that this alone does not establish counterfeiting and compares €14.99 single-unit pricing with €16.38 bulk distributor pricing excluding VAT. Those differing purchasing conditions and prices do not authenticate a particular sensor.
- Humidity changes the ventilation economics. Abekkus [[./thread#^c41625471|Abekkus]] argues that a fan plus dehumidifier is better value in humid regions and claims ERVs cost three to four times as much. jwong\_ [[./thread#^c41629873|jwong\_]] finds opening a window, operating a fan, and later dehumidifying cumbersome. Neither supplies a complete energy-cost or humidity-performance comparison.
- Weather, layout, and building construction constrain opening windows and running fans. imhoguy [[./thread#^c41623492|imhoguy]] points to winter; kupfer [[./thread#^c41623438|kupfer]] notes draft and temperature differences; pixelfarmer [[./thread#^c41622842|pixelfarmer]] contrasts cross-ventilation with still air and openings on one side. In coastal California, rallison [[./thread#^c41622253|rallison]] rarely needs heating or cooling and attributes some benefit from fan-only operation to an old, leaky house—conditions that should not be assumed for tightly sealed housing.

## Lived experiences

- fotta [[./thread#^c41621153|fotta]] reports satisfaction with a dual-channel NDIR SCD30, disabling automatic calibration and taking it outdoors annually, with reported drift of only a few ppm. breput [[./thread#^c41621089|breput]] prefers the Senseair S8 over the SCD41 because the latter required more manual calibration; their S8 occasionally read about 380 ppm in fresh air after roughly three months, which they considered within specification. Neither account includes an independent reference instrument.
- Reliability experiences are mixed. aftbit [[./thread#^c41626204|aftbit]] reports a DIY sensor becoming permanently stuck at 500 ppm while their AirGradient devices had not done so. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c41621770|ahaucnx]] reports more noise and some failures around five years for MH-Z19 sensors, compared with good S8 experience. These reports concern particular devices or a supplier's deployments, not measured failure rates for all units.
- ius [[./thread#^c41623171|ius]] bought AliExpress sensors that appeared to measure actual CO2 but had default-calibration problems and temperature-reading difficulties attributed to self-heating. bubulina [[./thread#^c41714049|bubulina]] subsequently received one SCD40 with the expected laser marking and two SCD41 modules without it. Missing markings prompted an authenticity question; the supplied replies do not resolve it.
- Reported residential readings vary greatly. kayson [[./thread#^c41621170|kayson]] sees about 600 ppm indoors even overnight; fotta [[./thread#^c41621186|fotta]] recalls about 700 ppm at night in a former warehouse apartment with a mini-split; \_zoltan\_ [[./thread#^c41622651|\_zoltan\_]] reports spikes to 5000 ppm in one room. Occupancy, room dimensions, calibration, and measurement timing are insufficiently described to make these directly comparable.
- ahaucnx [[./thread#^c41621826|ahaucnx]] reports schools with Friday-afternoon readings above 3000 ppm whose classrooms did not return to ambient over the unoccupied weekend. This is a deployment observation supporting caution about automatic baseline calibration; the comment supplies no traces, ventilation rates, or detailed classroom conditions.
- pixelfarmer [[./thread#^c41622842|pixelfarmer]] reports reaching ambient CO2 in under five minutes with favorable apartment airflow, but slower reduction in still conditions. They observed outdoor values between 416 and 432 ppm and readings above 500 ppm attributed to neighboring apartments airing out. The concentrations are reported observations; the attribution to neighbors is their explanation.
- space\_oddity [[./thread#^c41623809|space\_oddity]] felt better during a mountain vacation and subsequently became interested in home air quality. fotta [[./thread#^c41621186|fotta]] reports noticeably better quality of life after moving to a better-ventilated apartment. Neither isolates a pollutant or separates air quality from other differences between locations.
- twothamendment [[./thread#^c41621579|twothamendment]] moved two Awair Elements around their home to learn its patterns, then switched from gas cooking to induction, installed an air exchanger, and increased central-air circulation during spring and fall. High bedroom CO2 also led them to leave the door open more and use an earplug instead of closing the door for quiet. They report measurable results without supplying values or a measured sleep outcome.
- rallison [[./thread#^c41622253|rallison]] reports a large nighttime improvement after using central air in fan-only mode more often while usually keeping the bedroom door closed. They infer that their old, leaky house gains outdoor exchange as well as mixing, but provide no airflow measurement. They also use a portable induction burner instead of the gas range fairly often, without reporting a pollutant-specific reduction.
- Abekkus [[./thread#^c41633079|Abekkus]] describes a basement intake using a six-inch snorkel, mesh, a four-inch wall passage, a filter box, and a nominal 200 cfm fan, alongside a standard dehumidifier. They explicitly note that filters reduce delivered airflow and recommend 15 cfm per occupant, without showing a measured flow or CO2 result. jwong\_ [[./thread#^c41629873|jwong\_]] reports a less automated window-and-fan routine followed by dehumidification.
- Battery life and simple feedback matter in daily use. ctenb [[./thread#^c41622817|ctenb]] remained happy with a \$200 Aranet4 after a year without replacing its batteries; matznerd [[./thread#^c41621685|matznerd]] reports roughly a year from two AA batteries. tomaskafka [[./thread#^c41634389|tomaskafka]] reports month-plus battery capability for Qingping, while danw1979 [[./thread#^c41625297|danw1979]] has used a USB-powered desk monitor for years to prompt window opening. Satisfaction and runtime do not independently verify accuracy.
- Automation experiences depend on the household's network. SparkyMcUnicorn [[./thread#^c41622182|SparkyMcUnicorn]] successfully installed ESPHome on AirGradient for Home Assistant and values being able to replace sensors later. dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c41621601|dzhiurgis]] reports Qingping working with HomeKit and Home Assistant. After throwaranay4933 [[./thread#^c41628205|throwaranay4933]] explains Aranet4's Bluetooth integration, teekert [[./thread#^c41629735|teekert]] says Bluetooth coverage remains inadequate compared with their existing Zigbee network.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The CO2 level at which monitoring produces a meaningful health or cognitive benefit remains unsettled here. zik [[./thread#^c41622701|zik]] claims effects without a lower threshold; ctenb [[./thread#^c41622828|ctenb]] labels a broad range good; Aachen [[./thread#^c41628382|Aachen]] reports no recognizable warning at about 3000 ppm. These statements address different outcomes and do not resolve causation, effect size, individual susceptibility, or a practical intervention threshold. twothamendment [[./thread#^c41621579|twothamendment]] supplies a sleep-related ventilation tradeoff, but no measured improvement in sleep.
- The unit provides little direct evidence about PM2.5 or NO2 exposure. SparkyMcUnicorn [[./thread#^c41622182|SparkyMcUnicorn]] lists PM2.5 sensing, and telcal [[./thread#^c41632612|telcal]] mentions NOx sensing, which is not a reported NO2 concentration. lucubratory [[./thread#^c41622169|lucubratory]] mentions brake and tire dust as examples rather than measured traffic exposure; twothamendment [[./thread#^c41621579|twothamendment]] reports changing cooking appliances without pollutant-specific results. No wildfire-smoke experience or numerical PM2.5/NO2 series is supplied.
- When outdoor pollution should outweigh the benefit of opening windows is not quantified. kupfer [[./thread#^c41623438|kupfer]] explicitly describes this compromise, while pixelfarmer [[./thread#^c41622842|pixelfarmer]] demonstrates that windows can quickly lower CO2 under favorable airflow. There are no simultaneous indoor/outdoor particle readings or comparative filtration results establishing when to open, close, or use filtered mechanical ventilation.
- The infection-risk proxy and aircraft-filtration dispute remain unvalidated by direct measurements. matznerd [[./thread#^c41621685|matznerd]] describes airplane HEPA filtration, high air-change rates, and reduced ventilation before flight; mapt [[./thread#^c41624985|mapt]] questions strong effectiveness claims. avianlyric [[./thread#^c41625641|avianlyric]] explains why newer HVAC can be economical, but mapt [[./thread#^c41627003|mapt]] maintains that this leaves internal filtration effectiveness unanswered. No cabin measurements or infection outcomes settle the disagreement.
- The ERV-versus-fan-and-dehumidifier comparison lacks a common measured basis. monkburger [[./thread#^c41624844|monkburger]] favors ERVs, whereas Abekkus [[./thread#^c41625471|Abekkus]] challenges their humid-climate efficiency and cost. Abekkus's [[./thread#^c41633079|Abekkus]] installed alternative demonstrates a practical configuration, but the unit supplies no matched measurements of humidity, delivered ventilation, energy consumption, noise, or total cost.
- The discussion does not establish a general winner between photo-acoustic and NDIR technology or authenticate the disputed marketplace sensors. breput [[./thread#^c41621089|breput]] supplies model-specific experience; ius [[./thread#^c41623171|ius]] says a reputable-distributor comparison was unavailable; bubulina [[./thread#^c41714049|bubulina]] leaves the missing-marking question open. transcriptase [[./thread#^c41622591|transcriptase]] also questions the article's R versus R² terminology, but the underlying analysis is not supplied for assessment.
