# [Engineered ‘Superplant’ Cleans Indoor Air Like 30 Regular Plants](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=33483372)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants assess a bioengineered pothos marketed as cleaning indoor air more effectively than ordinary plants. The discussion questions the scale and measurement of the claimed benefit, compares plants with ventilation and mechanical filtration, and considers microbiome maintenance, price, propagation and intellectual property, genetic modification risks, and whether houseplants meaningfully remove VOCs.
Tags: bioengineered-plants, indoor-air-quality, VOCs, air-filtration, plant-care, genetic-modification, product-claims

## Relevance

The thread evaluates claims that plants remove indoor VOCs, compares plants with ventilation and filtration, and questions the strength of the supporting measurements. Comments distinguish VOC removal from particulate filtration and discuss CO2 only briefly. ## Arguments

- Measure the suspected problem before choosing a remedy. SapporoChris [[./thread#^c33483837|SapporoChris]] recommends checking VOC levels with a suitable monitor, then using ventilation and particle filtration. driverdan [[./thread#^c33483949|driverdan]] argues that significant household VOCs warrant addressing the underlying problem. These are proposed approaches; neither commenter supplies household measurements or intervention results.
- Ventilation is presented as more practical than large numbers of ordinary plants. thesausageking [[./thread#^c33487144|thesausageking]] says that examining a plant-related TED talk and its study convinced them that a noticeable office benefit would require plants covering essentially all available floor space, whereas introducing outdoor air through HVAC was substantially more effective and economical. This is their interpretation of referenced material, not a reported home experiment.
- Treatment needs to match the pollutant. googlryas [[./thread#^c33484202|googlryas]] distinguishes particulate filtration from activated-carbon adsorption of VOCs. In the reply ancestry, jrockway [[./thread#^c33486520|jrockway]] clarifies that charcoal was already implicit in his fan-and-furnace-filter suggestion because he was answering a charcoal comparison. Dylan16807 [[./thread#^c33486755|Dylan16807]] corrects the stated molecular scale to nanometres; that correction does not dispute the broader distinction between particles and gases.
- The manufacturer proposes a targeted VOC intervention. Neoplants co-founder Lionel-M [[./thread#^c33520822|Lionel-M]] identifies benzene, toluene, xylene, and formaldehyde as the four targets. CTO Patrick-T [[./thread#^c33520783|Patrick-T]] describes performance as 30 times that of ordinary indoor plants and links a white paper. These are manufacturer-reported claims; the supplied comments contain no absolute removal rates or independent household tests.
- Several comments suggest that the soil, microbes, and airflow matter alongside the plant. JohnJamesRambo [[./thread#^c33484231|JohnJamesRambo]] recalls an experiment in which removing a plant’s leaves did not reduce VOC removal, attributing the effect to potting soil and its organisms. Patrick-T [[./thread#^c33520884|Patrick-T]] says the product’s pot increases the soil–air interface. The former is an explicitly remembered experiment, and the latter a manufacturer’s design explanation.
- sandworm101 [[./thread#^c33484370|sandworm101]] proposes baking soda as a faster, more space-efficient VOC treatment than plants. Their explanation relies on acidic compounds interacting with alkaline baking soda in a moist environment, illustrated by permeable refrigerator boxes [[./thread#^c33484626|sandworm101]]. This is a proposed inexpensive alternative, without measured room-scale performance in the supplied comments.

## Counterarguments

- A large relative improvement can still leave negligible practical capacity. Nathanael\_M [[./thread#^c33483995|Nathanael\_M]] asks for absolute numbers and suggests that the product might principally fund more effective future versions. googlryas [[./thread#^c33483902|googlryas]] considers ordinary plants’ air-cleaning capacity negligible, while tripletao [[./thread#^c33484311|tripletao]] disputes a meaningful health benefit from either one ordinary pothos or thirty. These are skeptical assessments, not tests demonstrating that Neo P1 fails.
- The NASA reference may not describe ordinary passive houseplants. Kon-Peki [[./thread#^c33487212|Kon-Peki]] says the report used specially constructed pots with powered fans and ducts, and cautions against applying it directly to homeowners. That account challenges simple extrapolation from experimental arrangements to a pot sitting in a room; the underlying report is not reproduced here.
- The claim that three shoulder-height plants can recycle an adult’s CO2 is strongly contested. schainks [[./thread#^c33484485|schainks]] cites a TED talk for that claim. gus\_massa [[./thread#^c33485386|gus\_massa]] and colechristensen [[./thread#^c33485840|colechristensen]] respond with rough carbon-balance arguments requiring approximately half a kilogram of daily plant growth per person. alar44 [[./thread#^c33486339|alar44]] contrasts estimated human emissions with tree uptake, and lazide [[./thread#^c33485967|lazide]] adds an optimistic sunlight-budget calculation. These are theoretical rebuttals, not measured indoor CO2 trials.
- Patrick-T [[./thread#^c33520861|Patrick-T]], responding to the recommendation to buy a VOC filter, claims that EPA material shows no VOC-purifying technology is both effective and free of harmful by-products. This challenges the assumption that any advertised VOC filter provides an uncomplicated solution. However, the statement comes from the plant manufacturer, and the linked report’s findings and qualifications are not supplied for assessment.
- The HEPA comparison does not directly test the advertised claim. vasco [[./thread#^c33483852|vasco]] doubts that a passive plant could approach an active HEPA purifier, while cinntaile [[./thread#^c33484158|cinntaile]] replies that the stated comparison is with thirty ordinary plants. Combined with googlryas’s particle-versus-VOC distinction [[./thread#^c33484202|googlryas]], this leaves a need for comparisons using the same pollutant and outcome.
- Plants might introduce their own indoor-air problems. sagebird [[./thread#^c33489170|sagebird]] suggests that mold associated with houseplants could sometimes outweigh their benefits. This is a hypothetical counterargument; the commenter reports no mold measurements, illness, or comparison of rooms with and without plants.

## Assumptions

- In this source unit, story 33483372, part 0, air cleaning mainly means VOC removal. googlryas [[./thread#^c33484544|googlryas]] explicitly distinguishes that subject from CO2, and Lionel-M [[./thread#^c33520822|Lionel-M]] names four VOC targets. The advertised 30-fold improvement therefore cannot be treated as evidence of equivalent reductions in CO2, PM2.5, or NO2.
- The numerical plant-density extrapolation has substantial contextual uncertainty. Nathanael\_M [[./thread#^c33483995|Nathanael\_M]] reports a range of 10–1,000 ordinary plants per square metre to match outdoor–indoor air exchange and divides it by thirty, while questioning floor-area versus volume units. Nathanael\_M [[./thread#^c33488758|Nathanael\_M]] later switches to square feet. These comments do not establish a dependable sizing rule for a home.
- An early criticism depends on a deliberately unusual exposure scenario. googlryas [[./thread#^c33484170|googlryas]] explains that they had imagined a sealed house containing perfectly clean air being contaminated by incoming outdoor air. That clarification follows zug\_zug’s objection that outdoor VOC concentrations would usually be lower [[./thread#^c33484098|zug\_zug]]. The initial example is not evidence that outdoor air generally worsens indoor VOC exposure.
- Future effectiveness is speculative. Nathanael\_M [[./thread#^c33483969|Nathanael\_M]] suggests that improvements of 300 or 3,000 times might eventually become useful. Algae-device owner jonahbenton [[./thread#^c33483986|jonahbenton]] similarly estimates that roughly 3,000-fold performance would be needed for a noticeable CO2 effect and hopes for major future gains. Neither supplies measurements validating those thresholds or forecasts.
- The health rationale is indirect. Lionel-M [[./thread#^c33520822|Lionel-M]] invokes the target VOCs’ carcinogenicity, while lob\_it [[./thread#^c33484756|lob\_it]] cites an estimated 3.2 million annual deaths from indoor air pollution globally. The supplied text does not independently establish the manufacturer’s collective hazard characterization, quantify exposure in a particular home, or connect either claim to a demonstrated health benefit from the product.

## Conditions

- Window opening depends on local outdoor air and weather. zug\_zug [[./thread#^c33484098|zug\_zug]] expects lower outdoor VOCs, but googlryas [[./thread#^c33484170|googlryas]] qualifies that by location. D13Fd [[./thread#^c33484213|D13Fd]] points to outdoor allergens, smoke or exhaust particles, and unwanted temperatures. Opening windows could therefore improve one exposure while worsening another; heat exchangers are suggested for the temperature penalty, without evidence here that they resolve incoming pollution.
- Purchase prices make demonstrated effectiveness consequential. Maursault [[./thread#^c33484191|Maursault]] compares a quoted \$179 plant with a \$25 fan-and-furnace-filter arrangement, although those are not established as equivalent VOC treatments. For the separate Aerium algae product, fbdab103 [[./thread#^c33484226|fbdab103]] cites \$225 initially plus monthly costs and contrasts that with \$5–\$10 ordinary plants. jonahbenton [[./thread#^c33487540|jonahbenton]] agrees that Aerium is a luxury purchase.
- Affordable commercial purifiers were available to one participant, but availability was local and time-dependent. silisili [[./thread#^c33485758|silisili]] reports paying \$34.99 for a model whose listing was subsequently unavailable and mentions a \$99 Costco alternative with extra filters. thecoppinger [[./thread#^c33485369|thecoppinger]] says purifiers seem expensive where they are. These examples do not establish universal prices or long-term replacement-filter costs.
- Recurring consumables and the complete kit affect the plant’s economics. davidmurdoch [[./thread#^c33483821|davidmurdoch]] worries about a proprietary microbiome subscription. Lionel-M [[./thread#^c33520822|Lionel-M]] replies that subscriptions were not planned at that time, only bundles, while Lionel-M [[./thread#^c33520789|Lionel-M]] recommends regular microbiome additions for maximum performance. Lionel-M [[./thread#^c33520810|Lionel-M]] also says cuttings may be shared and will survive without the special soil or microbes, but claims purification performance requires the full system, including plant size, shell, and microbiome.
- Housing space and maintenance constrain scaling up plants. ch4s3 [[./thread#^c33485642|ch4s3]] already owns pothos but has limited window and shelf space, making a genuine efficiency gain attractive. masklinn [[./thread#^c33483886|masklinn]] suggests using their trailing growth to save space. mrweasel [[./thread#^c33483931|mrweasel]] questions whether people who forget watering would reliably maintain bacterial additions. These considerations affect practicality without demonstrating air-cleaning performance.

## Lived experiences

- silisili [[./thread#^c33484071|silisili]] reports that inexpensive purifiers substantially reduced persistent dust after tile removal, estimating at least a 90\% reduction without supplying an instrument or readings. In another example, silisili [[./thread#^c33489900|silisili]] says one Air400 serving roughly 2,000 square feet extended glass-cabinet cleaning intervals from about three days to two or three weeks. These are firsthand observations of dust and cleaning burden, not quantified PM2.5 exposure reductions.
- Sunspark [[./thread#^c33488267|Sunspark]] reports continued heavy dust despite using MERV 11 filtration, together with musty-smelling ductwork in spring and autumn that was less noticeable in summer and winter. This is a contrasting household experience, but it supplies neither pollutant readings nor a diagnosis of the dust or odor source.
- daleswanson [[./thread#^c33488104|daleswanson]] describes a personally assembled monitoring system: an SGP40 for VOCs, an SCD40 described as measuring true CO2, and a PMSA003I for dust, connected through an ESP32 and ESPHome to Home Assistant via MQTT. They found the Qwiic connections straightforward. This establishes hands-on setup experience, but includes no concentrations, calibration results, or accuracy comparisons.
- dylan604 [[./thread#^c33484461|dylan604]] reports feeling better in rooms containing plants than in barren rooms, explicitly allowing that this might have nothing to do with air cleaning. Thiez [[./thread#^c33484496|Thiez]] responds that an engineered plant is unnecessary for that feeling. The reported benefit is subjective environmental comfort, not demonstrated pollutant removal.
- jonahbenton [[./thread#^c33483986|jonahbenton]] owns an Aerium and is happy with its appearance and sound despite considering its CO2 consumption inadequate for a noticeable effect. jonahbenton [[./thread#^c33487540|jonahbenton]] describes enjoying its living-lava-lamp appearance and bubbling water. Satisfaction with the device is therefore expressly separate from a measured air-quality improvement.
- Plant-care experiences differ. agilob [[./thread#^c33485163|agilob]] reports keeping approximately 120 snake plants and receiving no reports of deaths among gifted plants, whereas throwawaymaths [[./thread#^c33486273|throwawaymaths]] says a snake plant was the only supposedly low-maintenance plant they killed. jordemort [[./thread#^c33484454|jordemort]] reports propagating one pothos into twelve office plants. These accounts inform maintenance and propagation feasibility, not purification efficacy.
- fuzzfactor [[./thread#^c33487404|fuzzfactor]] recalls a 1970s job servicing decorative plants in hotels and restaurants: weekly outdoor watering and draining, brief sunlight, and replacement or rehabilitation of sick plants. This is firsthand evidence of the labor behind a plant service, without an air-quality outcome.
- ChrisMarshallNY [[./thread#^c33483668|ChrisMarshallNY]] relays a former coworker’s account that tea plants in her home in China smelled pleasant and cleaned the air. This is secondhand testimony about scent and perceived cleaning, with no pollutant measurements or reported health effects.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Does the engineered plant remove enough pollution under ordinary household conditions to matter? Nathanael\_M [[./thread#^c33483995|Nathanael\_M]] asks for absolute performance, while Patrick-T [[./thread#^c33520783|Patrick-T]] responds with relative claims and a white-paper link. Because its performance tables are not reproduced, this unit cannot establish removal rates, sustained room-level reductions, or superiority over ventilation and other treatments.
- Which affordable sensors are reliable remains unanswered. theptip [[./thread#^c33487724|theptip]] asks for a reputable VOC meter to establish need and compare plants; daleswanson [[./thread#^c33488104|daleswanson]] supplies a DIY component list. No supplied comment establishes calibration quality, selectivity, drift, measurement uncertainty, or agreement with a reference instrument.
- Purifier sizing remains disputed because commenters use different success criteria. panarky [[./thread#^c33488934|panarky]] recommends halving advertised coverage and estimates eight nominally 360-square-foot units for a 1,500-square-foot apartment. silisili [[./thread#^c33489900|silisili]] counters with visible dust reduction from one purifier in approximately 2,000 square feet. Reduced surface dust does not, on the evidence supplied, settle adequate fine-particle exposure control throughout the space.
- The relative effectiveness of inexpensive gas treatments is unresolved. dinvlad [[./thread#^c33483962|dinvlad]] asks about activated-carbon bags, schainks [[./thread#^c33484465|schainks]] requests evidence for the baking-soda claim, and Patrick-T [[./thread#^c33520861|Patrick-T]] disputes the availability of effective, harmless VOC-purifying technologies. No supplied comparison measures removal, saturation, replacement frequency, by-products, or cost per useful reduction.
- Major parts of the approved question have no direct evidence in this unit. daleswanson [[./thread#^c33488104|daleswanson]] lists sensors without numeric PM2.5, NO2, or CO2 exposure results; D13Fd [[./thread#^c33484213|D13Fd]] mentions smoke and exhaust only generally. There are no supplied cooking-emission experiments, traffic-specific or wildfire-specific exposure histories, or firsthand cognition and sleep outcomes. The comfort accounts from dylan604 [[./thread#^c33484461|dylan604]] and jonahbenton [[./thread#^c33487540|jonahbenton]] cannot resolve those health and exposure questions.
