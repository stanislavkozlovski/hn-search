# [An airport cooled by natural ventilation](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49842462)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants debate whether an airport designed around natural ventilation and air movement can provide comfort without conventional air conditioning. They compare experiences in passive buildings, discuss the roles of sea breezes, thermal mass, shading, humidity, airflow, and occupant acclimatization, and question what temperatures count as comfortable or productive. The thread clarifies distinctions between ventilation and air conditioning, including fresh-air intake and recirculation, and considers fumes near airports. Commenters also weigh the energy and design tradeoffs of passive cooling against installing AC and rooftop solar.
Tags: passive cooling, natural ventilation, airport design, thermal comfort, humidity, air conditioning, building design

## Relevance

The discussion includes firsthand accounts of comfort in naturally ventilated buildings and contrasts a breezy new airport with a stuffy older terminal. It raises how moving air affects perceived temperature, humidity and heat tradeoffs, and whether ventilation brings in outside fumes; the evidence is about comfort and ventilation more than measured pollutant levels. ## Arguments

- Cooling and ventilation serve different functions. viraptor [[./thread#^c49853712|viraptor]] says closed-loop AC supplies no ventilation, while klardotsh [[./thread#^c49852729|klardotsh]] argues that a crowded airport must receive fresh air, potentially through an ERV/HRV or another intake. After initially treating mechanical ventilation as AC, rrr\_oh\_man [[./thread#^c49852057|rrr\_oh\_man]] accepts these distinctions [[./thread#^c49854162|rrr\_oh\_man]] [[./thread#^c49854165|rrr\_oh\_man]]. The exchange does not establish any particular system's air quality.
- Air movement can improve comfort without lowering indoor air temperature. lostlogin [[./thread#^c49851140|lostlogin]] makes this argument in response to fouc [[./thread#^c49850925|fouc]], who objects that maintaining outdoor temperature indoors is not cooling. Separately, viraptor [[./thread#^c49856875|viraptor]] answers nitwit005's objection about powered ceiling fans [[./thread#^c49851174|nitwit005]] by distinguishing indoor circulation from ventilation across the building boundary.
- Passive design and AC can complement each other. lostlogin [[./thread#^c49851068|lostlogin]] argues that reducing solar exposure, particularly through large sun-facing glass surfaces, can reduce the cooling burden. This supports considering building design alongside mechanical cooling, although the comment supplies no measured energy savings or indoor temperatures.
- Ventilation still requires attention to individual sources. viraptor [[./thread#^c49851051|viraptor]] says airport food outlets need proper extraction and notes that mechanical ventilation also draws outdoor air, potentially sharing the outdoor sources raised in the discussion. This is a practical design argument; no cooking-emission measurements or extraction-performance results are supplied.

## Counterarguments

- Passive cooling can impose other comfort costs when it performs poorly. teruakohatu [[./thread#^c49852059|teruakohatu]] reports an office where most of the windows were blocked to address the problem, leaving inadequate lighting. This adds a daylight tradeoff to plantain's complaint that naturally ventilated facilities have consistently felt too hot [[./thread#^c49850642|plantain]].
- Heat tolerance does not necessarily establish satisfactory working conditions. trhway [[./thread#^c49853760|trhway]] argues that sweating and lost focus make inexpensive AC worthwhile. rayiner [[./thread#^c49853230|rayiner]] invokes Lee Kuan Yew's account of AC improving tropical office productivity. Neither supplies a cognitive study, controlled comparison, or evidence linking the claimed effects to CO2 or another pollutant.
- Acclimatization has disputed limits. zamadatix [[./thread#^c49852518|zamadatix]] argues that widespread demand for heating and cooling suggests adaptation is not equally enjoyable. hyperpape [[./thread#^c49851734|hyperpape]], responding to a claim that reduced AC use makes 30°C comfortable, says they already live without AC and still find that temperature tempting grounds to install it.
- Moving humid air may leave substantial discomfort and moisture problems. xattt [[./thread#^c49851953|xattt]] anticipates stickiness and condensation on cooler surfaces, including wet spots and puddles. These are proposed failure mechanisms, without a reported inspection, humidity log, or documented condensation event at this airport.

## Assumptions

- Evidence boundary for story 49842462, part 0: athrun [[./thread#^c49851718|athrun]] reports perceived comfort, and comrade1234 [[./thread#^c49850623|comrade1234]] reports headaches associated with jet-fuel odor. No supplied comment reports PM2.5, NO2 or CO2 concentrations, sensor calibration or reliability tests, filtration performance, or wildfire-smoke exposure. This unit therefore cannot answer those parts of the approved question quantitatively.
- The most specific airport figures are quoted design claims. fouc [[./thread#^c49850925|fouc]] quotes a target of indoor temperature matching outdoors with airflow of 1 metre per second. rayiner [[./thread#^c49853230|rayiner]] quotes 30°C humid summer conditions and a claimed 4–5°C reduction in perceived temperature. These are not commenter measurements of achieved airflow, lower air temperature, or pollutant removal.
- Descriptors and symptoms do not identify pollutants. athrun's description of the old terminal as stuffy [[./thread#^c49851718|athrun]] does not establish elevated CO2. comrade1234's fuel-odor headaches [[./thread#^c49850623|comrade1234]] do not identify the responsible substance or dose. Likewise, the work-performance concerns raised by trhway [[./thread#^c49853760|trhway]] cannot be attributed to measured air contamination.
- Reported temperatures and humidity have limited measurement provenance. jltsiren [[./thread#^c49852710|jltsiren]] gives an annual indoor temperature range, and pezezin [[./thread#^c49852375|pezezin]] describes 30°C with 90\% humidity in Japan, but neither names instruments, locations of sensors, calibration, or logging methods. Their accounts support descriptions of personal conditions and preferences, rather than sensor validation or controlled exposure comparisons.

## Conditions

- Geography and building exposure matter. athrun [[./thread#^c49851718|athrun]] attributes the Réunion arrivals hall's success to its coastal position and powerful sea breeze. The adjacent older terminal feels much stuffier despite also being mostly passively cooled. This supports a site- and design-dependent account rather than a conclusion about every naturally ventilated airport.
- Nighttime conditions constrain thermal-mass cooling. throwaway198846 [[./thread#^c49851194|throwaway198846]] describes a comfortable building with substantial mass cooled at night. daedrdev [[./thread#^c49851711|daedrdev]], replying to another successful-house example, argues that sustained heatwaves with hot nights defeat passive cooling and create serious heat risk. The thread does not test that absolute claim or establish a failure threshold.
- The financial comparison remains unquantified. plantain [[./thread#^c49850642|plantain]] asks whether rooftop solar plus AC is really uneconomical, while trhway [[./thread#^c49853760|trhway]] calls AC cheap in both equipment and energy terms. l1tany11 [[./thread#^c49858138|l1tany11]] asserts that the airport's roof prevents solar installation. No commenter supplies project costs, electricity prices, cooling loads, solar yield, or a demonstrated roof constraint.
- The window-opening implication is conditional and inferred. klardotsh [[./thread#^c49852729|klardotsh]] emphasizes fresh-air supply for crowded spaces; drekipus [[./thread#^c49850704|drekipus]] worries about airport fumes; xattt [[./thread#^c49851953|xattt]] worries about humid outdoor air. Outdoor exchange could support ventilation and comfort while also admitting undesirable outdoor conditions. Nobody reports an open-versus-closed-window test or establishes the resulting PM2.5, NO2 or CO2 change.
- Thermostat expectations also vary with institutional constraints. theodric [[./thread#^c49853671|theodric]] cites an Italian summer AC minimum of 27°C while expressing uncertainty about whether it continued; hollerith [[./thread#^c49857176|hollerith]] recalls a historical US minimum of 78°F. These are claims about prescribed settings, not measurements of this airport or demonstrations that occupants found those temperatures comfortable.

## Lived experiences

- athrun [[./thread#^c49851718|athrun]], who says they know the airport well, finds the new arrivals hall substantially more comfortable than the old departures building. They describe the old terminal as a stuffy concrete bunker and the new hall as resembling a ship's deck. This is direct, location-specific testimony about perceived comfort, without pollutant readings.
- plantain [[./thread#^c49850642|plantain]] reports that every passive-cooling facility they have visited was uncomfortably hot, but identifies none. teruakohatu [[./thread#^c49852059|teruakohatu]] supplies a more concrete negative office experience: blocking windows produced dark rooms with inadequate lighting. These accounts conflict with the successful examples without demonstrating that the buildings shared comparable designs or climates.
- throwaway198846 [[./thread#^c49851194|throwaway198846]] reports no comfort problem in a building with a large thermal mass cooled overnight. drekipus [[./thread#^c49850704|drekipus]] reports that their father-in-law's deliberately naturally ventilated house, including a central concrete slab, stays comfortably cool on 40-degree days. The latter comment does not specify the temperature unit or provide indoor readings, humidity, or overnight conditions.
- polycancel [[./thread#^c49852360|polycancel]] describes being unable to fall asleep during the first few nights without AC in Brazil, then adapting over several days and enjoying the temperature. Air-conditioned malls subsequently felt too cold. This is a firsthand account of changing sleep comfort, with no measured sleep outcomes or pollutant exposure.
- jltsiren [[./thread#^c49852710|jltsiren]] reports indoor temperatures of 17–27°C across the year without much intervention, preferring 24–25°C during summer days and 21–22°C in winter, with cooler sleeping conditions. US conference rooms often feel too cold to them; after mountain hikes, even 17°C can temporarily feel too warm for sleep. Their preferences vary with recent exposure and activity.
- Humidity strongly changes several commenters' reported comfort. dgacmu [[./thread#^c49852840|dgacmu]] tolerated 100°F in Salt Lake but finds Pittsburgh temperatures above about 93°F oppressive, describing heat indices reaching 110°F. throw-the-towel [[./thread#^c49860302|throw-the-towel]] similarly finds dry Yerevan at 35°C more tolerable than damp Moscow at 25°C. These are personal comparisons across different places and conditions.
- pezezin [[./thread#^c49852375|pezezin]] finds 30°C tolerable in dry central Spain but painful at a reported 90\% humidity in Japan. PaulDavisThe1st [[./thread#^c49852448|PaulDavisThe1st]] finds 30°C outdoors in dry New Mexico tolerable but unacceptable for demanding programming in an office. In reply, pezezin [[./thread#^c49853330|pezezin]] proposes airflow and indoor stagnation as additional explanations; neither establishes those mechanisms through measurements.
- OptionOfT [[./thread#^c49852769|OptionOfT]] reports being acclimatized to Phoenix heat and shivering when stepping outside at 75°F. dlisboa [[./thread#^c49852783|dlisboa]] says people where they come from play sports at 100°F and later describes shaded, breezy indoor conditions around 90°F as comfortable [[./thread#^c49855512|dlisboa]]. These accounts illustrate tolerance differences without establishing safe or productive conditions for other people.
- comrade1234 [[./thread#^c49850623|comrade1234]] says jet-fuel smell often, though not always, causes a splitting headache. The comment does not establish that this happened at the featured airport. lostlogin [[./thread#^c49851042|lostlogin]] replies that small amounts smell pleasant; this is a different subjective odor response, not evidence against the headaches or a measured safe exposure level.
- theodric [[./thread#^c49853671|theodric]] recalls a Swiss workplace without central AC where engineers bought portable units to blow cold air toward their desks while discharging heat from rear vents. They also report having encountered 40°C weather in Switzerland. The anecdote shows occupants improvising local comfort, but supplies no whole-room temperature or energy measurements.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Whether passive cooling works well remains dependent on unspecified building and climate differences. plantain [[./thread#^c49850642|plantain]] reports uniformly poor experiences, while athrun [[./thread#^c49851718|athrun]] and throwaway198846 [[./thread#^c49851194|throwaway198846]] report successful examples. mejutoco [[./thread#^c49855230|mejutoco]] explicitly asks for details about the failed facilities before accepting broad accusations. Comparable temperatures, humidity, occupancy, airflow and operating conditions are missing.
- The extent to which acclimatization preserves productivity remains disputed. dlisboa [[./thread#^c49855512|dlisboa]] says adapted people can live comfortably at these temperatures; trhway [[./thread#^c49861567|trhway]] insists work and household productivity suffer, invoking siestas and concerns about pets. Those generalizations are not supported here by measured performance or physiological evidence, and polycancel's adaptation experience [[./thread#^c49852360|polycancel]] does not resolve them.
- The limits of fan-assisted comfort are not established. lostlogin [[./thread#^c49851140|lostlogin]] says moving warm air cools a person more than still air; excannuk [[./thread#^c49851556|excannuk]] counters that moving air at 35°C heats a person like a convection oven. The supplied replies provide no experiment or specification of humidity and other conditions sufficient to adjudicate that threshold.
- The airport's pollution tradeoff remains unknown. drekipus [[./thread#^c49850704|drekipus]] raises fumes as a possible drawback, and l1tany11 [[./thread#^c49858138|l1tany11]] asserts worse indoor air quality. viraptor [[./thread#^c49851051|viraptor]] observes that mechanically ventilated buildings also take in outside air. No comment documents intake placement, filtration, aircraft or traffic pollutant concentrations, or indoor/outdoor comparisons, so neither ventilation approach is shown to provide cleaner air.
