# [Stoßlüften: Shock Ventilation](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=38813856)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants discuss Stoßlüften, or briefly opening windows to exchange indoor air, and compare its effects on stale air, humidity, mold, comfort, and heat loss. They contrast rapid ventilation with leaving windows ajar and with mechanical systems that recover heat, while sharing practices and terminology across European countries and debating building standards, landlord expectations, and consumer CO2 monitoring.
Tags: ventilation, shock ventilation, indoor air quality, humidity, mold prevention, heat recovery ventilation

## Relevance

Discusses shock ventilation as a way to clear stale air and CO2, with firsthand reports and competing views on comfort, heat loss, humidity, and mold. It compares brief window opening with continuous mechanical ventilation and heat-recovery systems. ## Arguments

- Brief airing is presented as a practical way to clear accumulated indoor air: blub [[./thread#^c38818855|blub]] describes opening several windows for a few minutes to create a draft, principally removing odors and CO2. layer8 [[./thread#^c38815230|layer8]] emphasizes that the defining feature is short duration.
- The proposed comfort advantage is retaining heat in the building while exchanging air. wongarsu [[./thread#^c38814253|wongarsu]] attributes this to German homes’ substantial masonry; upofadown [[./thread#^c38819042|upofadown]] emphasizes air’s low thermal mass; \_Microft [[./thread#^c38818917|\_Microft]] argues that prolonged airing gives walls and furniture more time to lose heat. These are physical explanations, not measured energy comparisons.
- Continuous mechanical ventilation is advocated for steadier air quality. throwaway8877 [[./thread#^c38818507|throwaway8877]] argues that ventilation sized for occupancy avoids intermittent CO2 and moisture buildup and reduces other indoor contaminants. ctroein89 [[./thread#^c38820105|ctroein89]] explains how heat recovery transfers heat between outgoing and incoming air, reducing the heating or cooling needed while supplying outside air.
- Moisture removal may require a different schedule from quickly clearing CO2. Summarizing a Fraunhofer booklet, blub [[./thread#^c38818855|blub]] says prolonged partial opening with heating allows moisture stored in plaster, carpets and other materials to evaporate and leave. This is an attributed publication claim, not a firsthand comparison of mold outcomes.
- Heating controls may materially affect window-ventilation costs. genman [[./thread#^c38819629|genman]] argues that radiators beneath open windows can lose heat directly outdoors and that thermostatic valves may increase output in response to incoming cold air. They recommend shutting heating off during brief airing and favor heat-recovery ventilation as the longer-term solution.
- A competing practical position favors established habits over instrumentation. the\_mitsuhiko [[./thread#^c38814225|the\_mitsuhiko]] argues that regular ventilation makes CO2 sufficiently predictable that a monitor is unnecessary, and later predicts that interest in monitoring will fade [[./thread#^c38814572|the\_mitsuhiko]]. Neither claim is backed by a documented ventilation schedule or longitudinal monitor-use evidence.

## Counterarguments

- Quickly refreshing a bedroom does not establish adequate ventilation throughout the night. throwaway8877 [[./thread#^c38818635|throwaway8877]] argues that CO2 and humidity can accumulate again by morning and associates this with poor sleep and headaches. These are asserted consequences; the comment supplies no overnight measurements or health observations.
- The claim that occupants can reliably sense when to ventilate is directly challenged. flemhans [[./thread#^c38814336|flemhans]] says 2000 ppm is imperceptible to them, while Smaug123 [[./thread#^c38814471|Smaug123]] says inattention and a depressed state can prevent recognition or action. These accounts undermine a universal sensory rule without establishing its reliability across people.
- The objection that mechanical ventilation means energy-intensive air conditioning rests on a terminology mismatch. In replies to sjfjsjdjwvwvc [[./thread#^c38819871|sjfjsjdjwvwvc]], ctroein89 [[./thread#^c38820105|ctroein89]] distinguishes fans and heat recovery from air conditioning; ricardobeat [[./thread#^c38819949|ricardobeat]] describes extraction from wet rooms with replacement air entering through vents. The systems being discussed are not interchangeable.
- moron4hire [[./thread#^c38815118|moron4hire]] dismisses shock ventilation as largely unnecessary, drawing on homes where proper ventilation prevented mold problems. dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c38818366|dzhiurgis]] disputes that dismissal, claiming that pandemic-era CO2 monitoring exposed widespread stuffiness. The first supplies a limited personal housing history; the second supplies no readings supporting its broader claim.
- Mold cannot automatically be attributed to occupants failing to air rooms. fabian2k [[./thread#^c38814227|fabian2k]] says poor construction can also cause it and that responsibility may be shared. This qualifies landlord instructions to ventilate and prevents interpreting mold alone as proof of inadequate airing.
- Heat-recovery advocates encounter objections about electricity consumption, incomplete recovery and moisture. bgnn [[./thread#^c38820431|bgnn]] raises all three, including possible mold when outside air is warmer and humid. Dylan16807 [[./thread#^c38820936|Dylan16807]] asks why that would be worse than window airing at the same exchanged-air volume; no answer establishing the difference follows.

## Assumptions

- Calling incoming air fresh implicitly assumes it improves the relevant exposure. euroderf [[./thread#^c38814153|euroderf]] describes flushing airborne contamination outside, while throwaway8877 [[./thread#^c38818507|throwaway8877]] advocates continuous replacement air. Neither compares indoor and outdoor pollutant concentrations, so this assumption is untested for polluted outdoor conditions.
- The monitor-free approach assumes the ventilation routine remains adequate for the room and its occupants. the\_mitsuhiko [[./thread#^c38814572|the\_mitsuhiko]] supplies no frequency, room volume or occupancy information, while throwaway8877 [[./thread#^c38818507|throwaway8877]] explicitly makes adequate mechanical ventilation conditional on sufficient capacity for the people present.
- The numerical CO2 reports have unverified measurement quality. timrichard [[./thread#^c38814011|timrichard]] identifies using a monitor but provides no model, calibration, placement or time series. flemhans [[./thread#^c38814336|flemhans]] gives ppm-based sensory thresholds without explaining how concentrations were established. Neither account supports judging sensor accuracy or repeatability.
- Rapid recovery of room temperature is sometimes treated as evidence of little energy loss. Daniel\_sk [[./thread#^c38819417|Daniel\_sk]] and the\_mitsuhiko [[./thread#^c38814474|the\_mitsuhiko]] attribute reheating to stored warmth in walls or heating systems. That observation alone does not quantify energy consumption; upofadown [[./thread#^c38819042|upofadown]] explicitly notes that replacement air still has to be heated.

## Conditions

- The strongest arguments for comfortable brief airing concern cold-weather homes with substantial thermal mass and radiator or underfloor heating. wongarsu [[./thread#^c38814253|wongarsu]] describes German masonry construction, and the\_mitsuhiko [[./thread#^c38814474|the\_mitsuhiko]] contrasts European heating arrangements with forced-air heating. These contextual descriptions do not establish equivalent results in every building.
- Existing ventilation matters more than the label of the practice. spinningarrow [[./thread#^c38814790|spinningarrow]] describes Oslo apartments ranging from passive inlets plus wet-room extraction to balanced heat-recovery systems. froh [[./thread#^c38815232|froh]] says newer buildings retain similar thermal mass, suggesting that the move toward mechanical ventilation is about heat recovery rather than necessarily different construction materials.
- Installation expense and available space constrain alternatives. ricardobeat [[./thread#^c38819949|ricardobeat]] describes systems with heat recovery and filters as substantially more expensive than basic extraction. bgnn [[./thread#^c38820431|bgnn]] reports finding roughly 90\% maximum recovery within their researched budget and size range, but gives no products or prices. jeffrallen [[./thread#^c38814431|jeffrallen]] also calls Swiss Minergie systems expensive without supplying costs.
- Window availability limits practical choices. palemoonale [[./thread#^c38814978|palemoonale]] points to sealed US offices and hotel rooms; jeffrallen [[./thread#^c38814431|jeffrallen]] describes Swiss homes with very large or non-opening windows. These are commenters’ descriptions, not evidence that all buildings in either country have those constraints.
- Rental rules can prescribe ventilation or restrict other moisture-management options. BrandiATMuhkuh [[./thread#^c38814118|BrandiATMuhkuh]] reports an Austrian flat manual requiring winter airing, and groestl [[./thread#^c38818343|groestl]] corroborates the Austrian lease-requirement discussion. Conversely, fch42 [[./thread#^c38822389|fch42]] reports English agreements requiring indoor clothes drying because outdoor drying was considered unsightly.
- Weather changes both the tradeoffs and whether airing happens. fulafel [[./thread#^c38824656|fulafel]] argues that heat recovery saves particularly substantial energy in colder climates; bgnn [[./thread#^c38820431|bgnn]] raises unresolved concerns about warm, humid outdoor air. drsim [[./thread#^c38818079|drsim]] reports automated windows that refuse to open during rain or excessive cold, limiting the availability of that ventilation method.

## Lived experiences

- timrichard [[./thread#^c38814011|timrichard]] provides the clearest firsthand before-and-after measurement account: after buying a CO2 monitor, they began airing their home office and report returning close to 400 ppm with little perceived room-temperature loss. Starting concentration, duration, occupancy and measured temperature change are absent.
- vidarh [[./thread#^c38814053|vidarh]] reports opening several windows at least a couple of times daily in winter, following a family habit. They find short airing more comfortable than leaving a window cracked because their body and the building do not cool much. sirobg [[./thread#^c38814145|sirobg]] similarly describes brief winter airing among people they know in France; neither supplies pollutant measurements.
- Phelinofist [[./thread#^c38814149|Phelinofist]] reports being taught the practice in childhood, encountering it in landlord requirements, and airing before bed because fresher or cooler air helps them sleep. This is a subjective sleep benefit with no CO2 readings or separation of cooling from other air-quality changes.
- flemhans [[./thread#^c38814336|flemhans]] reports no sensory warning at 2000 ppm but stuffiness around 4000 ppm. In the opposing reply chain, the\_mitsuhiko [[./thread#^c38814809|the\_mitsuhiko]] says home CO2 observations validate their own and others’ ability to notice deteriorating air, but provides no paired concentrations and symptom records.
- Smaug123 [[./thread#^c38814471|Smaug123]] describes a self-reinforcing difficulty: when not paying attention, they can become too depressed to recognize the situation. The account concerns awareness and subjective state; it includes no CO2 measurement and does not establish that CO2 caused depression.
- moron4hire [[./thread#^c38815118|moron4hire]] reports mold problems confined to bathrooms lacking ventilation fans and no mold problems in places they considered properly ventilated. This supports the relevance of bathroom extraction in their experience, without testing bedroom CO2 or the usefulness of window airing elsewhere.
- drsim [[./thread#^c38818079|drsim]] reports successful daily automatic airing through Velux roof windows but says radiators continue running, wasting energy. They already have Tado valves and Home Assistant but hesitate to replace useful existing automations [[./thread#^c38822836|drsim]]. karolist [[./thread#^c38818599|karolist]] proposes integration for similar reasons; that proposal is not a completed or measured improvement.
- fch42 [[./thread#^c38822389|fch42]] reports surprise after moving to England and finding both a tenancy agreement and a later lease required indoor clothes drying. Their criticism links this restriction to complaints about mold and reluctance to ventilate, but does not provide humidity measurements or a demonstrated causal sequence in their own flat.
- sjfjsjdjwvwvc [[./thread#^c38819871|sjfjsjdjwvwvc]] reports that air conditioning causes illness for them, without describing symptoms or conditions. Because the comment mistakenly equates air conditioning with the proposed mechanical ventilation, it should not be treated as a firsthand adverse outcome from an HRV.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The net energy comparison remains unquantified. konschubert [[./thread#^c38823095|konschubert]] speculates that recovered heat could outweigh fan energy by 1000 times; jeffreygoesto [[./thread#^c38823313|jeffreygoesto]] challenges the calculation, and konschubert [[./thread#^c38823340|konschubert]] offers a rough laptop-fan-versus-heating analogy. zeitgeistcowboy [[./thread#^c38815075|zeitgeistcowboy]] separately claims humidity reduction can make airing save heating energy. No metered comparison or sufficiently specified calculation supports these numerical or net-saving claims.
- The best moisture-control schedule is unsettled. blub [[./thread#^c38818855|blub]] contrasts customary two-to-three daily bursts with a booklet’s preference for prolonged partial opening to dry materials. bgnn [[./thread#^c38820431|bgnn]] raises humid-weather mold concerns about mechanical systems, while Dylan16807 [[./thread#^c38820936|Dylan16807]] questions whether equal-volume window airing would differ. Construction-related causes identified by fabian2k [[./thread#^c38814227|fabian2k]] further limit any universal schedule.
- Perception thresholds and health thresholds are not resolved. the\_mitsuhiko [[./thread#^c38814467|the\_mitsuhiko]] associates 2000 ppm with reported poor air and drowsiness; Zetobal [[./thread#^c38814639|Zetobal]] invokes 5000 ppm as a sustained-work limit without citing a standard. Zetobal later distinguishes noticing from nausea [[./thread#^c38815129|Zetobal]], but the\_mitsuhiko [[./thread#^c38815695|the\_mitsuhiko]] points out that nausea was not their claim. This exchange establishes neither nausea at 2000 ppm nor harmlessness below 5000 ppm.
- Reported benefits cannot be assigned specifically to CO2 reduction. Phelinofist [[./thread#^c38814149|Phelinofist]] combines fresher and cooler air in their sleep account, while throwaway8877 [[./thread#^c38818635|throwaway8877]] predicts headaches and poor sleep from overnight CO2 and humidity buildup. This part supplies no controlled health, cognitive-performance or sleep measurements, and no study evidence resolving those causal claims.
- There is an explicit disagreement about the preferred method. konschubert [[./thread#^c38818581|konschubert]] declares heat-exchanger ventilation best; sjfjsjdjwvwvc [[./thread#^c38819894|sjfjsjdjwvwvc]] declares the original window-airing method best without supporting reasoning. Dylan16807 [[./thread#^c38820268|Dylan16807]] argues for more continuous fresh air with lower thermal losses. The discussion does not establish a universal choice across installation costs, building constraints and weather.
- This source unit cannot resolve the approved question’s PM2.5, NO2, cooking, traffic or wildfire dimensions: it supplies no relevant concentration measurements or exposure accounts. ricardobeat [[./thread#^c38819949|ricardobeat]] mentions optional filters but provides no specifications or effectiveness results, while timrichard [[./thread#^c38814011|timrichard]] reports only CO2 improvement. Consequently, the unit does not establish when opening windows would worsen outdoor-pollution exposure or how filtration would compare.
