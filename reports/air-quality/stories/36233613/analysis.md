# [The Cuboid: A DIY air purifier that's better than a box-fan (2021)](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=36233613)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants assess a DIY air purifier design and compare it with box-fan and Corsi-Rosenthal builds and commercial purifiers. Discussion covers filtration performance, test methodology, fan type and static pressure, airflow, noise, energy use, sealing and construction, filter cost and replacement, fire concerns, room size, and ways to monitor particulate levels.
Tags: DIY-air-purifier, Corsi-Rosenthal-box, particulate-matter, airflow, filters, testing, indoor-air-quality

## Relevance

The comments compare DIY and commercial filtration, report PM2.5 readings and wildfire-smoke experience, and discuss noise, cost, filter loading, airflow, and measurement limitations. They include both personal measurements and disagreements about test comparability. ## Arguments

- DIY filtration is presented as an accessible way to reduce indoor particles. breput [[./thread#^c36234172|breput]] says Corsi–Rosenthal boxes have been tested and verified as effective and inexpensive, but supplies a link rather than results. binarymax [[./thread#^c36234333|binarymax]] reports building a successful version and notes that four filters plus a cardboard bottom avoid buying a fifth expensive MERV 13 filter.
- Several commenters emphasize filter area and airflow. DeRock [[./thread#^c36234725|DeRock]] proposes a single deep pleated filter as a simpler alternative to a cube of thin filters. adanto6840 [[./thread#^c36236316|adanto6840]] argues that sufficient air movement is crucial in demanding spaces and that PM2.5/PM10 monitoring can reveal differences that product descriptions obscure. Neither supplies a standardized comparison in the comment itself.
- Filtered outdoor-air supply is proposed as a way to address both smoke infiltration and ventilation. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36234986|ahaucnx]], who discloses an AirGradient affiliation and experience in Northern Thailand, argues that positive pressure prevents dirty air entering through gaps while fresh air keeps CO2 low. They claim consistently achieving 'zero PM' even during severe outdoor pollution, citing company experience and linked tests whose measurements are not reproduced here.
- Particle removal is distinguished from control of other pollutants. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36234986|ahaucnx]] says wildfire exposure also includes NOx and VOCs, proposes carbon filtration for those gases, and describes increasing CO2 when rooms are sealed. These are broader exposure claims; the comment does not establish NO2-specific removal performance or show measured gas concentrations.
- Monitoring and adjustable operation are presented as practical improvements. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36234986|ahaucnx]] favors automatic speed control based on pollution levels; adanto6840 [[./thread#^c36236316|adanto6840]] recommends monitoring to judge actual effectiveness; hedora [[./thread#^c36235082|hedora]] points to a commercial purifier with a PM2.5 meter and automatic regulation. The discussion supports evaluating operation in the intended space, but supplies little numerical evidence about these control strategies.
- Existing forced-air equipment is proposed as another filtration route. jacobsenscott [[./thread#^c36234637|jacobsenscott]] suggests running the furnace fan continuously with a HEPA filter. This is a suggestion rather than a reported installation or measurement, and the replies substantially qualify its compatibility, power consumption, and ventilation assumptions.

## Counterarguments

- The claimed superiority of the Cuboid is challenged because several variables change together. carabiner [[./thread#^c36235042|carabiner]] identifies different fans, ducting, elevation, orientation, and apparently limited trials, and asks for particle-count curves and repeated measurements. Jeff\_Brown [[./thread#^c36234829|Jeff\_Brown]] also notes four fully used filters versus three incompletely used ones. tedivm [[./thread#^c36235179|tedivm]] argues that a modern four-filter box-fan design would be a more relevant comparator.
- The importance of sealing remains contested in this particular comparison. yumraj [[./thread#^c36234913|yumraj]] and afterburner [[./thread#^c36242357|afterburner]] argue that gaps let air bypass the filters. Author dynm [[./thread#^c36235422|dynm]] says taping was tested and links results; yumraj [[./thread#^c36236015|yumraj]] acknowledges apparently similar taped and untaped results but finds them surprising. SkyPuncher [[./thread#^c36235171|SkyPuncher]] argues that even the imperfect arrangement captures some particles. The supplied comments do not quantify bypass or resolve its effect.
- The assertion that a four-inch filter imposes vastly greater resistance receives a direct technical rebuttal. oh\_sigh [[./thread#^c36236291|oh\_sigh]] predicts approximately 16 times the pressure difference and possible premature fan failure. sgc [[./thread#^c36241315|sgc]] explains that deeper pleats increase media area rather than requiring air to traverse four sequential layers. sfink [[./thread#^c36237354|sfink]] recognizes the pleating argument but questions interference between pleats. No matched airflow measurements settle equivalence between the designs.
- Higher static-pressure capability is not unanimously considered better for these filters. rainbowzootsuit [[./thread#^c36234497|rainbowzootsuit]] initially favors centrifugal fans. londons\_explore [[./thread#^c36234699|londons\_explore]] reports trying both and finding propeller fans deliver more air per watt through typical filters, while explicitly lacking a manometer. rainbowzootsuit [[./thread#^c36236046|rainbowzootsuit]] accepts that larger filter area could explain this, citing manufacturer pressure-versus-velocity figures rather than measurements of either home-built device.
- Positive-pressure ventilation has installation costs beyond assembling a room purifier. dogline [[./thread#^c36235106|dogline]] argues that it requires more work and that an effective recirculating device satisfies the immediate task. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36235164|ahaucnx]] acknowledges DIY boxes as useful emergency measures while arguing for more permanent protection where exposure recurs. This is a disagreement about scope and practicality, not evidence that either approach never works.
- Installing a high-efficiency furnace filter is challenged as a universal solution. geoffeg [[./thread#^c36235361|geoffeg]] reports technicians warning about airflow restriction and recommending much lower MERV ratings. jefftk [[./thread#^c36235506|jefftk]] recommends checking system specifications, while sgc [[./thread#^c36236870|sgc]] argues that duct resistance and minimum airflow also matter and describes overheating risks. Suggested MERV limits differ, including whycombagator's MERV 8 suggestion [[./thread#^c36236100|whycombagator]]; the thread establishes no universally safe rating or seasonal switching rule.
- The claim that a furnace fan necessarily brings in outdoor air is contradicted by firsthand descriptions. turtlebits [[./thread#^c36234836|turtlebits]] assumes outside air is supplied and filtered only once; willcipriano [[./thread#^c36234859|willcipriano]] and Waterluvian [[./thread#^c36234885|Waterluvian]] describe recirculating systems. afterburner [[./thread#^c36235282|afterburner]] distinguishes residential systems from offices and schools, while SkyPuncher [[./thread#^c36235217|SkyPuncher]] raises outdoor makeup-air dampers in older homes. The relevant exposure depends on the actual installation.
- Commercial purifiers are not uniformly dismissed as poor value. odensc [[./thread#^c36234338|odensc]] compares the approximately \$100 Cuboid with a similarly priced Levoit. crazygringo [[./thread#^c36234992|crazygringo]] values adjustable speed, timers, replacement reminders, and appearance. This challenges comparisons against only expensive commercial units, although adanto6840 [[./thread#^c36236316|adanto6840]] argues that consumer units can lack the throughput needed for demanding spaces.

## Assumptions

- Comparing reported CADR values assumes compatible definitions. bsilvereagle [[./thread#^c36234191|bsilvereagle]] says the article uses an ASHRAE airflow-times-removal-efficiency definition rather than AHAM's calculation accounting for contamination rate. Without reconciling those definitions and test conditions, apparently comparable performance figures may answer different questions.
- Meter categories and claims of zero particles are being treated as proxies for successful protection. BizarroLand [[./thread#^c36235084|BizarroLand]] reports moving from a warning category to a good category, and ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36234986|ahaucnx]] claims 'zero PM.' Neither supplies concentrations, category thresholds, detection limits, or sufficient measurement details in the comment. Those descriptions do not establish the absence of gaseous pollutants or a quantified health benefit.
- A causal interpretation of comfort and cognition would exceed the evidence. BizarroLand [[./thread#^c36235084|BizarroLand]] attributes better sleep to a gentle breeze and white noise alongside improved particle readings. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36234986|ahaucnx]] links elevated CO2 with drowsiness and decreased mental performance, but provides no CO2 series or cognitive testing. These accounts cannot isolate particle reduction, ventilation, temperature, and sound as causes of the reported or proposed effects.
- Filter-lifetime and ownership-cost estimates depend on untested extrapolations. londons\_explore [[./thread#^c36237938|londons\_explore]] speculates that ten times the filter area could permit decade-long service. crazygringo [[./thread#^c36235360|crazygringo]] uses a hypothetical extension of replacement intervals when comparing four DIY filters with a commercial unit. Neither is a measured lifespan comparison under matched pollution loads.
- Several airflow explanations remain hypotheses. \_Nat\_ [[./thread#^c36234994|\_Nat\_]] speculates about turbulence near blades, while Eisenstein [[./thread#^c36236599|Eisenstein]] proposes that suction closes enclosure gaps and blowing expands them. These mechanisms may motivate testing, but the supplied comments do not establish their magnitude or show that they explain the article's performance difference.

## Conditions

- Upfront affordability depends strongly on the comparator. In these June 2023 comments, fragmede [[./thread#^c36234862|fragmede]] quotes roughly \$50 for a filter strapped to a fan versus potentially \$500 for a dedicated product; hedora [[./thread#^c36235082|hedora]] cites a \$700 model with sensing and automatic control; odensc [[./thread#^c36234338|odensc]] points to a roughly \$100 Levoit. These are different products with unestablished equivalent performance, not a demonstrated universal DIY saving. fragmede's broader household-affordability assertion is not substantiated here.
- Replacement costs are disputed and depend on service life. Forbo [[./thread#^c36234380|Forbo]] claims Levoit replacements cost twice as much. odensc [[./thread#^c36234549|odensc]] counters with \$55 for two filters and a manufacturer-recommended six-to-eight-month interval, against approximately \$70 for the DIY filter set and no documented replacement interval. crazygringo [[./thread#^c36235360|crazygringo]] quotes \$23 per Levoit filter. Advertised intervals, uncertain DIY lifespan, and trust in cheaper third-party filters prevent a settled annual-cost comparison.
- Available materials and living space affect feasibility. miked85 [[./thread#^c36234896|miked85]] questions calling tape, bungees, and foam free; qup [[./thread#^c36235271|qup]] explains that DIY accounting often excludes already-owned supplies. chefandy [[./thread#^c36234972|chefandy]] describes the domestic difficulty of a bulky, improvised-looking purifier in a small apartment. These constraints differ from BizarroLand's large, substantially open-plan home [[./thread#^c36235084|BizarroLand]].
- Wildfire geography and availability matter. carabiner [[./thread#^c36233946|carabiner]] reports buying fans and filters during Seattle winters because stores sell out during smoke season. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36234986|ahaucnx]] describes recurrent severe wildfire pollution in Northern Thailand as the setting for years of home-protection work. These accounts concern repeated regional smoke exposure, not ordinary background conditions everywhere.
- Operating duration can change the financial tradeoff. Rebelgecko [[./thread#^c36235959|Rebelgecko]] argues that these DIY fan arrangements use more electricity than similarly performing dedicated purifiers, making prolonged operation different from a few emergency days, but provides no wattage measurements. hedora [[./thread#^c36235041|hedora]] similarly warns about furnace-fan consumption and suggests checking the home's meter with the fan on and off.
- Housing and ventilation arrangements vary geographically. dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c36236868|dzhiurgis]] describes single-room HRVs as widespread in renovated Lithuanian flats, without supporting prevalence data. Waterluvian [[./thread#^c36234885|Waterluvian]] describes recirculating central air and frustration when air conditioning runs despite cooler outdoor conditions. These comments identify different infrastructure and comfort opportunities, but do not quantify energy savings or the pollutant consequences of outdoor-air cooling.

## Lived experiences

- lamontcg [[./thread#^c36235254|lamontcg]] reports a four-filter cube with a cardboard base and duct-taped corners that worked during Seattle smoke days according to a PM2.5 meter; no numerical readings are supplied. Their fan blows into the filter enclosure, which is distinct from pressurizing a house with filtered outdoor air. binarymax [[./thread#^c36234333|binarymax]] also reports a successful recent build, but offers a qualitative impression rather than meter results.
- BizarroLand [[./thread#^c36235084|BizarroLand]] provides the clearest before-and-after account: a roughly \$65 box-fan build using four MERV 15 filters in a 2,600-square-foot dome home moved PM2.5 and PM10 indications from a low warning range to good within half a day. Approximately half the home formed a connected open area. They also report more uniform conditions and better sleep from breeze and white noise, while explicitly questioning transferability to a more compartmentalized home.
- Single deep-filter arrangements also have favorable reports. yumraj [[./thread#^c36234906|yumraj]] describes excellent airflow with some noise, and lancefisher [[./thread#^c36234963|lancefisher]] uses two box fans with five-inch, 20-by-25-inch MERV 16 filters during smoky summers and says they work very well. Neither provides concentrations. postpawl [[./thread#^c36234783|postpawl]] reports Lasko fans becoming loud after several months, with bearing failure offered as a suspicion rather than a diagnosis.
- fellowniusmonk [[./thread#^c36234390|fellowniusmonk]] describes a circular HEPA filter with an inline duct fan, rather than the exact Cuboid, running continuously in a kitchen because it is unobtrusively quiet. Turning it up reportedly clears air quickly when food burns. Improvised sound panels made no noticeable difference. This is a practical cooking-smoke and noise account without particle decay curves, NO2 readings, or other gas measurements.
- YZF [[./thread#^c36235689|YZF]] built a workshop filter and believes a much smaller unit suffices at home with windows and doors closed because little smoke or particulate continues entering. The comment labels this as an impression and supplies no measurements. duxup [[./thread#^c36234453|duxup]] built a unit before a remodel to address anticipated dust; that is a preparation account, not a demonstrated exposure reduction.
- Commercial-versus-DIY experiences directly conflict. adanto6840 [[./thread#^c36236316|adanto6840]] describes demanding filtration needs in a 20-by-20-foot room with a 12-foot ceiling, favors Corsi–Rosenthal boxes, and says they discarded Levoit units after finding them ineffective, invoking monitoring as the basis for comparison. crazygringo [[./thread#^c36259363|crazygringo]] reports effective Levoit operation in ordinary rooms checked with an Awair. Neither supplies comparable pollutant loads, airflow settings, or concentration curves; neogodless [[./thread#^c36256131|neogodless]] and crazygringo [[./thread#^c36259389|crazygringo]] also disagree over the room size the small Levoit can serve.
- Noise affects sustained use differently across people. ipsum2 [[./thread#^c36234150|ipsum2]] reports replacing a successful DIY box with a Coway because of noise. ary [[./thread#^c36234403|ary]] remembers box-fan sound making sleep easier during hot nights without central air conditioning, and xen2xen1 [[./thread#^c36234608|xen2xen1]] says both airflow and noise are needed because they sleep warm. Dylan16807 [[./thread#^c36235406|Dylan16807]] objects that acceptable sleeping noise can still be intrusive during the day.
- Other commercial-product accounts emphasize comfort and maintenance rather than measured removal. kennywinker [[./thread#^c36235150|kennywinker]] describes a Blueair 211+ as effective and quiet; bombcar [[./thread#^c36235345|bombcar]] likes a Smart Health unit's appearance but finds replacements expensive and wants washable prefilters. bullen [[./thread#^c36238460|bullen]] describes a quiet Sharp purifier-humidifier consuming one water container daily, filtering its tap water to limit clogging, and disabling ionization because they believe it deposits particles on surfaces. No numerical air-quality results accompany these accounts.
- adanto6840 [[./thread#^c36235322|adanto6840]] reports finding hot glue faster and easier than tape for assembly and partial disassembly, using approximately ten sticks for sealing. They also use a cheaper carbon/charcoal prefilter around MERV 10 ahead of a higher-rated filter around MERV 13, replacing the former more often. The claimed maintenance advantage is firsthand practice, but actual filter-life extension and gas removal are not measured.
- The sensor recommendations are usability accounts. entrepy123 [[./thread#^c36234981|entrepy123]] calls the ZGm053U a good CO2 device and requests a non-wireless dust meter. Rebelgecko [[./thread#^c36235936|Rebelgecko]] reports using an AirGradient Basic for CO2, PM1, PM2.5, and PM10, with database/Home Assistant integration or a USB battery and screen. Neither reports calibration, reference-instrument comparison, or an accuracy test.

## Unresolved disagreements

- When opening windows helps remains conditional and untested here. YZF [[./thread#^c36235689|YZF]] associates closed windows and doors with a manageable indoor particle load; ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36234986|ahaucnx]] warns that tighter sealing can raise CO2 while outdoor smoke enters through leakage. Together these suggest a ventilation-versus-smoke tradeoff: outdoor air could dilute accumulated CO2 while also importing particles. No paired window-open/window-closed measurements establish when that balance favors opening, and filtered fresh-air supply is proposed as an alternative.
- The broader health question remains largely unanswered by this unit. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36234986|ahaucnx]] mentions drowsiness, mental performance, NOx, and VOCs, but supplies no NO2-specific readings, CO2 concentrations, symptom tracking, cognitive tests, or demonstrated carbon-filter gas-removal rates. The supplied discussion contains no traffic-exposure comparison or clinical study results. BizarroLand's sleep improvement [[./thread#^c36235084|BizarroLand]] is an individual comfort account with simultaneous changes in airflow and sound.
- Sensor reliability cannot be ranked from these comments. entrepy123 [[./thread#^c36234981|entrepy123]] endorses a CO2 device, Rebelgecko [[./thread#^c36235936|Rebelgecko]] describes multipollutant monitoring, and crazygringo [[./thread#^c36259363|crazygringo]] relies on an Awair, but there are no side-by-side reference comparisons. Even whether the requested visible household dust is adequately represented by the suggested PM measurements is left uncertain in Rebelgecko's reply [[./thread#^c36235936|Rebelgecko]]. Positive user experience does not resolve accuracy, detection limits, or agreement between devices.
- There is no settled best design or room-coverage rule. carabiner [[./thread#^c36235042|carabiner]] calls for controlled, repeated comparisons; crazygringo [[./thread#^c36259389|crazygringo]] considers a large Corsi–Rosenthal box excessive for an apartment, while adanto6840 [[./thread#^c36236316|adanto6840]] favors its throughput in difficult conditions. tiahura [[./thread#^c36235654|tiahura]] asks whether two distributed single-filter fans would outperform one cube, without receiving results. The PC-filter proposal similarly draws opposing expectations from dsr\_ [[./thread#^c36234740|dsr\_]] and dogline [[./thread#^c36235124|dogline]], not measured room-cleaning performance.
- Filter discoloration does not resolve declining capture efficiency. londons\_explore [[./thread#^c36234663|londons\_explore]] observes darkening on the outlet side and wonders whether aged media allows more fine particles through. After they reject leakage as the explanation [[./thread#^c36237591|londons\_explore]], winrid [[./thread#^c36239145|winrid]] clarifies an alternative: turbulent room-air mixing against the clean side, rather than an enclosure leak. No penetration or pressure-drop measurements across filter age distinguish these explanations.
- Construction-material exposure is debated without emission measurements. whycombagator [[./thread#^c36236076|whycombagator]] asks about hot-glue VOCs; Eisenstein [[./thread#^c36236666|Eisenstein]] categorically dismisses them based on thermoplastic operation; diydsp [[./thread#^c36240074|diydsp]] raises possible plasticizer exposure. Separately, stinos [[./thread#^c36240875|stinos]] disputes blanket claims that caulk is necessarily smelly and messier, noting formulation differences and elasticity. Neither material's indoor exposure impact is established by these exchanges.
- Fan direction, durability, and maintenance remain partly speculative. Eisenstein [[./thread#^c36236599|Eisenstein]] favors suction for keeping enclosure gaps closed; YZF [[./thread#^c36235652|YZF]] favors filtering air before it reaches the fan. culopatin [[./thread#^c36236063|culopatin]] argues that these fans normally run unfiltered, while masukomi [[./thread#^c36240254|masukomi]] emphasizes accumulated dirt and cleaning difficulty. lamontcg's successful blowing configuration [[./thread#^c36235254|lamontcg]] shows one workable arrangement, but does not establish equal leakage, cleaning burden, or motor life across configurations.
