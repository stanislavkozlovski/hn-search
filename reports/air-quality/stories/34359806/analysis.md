# [Banning Gas Stoves Is a Terrible Idea](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=34359806)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Commenters debate proposals to restrict gas stoves, weighing indoor air pollution and asthma concerns against retrofit cost, cooking preferences, ventilation, and the small climate contribution of cooking relative to heating and industry. The thread compares induction and gas costs, performance, cookware, electrical upgrades, and grid reliability, and discusses whether incentives or building rules are preferable to bans. Participants also dispute claims about childhood asthma risk and whether better ventilation is an adequate alternative.
Tags: gas-stoves, induction-cooking, indoor-air-quality, asthma, electrification, energy-policy

## Relevance

The discussion directly addresses indoor air pollution from gas stoves, including NO2 and asthma claims, ventilation as a mitigation, and switching to induction. It includes disagreement over health evidence, costs, and whether ventilation retrofits or appliance replacement are more practical; claims about attributable asthma are study-based rather than firsthand measurements. ## Arguments

- wwweston [[./thread#^c34382980|wwweston]] presents gas cooking as an indoor exposure concern, quoting reporting about nitrogen dioxide (NO2), carbon monoxide and fine particulate matter at reportedly unsafe levels, with links to respiratory illness, cardiovascular problems and cancer. The quotation also mentions Consumer Reports testing of nitrogen oxide gases. These are secondhand claims; no concentrations, test methods or pollutant-specific health estimates appear in this unit.
- omg\_ponies [[./thread#^c34360907|omg\_ponies]] cites a study said to attribute 6–19\% of existing US childhood asthma cases to gas stoves, arguing that household choices may reflect incomplete health information and costs borne by others. This is a claim about research, not a firsthand illness account.
- bgentry [[./thread#^c34360445|bgentry]] [[./thread#^c34360956|bgentry]] argues that adequate, energy-efficient ventilation offers broader benefits than changing the stove alone: removing combustion byproducts, CO2 buildup, dust, cleaning-related VOCs and respiratory viruses. They contend that ventilation is a better and cheaper response to indoor air quality and asthma concerns, especially with existing central HVAC; no measured exposure or health improvements accompany that contention.
- omg\_ponies [[./thread#^c34361089|omg\_ponies]] argues that better ventilation and less harmful cooking equipment can be complementary improvements. This directly challenges treating ventilation and stove replacement as mutually exclusive solutions.

## Counterarguments

- norvvryo [[./thread#^c34361901|norvvryo]], replying to omg\_ponies [[./thread#^c34360907|omg\_ponies]], challenges converting the proportion of asthma cases attributed to gas stoves into a 5–20\% chance that a stove causes an individual child's asthma. They tentatively suggest 0.4–1.8\% using asthma prevalence and explicitly express uncertainty about their arithmetic. Neither calculation establishes the excess risk for a child in a gas-using household.
- omg\_ponies [[./thread#^c34361089|omg\_ponies]] disputes bgentry's [[./thread#^c34360956|bgentry]] claim that ventilation retrofits are cheaper: a heat exchanger alone could exceed the price of an entry-level portable induction cooker, before ductwork, wall penetrations and sealing. bgentry's reply [[./thread#^c34361170|bgentry]] narrows the favorable ventilation comparison to homes with suitable existing infrastructure.
- spoils19 [[./thread#^c34362159|spoils19]] claims gas offers fast heating and responsive control, but basch [[./thread#^c34364089|basch]] distinguishes induction from other electric stoves and claims induction heats pans faster. basch [[./thread#^c34364098|basch]] additionally asserts twice the speed without supplying a timed test. basch [[./thread#^c34364089|basch]] retains visible flames and pan-height control as gas advantages, while gtvwill [[./thread#^c34360649|gtvwill]] favors induction's temperature control but identifies wok burners as a gas strength.

## Assumptions

- bgentry's [[./thread#^c34360445|bgentry]] [[./thread#^c34360956|bgentry]] proposed health solution assumes that adequate ventilation sufficiently reduces relevant cooking exposures. The discussion supplies no airflow rates, capture effectiveness, operating practices or exposure measurements with which to assess that assumption.
- The cost dispute compares different interventions: omg\_ponies [[./thread#^c34361089|omg\_ponies]] uses an entry-level portable induction cooker, whereas bgentry [[./thread#^c34361170|bgentry]] compares replacing an existing ventilator with a professionally installed ERV against a permanent, expensive induction installation intended to match their preferred cooking performance. These are not equivalent project specifications.
- Applying the health claims to a particular household requires assumptions about how the cited findings transfer to its occupants, cooking practices and ventilation. omg\_ponies [[./thread#^c34360907|omg\_ponies]] and wwweston [[./thread#^c34382980|wwweston]] provide no study methods or household-specific evidence that would establish causality, individual risk or the contribution of each pollutant.

## Conditions

- Existing infrastructure changes the practical choice. bgentry [[./thread#^c34361170|bgentry]] describes central HVAC and an existing ventilator as favorable conditions for an ERV retrofit. Their proposed induction conversion would require opening walls, running a new 220 V line from an outdoor panel because the garage subpanel is insufficient, and disconnecting the gas supply. Their assertion that most US homes from the preceding 40-plus years have central HVAC is not documented in the supplied discussion.
- Cooktop prices vary across the examples. thealistra [[./thread#^c34367228|thealistra]], replying to microtonal [[./thread#^c34360468|microtonal]], reports \$250 four-zone induction stoves at IKEA in Poland; microtonal reports approximately €500 for an AEG cooktop. These figures do not establish the complete installed cost for a home needing electrical work.
- bgentry [[./thread#^c34360733|bgentry]] proposes installing spare or dedicated 220 V wiring during construction to make later appliance conversion easier. blacksmith\_tb [[./thread#^c34360555|blacksmith\_tb]] favors retrofit incentives to avoid penalizing people unable to afford switching. Both identify financial access as a condition of practical adoption.
- spoils19 [[./thread#^c34362159|spoils19]] claims gas can offer lower operating costs and cooking during power outages. These are potential household tradeoffs, but the comment supplies no local tariffs, metered consumption, appliance-specific outage requirements or firsthand outage experience.

## Lived experiences

- microtonal [[./thread#^c34360468|microtonal]] reports switching from gas to an AEG induction cooktop after moving from gas heating to a heat pump. The cooktop cost about €500; some older pots performed poorly and €10 IKEA replacements worked. Adjustment took a few days, and cleaning became substantially easier. This is firsthand evidence about cost and usability, with no reported air measurements or health changes.
- bgentry [[./thread#^c34361170|bgentry]] reports spending under \$2,500 for professional installation of a high-end, oversized energy recovery ventilator replacing an inefficient, code-installed ventilator. This supports the feasibility and price of that particular retrofit, but supplies no before-and-after NO2, particulate, CO2 or symptom results.
- kodyo [[./thread#^c34360671|kodyo]] [[./thread#^c34361317|kodyo]] reports satisfaction with gas cooking speed and operating cost and prefers the current stove to a previous electric stove. The previous electric technology is unspecified, so this does not establish a comparison with induction. No personal respiratory symptoms or measured exposure are reported.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Whether ventilation or stove replacement delivers better health protection per dollar remains unresolved. bgentry [[./thread#^c34360445|bgentry]] [[./thread#^c34361170|bgentry]] favors ventilation under suitable infrastructure conditions, while omg\_ponies [[./thread#^c34361089|omg\_ponies]] disputes the cost ranking and favors combining interventions. Neither supplies comparable exposure reductions or health outcomes for the competing projects.
- This source unit contains no firsthand PM2.5, NO2 or CO2 measurements and no sensor models, calibration checks or reliability comparisons. wwweston [[./thread#^c34382980|wwweston]] quotes external testing without numerical results; bgentry [[./thread#^c34360956|bgentry]] mentions CO2 only as something ventilation can address. The reference to fine particulate matter is not a reported PM2.5 reading.
- No firsthand changes in asthma symptoms, cognition, sleep or thermal comfort are reported. The asthma discussion is a contested interpretation of research in omg\_ponies [[./thread#^c34360907|omg\_ponies]] and norvvryo [[./thread#^c34361901|norvvryo]]; microtonal's [[./thread#^c34360468|microtonal]] positive experience concerns adaptation and cleaning. The supplied evidence cannot establish those health or comfort outcomes after either intervention.
- The unit cannot determine when opening windows helps or worsens exposure. bgentry [[./thread#^c34360956|bgentry]] advocates ventilation generally and describes a mechanical ERV installation [[./thread#^c34361170|bgentry]], but provides no window-opening observations or indoor–outdoor comparisons. Traffic pollution, wildfire smoke and measured filtration performance are not addressed.
- Household autonomy and the adequacy of subjective satisfaction remain contested. kodyo [[./thread#^c34361317|kodyo]] treats satisfactory performance and personal choice as sufficient reasons to retain gas; omg\_ponies [[./thread#^c34360939|omg\_ponies]] argues that satisfaction alone does not justify the technology, and wwweston [[./thread#^c34382980|wwweston]] argues that convenience and low cost leave pollution harms unaccounted for. The exchange does not resolve that value disagreement.
