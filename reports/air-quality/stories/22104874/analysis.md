# [A small DIY air purifier](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=22104874)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants assess whether a compact DIY purifier built with computer fans and a HEPA filter can move enough air to meaningfully clean a room. They debate fan static pressure, filter resistance and surface area, airflow, sealing, and how to measure performance, often contrasting the small build with box-fan and furnace-filter designs. The conversation also covers purifier noise, power use, filter types and costs, wildfire smoke, allergies, HVAC constraints, and whether room filtration has demonstrated health benefits. Alternative approaches include positive-pressure filtered ventilation, air washers, ionizers, ozone devices, plants, and DIY air-quality monitors.
Tags: DIY air purifier, HEPA, airflow, filtration, wildfire smoke, air-quality measurement

## Relevance

The discussion evaluates DIY air purifiers for particulate removal, including PM2.5 measurement, fan and filter performance, and firsthand experiences with wildfire smoke, allergies, and dust. It compares designs and discusses practical limits such as airflow, noise, filter cost, and HVAC restrictions; claims about health benefits are treated with varying degrees of uncertainty. ## Arguments

- kn100 [[./thread#^c22105502|kn100]] and adrianN [[./thread#^c22105459|adrianN]] argue that particle filtration can be inexpensive: attach a suitable filter to a box fan. dublinben [[./thread#^c22111304|dublinben]] points to a University of Michigan demonstration as supporting evidence. That linked demonstration is not reproduced in the supplied export, so it is distinct from commenters’ own measurements and experiences.
- ddeck [[./thread#^c22105693|ddeck]] argues that ample pleated filter area reduces resistance and increases dust-holding capacity. post\_break [[./thread#^c22106915|post\_break]] proposes a two-filter triangle, while logfromblammo [[./thread#^c22108150|logfromblammo]] proposes five filters around a fan. DoctorOetker [[./thread#^c22113373|DoctorOetker]] explains the underlying proposal as reducing resistance through parallel filter area, allowing useful flow without requiring unusually high fan pressure.
- amluto [[./thread#^c22109958|amluto]] distinguishes single-pass filtration from repeated room-air cleaning: a less restrictive MERV 13 filter might remove particles faster overall than HEPA. Their follow-up explicitly uses a hypothetical filter capturing 50\% of a target particle size while moving twice as much air [[./thread#^c22120018|amluto]]. This is an efficiency-versus-throughput argument, not a measured comparison of products.
- qpiox [[./thread#^c22106009|qpiox]] says true HEPA captures PM2.5. trehalose [[./thread#^c22107494|trehalose]] distinguishes particle removal from VOC removal, arguing that HEPA does not address the VOCs discussed in the plant study and that activated charcoal serves a different purpose. kn100 [[./thread#^c22105502|kn100]] proposes adding aquarium carbon, but qualifies its suitability for air with 'as far as I know.'
- Nexxxeh [[./thread#^c22108325|Nexxxeh]] recommends a washable coarse prefilter to intercept pet hair before it loads the finer filter. spookthesunset [[./thread#^c22109073|spookthesunset]] recommends filtering on the intake to keep the fan clean; wang\_li [[./thread#^c22111831|wang\_li]] suggests window screen as a cheap prefilter. These are maintenance proposals, not demonstrations that coarse screening captures PM2.5.
- spookthesunset [[./thread#^c22109183|spookthesunset]] proposes filtered outdoor supply through a sealed window opening, with other windows and doors closed, to maintain positive pressure and reduce unfiltered infiltration. Their later suggestion combines a low-volume filtered supply with an indoor recirculating purifier [[./thread#^c22113413|spookthesunset]]. The claimed advantage concerns controlling entry paths; no household test of the combined arrangement is supplied.
- crispyambulance [[./thread#^c22108448|crispyambulance]] suggests that a device too small for an apartment might still suit localized solder-fume extraction after adding tubing and appropriate filtration. This preserves a narrower potential use rather than assuming that inadequate whole-room capacity makes every application ineffective.
- jwr [[./thread#^c22105621|jwr]] emphasizes health concerns from small particles, and storyinmemo [[./thread#^c22109147|storyinmemo]] invokes a linked report of mortality associations even at pollution levels meeting national standards. These comments motivate reducing PM2.5 exposure, but neither supplies firsthand health measurements or directly establishes the benefit of an additional purifier in an already relatively clean home.
- Eric\_WVGG [[./thread#^c22106865|Eric\_WVGG]] cites reporting about higher test scores after filters were installed in a California school following a gas scare. This is the unit’s principal cognition-related claim. It is a secondhand account of a comparison with other schools, without numerical results or enough study detail in the export to assess causation or identify the responsible pollutant.

## Counterarguments

- lmilcin [[./thread#^c22105641|lmilcin]] challenges the miniature purifier’s ability to move enough air through HEPA and demands before-and-after testing. qpiox [[./thread#^c22106087|qpiox]] similarly rejects extrapolating from a desktop-sized enclosure to a real room. storyinmemo [[./thread#^c22108341|storyinmemo]] estimates that filtering a 1,000-square-foot apartment would take two to three days, versus their proposed roughly half-hour turnover target; those figures are estimates, not demonstrated performance measurements.
- 0-\_-0 [[./thread#^c22105857|0-\_-0]] counters the blanket objection to PC fans by identifying models designed for static pressure. However, qpiox [[./thread#^c22106130|qpiox]] points to example HEPA specifications around 250 Pa and argues that adequacy depends on the actual filter. maxerickson [[./thread#^c22106238|maxerickson]] likewise questions whether radiator-oriented fans can deliver enough flow through HEPA. Fan category alone does not settle the specific design’s performance.
- driverdan [[./thread#^c22108025|driverdan]] distinguishes detecting pollution from removing it, then asks for a no-purifier control because particles can settle after cooking stops [[./thread#^c22111993|driverdan]]. cosmolev [[./thread#^c22107324|cosmolev]] also requests a comparable occupied-room control; their follow-up explicitly describes inhalation as harmful [[./thread#^c22107701|cosmolev]]. Their claim that breathing itself can drive room counts almost to zero is unverified here and was not a recommendation to use lungs as filters.
- majos [[./thread#^c22107629|majos]] questions meaningful health gains for generally healthy people outside highly polluted settings. Their reading of a linked review is that studies are small, often concern asthma or pet allergies, and offer limited evidence for ordinary homes. They characterize an approximately 5 mmHg blood-pressure reduction as minor; that characterization is the commenter’s appraisal, not an independently established clinical conclusion.
- dr\_orpheus [[./thread#^c22108209|dr\_orpheus]] and lotsofpulp [[./thread#^c22109087|lotsofpulp]] argue that dense filters can reduce HVAC airflow and impair heating or cooling. Their accounts draw partly on technician advice. lotsofpulp later acknowledges that outcomes depend on the particular system and return arrangement [[./thread#^c22110347|lotsofpulp]], limiting the earlier blanket description of such filters as a scam.
- dredmorbius [[./thread#^c22113192|dredmorbius]] directly opposes putting the purifier in a window during outdoor particle pollution: increasing outdoor-air throughput while filtering each incoming parcel only once could leave more particles indoors than repeatedly filtering existing room air. This challenges the proposed positive-pressure arrangement; it does not establish the best ventilation strategy for every pollutant.
- loldot\_ [[./thread#^c22106725|loldot\_]] prefers plants and cites a NASA study, but minikites [[./thread#^c22107245|minikites]] argues that an ordinary home would need roughly 680 plants for a comparable effect. csytan [[./thread#^c22107904|csytan]] narrows that assertion to matching the study’s effect, rather than proving literally no effect from fewer plants. trehalose [[./thread#^c22107494|trehalose]] further notes that the cited work concerns selected VOCs, not demonstrated particle removal.
- littlejohnny [[./thread#^c22106199|littlejohnny]] proposes water washing as potentially excellent for both particles and chemicals, but pritovido [[./thread#^c22106410|pritovido]] warns about microbial growth and maintenance beyond water replacement. dredmorbius [[./thread#^c22113221|dredmorbius]] adds that substantial humidification may be undesirable in already humid conditions. These are competing mechanism and maintenance claims, without comparative removal measurements in the export.
- bipson [[./thread#^c22107198|bipson]] cites a video to argue that at least some ionizers do not work. htfu [[./thread#^c22108574|htfu]] notes that some commercial devices combine prefilters, charcoal, HEPA, and ionization, but that observation does not isolate the ionizer’s contribution. qpiox [[./thread#^c22105977|qpiox]] also challenges ozone-based purification on health grounds, directly opposing Zardoz84’s favorable claims [[./thread#^c22105820|Zardoz84]].

## Assumptions

- For story 22104874, part 0, the substantive evidence concerns particles, smoke, allergens, and some VOC-related claims. qpiox [[./thread#^c22106087|qpiox]] requests PM2.5 testing, while Eric\_WVGG [[./thread#^c22106865|Eric\_WVGG]] discusses cooking and a school gas scare. No supplied non-deleted comment provides a NO2 or CO2 measurement, a pollutant-specific account of their effects, or a traffic-exposure measurement. The cooking and gas-scare references cannot fill those gaps.
- qpiox [[./thread#^c22106087|qpiox]] describes inexpensive laser particle sensors, including approximately \$17 Nova SDS devices, and proposes testing across a real room until concentrations fall below 8 micrograms/m³ within a reasonable time. That concentration is their proposed test criterion, not an observed result or a demonstrated official threshold. chewz [[./thread#^c22116265|chewz]] suggests an SDS011/Raspberry Pi monitor; neither comment supplies calibration or accuracy comparisons.
- The fan-pressure disagreement partly concerns different products: eeZah7Ux [[./thread#^c22106112|eeZah7Ux]] links an NF-F12 PWM specification of 2.61 mmH2O, whereas 0-\_-0 [[./thread#^c22106253|0-\_-0]] identifies the industrial variant behind the 7.63 mmH2O figure. These specifications, and labawi’s estimated working pressure [[./thread#^c22112595|labawi]], are not measurements of airflow through the assembled purifier.
- Particle-size statements require caution. lmilcin [[./thread#^c22106117|lmilcin]] describes filters as potentially missing the particles of interest, while mrspuratic [[./thread#^c22106006|mrspuratic]] asserts that HEPA 'starts at 0.3μm' and distinguishes H13 retention efficiency from particle size. qpiox [[./thread#^c22106009|qpiox]] says true HEPA captures PM2.5. The export supplies no efficiency-versus-size measurements establishing a hard lower cutoff or resolving the finer-soot claim.
- Sizing arguments depend on disputed building assumptions. qpiox [[./thread#^c22106039|qpiox]] asserts approximately two to three air exchanges per hour for eco-rated homes and five to six for older well-insulated ones. labawi [[./thread#^c22113102|labawi]] questions those figures, describing winter sealing practices and considering even one exchange per hour high for their closed room. These are not measured infiltration rates for a shared test building.
- Whole-room mixing is assumed rather than characterized. 867-5309 [[./thread#^c22105588|867-5309]] speculates that purification remains local unless a breeze can be felt everywhere; qpiox [[./thread#^c22106087|qpiox]] instead calls for a sensor on the opposite side of the room. Neither supplies spatial measurements demonstrating how the miniature purifier distributes cleaned air.
- Several proposed checks are useful observations but incomplete efficacy evidence: pureliquidhw [[./thread#^c22106766|pureliquidhw]] uses filter suction as a sign of flow, Spooks [[./thread#^c22106604|Spooks]] suggests feeling airflow with a wet hand, and Eric\_WVGG [[./thread#^c22110891|Eric\_WVGG]] treats automatic shutdown as evidence of cleaner air. None alone establishes room-wide PM2.5 reduction, sensor accuracy, gas removal, or a causal health benefit.

## Conditions

- Local availability changes what counts as a practical cheap purifier. packetslave [[./thread#^c22105461|packetslave]] reports that box-fan builds were popular when commercial purifiers sold out during California fires, and starpilot [[./thread#^c22105690|starpilot]] says they also worked in Seattle. dredmorbius [[./thread#^c22113171|dredmorbius]] says US-style box fans and matching filters were scarce in Australia, and describes Canberra workshops using floor fans and PM2.5-suitable automotive cabin filters instead [[./thread#^c22112983|dredmorbius]].
- brnt [[./thread#^c22117596|brnt]] reports spending nearly an hour unsuccessfully looking for a US-style box fan in Europe. thehesiod [[./thread#^c22105863|thehesiod]] reports roughly tenfold costs when shipping the suggested product to the US, whereas insensible [[./thread#^c22106362|insensible]] recommends locally sourced fans and furnace filters. mundu\_wa\_hinya [[./thread#^c22106261|mundu\_wa\_hinya]] also reports geographic blocking of the project’s parts retailer. US shopping assumptions therefore do not transfer automatically.
- toastal [[./thread#^c22107905|toastal]], writing from Bangkok during smog attributed to pollution and rice-field burning, raises unequal access to personally purchased clean air. This is a geographically situated exposure and affordability concern, not a quantified source-apportionment study or evidence that all residents experience the same exposure.
- Upfront cost comparisons vary substantially. JonathanFly [[./thread#^c22106018|JonathanFly]] reports using a \$20 box fan and furnace filter; TaylorAlexander [[./thread#^c22108989|TaylorAlexander]] cites a \$150 Honeywell and estimates much higher throughput than the miniature design. htfu [[./thread#^c22108644|htfu]] contrasts an approximately \$100 small build with \$200–300 commercial units and argues that replacement filters dominate long-run costs. These are commenter prices and estimates, not a standardized cost-effectiveness comparison.
- Filter abundance matters to multi-filter designs. sliken [[./thread#^c22113513|sliken]] argues that five filters increase airflow and last longer, making monthly costs similar despite greater startup expense. dredmorbius [[./thread#^c22113633|dredmorbius]] emphasizes scarcity during wildfires and diminishing returns, claiming a single-filter design already reduces particles about 90\% in 30 minutes. That reduction is not accompanied here by a firsthand test protocol, and its sufficiency depends on exposure goals.
- sl1ck731 [[./thread#^c22107784|sl1ck731]] assumes most US residents already receive adequate central-air filtration. 0xffff2 [[./thread#^c22113359|0xffff2]] challenges the availability premise for older California buildings, while cptskippy [[./thread#^c22107999|cptskippy]] reports that their HVAC rarely runs outside deep summer and winter. An installed HVAC system therefore cannot simply be equated with continuous filtration; no population prevalence data is supplied.
- Climate influences operating tradeoffs. djannzjkzxn [[./thread#^c22110116|djannzjkzxn]] accepts possible furnace risk partly because their mild climate requires little heating, and doubts that the risk-adjusted cost exceeds buying standalone units. For filtered outdoor ventilation, driverdan [[./thread#^c22107993|driverdan]] considers the cited Xiaomi expensive without an efficient heat exchanger. Neither provides measured energy costs or a quantified equipment-risk comparison.
- Quiet operation has a price and capacity constraint. qpiox [[./thread#^c22106001|qpiox]] compares local \$100–500 products under a claimed sub-40 dB constraint and says dirty filters increase resistance. clarry [[./thread#^c22106169|clarry]] is willing to pay more for quality, while aidenn0 [[./thread#^c22109946|aidenn0]] describes pricier Levoit models as quieter but still not truly quiet. These assessments are not a common acoustic test.
- Vacuum cleaners introduce a different energy-and-noise tradeoff. ivanhoe [[./thread#^c22105739|ivanhoe]] proposes a secondhand HEPA vacuum; adrianN [[./thread#^c22105988|adrianN]] objects to roughly kilowatt operation for a task potentially requiring only a few dozen watts. robryk [[./thread#^c22106395|robryk]] asks whether greater airflow and intermittent operation could offset that difference. No measured energy per volume of cleaned air resolves the comparison.

## Lived experiences

- djannzjkzxn [[./thread#^c22110116|djannzjkzxn]] reports the clearest numerical before-and-after household reading: during wildfire smoke, displayed AQI fell from 200 to 0 after installing a filter and running the house fan for an hour. The sensor, AQI calculation, filter specification, and control conditions are unspecified. A displayed zero is not a supplied PM2.5 concentration or proof that every pollutant disappeared.
- qpiox [[./thread#^c22106039|qpiox]] reports living in a frequently highly polluted city and maintaining indoor pollution five to six times below outdoor levels with a purifier continuously running at approximately 30\% power. Higher operation was too loud. This is a firsthand reported comparison, but the comment gives no absolute concentrations, sensor identity, pollutant breakdown, or measurement schedule.
- Eric\_WVGG [[./thread#^c22106865|Eric\_WVGG]] bought a purifier during asbestos-removal work with a bed-bound relative at home and observed activation within seconds of cooking and roughly three minutes of sweeping. They subsequently report activation during rooftop construction [[./thread#^c22107250|Eric\_WVGG]] and shutdown 15–30 minutes after cooking or one to two minutes after sweeping [[./thread#^c22110891|Eric\_WVGG]]. These are behavioral observations of the appliance, not independent pollutant measurements or verification of asbestos or combustion-gas removal.
- bradknowles [[./thread#^c22125488|bradknowles]] reports that household sensors show several IQAir units filtering effectively when run sufficiently high, although no numerical readings or sensor models are given. Four pets and seasonal 'Cedar Fever' make filtration valuable to their household. They explicitly distinguish this experience from activated-carbon performance, for which they have a favorable intuition but have not seen a clear demonstrated benefit.
- asah [[./thread#^c22111184|asah]] reports that a DIY filter stopped their coughing while working at home during San Francisco wildfire smoke and that its filters turned black; they explicitly call their lungs the 'measurement.' chrisparton1991 [[./thread#^c22105626|chrisparton1991]] describes visible, tasteable Sydney-bushfire smoke indoors: a secondhand AUD\$60 purifier struggled overall but improved the computer area, especially after curtains reduced the space being treated. Neither account supplies particle readings.
- jakeogh [[./thread#^c22109962|jakeogh]] reports that allergy season effectively disappeared while inside their room after years of operating a \$20 box fan continuously on low with non-HEPA filters rated 12 at the inlet and 7 at the outlet. A different output filter rated 3 also helped but felt more restrictive. Filters were changed every few months; no particle concentrations or standardized comparison explain those rating-and-flow observations.
- kevinconroy [[./thread#^c22106919|kevinconroy]] reports substantial improvement in bedroom cat fur and dander using two box fans with HEPA filters, requiring monthly replacements. This is perceived benefit and visible collection, without measured PM2.5 or allergy outcomes. Separately, clairity [[./thread#^c22112941|clairity]] describes checking supposedly air-purifying plants against a pet-safety list and doubts their air-quality benefit, contextualizing 24gttghh’s toxicity concern [[./thread#^c22107708|24gttghh]].
- magicalhippo [[./thread#^c22106166|magicalhippo]] reports coughing and waking stuffy after returning to a neglected childhood bedroom. After several days of running a HEPA purifier at maximum while away at work, those problems stopped. This supports a personal symptom-improvement account; it does not measure the contaminant, establish causality with a control, or demonstrate a change in sleep duration or sleep architecture.
- marcinzm [[./thread#^c22106325|marcinzm]] reports a major airflow loss when adding filters and ducting to a cheap box fan. UnFleshedOne [[./thread#^c22111381|UnFleshedOne]] likewise observed reduced flow but a visibly dirty furnace filter, while judging the setup badly undersized for intensive belt sanding. These accounts show that some collection can coexist with inadequate capacity; visible dust does not establish fine-particle protection.
- opencl [[./thread#^c22107069|opencl]] and nathancahill [[./thread#^c22107427|nathancahill]] report years of using filters directly against cheap box fans without failures, opposing the prediction of rapid fan death. Their accounts do not specify comparable filter resistance or motor temperatures. verdverm [[./thread#^c22114103|verdverm]] separately reports that their particular setup worked with filters on both sides but not with one, without quantitative performance data.
- showerst [[./thread#^c22107984|showerst]] reports that replacing a rental house’s furnace filter with a finer one, described as moving from rating 8 to 13 on an unspecified scale, produced noticeably lower airflow and more noise. They ask whether a coarser central filter plus room purifiers would help; the supplied replies do not include a measured trial of that alternative in the same house.
- cosmolev [[./thread#^c22107733|cosmolev]] reports owning a Smart Air Cannon and finding it useful only with windows completely closed, then installing filtered outdoor-air ventilation units in every bedroom. The comment generalizes the closed-window requirement to standalone purifiers, but supplies no paired open/closed readings or measurements showing how the replacement system affected particles, CO2, NO2, or thermal comfort.
- clarry [[./thread#^c22105973|clarry]] reports that two Electrolux EAP300s in small rooms do little for air quality on their lowest manual setting while remaining irritatingly audible, with uneven tone, speed changes, and vibration. Their later 26 dBA figure is an advertised rating, not a measured bedroom exposure [[./thread#^c22106169|clarry]]. This illustrates why a nominal quiet mode may be unacceptable or insufficient for a particular user.
- leetbulb [[./thread#^c22108086|leetbulb]] reports years of continuous use of two Winix HR1000s, finding them quiet outside turbo mode and enjoying turbo as sleep-time white noise. frank2 [[./thread#^c22110048|frank2]] runs a purifier partly to mask neighboring-apartment noise. These are comfort preferences, contrasting with clarry’s noise intolerance [[./thread#^c22105973|clarry]], rather than measured improvements in sleep or evidence about hearing effects.
- Raphmedia [[./thread#^c22107410|Raphmedia]] disputes jwr’s unfavorable Dyson assessment [[./thread#^c22105628|jwr]], reporting that their unit clears indoor smoking from a two-floor apartment. They describe HEPA and carbon filters and clarify that the fan is concealed in the base. No concentration measurements or matched comparison support either commenter’s brand-level judgment.
- bipson [[./thread#^c22107166|bipson]] owns the Philips product discussed as an air washer and says its particle filtration comes from an intake HEPA filter before humidification. They run it without water and report returning two units for quality problems. This is firsthand evidence about that product’s mechanism and reliability, not proof that water-only washing performs like HEPA.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The miniature project’s actual room-cleaning capacity remains unestablished in the supplied comments. labawi [[./thread#^c22112595|labawi]] says the article’s video and data demonstrate that it works, while lmilcin [[./thread#^c22105641|lmilcin]] and qpiox [[./thread#^c22106087|qpiox]] demand realistic room testing. The export contains neither that underlying dataset nor a shared test resolving room size, operating airflow, sensor placement, and clearance time.
- The optimal filter arrangement remains disputed, with different geometries sometimes discussed together. post\_break [[./thread#^c22106915|post\_break]] advocates parallel-area expansion; verdverm [[./thread#^c22114103|verdverm]] says front-and-back filters are necessary in their setup; dredmorbius [[./thread#^c22114382|dredmorbius]] points to working single-filter builds. These are not interchangeable configurations, and no matched measurements establish their relative benefit, cost, or pressure loss.
- Rapid fan damage from restrictive filters is asserted by post\_break [[./thread#^c22106915|post\_break]], qualified by their concern about high-MERV smoke filters [[./thread#^c22107226|post\_break]], and contradicted by opencl’s years without failures [[./thread#^c22107069|opencl]]. The anecdotes do not determine which fan/filter combinations are durable. Likewise, amluto’s throughput-based MERV 13 argument [[./thread#^c22120018|amluto]] and dredmorbius’s concern about particles passing through weaker filters [[./thread#^c22113241|dredmorbius]] lack a controlled comparison.
- The ventilation tradeoff is unresolved beyond particle-focused reasoning. spookthesunset’s combined filtered supply and recirculation proposal [[./thread#^c22113413|spookthesunset]] is not tested against dredmorbius’s preference for recirculation during outdoor pollution [[./thread#^c22113192|dredmorbius]]. cosmolev’s closed-window experience [[./thread#^c22107733|cosmolev]] does not establish when opening windows would help with indoor-generated pollution. No simultaneous indoor/outdoor PM2.5, NO2, or CO2 measurements support a general window-opening rule.
- The incremental health benefit for relatively healthy people in relatively clean homes remains contested: majos [[./thread#^c22107629|majos]] questions intervention evidence, while storyinmemo [[./thread#^c22109147|storyinmemo]] emphasizes risks associated with lower pollution levels. Eric\_WVGG’s school-score report [[./thread#^c22106865|Eric\_WVGG]] raises a cognition hypothesis but does not resolve it. Symptom improvements in smoke or allergy settings cannot automatically establish comparable benefits for other households.
- VOC-control performance remains uncertain. kn100’s aquarium-carbon proposal is explicitly tentative [[./thread#^c22105502|kn100]], and bradknowles [[./thread#^c22125488|bradknowles]] has not demonstrated a clear benefit from their charcoal-equipped units. trehalose [[./thread#^c22107494|trehalose]] establishes a distinction in the discussion between particle and VOC approaches, but the export supplies no gas-concentration tests, capacity measurements, or evidence that these configurations control NO2 or CO2.
- Ozone’s claimed cleaning benefit and exposure risk are not reconciled. Zardoz84 [[./thread#^c22106177|Zardoz84]] describes seeing hotels use ozone bursts and argues that small quantities are harmless, while qpiox [[./thread#^c22105977|qpiox]] says ozone is harmful. No concentration, exposure, or clearance measurements establish safe operation here. unlinked\_dll [[./thread#^c22106054|unlinked\_dll]] dislikes its smell and regularfry [[./thread#^c22106402|regularfry]] likes it; odor preference does not resolve the exposure question.
- Water-only filtration effectiveness and maintenance remain unanswered. littlejohnny [[./thread#^c22113441|littlejohnny]] asks about ownership and water-change frequency, and dredmorbius [[./thread#^c22113577|dredmorbius]] explicitly says they do not own the device they mentioned. pritovido’s microbial-growth warning [[./thread#^c22106410|pritovido]] and bipson’s experience with a HEPA-plus-humidification product [[./thread#^c22107166|bipson]] do not supply comparative particle removal or a validated maintenance schedule for water-only designs.
- Noise produces conflicting comfort accounts without a measured health comparison. leetbulb [[./thread#^c22108086|leetbulb]] and frank2 [[./thread#^c22110048|frank2]] value masking sound, clarry [[./thread#^c22105973|clarry]] finds their purifiers intrusive, and emrehan [[./thread#^c22115442|emrehan]] invokes hearing and psychological harms from noise generally. The unit provides no exposure-duration study or sleep testing establishing either benefit or harm from the specific operating levels discussed.
