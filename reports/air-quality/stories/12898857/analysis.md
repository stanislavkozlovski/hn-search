# [Smog Chokes Delhi, Leaving Residents ‘Cowering by Our Air Purifiers’](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=12898857)

## Description

Discussion examines Delhi’s severe smog, with participants emphasizing agricultural residue burning, the cost of alternatives, and barriers that keep farmers from adopting seeding or crop-management equipment. Others discuss traffic, motorcycles, solar transport, local responses, and comparisons with London’s historical and present-day pollution. A long branch debates whether environmental catastrophe justifies Mars colonization, the feasibility and ethics of off-world settlements, and whether Earth’s problems are more practical to address. Participants also discuss paywalls and access to the linked article.
Tags: Delhi, smog, agricultural-burning, air-pollution, farm-policy, transport, Mars-colonization, environment

## Arguments

- sandGorgon attributes the Delhi episode to agricultural residue burning in neighboring states, explicitly disputing industrial pollution as its cause. They propose affordable equipment for planting without burning the previous harvest’s residue. This is source attribution supported by links to outside reporting, not a firsthand measurement or an established source breakdown within the supplied comments [[./thread#^c12899258|sandGorgon]].
- Faaak argues that motorcycle use contributes to pollution, and ptaipale emphasizes that millions of motorcycles constitute a significant source [[./thread#^c12899151|Faaak]] [[./thread#^c12899373|ptaipale]]. eklavya proposes cleaner transport, subsequently clarifying that the intended proposal was electric vehicles after initially calling it solar transport. No commenter quantifies the resulting exposure reduction [[./thread#^c12899384|eklavya]] [[./thread#^c12899431|eklavya]].
- Fifer82 argues that air pollution remains a problem even when it is invisible, citing London’s alleged air-quality target violations and fines while contrasting present pollution with visible coal-era smog [[./thread#^c12899348|Fifer82]]. Reason077 describes unpleasant diesel fumes near major roads in London and Paris. These are regulatory claims and qualitative descriptions, without PM2.5 or NO2 readings [[./thread#^c12899391|Reason077]].
- f\_allwein invokes London’s Great Smog as a historical comparison suggesting that Delhi could eventually improve. This expresses optimism about achievable cleanup; it does not supply evidence about a particular intervention or timetable for Delhi [[./thread#^c12899067|f\_allwein]].

## Counterarguments

- Replying to Fifer82’s account of continuing London pollution, Angostura agrees that London misses targets but says conditions are nothing like the old smogs [[./thread#^c12899362|Angostura]]. mafribe likewise rejects equivalence with the 1950s and argues that standards have become substantially stricter [[./thread#^c12899567|mafribe]]. Their replies qualify historical severity comparisons rather than establish that contemporary London air is harmless.
- ericzawo accepts the motorcycle-pollution connection but challenges individual blame, emphasizing compassion and problems extending beyond motorcycles [[./thread#^c12899190|ericzawo]]. throwanem questions whether finger-wagging helps [[./thread#^c12899429|throwanem]]. Separately, Delhi resident mani04 refuses to blame farmers despite personally suffering, emphasizing food production and barriers to alternatives. These are arguments about responsibility and workable responses, not denials of pollution’s harm [[./thread#^c12899616|mani04]].
- Cthulhu\_ challenges eklavya’s initial solar-transport proposal on sunlight availability during smog and competitiveness with cheap scooters [[./thread#^c12899394|Cthulhu\_]]. eklavya responds that the episode is temporary, Delhi ordinarily receives substantial sunshine, and solar receives subsidies, then clarifies that electric vehicles were intended. That clarification changes the proposal; it does not resolve vehicle affordability or demonstrate an exposure benefit [[./thread#^c12899431|eklavya]].
- After rm2889 suggests Twitter pressure against an unresponsive official [[./thread#^c12899621|rm2889]], andrewvijay replies that Twitter has little influence in their rural Indian setting and that public confrontation could instead risk police action [[./thread#^c12899890|andrewvijay]]. This challenges the local usefulness of the suggested organizing tactic, not the need for pollution reduction.

## Assumptions

- mani04 assumes that farms absorbing emissions during roughly 350 days meaningfully offsets approximately 15 days of burning and emissions from their petrol car. The comment provides no pollutant-specific uptake, exposure, or health evidence establishing that compensation; its annual accounting should not be treated as evidence that the smoke episode is benign [[./thread#^c12899616|mani04]].
- sandGorgon’s equipment proposal assumes that an affordable alternative can practically replace residue burning [[./thread#^c12899258|sandGorgon]]. meric’s reply, “Feed it to goats,” similarly assumes the residue is suitable and manageable as feed. Neither supplies farmer trials, operating costs, logistical evidence, or measured reductions in exposure [[./thread#^c12899291|meric]].
- Inferring safe air from reduced fog would require an unsupported connection between visibility and harmful exposure: ajb says London no longer has its literary reputation’s fog [[./thread#^c12899413|ajb]], while Fifer82 explicitly argues that contemporary pollution can be invisible [[./thread#^c12899348|Fifer82]]. Likewise, Reason077’s descriptions of fumes and choking smoke cannot by themselves establish pollutant concentrations or comparable health risks [[./thread#^c12899391|Reason077]].

## Conditions

- The proposed agricultural alternative has a substantial reported upfront cost: sandGorgon gives approximately \$1,900 and calls it unaffordable to farmers [[./thread#^c12899258|sandGorgon]]; mani04 gives approximately \$2,000 for a “happy seeder” and alleges that subsidy access entails difficult paperwork, travel to a distant capital, and bribery. These are commenters’ accounts of financial and administrative barriers, not independently verified program details [[./thread#^c12899616|mani04]].
- Transport substitution depends on household purchasing power. Cthulhu\_ stresses competition with inexpensive scooters [[./thread#^c12899394|Cthulhu\_]], while mani04 says they cannot yet afford to replace their petrol car with an electric vehicle [[./thread#^c12899616|mani04]]. eklavya’s claim about solar subsidies does not establish that electric vehicles are affordable to those users [[./thread#^c12899431|eklavya]].
- The geographic comparisons concern different exposure settings: Reason077 describes roadside diesel fumes in London and Paris, then asserts much more extensive choking smoke across Indian cities and regions and comparatively cleaner conditions in China. No locations, dates, or common measurement scale substantiate that broad ranking [[./thread#^c12899391|Reason077]].
- Timing also matters in these November 2016 comments. sandGorgon emphasizes neighboring states’ agricultural burning [[./thread#^c12899258|sandGorgon]]; mani04 describes a roughly 15-day burning period [[./thread#^c12899616|mani04]]; eklavya calls the smog temporary and attributes it to Diwali fireworks [[./thread#^c12899431|eklavya]]. These accounts do not establish either typical annual exposure or the duration of every severe episode.

## Lived experiences

- mani04 explicitly identifies themselves as a Delhi resident suffering from the pollution. They report owning a petrol car and being unable to afford an electric replacement. This is a firsthand account of distress and constrained choices, but it contains no specific symptoms, diagnoses, exposure readings, or comparison before and after an intervention [[./thread#^c12899616|mani04]].

Their concern about police arrest is an anticipated risk, not a reported arrest [[./thread#^c12899890|andrewvijay]].
