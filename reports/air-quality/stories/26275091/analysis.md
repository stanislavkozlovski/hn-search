# [The Cuboid: A DIY air purifier that's better than a box fan](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=26275091)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

A technical discussion of a DIY cuboid air purifier and how it compares with box-fan filters and commercial units. Participants debate fan and filter configurations, static pressure, airflow, noise, power use, filter efficiency and availability, sealing, maintenance, and the fairness of the comparison. Related topics include air-quality sensors, wildfire smoke, VOCs, and using filtered airflow to keep a computer clean.
Tags: DIY-air-purifier, airflow, static-pressure, filters, Corsi-Rosenthal-box, air-quality-monitoring, wildfire-smoke

## Relevance

The discussion compares DIY purifier designs and filter performance, includes firsthand PM2.5 and CO2 measurements, and covers smoke, dust, allergens, cooking odors, filter availability, noise, and maintenance. Participants also question test methods and sensor accuracy. ## Arguments

- Standardized filters make DIY particulate filtration attractive on cost and maintainability. driverdan [[./thread#^c26278331|driverdan]] recommends thick furnace filters for price and airflow; sbierwagen [[./thread#^c26281742|sbierwagen]] argues that widely available furnace formats avoid expensive proprietary replacements and discontinued supplies. These are practical purchasing arguments, not proof that all filter grades perform equally.
- Useful airflow through the filter matters more than an unobstructed fan rating. lmilcin [[./thread#^c26279076|lmilcin]] recommends sealing gaps and selecting a fan suited to filter resistance; tgsovlerkhgsel [[./thread#^c26278600|tgsovlerkhgsel]] explains that performance depends on the intersection of fan and filter pressure-versus-flow curves. mnw21cam [[./thread#^c26280723|mnw21cam]] proposes greater filter area or pressure-capable centrifugal fans when resistance limits delivery.
- Combining computer cooling with room filtration can reuse equipment already running. hinkley [[./thread#^c26281121|hinkley]] points to quiet, continuously operating computer fans, while CarVac [[./thread#^c26279830|CarVac]] distinguishes their design's aim of cleaning substantial room air from a commercial case designed primarily to protect its components.
- Opening windows can reduce accumulated CO2, but its particulate consequences depend on outdoor conditions. everdrive [[./thread#^c26297765|everdrive]] reports substantially lower CO2 with windows cracked, while Netcob [[./thread#^c26279847|Netcob]] observes dust increases during some window-opening episodes but not others. Together these accounts support treating ventilation and particle filtration as separate, sometimes competing interventions.
- Monitoring can support selective purifier operation. Netcob [[./thread#^c26279847|Netcob]] describes switching a purifier through remote-controlled sockets using personally chosen dust thresholds. abeppu [[./thread#^c26279815|abeppu]] questions continuous operation and proposes concentrating filtration around cooking, cleaning, or outdoor-pollen entry; the thread does not establish an optimal schedule.
- For odors and gaseous contaminants, commenters propose approaches beyond ordinary particle filters. TylerE [[./thread#^c26281303|TylerE]] suggests activated carbon for paint VOCs; jjeaff [[./thread#^c26280601|jjeaff]] recommends it for cooking odors. lovemenot [[./thread#^c26281279|lovemenot]] suggests trying sous-vide cooking for fish, but supplies no resulting odor measurements or experience.
- Absence of current symptoms is not necessarily a reason to dismiss exposure. Netcob [[./thread#^c26280296|Netcob]] advocates considering local outdoor air quality and possible long-term lung benefits; bogdanu [[./thread#^c26280161|bogdanu]] recounts developing ragweed allergies after previously having none. Neither establishes the preventive benefit of a purifier for an otherwise asymptomatic person.

## Counterarguments

- MERV ratings should not be treated as interchangeable with HEPA. CarVac [[./thread#^c26286499|CarVac]] corrects lambda\_obrien's initial equivalence claim, and lambda\_obrien [[./thread#^c26289794|lambda\_obrien]] explicitly accepts that correction. CarVac [[./thread#^c26285069|CarVac]] also clarifies that their computer-integrated filter is not HEPA.
- The proposed shop-towel shortcut receives substantial objections. jackfoxy [[./thread#^c26278888|jackfoxy]] tentatively connects a shop's disappearing towel-filtration claim with N95 and HEPA performance; starpilot [[./thread#^c26279678|starpilot]] challenges the measurement basis, jacob019 [[./thread#^c26278986|jacob019]] disputes equivalence to specialized mask material, and colechristensen [[./thread#^c26279832|colechristensen]] rejects equating N95 with HEPA. No supplied test validates the towel proposal.
- Adding fans is not a cost-free answer to filter resistance. Responding to that suggestion, dynm [[./thread#^c26276919|dynm]] identifies electricity and noise costs; waynesonfire [[./thread#^c26281999|waynesonfire]] notes that unfiltered computer cooling could require fewer fans. lmilcin [[./thread#^c26281112|lmilcin]] also argues that smaller, faster-running fans may produce more objectionable noise.
- The claim that box-fan purifiers inherently push air through filters and redistribute captured dust is challenged by actual configurations. gregschlom [[./thread#^c26277646|gregschlom]] raises that concern, but Tostino [[./thread#^c26278571|Tostino]] describes an intake-side filter and chrismeller [[./thread#^c26278275|chrismeller]] says most designs they have seen pull through it. rachelbythebay [[./thread#^c26278675|rachelbythebay]] adds that one commercial design has filters on both sides of its fan.
- DIY does not automatically win at every room size or performance target. airhead969 [[./thread#^c26299529|airhead969]] argues that a larger commercial Blueair unit could outperform a collection of DIY devices on energy, noise, maintenance, space, and total cost. Their assumed requirement for ten DIY units is speculative, rather than a supplied head-to-head comparison.
- Symptomatic improvement is not assured. epmaybe [[./thread#^c26280475|epmaybe]] reports no apparent relief of itchy eyes and more benefit from an allergy pill, whereas zoom6628 [[./thread#^c26282161|zoom6628]] associates a purifier with their daughter no longer needing nighttime doses of a medication. These differing anecdotes do not establish either universal benefit or universal ineffectiveness.
- CO2 readings receive an explicit reliability challenge: RL\_Quine [[./thread#^c26280832|RL\_Quine]] claims consumer sensors rely on assumed baselines rather than absolute measurement. The comment supplies no model-specific tests, calibration records, or error estimates, so its sweeping criticism cannot establish the accuracy of the other commenters' particular meters.

## Assumptions

- Coverage is limited to story 26275091, part 0. Netcob [[./thread#^c26279847|Netcob]] discusses dust and CO2 monitoring; cooking appears as a proposed particulate-generating event in abeppu [[./thread#^c26279815|abeppu]] and as fish-odor discomfort in manishsharan [[./thread#^c26280564|manishsharan]]. This unit supplies no NO2 readings, traffic-exposure accounts, or measurements of cooking combustion gases.
- Reported low particle readings should not be interpreted as demonstrated absence of exposure. bryceacc [[./thread#^c26282784|bryceacc]] reports approximately zero displayed particles, while airhead969 [[./thread#^c26299529|airhead969]] calls their outlet reading undetectably low and labels particle measurements in 'ppm.' Calibration, detection limits, and comparable reporting units are not supplied; these accounts cannot be silently converted into verified PM2.5 mass concentrations.
- The proposed cognitive and sleep implications exceed the observations. Netcob [[./thread#^c26280981|Netcob]] hypothesizes that elevated CO2 could contribute to an afternoon office slump, but reports no cognitive testing. Their lower overnight CO2 accumulation [[./thread#^c26281074|Netcob]] concerns concentration patterns, not measured sleep quality. zoom6628's account of reduced nighttime medication use [[./thread#^c26282161|zoom6628]] likewise supplies no sleep assessment.
- Claims based on outside testing remain secondhand within this export. Netcob [[./thread#^c26281116|Netcob]] cites favorable commercial-purifier tests, and zoom6628 [[./thread#^c26282161|zoom6628]] invokes a previously shared article. bluGill [[./thread#^c26279672|bluGill]] speculates about a roughly 5\% reduction in COVID risk. The supplied comments contain no results establishing those claimed performance or health benefits.
- The case for intermittent filtration assumes limited continuing particle entry or production. abeppu [[./thread#^c26279815|abeppu]] explicitly assumes a reasonably sealed building without constant burning or smoking. Netcob's reports of building leakage [[./thread#^c26281074|Netcob]] and occasional dust entry through windows [[./thread#^c26279847|Netcob]] show why that premise must be checked locally rather than generalized.
- Health attribution remains uncertain even where the experience is firsthand. cmckn [[./thread#^c26280184|cmckn]] reports symptoms during severe wildfire pollution but gives no post-purifier symptom comparison; epmaybe [[./thread#^c26280475|epmaybe]] suspects a dog-related allergen without confirming the source; zoom6628 [[./thread#^c26282161|zoom6628]] reports a favorable change without a controlled comparison. None isolates filtration as the cause of the health outcome.

## Conditions

- Window-opening advice is seasonal and geographically conditional. In the Pacific Northwest, cmckn [[./thread#^c26280184|cmckn]] contrasts wildfire-period indoor AQI around 200 with later outdoor AQI around 10, and considers brief ventilation sufficient in the latter setting. Netcob [[./thread#^c26280296|Netcob]] cautions that prolonged open windows can undermine purifier effectiveness. The discussion does not establish a universal five-minute ventilation rule.
- Wildfire preparation is constrained by availability during demand spikes. In Portland, jdeibele [[./thread#^c26283035|jdeibele]] recalls empty filter shelves across several retailers during the 2020 fires and subsequently buys supplies ahead of time. carabiner [[./thread#^c26279212|carabiner]] similarly urges advance purchasing. danudey [[./thread#^c26280399|danudey]] describes recurring wildfire seasons in British Columbia as the reason poor air became conspicuous locally.
- Prices are commenters' contemporaneous 2021 reports, not current quotations. jdeibele [[./thread#^c26283035|jdeibele]] buys three approximately \$18 fans and six filters for \$42, totaling under \$100; CarVac [[./thread#^c26286481|CarVac]] sources 16-by-20-inch MERV 13 filters for under \$8 each. Commercial examples include bryceacc's approximately \$110 Winix [[./thread#^c26282784|bryceacc]] and cmckn's approximately \$150 Coway [[./thread#^c26280184|cmckn]], with differing capacities and features.
- Specialized hardware can change the economics substantially. Forbo [[./thread#^c26279988|Forbo]] quotes over \$500 for the suggested Silverstone computer case and over \$160 for replacement filters. CarVac [[./thread#^c26279830|CarVac]] says that case serves a different purpose from their room-cleaning design, while sbierwagen [[./thread#^c26281742|sbierwagen]] identifies proprietary filter cost and continuity as reasons to prefer standardized parts.
- Local supply chains determine which DIY designs are practical. oliwarner [[./thread#^c26284677|oliwarner]] says furnace filters are much less readily available in the UK than in the US and finds automotive or industrial filtration easier to source. CarVac [[./thread#^c26275536|CarVac]] questions whether HVAC filter dimensions are sufficiently standardized internationally for a single computer-case design.
- Replacement authenticity and longevity remain purchasing concerns. jdeibele [[./thread#^c26283035|jdeibele]] worries about possible counterfeit marketplace filters and hopes for one to two weeks of use per filter, without reporting that lifespan as tested. sgerenser [[./thread#^c26293103|sgerenser]] describes good experience ordering directly from Nordic Pure to avoid that concern; neither establishes that the marketplace purchase was counterfeit.
- Room volume, leakage, occupancy, and activity constrain comparisons. Netcob [[./thread#^c26281074|Netcob]] describes an old, leaky apartment and changes associated with sleep, exercise, and open internal doors. Their suggestion that tighter construction trades ventilation for heating efficiency [[./thread#^c26280981|Netcob]] is an explanatory hypothesis, not a measured energy comparison. Results from zoom6628's 700-square-foot flat [[./thread#^c26282161|zoom6628]] and airhead969's 500-cubic-meter space [[./thread#^c26299529|airhead969]] cannot be directly equated.
- Comfort and household practicality affect usable filtration. Netcob [[./thread#^c26279847|Netcob]] disables both purifier and dust sensor in silent mode; bryceacc [[./thread#^c26282784|bryceacc]] finds maximum purifier speed extremely loud. netrap [[./thread#^c26276729|netrap]] requires an attractive appliance, dynm [[./thread#^c26277202|dynm]] anticipates substantial extra work for a furniture-like enclosure, and Tempest1981 [[./thread#^c26287959|Tempest1981]] raises filter rigidity and exposed-blade protection for children and pets.

## Lived experiences

- Netcob [[./thread#^c26279847|Netcob]] connects an SDS011 dust sensor to a Raspberry Pi and uses it to control a BlueAir Pure 221. They report dust increases when opening windows in the morning and the previous night, but none during an afternoon opening. A separate CO2 sensor tracks ventilation episodes. Their dust sensor is audible, whereas fpgaminer [[./thread#^c26280519|fpgaminer]] reports being unable to hear the fan in a different HPMA115S0-XXX sensor.
- In the same monitoring account, Netcob [[./thread#^c26280097|Netcob]] attributes CO2 cycling to opening windows for a few minutes while otherwise keeping them shut. They report rarely approaching 2000 ppm, gradual movement toward the outdoor baseline during extended absence, and less overnight accumulation [[./thread#^c26281074|Netcob]]. Their explanations invoke leakage, activity, and the available air volume; no independent calibration is supplied.
- everdrive [[./thread#^c26297765|everdrive]] reports household CO2 easily reaching 2000 ppm without cracked windows and usually remaining at 500–900 ppm with them cracked. sbierwagen [[./thread#^c26282060|sbierwagen]] independently reports that exceeding 1500 ppm is easy and describes stuffiness occurring well before the much higher concentration they consider concerning.
- During California fires, bryceacc [[./thread#^c26282784|bryceacc]] uses a PMS7003 sensor with a Raspberry Pi and five-minute averaged displays to observe room clearing. They report a Winix 545 reaching approximately zero displayed particles in fifteen minutes at maximum speed. They find the sensor barely audible but the purifier's maximum setting dramatically louder than its lower settings; room size and starting concentration are unspecified.
- airhead969 [[./thread#^c26299529|airhead969]] reports using Plantower PMS5003 sensors with a Blueair 680i in a 500-cubic-meter space, reaching a stated '1 ppm' in four to five hours and an outlet reading of '0 ppm,' described as undetectably low. They also claim 400 CFM of clean air. The comment's particle units and measurement procedure are insufficiently specified for direct comparison with other accounts.
- CarVac [[./thread#^c26275262|CarVac]] reports a computer-integrated MERV 13 filter moving over 100 CFM using Noctua fans at a tolerable noise level, while keeping the computer cool and exceptionally clean. This is firsthand operation and component-cleanliness evidence; the comment supplies no room PM2.5 readings or health outcomes.
- cmckn [[./thread#^c26280184|cmckn]] reports indoor AQI around 200 during Pacific Northwest wildfire smoke, esophageal irritation, and feeling generally awful despite having neither asthma nor allergies. They purchase a HEPA-and-ionizer Coway and subsequently run it continuously on automatic mode in the bedroom. They do not report a measured post-installation AQI or quantified symptom improvement.
- lambda\_obrien [[./thread#^c26289794|lambda\_obrien]] says their home's MERV 14 filtration eliminates forest-fire smoke but not allergens, prompting an intention to try HEPA. Separately, epmaybe [[./thread#^c26280475|epmaybe]] buys a bedroom purifier because of their wife's allergies, possibly related to a dog, but notices no benefit for their own itchy eyes. Neither account includes particle measurements or confirmation of the allergen source.
- zoom6628 [[./thread#^c26282161|zoom6628]] reports that one Sqair purifier keeps a 700-square-foot village flat clean and that this is the first year their daughter has not needed nighttime doses of 'siflex.' The perceived cleanliness and medication change are firsthand claims, without pollutant readings, clinical details, or evidence establishing causation.
- totetsu [[./thread#^c26281128|totetsu]] describes a rental made essentially unusable for a month by fumes from freshly painted floors and says a plant did not solve the problem. manishsharan [[./thread#^c26280564|manishsharan]] describes pervasive, objectionable fish-cooking odor. Responding to the latter, jjeaff [[./thread#^c26280601|jjeaff]] reports installing charcoal behind an HVAC intake filter, but gives no measured odor-removal result.
- nullserver [[./thread#^c26280772|nullserver]] recounts an office with constant indoor smoking, computers repeatedly failing, and extensive yellow deposits despite being told the machines were less than six months old; they subsequently resigned. dmix [[./thread#^c26281950|dmix]], drawing on personal and parental smoking experience, describes such working conditions as intolerable without ventilation. These accounts establish discomfort and visible contamination, not measured PM exposure or demonstrated protection from ventilation.
- Tostino [[./thread#^c26278571|Tostino]] describes taping a four-inch MERV 13 filter to the intake side of a 20-by-20-inch box fan and reports satisfactory operation without dust being pushed off the filter. Meleagris [[./thread#^c26276985|Meleagris]] also reports using a box-fan design and considers switching to the duct-fan approach for anticipated electricity savings. Neither supplies comparative energy or particle-removal measurements.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The thread does not settle what indoor CO2 level should trigger intervention. jxy [[./thread#^c26280769|jxy]], who says they have not measured their own home, considers roughly 1400 ppm alarming and suggests action above 1000. Netcob [[./thread#^c26280981|Netcob]] calls 1000–2000 ppm common but unhealthy, while sbierwagen [[./thread#^c26282060|sbierwagen]] places concern above 5000 ppm and attributes earlier stuffiness to other exhaled compounds. These comments may address different endpoints, but provide no evidence establishing cognition, comfort, or health thresholds.
- Filter-grade expectations conflict with reported allergy experience. CarVac [[./thread#^c26290553|CarVac]] says pollen and animal allergens should be captured by MERV 14 and smoke is harder to filter, contrasting with lambda\_obrien's reported smoke success and persistent allergens [[./thread#^c26289794|lambda\_obrien]]. More broadly, Der\_Einzige [[./thread#^c26280272|Der\_Einzige]] argues high-MERV furnace filters are sufficient for most purposes, while ska [[./thread#^c26279002|ska]] stresses application-dependent differences. No exposure or installation testing resolves those practical disagreements.
- The claimed purifier comparison lacks controls requested by commenters. chaimanmeow [[./thread#^c26295531|chaimanmeow]] wants a no-fan PM2.5 decay baseline to distinguish filtration from settling and diffusion. neolog [[./thread#^c26279317|neolog]] questions reusing filters across trials; bluGill [[./thread#^c26279708|bluGill]] replies that loading may improve capture while reducing airflow. The supplied discussion does not quantify these competing effects.
- The box-fan benchmark and importance of sealing remain contested. chrismeller [[./thread#^c26278292|chrismeller]] questions using three small filters instead of one matching the fan; dicerson [[./thread#^c26278513|dicerson]] and klyrs [[./thread#^c26278458|klyrs]] question uncovered areas and comparability. dynm [[./thread#^c26278821|dynm]] counters with a link to earlier taping experiments, but their results are not reproduced here, so the export does not resolve the challenge.
- Restricted airflow's effects on power, wear, and safety are disputed. sgc [[./thread#^c26278704|sgc]] argues restriction reduces work and electricity use; CyberDildonics [[./thread#^c26288747|CyberDildonics]] argues the same power continues while rotation slows. mnw21cam [[./thread#^c26280740|mnw21cam]] raises inadequate cooling as a separate overheating mechanism. giantg2 [[./thread#^c26279532|giantg2]] reports a hot motor, and klyrs [[./thread#^c26280441|klyrs]] reports two burned-out fans, but neither establishes the cause or a general failure rate.
- No supplied comparison settles the best fan arrangement for a given noise, price, and filtration target. giantg2 [[./thread#^c26278739|giantg2]] favors a suitable motor and squirrel-cage fan; mnw21cam [[./thread#^c26280723|mnw21cam]] proposes higher static pressure or fans in series; lmilcin [[./thread#^c26281112|lmilcin]] cautions about smaller fans' noise. tgsovlerkhgsel [[./thread#^c26278600|tgsovlerkhgsel]] identifies missing performance curves, and CarVac [[./thread#^c26285057|CarVac]] reports difficulty comparison-shopping centrifugal fans.
- Activated carbon remains a proposed solution rather than demonstrated success for these particular VOC and odor problems. TylerE [[./thread#^c26281303|TylerE]] suggests it for paint fumes, and jjeaff [[./thread#^c26280601|jjeaff]] recommends it for fish odors while dismissing baking soda based on unspecified testing. Carbon quantity, airflow, service life, target compounds, and before-and-after measurements are absent, leaving effectiveness and replacement needs unresolved.
