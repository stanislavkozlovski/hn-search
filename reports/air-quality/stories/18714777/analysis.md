# [Real-time air pollution map](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=18714777)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants compare real-time air-pollution maps and the sensor networks behind them, discussing coverage, local resolution, visualization, and how crowdsourced readings complement government monitoring. Many call for independent projects and companies to aggregate data, while others point to quality differences that complicate combining low-cost sensors. The thread covers DIY and commercial sensor options, open firmware, personal data collection, calibration and AQI conversions, and examples of maps used during wildfire smoke. Commenters also note block-by-block variation, possible traffic and industrial sources, gaps in coverage, and interest in measuring pollutants beyond particulates.
Tags: air-pollution maps, crowdsourced data, PurpleAir, open data, air-quality sensors, AQI, wildfire smoke

## Relevance

The thread discusses local PM2.5 monitoring, sensor-network coverage and data quality, wildfire smoke, and how traffic can cause sharply local exposure differences. ## Arguments

- Hyperlocal monitoring may support decisions about outdoor exposure. clumsysmurf [[./thread#^c18739208|clumsysmurf]] relays findings from Google/Aclima monitoring that pollution varied substantially between blocks; hedora [[./thread#^c18741217|hedora]] argues that dense PurpleAir coverage helps people locate clearer air during wildfires. Neither supplies a measured reduction in personal exposure from following a map.
- Combining networks could reveal monitoring that a single commercial map misses. TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c18739984|TeMPOraL]] reports that Poland has Luftdaten and local-company sensors absent from PurpleAir's map. abido [[./thread#^c18756385|abido]], who operates a citizen monitoring initiative in Pakistan, independently argues for aggregation across platforms.
- More responsive reporting can help immediate exposure decisions. jonathankoren [[./thread#^c18741554|jonathankoren]] describes PurpleAir's PM2.5 NowCast as a weighted calculation using the preceding 12 hours, contrasting it with a conventional 24-hour average. This is an explanation of reporting methodology, not evidence that the underlying sensor is more accurate.
- DIY monitoring offers access to local measurements and control over the data. TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c18741875|TeMPOraL]] describes a solder-free Luftdaten build using an ESP8266 and particulate sensor, with open-source firmware that can send readings both to the project and to a personal database. No build price or accuracy comparison is supplied.
- Visualizing pollution movement may add context beyond individual station readings. simongray [[./thread#^c18741213|simongray]] prefers AirVisual's presentation of moving pollution clouds. amelius [[./thread#^c18740723|amelius]] points to satellite monitoring, but provides no evidence about its resolution or suitability for estimating someone's immediate exposure.

## Counterarguments

- A particulate map cannot establish overall air quality. hedora [[./thread#^c18741217|hedora]] notes that PurpleAir does not measure ozone; jonathankoren [[./thread#^c18741554|jonathankoren]] distinguishes its PM2.5/PM10 measurements from broader AQIs that can include NO2, CO, ozone, and SO2, and expresses uncertainty about which particulate measure the displayed index uses.
- Aggregation has quality and funding constraints as well as organizational barriers. Responding to the broader fragmentation problem, abido [[./thread#^c18756385|abido]] attributes separate networks partly to low-cost sensor data quality and says OpenAQ lacks resources to incorporate those measurements. This qualifies TeMPOraL's [[./thread#^c18739984|TeMPOraL]] argument that organizations should simply cooperate on one map.
- Block-to-block readings need not identify persistent neighborhood differences. In reply to clumsysmurf's [[./thread#^c18739208|clumsysmurf]] account of mobile monitoring, londons\_explore [[./thread#^c18739920|londons\_explore]] asks whether the monitoring car might have been following a truck. That is a plausible confound raised in the discussion, not an established explanation of the reported findings.
- Higher displayed AQI does not by itself establish which service is more accurate. minimaxir [[./thread#^c18739019|minimaxir]] reports higher default PurpleAir values than AIRNow and recommends a conversion setting. jonathankoren [[./thread#^c18741554|jonathankoren]] replies with averaging-period and pollutant distinctions, but the exchange supplies no paired measurements establishing the discrepancy's cause or the appropriate correction.
- Real-time information is useful only when accessible. minimaxir [[./thread#^c18739019|minimaxir]] reports that AIRNow crashed during California wildfire demand. craigzucchini [[./thread#^c18739874|craigzucchini]], ndnxhs [[./thread#^c18739913|ndnxhs]], and chris\_st [[./thread#^c18740694|chris\_st]] report PurpleAir display failures or sluggishness across browsers; cboy5675 [[./thread#^c18739796|cboy5675]] identifies missing right-to-left language support. These are access limitations, not demonstrations of inaccurate sensors.
- The claim that Nullschool shows only wind is directly contested. Jipazgqmnm [[./thread#^c18740057|Jipazgqmnm]] recommends its particulate display; edge17 [[./thread#^c18741294|edge17]] dismisses it as wind data; dredmorbius [[./thread#^c18751445|dredmorbius]] replies with descriptions and links for pollutant layers, including historical Camp Fire PM2.5. The supplied reply addresses feature availability, without validating the displayed concentrations.

## Assumptions

- Comparing map numbers requires knowing their pollutant, units, averaging period, and index standard. etxm [[./thread#^c18741451|etxm]] reports values falling from 133 to 53, and afterburner [[./thread#^c18739789|afterburner]] reports 197, without specifying those details. jonathankoren's [[./thread#^c18741648|jonathankoren]] implementation of six AQI standards further illustrates why an unexplained number should not automatically be treated as a comparable concentration.
- The humidifier explanation is an inference from a repeated sensor response. TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c18741875|TeMPOraL]] interprets PM10 spikes as dissolved substances being atomized and connects this to the manual's distilled-water instruction. The comment provides no water analysis, distilled-water comparison, independent instrument check, or measured health outcome; its PM10 result must not be relabeled PM2.5.
- Co-location creates an opportunity to assess sensor performance but does not itself demonstrate agreement. ronzensci [[./thread#^c18739829|ronzensci]] says many sensors in an Indian PM2.5 network are colocated with FEM/FRM-grade BAM monitors, but supplies no comparison results, error estimates, or calibration procedure.
- Subjective symptoms and perceived freshness do not identify a pollutant or establish a clinical effect. ndnxhs [[./thread#^c18739982|ndnxhs]] links breathing difficulty to peak traffic, while etxm [[./thread#^c18741451|etxm]] associates a move with fresher-feeling air. Neither reports concurrent personal measurements or controls for other differences.

## Conditions

- Affordability can constrain both personal monitoring and map coverage. hedora [[./thread#^c18741217|hedora]] describes a PurpleAir device costing a few hundred dollars in this 2018 discussion. black-tea [[./thread#^c18739723|black-tea]] finds the cost prohibitive for monitoring a busy port-access road and suspects coverage favors affluent residents. That sampling-bias concern is not quantified.
- Network coverage depends on geography. vackosar [[./thread#^c18739612|vackosar]] finds AQICN better covered in the Czech Republic, and angled [[./thread#^c18739971|angled]] endorses its coverage without specifying a location. hedora [[./thread#^c18741217|hedora]] instead reports hundreds of PurpleAir sensors versus roughly four government monitors in Silicon Valley, with one government monitor closing. TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c18739984|TeMPOraL]] describes missing Polish networks. These are historical local claims, not a universal ranking.
- The US purchasing question concerns Luftdaten specifically. toomuchtodo [[./thread#^c18741806|toomuchtodo]] clarifies this; theptrk [[./thread#^c18741844|theptrk]] links to a different product, PurpleAir. TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c18741875|TeMPOraL]] explains how to build a Luftdaten unit but only speculates that assembled units are sold, leaving their price and availability unresolved.
- Government monitoring gaps change the value of citizen measurements. abido [[./thread#^c18756385|abido]] says their country lacks government air-quality monitoring and describes working with residents to deploy IQAir AirVisual monitors in major Pakistani cities. This is firsthand participation in a monitoring initiative, alongside broader claims about worldwide coverage and data quality.
- Spatial gaps limit interpretations of reassuring map colors. black-tea [[./thread#^c18739723|black-tea]] is surprised by green readings across the UK, including London; kingosticks [[./thread#^c18739777|kingosticks]] notes absent sensors near major London airports. Neither establishes that those unmonitored locations are clean or polluted.

## Lived experiences

- TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c18739984|TeMPOraL]] had built a Luftdaten sensor but had not yet connected it to the public map. During indoor testing, TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c18741875|TeMPOraL]] reports PM10 reaching 600 μg/m³ whenever an ultrasonic humidifier was switched on. This is the part's clearest quantitative firsthand indoor sensor observation, although the inferred composition and health implications were not independently checked.
- While cycling in London, londons\_explore [[./thread#^c18739920|londons\_explore]] notices differences depending on which side of a bus its exhaust exits. Replying, ndnxhs [[./thread#^c18739982|ndnxhs]] reports braking and holding their breath behind buses, finding upward-pointing truck exhaust less troublesome, and experiencing breathing difficulties at peak traffic times. These are personal exposure and symptom accounts, without PM2.5 or NO2 measurements or evidence that the coping tactics are effective.
- ryan\_j\_naughton [[./thread#^c18739098|ryan\_j\_naughton]] installed a sensor outside a balcony in West Los Angeles/Santa Monica as part of a RAND study and valued the hyperlocal data. No readings or study results are given. swebs [[./thread#^c18739207|swebs]] asks about the resident's health, but the supplied thread contains no answer.
- heyjudy [[./thread#^c18739173|heyjudy]] used PurpleAir during the Camp Fire and describes small-particulate readings in east Chico as off the charts. This is firsthand use of a public map during smoke exposure conditions, not a reported personal sampler result, numerical concentration, or health outcome.
- After moving to Pasadena, etxm [[./thread#^c18741451|etxm]] felt that the air was fresher and interpreted map values of 133 versus 53 as confirmation. The comment does not specify the former neighborhood, measurement timing, index settings, or whether the difference persisted.
- clumsysmurf [[./thread#^c18739529|clumsysmurf]], replying within a discussion of nighttime pollution, reports seeing parking-lot vacuum trucks operating around midnight and apparently throwing debris into the air. The observation is firsthand; whether the equipment was malfunctioning and how much particulate exposure it caused remain unknown.
- jonathankoren [[./thread#^c18741648|jonathankoren]] reports monitoring a weather station and PurpleAir sensor through WeeWx and developing an extension supporting six AQI standards. This demonstrates a personal monitoring setup, but includes no concentration history or sensor-validation results.
- fluxty [[./thread#^c18740391|fluxty]] says they stay indoors for running when the weather is bad and would like to identify highly local traffic-pollution patterns. The comment expresses an existing behavior and a desired use for monitoring, not evidence that indoor exercise reduced pollution exposure.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The discussion favors broader access to measurements without settling how to combine unequal-quality sources. TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c18739984|TeMPOraL]] emphasizes missing data across platforms; abido [[./thread#^c18756385|abido]] emphasizes sensor quality and funding. No shared validation, aggregation, or financing approach is established.
- Nighttime Bay Area pollution receives competing, untested explanations. pwarner [[./thread#^c18739116|pwarner]] attributes it to fireplaces in California valleys. ndnxhs [[./thread#^c18739430|ndnxhs]] offers hearsay that industrial facilities emit more at night, while clumsysmurf [[./thread#^c18739529|clumsysmurf]] describes visible dust from nighttime cleaning trucks. The thread does not determine their relative contributions.
- Several geographic anomalies remain questions rather than identified sources: afterburner [[./thread#^c18739789|afterburner]] wonders whether a New Haven reading of 197 is near a factory; kingosticks [[./thread#^c18739777|kingosticks]] asks why northern Italy is red; rsync [[./thread#^c18741621|rsync]] asks about an apparent hotspot northeast of Mexico City and AirVisual's underlying sensor network. No supplied replies resolve these points.
- Affordable gas monitoring remains unanswered. jonathankoren [[./thread#^c18741697|jonathankoren]] wants an inexpensive ozone or other nonparticulate sensor but has not found one. NO2 appears only in jonathankoren's [[./thread#^c18741554|jonathankoren]] list of AQI pollutants, while CO2 appears in dredmorbius's [[./thread#^c18751445|dredmorbius]] description of map layers. This part supplies no firsthand NO2 measurements or indoor/personal CO2 readings.
- Health evidence remains limited to anecdotes and unanswered questions. ndnxhs [[./thread#^c18739982|ndnxhs]] reports traffic-associated breathing difficulty; swebs [[./thread#^c18739207|swebs]] asks about health near a sensor without receiving an answer. The RAND deployment described by ryan\_j\_naughton [[./thread#^c18739098|ryan\_j\_naughton]] includes no health findings. There are no supplied results about cognition, sleep, or long-term clinical effects.
- The part cannot establish when opening windows helps or worsens exposure, or compare ventilation with filtration. TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c18741875|TeMPOraL]] documents an apparent indoor particulate source, and heyjudy [[./thread#^c18739173|heyjudy]] describes severe outdoor wildfire readings, but neither compares simultaneous indoor/outdoor conditions or tests an intervention. No supplied account evaluates cooking emissions, air cleaners, window-opening outcomes, or associated comfort tradeoffs.
