# [Air pollution in London passes levels in Beijing (2017)](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=16324018)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion compares a London air-pollution reading with Beijing’s and challenges the headline’s framing, including the dates, measurement context, and a misleading image. Much of the thread weighs wood-burning stoves’ particulate pollution against heating costs, regulation, enforcement, and claims about carbon neutrality. Participants also discuss Beijing’s coal restrictions and their effects on heating, diesel emissions, idling rules, and the limits of personal experience as evidence about air quality.
Tags: air-pollution, London, Beijing, wood-burning, particulates, heating-costs, diesel, policy, measurement

## Relevance

The discussion covers outdoor particulate pollution and its sources, especially wood smoke and diesel exhaust, while disputing how London and Beijing air-quality readings compare. Comments also discuss smoke controls and pollution from household stoves; most claims are not personal exposure measurements. ## Arguments

- The main exposure concern is combustion pollution near people. Spooky23 [[./thread#^c16324324|Spooky23]] identifies particulates as the urban wood-burning issue; nwah1 [[./thread#^c16324984|nwah1]] warns about particulates, carcinogens, and heavy metals from combustion. Retric [[./thread#^c16324157|Retric]] argues that rising smoke can cool and become a nearby health hazard. These are commenters’ claims, without measured concentrations or health-study results in the supplied text.
- Several commenters favor reducing emissions at their source, but propose different interventions: Nux [[./thread#^c16324183|Nux]] wants a London wood-stove ban; laurent123456 [[./thread#^c16324294|laurent123456]] proposes taxing the pollution associated with heating savings; Retric [[./thread#^c16324157|Retric]] proposes banning stoves while helping households pay for alternatives. Retric’s claim that health savings would outweigh heating costs is asserted without a calculation.
- Centralized combustion and filtration are offered as alternatives to neighborhood smoke. Responding to chatmasta’s comparison of household wood burning with off-site energy production [[./thread#^c16324140|chatmasta]], dvfjsdhgfv [[./thread#^c16324166|dvfjsdhgfv]] argues that power plants must use advanced pollution filters. Separately, indescions\_2018 [[./thread#^c16324471|indescions\_2018]] proposes environmental-scale scrubbers alongside electric vehicles. Neither supplies filtration performance, exposure measurements, or an account of using such equipment.
- Cleaner-burning heating equipment is another proposed improvement. Asooka [[./thread#^c16324468|Asooka]] recommends gasifier or pyrolysis stoves, described as smokeless, while tyfon [[./thread#^c16324532|tyfon]] says pellet-burning equipment offers better particulate performance than ordinary wood burning. These are recommendations and comparative claims, not measured before-and-after results.
- seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c16324706|seanmcdirmid]] attributes Beijing’s improvement chiefly to removing coal from electricity generation, central heating, and household furnaces in nearby villages. In the reply chain, seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c16324724|seanmcdirmid]] corrects lumberjack’s uncertainty about confiscated wood or kerosene stoves: the fuel was coal. seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c16324794|seanmcdirmid]] reports fewer extreme pollution episodes in 2018, while emphasizing that substantial pollution continued.
- Traffic emissions and enforcement also receive attention. acd [[./thread#^c16325774|acd]] claims diesel emissions controls can stop operating in cold weather and links NOx-related reporting, without presenting personal NO2 measurements. kwhitefoot [[./thread#^c16324997|kwhitefoot]] argues that unnecessary stationary idling was already prohibited in the UK and describes drivers ignoring restrictions in Norway, suggesting that compliance matters alongside additional rules.

## Counterarguments

- The headline is challenged as an unrepresentative comparison. kbart [[./thread#^c16324405|kbart]] characterizes it as Beijing’s best conditions against London’s worst and claims Beijing’s previous annual average air quality was three times worse. clw8 [[./thread#^c16324519|clw8]] reads the article as reporting approximately 200 AQI, while whatyoucantsay [[./thread#^c16324248|whatyoucantsay]] points to Beijing historical values above 500, including January 2017. These are commenters’ interpretations of linked data, not independently inspected measurements.
- The chronology limits explanations of the headline. arekkas [[./thread#^c16324242|arekkas]] identifies the article as January 2017, and sctb [[./thread#^c16324437|sctb]] confirms updating the title. Consequently, seanmcdirmid’s account of improvement during the 2018 winter [[./thread#^c16324794|seanmcdirmid]] cannot itself explain or validate the article’s earlier comparison.
- hiharryhere [[./thread#^c16324333|hiharryhere]] disputes the visual evidence, identifying the photograph of a jogger as a foggy morning in a comparatively rural location more than 60 miles north of London. If that identification is correct, the photograph does not demonstrate London smog; the export provides no independent verification of the location.
- Heating affordability complicates blanket restrictions. chatmasta [[./thread#^c16324140|chatmasta]] worries that a ban could raise costs for poor households, and Feniks [[./thread#^c16324365|Feniks]] emphasizes expensive UK heating. albertgoeswoof [[./thread#^c16324189|albertgoeswoof]] and revelation [[./thread#^c16324181|revelation]] counter that poor London residents generally cannot buy or install these stoves. Their disagreement concerns which households actually benefit; none supplies representative household data.
- Existing certification complicates the case for treating all wood stoves alike. Angostura [[./thread#^c16324367|Angostura]] reports owning a stove certified for smoke-control zones and asks whether insufficient standards or illegal installations explain the problem. Angostura [[./thread#^c16324386|Angostura]] emphasizes that uncertified appliances were already prohibited in those zones. This challenges the proposed regulatory remedy without establishing that certified stoves produce negligible pollution.
- The cleaner-stove recommendation encounters an availability and classification dispute. gvurrdon [[./thread#^c16324518|gvurrdon]] would consider replacement when suitable equipment became readily available and the existing stove needed replacing. After m\_t [[./thread#^c16324827|m\_t]] points to Angostura’s certified stove, gvurrdon [[./thread#^c16326071|gvurrdon]] questions whether it is the same technology as Asooka’s proposed gasifier and says the latter example appears unavailable. The thread does not resolve that distinction.

## Assumptions

- Interpreting the cross-city figures as comparable exposure measurements requires compatible pollutants, index definitions, locations, and averaging periods. kbart [[./thread#^c16324405|kbart]], clw8 [[./thread#^c16324519|clw8]], and whatyoucantsay [[./thread#^c16324248|whatyoucantsay]] supply different summaries and historical references without those methodological details. Their numbers cannot be converted from this export into a consistent PM2.5 or NO2 comparison.
- A lower carbon footprint is treated by some participants as a reason to choose particular household equipment, but it does not establish its local exposure performance. Angostura [[./thread#^c16324367|Angostura]] says carbon concerns motivated buying both a wood stove and a diesel car; nwah1 [[./thread#^c16324984|nwah1]] challenges evaluating combustion only through CO2. These CO2 references concern emissions accounting, not measured indoor CO2 or ventilation adequacy.
- The proposed remedies depend on unmeasured economic and social assumptions. Retric [[./thread#^c16324157|Retric]] assumes avoided health costs exceed replacement-heating costs, while Nux [[./thread#^c16324183|Nux]] and zimablue [[./thread#^c16324178|zimablue]] assume London stove ownership is largely discretionary. gambiting’s account of substantial household savings [[./thread#^c16324289|gambiting]] establishes a possible practical use, but does not establish the income distribution or motivations of London stove owners.
- Accounts of Beijing’s improvement do not isolate its causes. PakG1 [[./thread#^c16324394|PakG1]] relays favorable reports from friends; carlmr [[./thread#^c16324463|carlmr]] recalls newspaper coverage about enforcement against corrupt officials; seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c16324706|seanmcdirmid]] emphasizes coal removal and wind. These are different kinds of evidence, and none provides a controlled attribution of the change to one intervention.

## Conditions

- Upfront and running costs affect feasible heating choices. chatmasta [[./thread#^c16324140|chatmasta]] quotes stove prices of £400–£7,000, while DanBC [[./thread#^c16324167|DanBC]] argues that even the lower price excludes poor households. Feniks [[./thread#^c16324365|Feniks]] attributes expensive UK heating partly to imported fuel; carlmr [[./thread#^c16324469|carlmr]] questions whether wood must also be imported. The exchange supplies no complete comparison of installed costs and operating costs.
- Access to cheaper energy tariffs is part of the affordability argument. albertgoeswoof [[./thread#^c16324189|albertgoeswoof]] associates poor credit with expensive prepayment arrangements, and gambiting [[./thread#^c16324270|gambiting]] describes debt-related switching to prepayment. albertgoeswoof [[./thread#^c16324269|albertgoeswoof]] quotes £1,026 versus £807 for prepay and credit, but gives no cost period or definition; thriftwy [[./thread#^c16327617|thriftwy]] interprets these as meter purchase prices. The figures’ basis remains ambiguous in the supplied text.
- Rural fuel access differs from London. gvurrdon [[./thread#^c16324422|gvurrdon]] can gather fallen wood near a nonurban home, but reports chimney-sweeping costs of about £50–£60 per visit, at least annually and preferably more often. tyfon [[./thread#^c16324488|tyfon]] describes obtaining wood directly from nearby forest in Norway; Retric [[./thread#^c16324681|Retric]] points out that this availability does not transfer readily to London.
- Beijing’s pollution reduction is described as carrying a winter-heating tradeoff. lumberjack [[./thread#^c16324612|lumberjack]] reports poor households left in freezing homes; seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c16324706|seanmcdirmid]] describes gas shortages, inadequate electricity subsidies, and some retreat from the aggressive approach elsewhere, including Hebei. These are reported consequences of the transition, without household temperature, exposure, or expenditure measurements.
- Transport restrictions depend partly on available alternatives and local governance. lumberjack [[./thread#^c16324612|lumberjack]] describes alternating vehicle restrictions in Beijing and considers comparable policies harder to implement in London. theBobBob [[./thread#^c16324673|theBobBob]] recalls alternating-number restrictions in Paris and, with explicit uncertainty, free public transport on those days. Neither reports a measured pollution reduction or personal exposure change.

## Lived experiences

- Angostura [[./thread#^c16324367|Angostura]] reports owning a certified wood stove and seeing no smoke emerge from the chimney while it operates. This is a firsthand visual observation, without particulate measurements, indoor readings, or a documented observation period covering ignition.
- Replying directly to that observation, gvurrdon [[./thread#^c16326182|gvurrdon]] reports substantial smoke during startup from their own stove and neighboring homes on a former rural street. They suggest that operating temperature and wood type affect smoke. The accounts can coexist because they concern potentially different stages of combustion; neither quantifies PM2.5.
- gambiting [[./thread#^c16324289|gambiting]] reports that heating the living room with a few logs was significantly cheaper than running central heating throughout a house whose radiators lacked individual regulation. gvurrdon [[./thread#^c16324422|gvurrdon]] similarly describes keeping the most-used room comfortable while leaving the rest cooler. These are firsthand reports of thermal comfort and heating savings, without accompanying air-quality or health measurements.
- osrec [[./thread#^c16324480|osrec]] says that, after spending time in both cities, London seemed nowhere near as polluted as Beijing. forkLding [[./thread#^c16324508|forkLding]] replies that experience is subjective and numerical evidence should guide comparison. The exchange preserves a personal impression while leaving its dates, locations, and relationship to measured exposure unspecified.
- albertgoeswoof [[./thread#^c16324189|albertgoeswoof]] describes wanting a stove for its heat, appearance, and enjoyment, then deciding against buying one because of urban pollution concerns. This is a firsthand purchasing decision, not an observed improvement in health or air quality.
- Housing arrangements can complicate affordable heating. dazc [[./thread#^c16324636|dazc]] recalls a bedsit with a landlord-operated coin meter charging a high rate and a supply cutoff because the landlord had not paid the bill. This responds to gambiting [[./thread#^c16324274|gambiting]], who reports having utilities in their own name across eight UK rentals. The contrasting anecdotes caution against assuming one rental or billing arrangement.
- seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c16324751|seanmcdirmid]] describes Beijing prepayment through the example of hot water expiring during a shower, while characterizing prices as tolerable and apartment heating as centralized. Separately, seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c16324706|seanmcdirmid]] says red pollution conditions are personally manageable whereas purple conditions are not. That expresses subjective tolerability without specifying symptoms or a medical threshold.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The unit provides no firsthand instrument readings of indoor PM2.5, NO2, or CO2, and no sensor model, calibration, or reliability comparison. clw8’s reported AQI value [[./thread#^c16324519|clw8]] and whatyoucantsay’s references to historical monitoring [[./thread#^c16324248|whatyoucantsay]] are numerical reports from other sources; acd’s NOx-related links [[./thread#^c16325774|acd]] do not establish measured personal NO2 exposure.
- Health harms are asserted, but cognition, sleep, and individual clinical outcomes are not documented. Retric [[./thread#^c16324157|Retric]] claims substantial public-health costs, and mannykannot [[./thread#^c16324494|mannykannot]] invokes historically lethal UK smog. Neither supplies a study analysis or a firsthand health outcome. The concrete comfort reports from gambiting [[./thread#^c16324289|gambiting]] and gvurrdon [[./thread#^c16324422|gvurrdon]] concern warmth, not demonstrated benefits from cleaner air.
- The thread cannot establish when opening windows helps or worsens exposure. varjag [[./thread#^c16324237|varjag]] mentions balanced ventilation only while discussing home energy ratings; dvfjsdhgfv [[./thread#^c16324166|dvfjsdhgfv]] discusses power-plant filtration. There are no window-opening experiments, household filter experiences, cooking-emission measurements, or wildfire-smoke accounts, so those parts of the approved question remain unanswered by this unit.
- The relative contribution of different sources remains disputed. Arnt [[./thread#^c16324460|Arnt]] quotes the article’s attribution of 10 percent of winter pollution to stoves and asks what produces the other 90 percent. tyfon [[./thread#^c16324177|tyfon]] claims wood contributes relatively little compared with diesel, partly pointing to its omission from another article. No pollutant-specific source breakdown resolves these claims.
- There is no measured comparison establishing whether bans, stronger certification, enforcement, or replacement stoves would provide the best exposure reduction for their cost. Angostura [[./thread#^c16324367|Angostura]] leaves standards versus enforcement unresolved; gvurrdon’s startup-smoke observation [[./thread#^c16326182|gvurrdon]] identifies a possible gap in judging an appliance only while hot; Asooka’s cleaner-stove proposal [[./thread#^c16324468|Asooka]] lacks comparative emissions data. The thread therefore supports several competing practical approaches without selecting a demonstrated winner.
