# [29\% of Bay Area's particulate air pollution comes from across the Pacific (2010)](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=6248383)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion considers how much particulate pollution reaches the Bay Area from Asia and whether older reports about Gobi dust reflect present conditions. Participants distinguish dust storms from industrial pollution, debate the effects of Chinese coal use and land management, and argue over the relative responsibility of major emitters, consumers, and governments. A side debate challenges the claim that cycling can cause more pollution than driving because of food production.
Tags: particulate pollution, transboundary pollution, China, Gobi dust, air quality, cycling

## Relevance

Discusses transported particulate pollution and includes firsthand accounts of dust storms, PM2.5 alerts, respirator use, and keeping a child indoors on bad-air days. The discussion also notes uncertainty about how older dust-storm reports relate to later pollution patterns. ## Arguments

- Distant emissions may matter to local outdoor exposure. dredmorbius [[./thread#^c6249089|dredmorbius]] argues that prevailing westerly winds, differing emissions controls, and California's lack of coal-burning facilities make pollution from elsewhere important. veidr [[./thread#^c6248589|veidr]] describes PM2.5 warnings affecting daily life in Tokyo. These comments support concern about transported pollution, but do not independently establish its geographic source or contribution.
- Particulate pollution must be distinguished from atmospheric CO2 emissions. Replying to national emissions comparisons, ars [[./thread#^c6248694|ars]] asks which pollutant is being discussed, and dredmorbius [[./thread#^c6249089|dredmorbius]] explicitly distinguishes carbon totals from particulates. Those comparisons provide no measurements of indoor CO2 or individual particulate exposure.
- seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c6248605|seanmcdirmid]] proposes upgrading combustion equipment, replacing rural coal heating with natural gas, and improving vehicle fuel quality. He further emphasizes fuel quality and emissions equipment [[./thread#^c6248658|seanmcdirmid]]. These are proposed ways to reduce pollution at its sources; the discussion supplies no measured exposure reductions or comparison with household ventilation and filtration.

## Counterarguments

- The suggested improvement in dust storms after the Beijing Olympics has geographic limits. Responding to seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c6248625|seanmcdirmid]], hkmurakami [[./thread#^c6248631|hkmurakami]] recalls a China-origin dust storm in Japan in 2009 or 2010, and po [[./thread#^c6248697|po]] reports yellow skies in Tokyo in March 2013. seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c6248730|seanmcdirmid]] subsequently allows that Beijing and Tokyo experienced different visible severity; these accounts need not contradict a local reduction in Beijing.
- tomhschmidt [[./thread#^c6248587|tomhschmidt]] interprets manufacturing's declining economic share and government initiatives as reasons for optimism. In reply, seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c6248605|seanmcdirmid]] emphasizes ongoing coal combustion, poorly maintained equipment, rural heating, and vehicle fuel problems around Beijing. Neither supplies a measured pollution trend establishing whether those changes had improved exposure.
- hkmurakami [[./thread#^c6248626|hkmurakami]] predicts continued pollution because of investment in Mongolian coal mines. seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c6248658|seanmcdirmid]] qualifies that prediction: the consequences depend on coal quality and the equipment burning it. Their exchange leaves the expected improvement disputed rather than establishing that continued coal use necessarily means unchanged pollution.
- tomhschmidt [[./thread#^c6248614|tomhschmidt]] jokingly blames outdoor kebabs and cigarette smokers. seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c6248648|seanmcdirmid]] treats blaming barbecue vendors as deflection from vehicles using poor fuel. This is a dispute about attribution, not measured evidence that cooking is either a major source or harmless.

## Assumptions

- seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c6248625|seanmcdirmid]] attributes reduced dust storms to improved land management and deforestation policies around the Olympics. His later account [[./thread#^c6248646|seanmcdirmid]] acknowledges that improvement might be specific to Beijing. The causal explanation assumes policy changes account for his observations; no comparative weather, land-use, or emissions data are supplied.
- Visible yellow skies are an observation of a dust event, not a validated measure of PM2.5 concentration or overall safety. seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c6248625|seanmcdirmid]] himself reports worse overall pollution despite fewer conspicuous dust storms, while po [[./thread#^c6248697|po]] describes a dramatic but brief yellow-sky episode. Appearance, particle size, concentration, and cumulative exposure remain distinct.
- veidr's [[./thread#^c6248589|veidr]] use of a respirator and decision to keep his child indoors assume those precautions reduce exposure under the circumstances. The account gives no respirator fit assessment, indoor–outdoor measurements, building leakage information, or filtration details with which to test their effectiveness.

## Conditions

- The proposed source controls are geographically and seasonally specific. seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c6248605|seanmcdirmid]] identifies winter coal heating in Hebei, outdated energy equipment around Beijing, and vehicle fuel quality. His explanation should not be generalized into a source breakdown for Tokyo, the Bay Area, or every Chinese city.
- Moving west is presented as an uncertain escape from pollution. rdl [[./thread#^c6248597|rdl]] speculates that land west of the pollution stream might gain value; seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c6248653|seanmcdirmid]] replies that Kunming still has pollution, albeit less than eastern China, and raises concerns about Kazakhstan and Iran. These are qualitative assessments, with no comparable concentrations or property-price evidence.
- Protection may depend on resources, but costs are unspecified. veidr [[./thread#^c6248589|veidr]] reports personally using a 3M respirator and describes, secondhand, wealthy Beijing children attending preschool in pressurized tents. The wealth distinction is explicit; the account supplies no prices, operating costs, indoor readings, or evidence that the tents achieved a particular exposure reduction.

## Lived experiences

- seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c6248646|seanmcdirmid]] recalls several yellow-sky dust storms while living in China in 2002, but only one in spring 2008 after returning in late 2007. In his edited account [[./thread#^c6248625|seanmcdirmid]], he remembers bad air during the March 2013 event without a yellow sky at his location, followed by cleaner air after it passed. These are firsthand visual and subjective observations, not instrument readings.
- po [[./thread#^c6248697|po]] reports that the March 2013 storm turned the sky yellow at his Tokyo location and came and went within a few hours. He links photographs, but the supplied text contains no particulate measurements. His experience establishes a locally conspicuous event, not its chemical composition or inhaled dose.
- hkmurakami [[./thread#^c6248631|hkmurakami]] reports experiencing a dust storm in Japan in 2009 or 2010 and attributes its origin to China. The experience is firsthand; the source attribution is not accompanied by tracing data in the comment.
- veidr [[./thread#^c6248589|veidr]] says Tokyo news carries PM2.5 reports resembling weather reports. On the worst days he wears a 3M respirator while cycling across town and keeps his child indoors on a pediatrician's advice. This is a concrete account of exposure precautions and disruption to daily activities, without measured changes in dose, symptoms, or health.
- Asked what had worsened during his time in China, seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c6250435|seanmcdirmid]] names traffic and pollution. Together with his claim that contemporary PM2.5 conditions were worse despite fewer dust storms [[./thread#^c6248625|seanmcdirmid]], this preserves his distinction between improvement in one visible phenomenon and deterioration in overall perceived air quality.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The headline's 29\% attribution cannot be evaluated from the supplied discussion. The title labels the article 2010, whereas seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c6248625|seanmcdirmid]] calls it a 2008 article and questions its relevance to conditions in 2013. The export contains no study methods, sampling period, uncertainty estimates, or basis for interpreting that percentage specifically as PM2.5 exposure.
- Whether substantial pollution crosses the Pacific without major dust storms remains unresolved here. seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c6248625|seanmcdirmid]] explicitly expresses uncertainty; dredmorbius [[./thread#^c6249089|dredmorbius]] argues for long-range transport and says transported particles tend to be smaller. Neither supplies transport measurements or source-apportionment results in the comment text.
- There are no firsthand numerical PM2.5 readings or sensor-reliability tests in this unit. veidr [[./thread#^c6248589|veidr]] relies on public news reports, while po [[./thread#^c6248697|po]] supplies a visual account and photo links. Neither provides instrument models for air monitoring, calibration, measurement units, indoor comparisons, or enough information to distinguish short peaks from sustained exposure.
- Health concern is clearer than demonstrated health effects. dredmorbius [[./thread#^c6249089|dredmorbius]] asserts that smaller transported particles are more harmful; veidr [[./thread#^c6248589|veidr]] reports pediatric advice; g123g [[./thread#^c6249819|g123g]] requests information about less-affected US locations for people with breathing issues. None reports an air-quality-related diagnosis, measured symptom improvement, or effects on cognition or sleep.
- The discussion cannot establish when opening windows helps or worsens exposure. veidr [[./thread#^c6248589|veidr]] describes staying indoors during bad outdoor conditions but supplies no indoor measurements or ventilation and filtration results. There are no indoor NO2 or CO2 readings; the CO2 distinction raised by ars [[./thread#^c6248694|ars]] concerns atmospheric emissions, not occupied-room air.
- Wildfire-smoke experience is absent. rdl [[./thread#^c6248553|rdl]] discusses Indonesian fires as pollution sources, jlgreco [[./thread#^c6248567|jlgreco]] questions whether some fires have a natural ecological role, and rdl [[./thread#^c6248586|rdl]] responds that these fires are predominantly human-caused. That exchange offers no firsthand smoke concentrations, health effects, or evidence about windows, filtration, or other personal precautions.
