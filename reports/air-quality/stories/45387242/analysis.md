# [They don't make 'em like that any more: Dyson Pure Cool-Me personal air purifier](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45387242)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

The thread discusses the Dyson purifier-fan’s design, airflow, noise, placement, dust buildup, price, and how it compares with other fans and purifiers. Commenters trade practical alternatives, including DIY filter boxes and other air-cleaning devices, and debate whether directed purified airflow meaningfully helps. Side conversations cover whether a purifier could help with soldering fumes, the hazards of different workshop fumes, and claims about indoor CO2 removal.
Tags: air-purifiers, Dyson, product-review, DIY, indoor-air-quality, soldering-fumes

## Relevance

Comments cover purifier performance, filtration design, noise, and alternatives, alongside a firsthand overnight CO2 measurement above 2,000 ppm that improved with a window and door open. Participants also discuss purifier-integrated sensor trustworthiness and whether CO2 removal claims are supported. ## Arguments

- Fr0styMatt88 [[./thread#^c45389019|Fr0styMatt88]] makes a practical case for addressing bedroom CO2: opening the window and door substantially reduced their measured overnight concentration. They see potential value in a CO2-removing appliance because sleeping with these openings is not always feasible.
- tehjoker [[./thread#^c45388807|tehjoker]] argues that air cleaning has a public-health purpose, particularly against COVID-19 in classrooms and other shared spaces. This is an argument for deployment; the comment supplies no infection-outcome measurements.
- ibaikov [[./thread#^c45387823|ibaikov]] argues that an ordinary consumer purifier can offer useful everyday benefits through smoke clearance, odor reduction, easier breathing for pet-allergic visitors, and somewhat less dust. These are household observations rather than quantified pollutant-removal results.
- imglorp [[./thread#^c45387787|imglorp]] proposes capturing soldering fumes with a hose and fan exhausting through a window, arguing that a filter is unnecessary when the exhaust goes outdoors. This offers source extraction as an alternative to cleaning air after it enters the room.
- ortusdux [[./thread#^c45388686|ortusdux]] emphasizes matching a DIY purifier's fan to filter resistance and reports good experience with NF-F12 PWM fans. oritron [[./thread#^c45389611|oritron]] recommends a centrifugal blower and describes an assembled shop-vac HEPA-filter system. Their contributions establish practical construction experience, without measured cleaning performance.

## Counterarguments

- jerlam [[./thread#^c45388938|jerlam]] challenges the CO2-removing appliance's value, citing unclear performance numbers and arguing that CO2's negative effects are overstated. Owning two CO2 monitors gives context to this opinion, but they provide no readings or health evidence supporting it.
- jerlam [[./thread#^c45389453|jerlam]] questions whether a personal purifier delivers substantially cleaner air at a distance, because filtered airflow can mix with surrounding air. They point to AirFanta's claimed laminar flow and a testing link, but no test results appear in the supplied text. astafrig [[./thread#^c45390042|astafrig]] separately describes entrainment in Dyson airflow; this is a mechanism claim, not an exposure measurement for the reviewed device.
- exmadscientist [[./thread#^c45388218|exmadscientist]] disputes imglorp's [[./thread#^c45387787|imglorp]] assertion that soldering filtration must capture metal vapor, identifying burned, potentially acidic flux as the fumes instead. This materially changes the proposed filtration problem, although the subsequent replies leave actual lead-vapor exposure unresolved.
- dathinab [[./thread#^c45388270|dathinab]] recalls ozone-producing electrically driven fans but explicitly does not know whether historical Dyson products were involved. foobarian [[./thread#^c45388679|foobarian]] associates the problem with other brands, tentatively Ionic Breeze, and describes Dyson's mechanical impellers. This exchange does not establish ozone emissions from the reviewed purifier.
- mh- [[./thread#^c45390738|mh-]] rejects the quoted review's suggestion that the fan feels as effective as an air conditioner until approximately 30°C/86°F; kyleee [[./thread#^c45392749|kyleee]] reports becoming uncomfortable around 82°F. Their disagreement demonstrates differing comfort tolerances, without establishing a technical comparison of cooling performance.
- cyanydeez's [[./thread#^c45390428|cyanydeez]] reply, 'Personal', challenges the relevance of universal public-space cleaning to a statement about personal purifiers. tehjoker [[./thread#^c45390606|tehjoker]] responds that they use one at work for protection. The reply narrows the practical context; it does not demonstrate either personal protection or protection of the whole workplace.

## Assumptions

- The manufacturer's sleep-and-focus pitch reported by neom [[./thread#^c45388816|neom]] assumes that reducing indoor CO2 produces those benefits. neom explicitly expresses uncertainty, and Fr0styMatt88 [[./thread#^c45389019|Fr0styMatt88]] reports concentration changes without corresponding sleep, cognition, or symptom measurements.
- The recirculation advice from vl [[./thread#^c45389274|vl]] and kccqzy [[./thread#^c45389337|kccqzy]] assumes that moving air around the home improves conditions in an occupied room. Their accounts do not establish whether lower room CO2 reflects redistribution, outdoor-air exchange, or another mechanism, and do not demonstrate whole-house CO2 removal.
- ibaikov's [[./thread#^c45387823|ibaikov]] recommendation of a purifier for soldering or frequent 3D printing extrapolates from the household uses they actually describe. Disappearing smoke odors, reduced visible dust, and perceived breathing relief do not establish removal of every relevant pollutant or effectiveness for those workshop sources.
- The DIY proposals from ortusdux [[./thread#^c45388686|ortusdux]] and oritron [[./thread#^c45389611|oritron]] depend on suitable filter media, sufficient airflow against resistance, and adequate sealing. Their fan recommendations and improvised adaptor describe implementation choices; the comments provide no airflow, leakage, or pollutant-removal measurements validating the completed systems.

## Conditions

- jerlam [[./thread#^c45388938|jerlam]] describes the CO2-removing appliance as starting around a thousand dollars, with subscription pods, continuing maintenance, and a large footprint. neom [[./thread#^c45389031|neom]] reports the founder's ambition to reach \$300 and half the size through later manufacturing changes. Those lower costs and dimensions are projections, not an available product specification.
- DIY economics depend on locally obtainable filters. gwbas1c [[./thread#^c45387731|gwbas1c]] objects to a £70 filter, while ortusdux [[./thread#^c45388686|ortusdux]] suggests \$20–30 shop-vac HEPA filters; these are different components, not demonstrated equivalents. ibaikov [[./thread#^c45387878|ibaikov]] says suitable Corsi–Rosenthal-style filters are unavailable where they live, without identifying the location.
- Majestic121 [[./thread#^c45387623|Majestic121]] considers the discontinued Dyson and expensive replacement filters a poor soldering solution. dangus [[./thread#^c45388205|dangus]] names a Coway purifier/fan alternative, while sampo [[./thread#^c45388391|sampo]] tentatively suggests IKEA UPPÅTVIND but questions its directed airflow. Owner tylerflick [[./thread#^c45388453|tylerflick]] advises against the IKEA unit without explaining why, leaving comparative value uncertain.
- hennell [[./thread#^c45390865|hennell]] describes a UK home that becomes hotter than the outdoor air, where a circulator pushing air out of a window helps with heat. That favorable temperature difference supports their comfort strategy, but they provide no indoor/outdoor pollutant comparison showing whether window opening also improves air quality.

## Lived experiences

- Fr0styMatt88 [[./thread#^c45389019|Fr0styMatt88]] reports AirGradient One measurements in a relatively large master bedroom with an ensuite, occupied by one sleeper. With the window and door closed, CO2 exceeded 2,000 ppm by morning and fell after doors were opened. With both window and door slightly open, it was approximately 700 ppm. Because both openings changed, the account does not isolate their individual contributions.
- neom [[./thread#^c45388816|neom]] personally encountered the appliance's founder and found a demonstration using off-the-shelf CO2 monitoring in two spaces impressive. They supply no concentrations, timing, comparison protocol, or independently observed sleep or focus benefits.
- vl [[./thread#^c45389274|vl]] runs the furnace fan continuously even when the burner is off and reports less stale air and more even temperatures. kccqzy [[./thread#^c45389337|kccqzy]] says a few minutes of HVAC fan operation each hour is enough to lower their CO2. Neither supplies numerical before-and-after readings, instrument details, or outdoor-air intake information.
- ibaikov [[./thread#^c45387823|ibaikov]] uses a basic Xiaomi purifier during movie nights that sometimes involve indoor smoking and reports rapid clearance with no remaining odor. They are not personally allergic but say pet-allergic visitors breathe more easily around their dog. They also report somewhat less dust; the benefit of running the purifier during robot vacuuming is explicitly tentative. High-speed noise, device size, and wanting separately replaceable activated carbon are practical concerns.
- testfrequency [[./thread#^c45388605|testfrequency]] donated their Dyson after struggling with placement and airflow direction and noticing persistent dust on its central surface. They felt it might blow old dust toward them, but report no airborne-particle measurement establishing that this occurred.
- foobarian [[./thread#^c45388679|foobarian]] prefers the Dyson fan's soothing white noise to a box fan's beating sound. vl [[./thread#^c45389223|vl]] reports much cheaper Vornado fans that are effectively inaudible or very quiet at low settings, after exchanging one noisy defective unit. These are subjective acoustic experiences with fans, not comparative purification results.
- dnemmers [[./thread#^c45388549|dnemmers]] reports using a dedicated desktop fume extractor to help with soldering. oritron [[./thread#^c45389611|oritron]] describes using a centrifugal blower mounted on a shop-vac HEPA filter with a plastic adaptor and hot-glue seal. Neither reports breathing-zone measurements or health outcomes.
- fsckboy [[./thread#^c45388578|fsckboy]] recalls enjoying burning-flux smells while doing electronics work and noticing no ill effects. They also acknowledge possible delayed harm. This is a personal history of exposure and perceived health, not evidence that the exposure was safe.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The supplied story part contains no interpretable PM2.5 or NO2 measurements and no accounts of cooking emissions, traffic exposure, or wildfire smoke. Fr0styMatt88 [[./thread#^c45389019|Fr0styMatt88]] measures CO2, while hennell [[./thread#^c45390865|hennell]] discusses outdoor air only through a temperature comparison. Consequently, this unit cannot establish when opening windows would import enough outdoor pollution to outweigh its ventilation benefits.
- Sensor reliability remains largely undocumented. Fr0styMatt88 [[./thread#^c45389019|Fr0styMatt88]] names an AirGradient One but gives no calibration or reference comparison; neom [[./thread#^c45388816|neom]] describes off-the-shelf monitoring without numerical results. ibaikov's [[./thread#^c45387823|ibaikov]] reported '1-5 ppm' identifies neither pollutant nor instrument and cannot be treated as a PM2.5 concentration or a comprehensive indication of clean air.
- The disagreement over CO2's health and cognitive significance remains open: neom [[./thread#^c45388816|neom]] relays uncertain manufacturer claims about sleep and focus, whereas jerlam [[./thread#^c45388938|jerlam]] calls negative effects overstated. Fr0styMatt88's [[./thread#^c45389019|Fr0styMatt88]] overnight measurements establish a reported exposure difference, but the supplied comments contain no cognitive testing, measured sleep outcomes, or health-study results connecting it to benefit or harm.
- The effectiveness of personal filtration at the breathing zone is unresolved. jerlam [[./thread#^c45389453|jerlam]] raises airflow mixing and distance, and tehjoker [[./thread#^c45390606|tehjoker]] reports workplace use, but neither supplies local particle counts, exposure reductions, or infection outcomes. The discussion does not establish equivalence between a directed personal stream, a room purifier, and shared-space cleaning.
- The soldering exchange does not quantify lead exposure. unshavedyak [[./thread#^c45388243|unshavedyak]] raises a conditional concern about lead-based solder; fsckboy [[./thread#^c45388629|fsckboy]] notes molten lead's vapor pressure but explicitly cannot assess hazardous temperatures or durations. These replies qualify exmadscientist's [[./thread#^c45388218|exmadscientist]] categorical rejection of metal vapor without supplying measurements that resolve the practical risk.
- Outdoor exhaust introduces an unresolved distinction between reducing the user's exposure and cleaning the discharge. imglorp [[./thread#^c45387787|imglorp]] recommends unfiltered outdoor exhaust for soldering, while exmadscientist [[./thread#^c45388218|exmadscientist]] specifically argues that laser-cutting fumes warrant substantial filtration before release. driverdan [[./thread#^c45388905|driverdan]] explains that laser cutting burns material, an explanation Our\_Benefactors [[./thread#^c45392464|Our\_Benefactors]] accepts; emission composition, filter performance, and exposure outside remain unmeasured.
