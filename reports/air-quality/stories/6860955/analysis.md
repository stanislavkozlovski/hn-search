# [Schoolchildren ordered indoors as air pollution cloaks Shanghai](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=6860955)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion compares Shanghai’s severe smog with historic pollution in London, Pittsburgh, and other cities, and considers health effects, pollution levels, and how quickly long-term impacts may appear. Participants debate public awareness and protest, government enforcement, economic growth, coal use, and energy choices including nuclear, renewables, and gas. Other threads cover pollution spreading across regions, masks and indoor protection, air-quality monitoring, and the tradeoffs of industrial development and consumer goods.
Tags: air pollution, Shanghai, public health, coal, energy policy, China, industrialization

## Relevance

The discussion includes reported severe outdoor particulate exposure, PM2.5 readings and health concerns, firsthand respiratory effects, and practical responses such as staying indoors, closing windows during pollution spikes, and using filtration. It also contains uncertainty about long-term effects and comparisons across places and eras. ## Arguments

- ddeck [[./thread#^c6862140|ddeck]] summarizes a 2013 WHO report as linking long-term particulate exposure to cardiovascular and respiratory mortality and lung cancer, with increased mortality even at low concentrations and no clearly safe level. This is an attributed research summary; the supplied discussion contains neither the underlying methods nor an individual risk calculation.
- Commenters invoke both acute and chronic mortality: Sharlin [[./thread#^c6861163|Sharlin]] cites roughly 10,000 deaths associated with London's 1952 Great Smog; ddeck [[./thread#^c6862225|ddeck]] cites estimated annual coal-power deaths of 22,000 in the EU and 10,000–20,000 in the US. throwaway\_yy2Di [[./thread#^c6862553|throwaway\_yy2Di]] supplies substantially higher US estimates: 90,000–380,000 deaths from combustion overall, including 23,000–98,000 attributed to power plants. These are reported historical or modeled estimates, with no reconciliation of their methods.
- ashray [[./thread#^c6865692|ashray]], responding to skepticism about closing windows, argues that closure helps during outdoor particulate spikes such as rush hour and Diwali fireworks. They also report running purifiers and air conditioners indoors. c0riander [[./thread#^c6863944|c0riander]] explicitly makes the benefit conditional on HVAC providing filtration. Together these comments support a conditional strategy of limiting polluted outdoor air while operating filtration, without quantifying the resulting exposure reduction.
- petermcd [[./thread#^c6861472|petermcd]] describes access to pollution numbers as a practical improvement: circulation of US embassy PM2.5 information allegedly helped prompt official reporting across major Chinese cities. This supports making otherwise difficult-to-assess exposure visible, although reporting availability does not establish measurement accuracy.
- petermcd [[./thread#^c6861472|petermcd]] reports cleaner gasoline, relocation of industrial sources, restrictions on dirty coal briquettes, construction-dust controls, and barbecue closures, while judging that these measures were helping without reversing the overall trend. Their clarification identifies street-food barbecue vendors, including ones near their home, as the cooking sources being closed [[./thread#^c6864486|petermcd]]; it supplies no measured contribution from cooking or evidence about household stoves.

## Counterarguments

- jpatokal [[./thread#^c6863573|jpatokal]] challenges reliance on closed windows alone: slowing exchange might protect against a temporary spike, but persistent outdoor pollution could eventually produce similarly polluted indoor air. The replies introduce active filtration and intermittent peaks as relevant conditions—c0riander [[./thread#^c6863944|c0riander]] and ashray [[./thread#^c6865692|ashray]] therefore narrow the disagreement, without demonstrating how a particular building behaves.
- Against the thread's frequent emphasis on coal, teyc [[./thread#^c6863938|teyc]] cites an article claiming road vehicles emit an order of magnitude more particulate matter than coal-fired stations and that coal stations are no longer the primary PM2.5 source. Their recollection of alternating vehicle-use days in Santiago and suggestion of electric vehicles are proposed responses, not measured intervention results or independently established source attribution.
- deletes [[./thread#^c6861351|deletes]] contrasts a week-long London disaster with persistent Shanghai pollution. nabla9 [[./thread#^c6861605|nabla9]] replies that London experienced bad smogs for almost a century and that the Great Smog was one episode. This prevents treating the historical comparison as a single isolated event versus an exclusively modern chronic problem.
- Nav\_Panel [[./thread#^c6861299|Nav\_Panel]] explicitly retracts their assumption that Beijing was cleaner than Shanghai. user1713952 [[./thread#^c6861362|user1713952]] points to linked data suggesting Beijing was typically worse, while matsur [[./thread#^c6861715|matsur]] discusses its geography. The retracted comparison should not be retained as evidence of a stable city ranking; a Shanghai peak and another city's typical conditions answer different questions.
- coldcode [[./thread#^c6861562|coldcode]] argues that leaders must eventually address pollution because everyone breathes the same air. jadoint [[./thread#^c6861349|jadoint]], explicitly relying on friends rather than local firsthand experience, counters that wealthy people can purchase expensive purification for homes, cars, and workplaces. This challenges equal exposure as an assumption, although neither commenter measures how much protection wealth buys.
- vinceguidry [[./thread#^c6863309|vinceguidry]] describes black mucus as the nose filtering dirty air and later attributes the discoloration to trapped particulates [[./thread#^c6864766|vinceguidry]]. iaskwhy [[./thread#^c6864511|iaskwhy]] remains uncertain whether their own black mucus comes from pollution. The filtering explanation does not establish the material's identity, how much was inhaled, or whether exposure was harmless.

## Assumptions

- These are December 2013 accounts and forecasts, rather than current conditions or a representative survey. For example, petermcd [[./thread#^c6861472|petermcd]] describes then-recent interventions, and johnzim [[./thread#^c6862009|johnzim]] predicts that fuller health consequences might take another decade to appear. This source unit contains no later follow-up testing those predictions.
- chockablock initially cites approximately 3,000 µg/m³ PM10 for London's Great Smog [[./thread#^c6862041|chockablock]], then offers what they regard as a better benchmark: a measured peak daily average of 1,620 µg/m³ total suspended matter [[./thread#^c6864088|chockablock]]. Their estimated PM2.5 of about 700 µg/m³ assumes PM10 equals 60\% of total suspended matter and PM2.5 equals 75\% of PM10. Its comparison with reported Shanghai PM2.5 of 602.5 µg/m³ is explicitly approximate, rather than a direct measurement of the same particle fraction under matched conditions.
- Numerical comparisons require preserving units and metrics. rahimnathwani [[./thread#^c6861133|rahimnathwani]] reports AQI 202, whereas chockablock [[./thread#^c6864088|chockablock]] discusses particle mass concentrations. tanzam75 [[./thread#^c6865157|tanzam75]] reports Beijing and Delhi peaks of 755 and 565 but writes mg/m³, unlike the µg/m³ used elsewhere. The supplied text does not resolve that unit inconsistency, so these values should not be silently normalized or treated as interchangeable.
- The belief that outdoor 'fresh air' is necessarily preferable is reported by kvnwng [[./thread#^c6861675|kvnwng]] in a hospital and by ashray [[./thread#^c6862139|ashray]] in their family. Conversely, recommending that people remain indoors presupposes some indoor protection. Neither account provides simultaneous indoor and outdoor measurements establishing the size or duration of that protection.
- Visual severity, smell, and symptoms cannot supply the missing dose measurements. ck2 [[./thread#^c6860958|ck2]] infers lost years of life from a photograph; iaskwhy [[./thread#^c6862154|iaskwhy]] reports black mucus and a strong gas smell; johnzim [[./thread#^c6861983|johnzim]] expressly cautions against attributing a relative's cancer to pollution through confirmation bias. These observations and concerns have different evidentiary weight from quantified exposure or causal health studies.

## Conditions

- Geography and weather complicate city comparisons. matsur [[./thread#^c6861715|matsur]] emphasizes Beijing's inland bowl and desert dust; rollo\_tommasi [[./thread#^c6861607|rollo\_tommasi]] also emphasizes dust. seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c6861835|seanmcdirmid]] argues that the bowl and inversions are the main problem, recalling clear visibility from their office just the previous day. knappe [[./thread#^c6861470|knappe]] describes coastal winds carrying pollution away from Shanghai during a recent visit. These accounts support substantial temporal variation without resolving the relative contributions of dust, emissions, and atmospheric trapping.
- Moving outside a major city may not guarantee clean air: poutine [[./thread#^c6861876|poutine]] describes smog extending hundreds of kilometers into the countryside, and haosdent [[./thread#^c6861323|haosdent]] asserts that the problem extends across China. However, knappe's multi-city trip found appreciable differences, with Shijiazhuang worst during that visit [[./thread#^c6861470|knappe]]. Broad geographic claims should therefore not imply uniform conditions everywhere.
- Cross-border exposure is repeatedly alleged. veidr [[./thread#^c6861964|veidr]] reports PM2.5 warnings in Japan, roughly 3,000 km from China, and attributes most of that pollution to China. nostromo [[./thread#^c6862893|nostromo]] cites a measure assigning 29\% of San Francisco pollution to Asia; jajathehut1 [[./thread#^c6861597|jajathehut1]] asserts that one-third of Los Angeles pollution comes from China. These concern different locations and geographic source definitions, and the comments do not provide methods sufficient to validate the percentages.
- Protective equipment can impose substantial upfront costs. seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c6861786|seanmcdirmid]] reports spending about \$1,000 on a purifier, later identifying it only as the second-largest Blue Air model [[./thread#^c6864504|seanmcdirmid]]. For masks, prawks [[./thread#^c6861199|prawks]] offers cost as a likely explanation for choosing flimsy masks over the alternatives mentioned by Cthulhu\_ [[./thread#^c6861183|Cthulhu\_]]. The thread supplies neither an affordability survey nor comparable equipment-performance data.
- Relocation and protected environments depend on resources and family circumstances. petermcd [[./thread#^c6861472|petermcd]] describes emigration among those who can afford it; wang0109 [[./thread#^c6861408|wang0109]] identifies pollution and children's education as migration motives from experience, without a sample supporting a population ranking. seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c6861868|seanmcdirmid]] mentions employer-funded schooling and 'huge bubbles' at better private schools, but provides no construction details or air-quality measurements.
- Source controls also have costs. ChuckMcM [[./thread#^c6864036|ChuckMcM]] argues that coal preprocessing and exhaust filtration can reduce pollution but increase construction, operating, and fuel expenses. ddeck [[./thread#^c6862225|ddeck]] describes living in an unnamed small city-state where coal stations sit beside housing, making source location directly relevant to potential residential exposure. Neither account quantifies the local benefit of a particular retrofit.
- Economic benefits and exposure burdens can fall on different people. huxley [[./thread#^c6861817|huxley]] describes industrial and agricultural odors being defended as the 'smell of money'; mikestew [[./thread#^c6862596|mikestew]] recalls telling their father, when passing a pig farm, that it was not their money. These comments document odor-related discomfort and its economic justification, without identifying PM2.5 or establishing toxicity from smell.

## Lived experiences

- johnzim [[./thread#^c6861274|johnzim]] reports growing up relatively healthy in Hong Kong, then returning with asthma and experiencing lung constriction within minutes each day, preventing their intended return home. On a later visit, Lantau was somewhat better but still unacceptable to them, and they describe breathlessness while crossing a road [[./thread#^c6861983|johnzim]]. They also report a grandfather's respiratory cancer while explicitly resisting confident attribution to pollution.
- jliechti1 [[./thread#^c6861918|jliechti1]] describes black mucus after outdoor exposure during a highly polluted Beijing winter. Returning to Chicago, even air around O'Hare felt unusually clean. This is a firsthand symptom and sensory comparison, with no personal monitor readings or medical determination of the mucus's composition.
- iaskwhy [[./thread#^c6862154|iaskwhy]] reports recurring black mucus in central London and a strong gas smell on Queen's Gate in Kensington. Their follow-up expressly questions whether pollution caused the mucus [[./thread#^c6864511|iaskwhy]]. The odor is not a measurement or identification of NO2.
- kvnwng [[./thread#^c6861675|kvnwng]], after seven years living and working in China, recalls difficulty persuading parents to close windows in an obstetric hospital containing newborns. They report a belief in the superiority of outdoor air and nurses who seemed unable to answer questions about children's pollution exposure. This is one person's institutional experience, not evidence about all Chinese hospitals or health workers.
- ashray [[./thread#^c6862139|ashray]] describes a father with lung disease who preferred sitting on a New Delhi balcony and subsequently died, while their mother continued opening windows for 'fresh air.' They report PM10/PM2.5 values of '1900+/700+' on some days, especially around Diwali, without specifying units or the measurement source. Their later account says household purifiers and air conditioners help somewhat [[./thread#^c6865692|ashray]]; it does not establish what caused the father's death.
- rahimnathwani [[./thread#^c6861133|rahimnathwani]] reports Beijing AQI of 202, described as 'very unhealthy,' while seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c6861786|seanmcdirmid]] reports a PM2.5-related AQI of 344 elsewhere in the discussion. These are contemporaneous ambient-reading reports, not documented measurements made with commenters' own instruments. Their different values do not establish sensor disagreement without station, timing, and averaging information.
- jread [[./thread#^c6861252|jread]] recalls Utah winter inversions, PM2.5 sometimes reaching '80 mcg,' and finding the conditions 'utterly depressing.' This establishes reported discomfort or mood impact, not a clinical depression finding. seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c6861934|seanmcdirmid]] contrasts local alarm around Salt Lake City values of 70 with Beijing values of 700 being regarded as a bad day, illustrating different expectations while leaving units incomplete.
- veidr [[./thread#^c6861964|veidr]] recalls childhood Los Angeles smog alerts preventing outdoor recess and reports similar occasional advice to keep children indoors in Japan. dlundqvist [[./thread#^c6861073|dlundqvist]] describes considering keeping their children inside during the Shanghai episode but ultimately sending them to school. Neither account establishes the children's actual indoor exposure.
- Lagged2Death [[./thread#^c6861799|Lagged2Death]] recalls childhood brown skies that changed the appearance of sunlight and later says the local improvement is pronounced while still living nearby [[./thread#^c6865014|Lagged2Death]]. Angostura [[./thread#^c6862352|Angostura]] remembers approaching central London by noticing coal-black buildings. mturmon [[./thread#^c6864537|mturmon]] separately offers Pittsburgh's blackened stonework as local historical lore, rather than explicitly claiming a personal childhood observation.
- tokenadult [[./thread#^c6863808|tokenadult]] describes Taipei becoming remarkably cleaner over time. Their account also includes other travelers' comparisons: a French visitor praising China's relative lack of pollution in 1982, and a Ukrainian visitor in 2001 preferring Taiwan's cleanliness to Hong Kong's. These illustrate changing relative impressions, without measured concentrations or proof of the proposed political explanation.
- Accounts of public concern conflict in context. knappe [[./thread#^c6861470|knappe]] found a largely nonchalant attitude during a three-week trip and in their brother's response to living in Shijiazhuang. petermcd [[./thread#^c6861472|petermcd]] perceived strong dissatisfaction in Beijing, especially about cancer and children. tonyplee [[./thread#^c6861865|tonyplee]] saw constant PM discussions among classmates on social networks but distinguished discussion and humor from an outcry. None establishes nationwide sentiment.
- biesnecker [[./thread#^c6860999|biesnecker]] says they loved Shanghai but could not live there again because of the air; their former job would have covered schooling but not an entirely protected air, water, and food environment [[./thread#^c6861927|biesnecker]]. mathattack [[./thread#^c6861122|mathattack]] recalls previously finding Shanghai attractive as a place to work and later feeling relieved that move never happened. These are personal residential decisions, not estimates of universal habitability.

## Unresolved disagreements

- How much protection do closed windows and filtration actually provide, and for how long? jpatokal's chronic-infiltration concern [[./thread#^c6863573|jpatokal]] receives conditional replies from c0riander [[./thread#^c6863944|c0riander]] and ashray [[./thread#^c6865692|ashray]], but no paired indoor/outdoor measurements, air-exchange estimates, or purifier removal results. The discussion supports concern about opening windows during dirty outdoor spikes; it supplies no measured example showing when opening them improves indoor air.
- Sensor reliability remains largely unexamined. matsur [[./thread#^c6861715|matsur]] recounts an off-scale reading triggering the US embassy monitor's 'crazy bad' software label, which concerns presentation and range handling rather than demonstrating an inaccurate particle sensor. Neither that anecdote nor the AQI reports from rahimnathwani [[./thread#^c6861133|rahimnathwani]] and seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c6861786|seanmcdirmid]] supplies calibration, instrument specifications, or independent validation.
- The apparent coexistence of severe pollution and high longevity is not resolved. Guillaume86 [[./thread#^c6861397|Guillaume86]] asks about Shanghai's reported life expectancy above 82; voidlogic [[./thread#^c6861456|voidlogic]] points to older residents having lived before conditions worsened, and johnzim [[./thread#^c6862009|johnzim]] predicts delayed consequences. These responses do not establish how the life-expectancy statistic was calculated or quantify pollution's contribution to it.
- No defensible cigarette-equivalent risk emerges. tcas [[./thread#^c6861146|tcas]] repeats a linked comparison with living in an airport smoking lounge, while crazygringo [[./thread#^c6862144|crazygringo]] asks how many cigarettes a day's exposure would equal specifically for cancer risk. The supplied replies provide no conversion accounting for exposure duration or pollutant composition.
- The historical photograph's interpretation remains contested. SiVal [[./thread#^c6863809|SiVal]] calls it a sepia print shaped by photographic choices, then clarifies that Lagged2Death's memories sounded credible [[./thread#^c6865759|SiVal]]. Lagged2Death [[./thread#^c6881764|Lagged2Death]] challenges the claim of an Atlantic-created presentation by pointing to the original EPA project; their final reply notes archival identification as a slide, favoring a color-photograph explanation [[./thread#^c6883759|Lagged2Death]]. This later evidence weakens the sepia assertion but is not independently verified here, and the photo provides no pollutant concentration.
- Mask performance is unanswered. Cthulhu\_ [[./thread#^c6861183|Cthulhu\_]] asks why people use flimsy masks instead of gas masks or carbon filters; autodidakto [[./thread#^c6861758|autodidakto]] dismisses the masks as placebo, while prawks [[./thread#^c6861199|prawks]] suggests cost. No commenter supplies filtration measurements, fit information, or a pollutant-specific comparison supporting either effectiveness or blanket dismissal.
- Source attribution and intervention effectiveness remain uncertain. teyc's vehicle-dominance claim [[./thread#^c6863938|teyc]] sits alongside strong coal emphasis and petermcd's mixed account of multiple controls [[./thread#^c6861472|petermcd]]. The thread supplies no local source-apportionment results showing which source dominates a person's PM2.5 exposure or which intervention produced the largest reduction.
- Important parts of the approved question have no substantive evidence in this unit. Symmetry [[./thread#^c6861108|Symmetry]] and danielweber [[./thread#^c6861321|danielweber]] distinguish CO2 emissions from particulate, sulfur, and NOx pollution, but provide no indoor CO2 or NO2 measurements or health results. There are no reported cognition or sleep tests; jread's distress [[./thread#^c6861252|jread]] is a subjective experience. SiVal's forest-fire comparison [[./thread#^c6863809|SiVal]] is visual, rather than a wildfire-exposure account, and petermcd's barbecue discussion [[./thread#^c6864486|petermcd]] does not establish household cooking or ventilation tradeoffs.
