# [Ask HN: Best way to remove CO2 in a bedroom?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40696110)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

The discussion focuses on reducing bedroom CO2 overnight, with ventilation emerging as the main practical approach: opening windows or doors, using fans, running an air handler, or installing heat-recovery ventilation. Participants compare energy costs and comfort, discuss monitoring and airflow, and consider why plants and chemical scrubbers are limited or impractical for ordinary homes.
Tags: indoor CO2, bedroom ventilation, air quality monitors, heat recovery ventilation, home airflow, CO2 removal

## Relevance

Directly addresses bedroom CO2, overnight readings, and ventilation options including windows, fans, open doors, bathroom exhaust, and heat-recovery systems. Accounts include measured reductions as well as tradeoffs in heat, cooling, humidity, noise, and outdoor air quality; commenters also disagree about CO2 effects and sensor assumptions. ## Arguments

- Measure first, then ventilate: hliyan [[./thread#^c40696305|hliyan]] recommends a CO2 monitor and argues that exchanging air, even when it must subsequently be heated or cooled, is usually more economical than scrubbing CO2. This is practical advice, not a documented cost comparison.
- Windows plus fans are presented as inexpensive improvements. gandalfgreybeer [[./thread#^c40696430|gandalfgreybeer]] favors adding a fan; everdrive [[./thread#^c40696313|everdrive]] reports using window fans in several modes and cracking windows during winter. Their argument is supported by household observations, with important differences between buildings and weather.
- When windows cannot be opened, participants suggest increasing the bedroom's connection to other rooms: ydnaclementine [[./thread#^c40697534|ydnaclementine]] recommends an open door, pawelduda [[./thread#^c40696244|pawelduda]] a doorway fan, and weissi [[./thread#^c40696321|weissi]] reports that opening the hallway door helps more than opening the ensuite door. These approaches depend on the air available beyond the bedroom.
- Continuous central circulation is another proposed improvement. giantg2 [[./thread#^c40697086|giantg2]] recommends setting a forced-air fan to on instead of auto; l1tany11 [[./thread#^c40700329|l1tany11]] suggests mixing bedroom air with a substantially larger house. bitshiftfaced [[./thread#^c40696637|bitshiftfaced]] separately proposes an HVAC fresh-air intake. Mixing existing indoor air and introducing outdoor air are distinct interventions.
- Heat recovery offers a proposed compromise between ventilation and thermal comfort. yuumei [[./thread#^c40696527|yuumei]] describes mechanical ventilation with heat recovery, including single-room options; colanderman [[./thread#^c40696500|colanderman]] recommends an energy recovery ventilator to exchange air while retaining heat and humidity. Neither supplies measured recovery efficiency.
- Filtration is discussed as a complementary intervention with a different purpose. gandalfgreybeer [[./thread#^c40696434|gandalfgreybeer]] explicitly separates an urban air purifier recommendation from CO2 removal; l1tany11 [[./thread#^c40700329|l1tany11]] suggests filtering ERV intake air for allergens and dust. giantg2 [[./thread#^c40697086|giantg2]] also values central-air particulate filtration while noting equipment limitations on filter ratings.

## Counterarguments

- tgv [[./thread#^c40696390|tgv]] initially argues that sleeping occupants produce little CO2, then asks whether another source could explain the high readings [[./thread#^c40706061|tgv]]. The direct replies from spiffytech [[./thread#^c40696745|spiffytech]] and GeoAtreides [[./thread#^c40707512|GeoAtreides]] report substantial overnight accumulation. These observations challenge the blanket reassurance, although neither account establishes a complete inventory of possible sources.
- guidedlight [[./thread#^c40696404|guidedlight]] invokes a typical bedroom ventilation rate of 0.35–1 air changes per hour, and epgui [[./thread#^c40696575|epgui]] says CO2 diffuses away quickly enough that concern is unnecessary. giantg2 [[./thread#^c40697169|giantg2]] questions assumptions about forced-air operating time, while hollerith [[./thread#^c40696772|hollerith]] emphasizes unusually tight rooms and windless nights. christina97 [[./thread#^c40696614|christina97]] challenges the dismissal but provides no readings in that reply.
- hrtdc [[./thread#^c40696609|hrtdc]] questions whether these airborne CO2 levels matter to health, citing physiological buffering and compensation. epgui [[./thread#^c40696556|epgui]] expresses similar skepticism based on biomedical training. Neither supplies exposure-response evidence establishing that the reported bedroom concentrations are harmless or harmful.
- The claim that suitable CO2-removal technology does not exist is challenged separately from its practicality. Against jonahbenton [[./thread#^c40696417|jonahbenton]], giantg2 [[./thread#^c40697124|giantg2]] cites submarine and space applications; hollerith [[./thread#^c40696806|hollerith]] argues that greater demand could eventually reduce costs. These establish the commenters' distinction between technical possibility and household affordability, not a demonstrated bedroom solution.
- Plant proposals receive substantial pushback. mousetree [[./thread#^c40696228|mousetree]] and ghthor [[./thread#^c40696560|ghthor]] suggest plants, while shikon7 [[./thread#^c40696540|shikon7]] adds a growing lamp. christina97 [[./thread#^c40696599|christina97]] argues that useful uptake would require greenhouse-like quantities; jonahbenton [[./thread#^c40696417|jonahbenton]] claims even enhanced biological systems fall far short of human emissions. ben\_w [[./thread#^c40696344|ben\_w]] highlights nighttime limitations, and hobs [[./thread#^c40696439|hobs]] describes a video showing large algae equipment rather than a firsthand bedroom test.
- Chemical removal introduces concerns beyond purchasing the sorbent. OutOfHere [[./thread#^c40696813|OutOfHere]] proposes particle filtration to contain soda-lime powder and temperature monitoring for reaction heat. OutOfHere [[./thread#^c40700353|OutOfHere]] also describes amine systems as unsuitable for home use and explicitly doubts a chatbot's suggested alternatives. None of these comments reports operating or validating such a home system.

## Assumptions

- Attributing next-day cognition specifically to CO2 requires an untested causal assumption. hollerith [[./thread#^c40697703|hollerith]] infers overnight concentrations from feeling mentally dull, without a CO2 meter; the same account involves heating, drafts, thermal discomfort, and previously interrupted sleep. Those factors were not separated experimentally.
- CO2 is sometimes treated as an indicator of broader ventilation problems. difosfor [[./thread#^c40696377|difosfor]] says other pollutants are likely elevated when CO2 is high; everdrive [[./thread#^c40696935|everdrive]] uses both CO2 and radon readings as indicators of air circulation. This does not establish a fixed relationship between CO2 and any particular particle or gaseous pollutant.
- The central-fan and open-door suggestions assume that adjacent rooms offer a useful dilution volume and adequate mixing. l1tany11 [[./thread#^c40700329|l1tany11]] makes the larger-house assumption explicit. everdrive [[./thread#^c40696313|everdrive]] cautions about air conditioning that recirculates air; the discussion does not establish that recirculation alone supplies outdoor air or reduces total household CO2.
- Exhaust ventilation assumes an adequate replacement-air path. colanderman [[./thread#^c40701024|colanderman]], replying to the proposed bedroom exhaust arrangement, warns that restricted inflow can draw air through walls and floors, potentially bringing odors and mold spores. No measurement of that contamination mechanism is supplied.
- A one-time scrubbing calculation is not an overnight operating budget. OutOfHere [[./thread#^c40697494|OutOfHere]] notes continuous emissions from occupants and the continuing need to ventilate for other reasons. Llamamoe [[./thread#^c40700087|Llamamoe]] adds that ventilation increases the scrubbing burden when the target is below outdoor CO2, and disputes the importance of oxygen depletion relative to elevated CO2. These are calculation premises and physiological claims, not measurements from a working scrubber.

## Conditions

- The original constraint matters: simonebrunozzi [[./thread#^c40696923|simonebrunozzi]], responding to dakiol's twice-daily window-opening routine [[./thread#^c40696454|dakiol]], clarifies that windows may be absent or unavailable at night. Consequently, many window recommendations only address situations outside that constraint; daytime airing is not demonstrated to prevent subsequent overnight accumulation.
- Outdoor conditions can reverse the appeal of opening a window. dsq [[./thread#^c40696216|dsq]] explicitly qualifies the recommendation when outdoor air is highly polluted. giantg2 [[./thread#^c40697086|giantg2]] adds climate, weather, neighborhood noise, crime, and construction as constraints. contrarian1234 [[./thread#^c40696522|contrarian1234]] claims open windows cause more dust than a leaky house, while acknowledging geographic variation; no dust measurements accompany that comparison.
- Thermal cost is disputed and context dependent. kkfx [[./thread#^c40696559|kkfx]] favors open windows when the outdoor climate permits and mechanical ventilation otherwise. vertnerd [[./thread#^c40696476|vertnerd]] expects higher heating and cooling costs, whereas everdrive [[./thread#^c40696935|everdrive]] reports little perceived heating penalty in a very well-insulated northeastern US home. The latter account does not include precise energy measurements.
- Reported ERV costs are not directly comparable quotations. joshdev [[./thread#^c40697100|joshdev]] reports approximately \$10,000 for an installed whole-house system; dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c40699710|dzhiurgis]] describes roughly \$1,000 and six hours of installation with a reasonably accessible attic. colanderman [[./thread#^c40696500|colanderman]] says window units are hard to find, while l1tany11 [[./thread#^c40700329|l1tany11]] points to 50-CFM equipment and emphasizes execution and layout, including California ventilation requirements that may not achieve the desired bedroom result.
- Soda-lime costs are speculative but illustrate the scale of consumable use. OutOfHere [[./thread#^c40696813|OutOfHere]] estimates up to \$100 daily. Llamamoe [[./thread#^c40697451|Llamamoe]], explicitly using possibly incorrect quickly found figures, assumes 100 g absorbs 10–20 liters of CO2 and a price of \$30/kg; reducing a 50 m³ room from 1,200 to approximately 421 ppm would then require 200–400 g. That calculation concerns an initial reduction, not continued occupancy or a verified daily bill.
- Evaporative cooling is proposed specifically for dry climates. therobots927 [[./thread#^c40696554|therobots927]] prefers it personally despite less cooling than air conditioning. Housing tenure also constrains modifications: therobots927 [[./thread#^c40705784|therobots927]] says renting deters trying the suggested UV modification. These comments provide no CO2 measurements establishing evaporative cooling as a bedroom CO2 remedy.

## Lived experiences

- spiffytech [[./thread#^c40696745|spiffytech]] reports overnight CO2 up to 2,600 ppm without active bedroom ventilation, versus approximately 500–750 ppm with different window fans and speeds. In the fuller account, spiffytech [[./thread#^c40696705|spiffytech]] reports roughly 700 ppm with an outward-blowing fan and lower readings at higher speed, but substantial noise, conditioned air escaping directly from a nearby HVAC outlet, insect-screen gaps, and muggy conditions when rain requires closing the window. Opening the window without a fan was comparatively ineffective; automated coordination with the AC was a plan, not a completed improvement.
- GeoAtreides [[./thread#^c40707512|GeoAtreides]], replying within the discussion questioning high overnight accumulation, reports 2,400–2,900 ppm using two named CO2 sensors, SCD30 and SCD41. A draft reportedly returns concentrations to background in about a minute. The account names the sensors but does not describe calibration, room volume, occupancy, or airflow sufficient to generalize that clearing time.
- everdrive [[./thread#^c40696935|everdrive]] reports winter CO2 around 1,200 ppm or higher without cracked windows, falling to approximately 600–800 with two windows slightly open. The home is unusually well insulated and located in the northeastern US. Radon buildup reportedly also decreases, but no radon values are given. The claim of little heating penalty rests on perceived heater operation, which the commenter explicitly says was not precisely measured.
- AifVI [[./thread#^c40696498|AifVI]] reports that switching on an upstairs bathroom exhaust fan lowers the CO2 reading in a basement utility room within hours. This demonstrates a reported effect away from the fan; the broader conclusion that CO2 falls throughout the house is inferred from that one described monitoring location.
- vertnerd [[./thread#^c40696476|vertnerd]] bought a monitor for teaching during COVID and was surprised by classroom readings well above 1,000 ppm, then by home readings above 1,000 ppm with a couple of people and a cat. The account links these observations to efforts to seal the house, but provides no sensor model, controlled comparison, or measured health outcome.
- giantg2 [[./thread#^c40697086|giantg2]] reports that continuous air-handler operation seems to keep a relatively newer, moderately sized house about 50–100 ppm above outdoor CO2. This is a favorable household account, with no supplied absolute readings, calibration procedure, or explanation of the home's outdoor-air exchange.
- Several accounts report qualitative improvements without numerical before-and-after values. anfractuosity [[./thread#^c40696376|anfractuosity]] saw a clear drop on a CO2 graph after opening a window; casenmgreen [[./thread#^c40696516|casenmgreen]] finds a gap of a few centimeters sufficient. weissi [[./thread#^c40696321|weissi]] reports success with an open bedroom door, and Grazester [[./thread#^c40696378|Grazester]] uses a fan bringing air from an adjoining room with an open window.
- hollerith [[./thread#^c40697703|hollerith]] describes decades sleeping in an unusually airtight room of about 45 square feet with an eight-foot ceiling and a door opening essentially outdoors. Without a meter, the commenter associates next-day mental dullness with presumed high overnight CO2 and believes a heater or fan improves exchange through a narrow doorway opening. An earlier routine involved waking every 105 minutes to air the room; it was abandoned because the sleep interruption became less tolerable. Warm weather and discomfort from opening the door limit this arrangement.
- joshdev [[./thread#^c40697100|joshdev]] reports a significant CO2 reduction after whole-house ERV installation, without numerical readings. colanderman [[./thread#^c40704872|colanderman]] reports that a small-company window-mounted unit worked very well in a bedroom. These are favorable firsthand accounts, but neither quantifies airflow, noise, energy consumption, or health effects.
- therobots927 [[./thread#^c40697307|therobots927]] reports no problems with an old evaporative cooler in a very dry climate and drains its reservoir after the season. The belief that long daily operation prevents growth is explicitly tentative. DougN7 [[./thread#^c40697368|DougN7]] similarly remembers no unpleasant smells from childhood use in a dry climate. Absence of remembered odor does not constitute microbial testing.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The thread does not resolve health or cognitive effects at the reported concentrations. hrtdc [[./thread#^c40696609|hrtdc]] questions the health premise, while hollerith [[./thread#^c40697703|hollerith]] reports subjective cognitive effects without measuring exposure. No controlled study results, objective sleep measurements, or demonstrated clinical threshold are supplied. Reduced CO2 readings and improved health therefore remain separate claims.
- The benefit of going below outdoor CO2 is disputed without testing. OutOfHere [[./thread#^c40697494|OutOfHere]] suspects levels below 300 ppm would maximize alertness and well-being; Llamamoe [[./thread#^c40700087|Llamamoe]] doubts a meaningful benefit over roughly 400 ppm. In a separate exchange, garrisonj [[./thread#^c40696382|garrisonj]] wants to experience preindustrial concentrations, and \_nalply [[./thread#^c40696450|\_nalply]] asserts there would be no perceptible difference. Neither position is supported here by a personal trial or study.
- Sensor selection and reliability remain unresolved. victor106 [[./thread#^c40696502|victor106]] asks which bedroom monitor is best, without receiving a direct recommendation in the supplied replies. GeoAtreides [[./thread#^c40707512|GeoAtreides]] names two sensors and reports high overnight values, but supplies no reference calibration or agreement statistics; spiffytech [[./thread#^c40696745|spiffytech]] does not identify the monitor. The accounts support reported patterns, not a validated ranking of instruments or precise uncertainty estimates.
- Participants disagree about how temperature, diffusion, and convection affect ventilation. tzs [[./thread#^c40697377|tzs]] argues that concentration differences dominate and temperature differences matter little; everdrive [[./thread#^c40697671|everdrive]] endorses that explanation. hollerith [[./thread#^c40697703|hollerith]] replies that convection is crucial with a narrow opening. guerrilla [[./thread#^c40696537|guerrilla]] reports a bathroom-humidity experiment in which a breeze helped greatly, but only speculates that CO2 behaves similarly. nequo's request for a measured temperature-difference threshold [[./thread#^c40696521|nequo]] remains unanswered.
- Optimal fan placement is not established. ratg13 [[./thread#^c40696580|ratg13]] recommends spacing a fan away from the window, invoking Bernoulli's principle; everdrive [[./thread#^c40697705|everdrive]] had instead sealed the fan tightly into the opening. ratg13 [[./thread#^c40697932|ratg13]] and user32489318 [[./thread#^c40699819|user32489318]] provide external demonstrations, not measurements reproduced in the thread. For doorway fans, sixthDot [[./thread#^c40696326|sixthDot]] favors outward flow, whereas pawelduda [[./thread#^c40696457|pawelduda]] makes direction conditional on height and whether cooling is also desired.
- The assertion that only whole-house ERVs can work is directly contradicted by a bedroom account: dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c40699710|dzhiurgis]] makes the broad claim, and colanderman [[./thread#^c40704872|colanderman]] replies that a window-mounted unit worked wonderfully. The exchange provides neither standardized performance comparisons nor enough installation detail to settle which small units are effective.
- Evaporative-cooler hygiene remains contested. slau [[./thread#^c40696754|slau]] warns about smells and microbial growth, while therobots927 [[./thread#^c40697307|therobots927]] and DougN7 [[./thread#^c40697368|DougN7]] report no such experience in dry climates. hagbard\_c [[./thread#^c40700812|hagbard\_c]] proposes periodic UV treatment; paulmd [[./thread#^c40698721|paulmd]] suggests bacteriostatic additives while retaining cleaning. therobots927 [[./thread#^c40698864|therobots927]] only says they will investigate additives. No follow-up demonstrates that either proposed treatment worked.
- UK ventilation requirements are disputed with new construction and replacement windows partly conflated. secondcoming [[./thread#^c40696441|secondcoming]] says new builds require trickle vents and later describes a recent flat containing them [[./thread#^c40698213|secondcoming]]. fartfeatures [[./thread#^c40716135|fartfeatures]] disputes a universal new-build requirement where MVHR is used. timrichard [[./thread#^c40699231|timrichard]] reports a window company's claim about mandatory replacement-window vents from June 2022; secondcoming [[./thread#^c40700121|secondcoming]] quotes guidance allowing an exception when useful ventilation is not reduced. These comments do not establish a verified, comprehensive account of the rules.
- This source unit cannot resolve the requested PM2.5, NO2, cooking, traffic, or wildfire comparisons: it contains no specific measurements or firsthand exposure episodes for those topics. dsq [[./thread#^c40696216|dsq]] supplies only the general warning that polluted outdoor air can undermine window ventilation, while gandalfgreybeer [[./thread#^c40696434|gandalfgreybeer]] recommends urban air purification without performance data. Whether a particular window-opening strategy lowers CO2 while worsening particle or NO2 exposure remains unmeasured here.
