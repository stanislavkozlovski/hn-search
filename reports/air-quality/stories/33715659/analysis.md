# [UV Devices Could Keep Indoor Air Free of Viruses](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=33715659)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion examines whether UV devices can reduce airborne viruses in homes and buildings, with attention to exposure risks to eyes and skin, ozone production, and possible chemical byproducts from indoor materials. Participants compare enclosed UV systems and HVAC installations with mechanical ventilation, HEPA and carbon filtration, and opening windows. The thread also covers airflow rates, device standards, costs, maintenance, CO2 buildup, and disagreements over the value of reducing pathogens indoors.
Tags: indoor-air-quality, UV-disinfection, airborne-viruses, ozone, ventilation, air-filtration, safety, CO2

## Relevance

Although focused on UV disinfection, the discussion also addresses indoor ventilation, CO2 and stuffiness, filtration, and concerns about ozone or chemical byproducts. It includes conflicting claims about cognitive effects at modest CO2 levels and firsthand observations about ventilation and window opening, with tradeoffs from cold weather, polluted outdoor air, and heating costs. ## Arguments

- slimsag [[./thread#^c33716113|slimsag]] recommends activated carbon for gases/VOCs and HEPA for particles. Gigachad [[./thread#^c33716654|Gigachad]] argues that engine exhaust and gas-stove combustion products justify filtration regardless of debates over microbial exposure. These are recommendations, without PM2.5 measurements, NO2-removal tests, or comparisons of cooking interventions.
- Ventilation is presented as complementary to filtration: beebeepka [[./thread#^c33716160|beebeepka]] recommends daily cross-ventilation, highwaylights [[./thread#^c33717321|highwaylights]] emphasizes moisture and mould concerns, and colechristensen [[./thread#^c33716071|colechristensen]] argues that enclosed spaces accumulate microbial exposure unlike outdoor environments. These comments address different reasons for exchanging indoor air.
- wincy [[./thread#^c33716764|wincy]] claims that elevated CO2 can cause headaches, brain fog and stuffiness, placing the onset at approximately 600 ppm. ajuc [[./thread#^c33716991|ajuc]] reports a correlation between room CO2 and remote chess performance, with some players subsequently opening windows and wearing warm clothing. Neither supplies study methods, underlying readings or evidence establishing causation.
- highwaylights [[./thread#^c33717321|highwaylights]] argues that room-level cleaning depends on airflow and repeated filtration, claiming that less restrictive, non-HEPA IKEA filters can achieve similar results at lower cost. This challenges judging a purifier solely by its filter classification; no comparative measurements accompany the claim.
- nicodds [[./thread#^c33716439|nicodds]] considers mechanical ventilation less risky and almost as effective as UV for reducing viral spread, and proposes combining it with enclosed UV. Animats [[./thread#^c33723289|Animats]] argues that upper-room UV could use ordinary convection, whereas an enclosed treatment box requires fans. Both are proposed engineering advantages, rather than demonstrated outcomes in this export.

## Counterarguments

- a1371 [[./thread#^c33715918|a1371]] argues that germicidal benefits do not establish overall air-quality benefits: reactions involving household offgassing, cooking emissions and ozone could create harmful secondary pollutants that laboratory conditions miss. yummypaint [[./thread#^c33716092|yummypaint]] similarly warns about ozone interacting with complex building materials. Neither provides household chemical measurements.
- colechristensen [[./thread#^c33716058|colechristensen]] stresses that ozone itself is a hazard. slimsag [[./thread#^c33716113|slimsag]] attributes respiratory harm and cardiovascular and premature-death associations to EPA conclusions. Responding to the hospital-use analogy, Fomite [[./thread#^c33716594|Fomite]] says such treatments generally involve intense concentrations in unoccupied rooms and does not recommend transferring those practices to homes.
- saurik [[./thread#^c33716317|saurik]] challenges slimsag's explanation that ventilation replaces depleted oxygen, arguing that CO2 would become problematic well before oxygen meaningfully declined. This is a correction to the proposed mechanism, not an objection to ventilation.
- sberder [[./thread#^c33716538|sberder]], citing conversations with facility managers, argues that common HVAC UV systems cannot treat rapidly moving air adequately at six air changes per hour. dtgriscom [[./thread#^c33717712|dtgriscom]] replies that complete sterilization on each pass is unnecessary: substantial repeated removal could lower steady-state viral concentrations. Neither quantifies the required removal fraction or delivered UV dose.
- blincoln [[./thread#^c33745284|blincoln]] worries about eye and skin injury from occupied-room UV and requests long-term far-UV evidence or fixtures preventing exposure. NikkiA [[./thread#^c33716823|NikkiA]] also raises eye-damage concerns. blincoln's examples involve other lamp configurations; they do not directly demonstrate harm from the proposed far-UVC systems.
- bradknowles [[./thread#^c33769462|bradknowles]] rejects broad concern about quality whole-home HEPA systems with UV, asserting that they avoid excessive ozone and mainly introduce cost and maintenance-exposure risks. mensetmanusman [[./thread#^c33719541|mensetmanusman]] offers a narrower explanation for problematic lamps: missing absorption below 200 nm could greatly increase ozone production. These replies make product construction relevant, but do not settle a1371's broader chemistry concern.
- ciconia [[./thread#^c33716622|ciconia]] and abdullahkhalids [[./thread#^c33716019|abdullahkhalids]] question excessive sterilization and loss of immune stimulation. Fomite [[./thread#^c33716597|Fomite]] responds that ordinary immune challenges remain abundant, while crazygringo [[./thread#^c33718951|crazygringo]] distinguishes gut microbes from dangerous airborne pathogens. notafraudster [[./thread#^c33717369|notafraudster]] invokes historical sanitation benefits against possible allergy costs. These are competing arguments, not health outcomes measured in these homes.

## Assumptions

- Advice to open windows assumes that outside air improves the relevant exposure. ls15 [[./thread#^c33716604|ls15]] explicitly challenges that premise where coal smoke is present. beebeepka [[./thread#^c33716228|beebeepka]] also emphasizes building configuration and wind, so the proposed benefit depends on both outdoor conditions and effective air exchange.
- clumsysmurf [[./thread#^c33716130|clumsysmurf]] asks whether chemical concerns also apply to 222-nm far-UVC and invokes research about sunlight reducing indoor microbes. raverbashing [[./thread#^c33716325|raverbashing]] suggests a tube plus carbon and HEPA filtration should suffice. The discussion does not establish that sunlight findings transfer to these wavelengths or that the proposed filters capture all treatment byproducts.
- Scoundreller [[./thread#^c33715956|Scoundreller]] wonders whether approximately 75°C heater output kills viruses during a split-second passage, explicitly recognizing exposure duration as uncertain. Similarly, enclosure proposals from unglaublich [[./thread#^c33716320|unglaublich]] and epicureanideal [[./thread#^c33716207|epicureanideal]] assume that air can receive effective treatment while radiation remains contained; no test results establish that balance.
- pifm\_guy [[./thread#^c33716691|pifm\_guy]] proposes fractional distillation, 23\% oxygen and zero CO2 for alertness and freshness without supporting evidence or a practical installation. knodi123's reply about spending millions monthly and switching to bottled gases [[./thread#^c33722446|knodi123]] appears satirical and should not be counted as a credible firsthand treatment or cost account.

## Conditions

- Cold-weather ventilation involves disputed comfort and heating costs. Auracle [[./thread#^c33716190|Auracle]] calls it unsuitable for most climates, while colechristensen [[./thread#^c33716261|colechristensen]] expresses reluctance at 25°F. crazygringo [[./thread#^c33719012|crazygringo]] predicts a fourfold electricity bill from periodically ventilating an entire home; this is an estimate, not a reported billing experiment. University-room experience may involve different heated space and responsibility for costs.
- Outdoor combustion can reverse the apparent benefit of window opening. ls15 [[./thread#^c33716604|ls15]] identifies coal smoke as a constraint, and bobthepanda [[./thread#^c33716756|bobthepanda]] reports wood-stove pollution in the Pacific Northwest. Gigachad [[./thread#^c33716654|Gigachad]] additionally raises proximity to coal facilities, but supplies no exposure measurements and explicitly expresses uncertainty about HEPA effectiveness for the dust discussed.
- slimsag [[./thread#^c33716113|slimsag]] quotes roughly \$150 for industrial greenhouse-style carbon filters and favors larger filters with more airflow. highwaylights [[./thread#^c33717321|highwaylights]] describes IKEA Fornuftig units as inexpensive but weak, and Starkvind units as stronger. These are commenters' price and performance judgments, without measured operating costs, replacement schedules or room-specific comparisons.
- Animats [[./thread#^c33715944|Animats]] identifies expensive far-UV lamps, counterfeit products and inadequate standards or inspection tools as deployment barriers. The proposed checks would assess germicidal wavelengths near the ceiling and unwanted exposure at eye level. bradknowles [[./thread#^c33769462|bradknowles]] also acknowledges added lighting cost and maintenance hazards.

## Lived experiences

- ac29 [[./thread#^c33733103|ac29]] reports that opening windows substantially reduced CO2 measured with what they call a good sensor, while barely changing indoor temperature despite much colder outdoor air. They describe a detached house and fan-assisted exchange of roughly 10,000 cubic feet. This is a firsthand measurement report, but no sensor model, ppm readings, temperature series or energy measurements are supplied; thermal mass is their explanation.
- Tade0 [[./thread#^c33716422|Tade0]] reports that their air purifier reacts strongly when a window is opened at an outdoor temperature of 4°C, in a reply about whether local heating is clean. This is a firsthand device-response observation, not an identified pollutant concentration or confirmed PM2.5 measurement.
- wincy [[./thread#^c33716764|wincy]] reports becoming drowsy during office meetings in rooms with poor airflow. They do not report contemporaneous CO2 measurements, headaches in that setting, or a controlled comparison, so their experience does not establish the claimed 600-ppm threshold.
- cfn [[./thread#^c33716293|cfn]] reports periodically ventilating a university room when outdoor temperatures were well below −20°C, with adequate heating. beebeepka [[./thread#^c33716351|beebeepka]] likewise says they ventilate at −20°C and below. These establish personal feasibility under particular circumstances, without establishing universal comfort or affordability.
- jansan [[./thread#^c33717637|jansan]] reports a distinct ozone-like smell after running a cheap eBay UV-C lamp for minutes. After several hours in a closed, unoccupied small room, the smell reportedly remained for a week. This is a sensory account, not an ozone measurement; it does not establish the chemical responsible for the lingering odour or its concentration.
- vigeek [[./thread#^c33716861|vigeek]] says their home installation works well and later describes enclosed UV in the HVAC return, running the fan when heating or cooling is inactive [[./thread#^c33716882|vigeek]]. No microbial counts, ozone readings, illness outcomes or other performance measurements accompany that assessment.
- highwaylights [[./thread#^c33717281|highwaylights]] reports that a purifier with an internal UV stage failed under warranty and was replaced without UV because of ozone concerns. solarist [[./thread#^c33716098|solarist]] similarly chose a filter-based purifier over an ozone generator after consulting EPA material. These are firsthand purchasing and equipment experiences, without demonstrated health changes.
- wesapien [[./thread#^c33716005|wesapien]] reports that an uncle uses home ozone generators and runs the industrial-strength unit while away. This is a relative's practice reported secondhand, followed by questions about byproducts; it supplies no exposure measurements, verified clearance time or health outcome.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Coverage is limited to story 33715659, part 0. Its closest exposure observations are ac29's qualitative CO2 measurement report [[./thread#^c33733103|ac29]] and Tade0's purifier response [[./thread#^c33716422|Tade0]]. The supplied comments contain no numerical PM2.5 or NO2 readings, no wildfire-smoke experience and no sleep-quality assessment. wincy's daytime drowsiness report [[./thread#^c33716764|wincy]] should not be treated as evidence about sleep.
- CO2 thresholds and cognitive causation remain unresolved. wincy [[./thread#^c33716764|wincy]] asserts effects around 600 ppm, while ajuc [[./thread#^c33716991|ajuc]] relays a performance correlation without methods or controls. ac29 [[./thread#^c33733103|ac29]] supplies no sensor specifications or calibration evidence. These accounts cannot establish measurement accuracy, a universal symptom threshold, or whether CO2 itself explains the reported effects. \_benedict's concern about future outdoor background concentrations [[./thread#^c33716978|\_benedict]] also supplies no supporting exposure data.
- The heating-cost dispute is not resolved by the available anecdotes. ac29's observed small temperature change [[./thread#^c33733103|ac29]] challenges the expectation of immediate severe cooling, but does not measure longer-term heating consumption. crazygringo's predicted fourfold bill [[./thread#^c33719012|crazygringo]] is likewise unmeasured. Different buildings, ventilation durations and heated areas prevent a direct comparison.
- Far-UVC safety remains disputed across wavelength, lamp quality, placement and household chemistry. a1371 [[./thread#^c33715918|a1371]] raises reaction products, bradknowles [[./thread#^c33769462|bradknowles]] trusts quality installations, and blincoln [[./thread#^c33745284|blincoln]] requests long-term exposure evidence. clumsysmurf's specific 222-nm question [[./thread#^c33716130|clumsysmurf]] is not conclusively answered by the supplied comments.
- Treatment effectiveness is not established comparatively. sberder [[./thread#^c33716538|sberder]] and dtgriscom [[./thread#^c33717712|dtgriscom]] disagree about the practical significance of short UV exposure per pass, while nicodds [[./thread#^c33716439|nicodds]] offers an unmeasured ventilation-versus-UV comparison. highwaylights [[./thread#^c33717281|highwaylights]] additionally relays a speculative concern that apparent particle reductions could reflect temporary surface deposition; no measurements verify that mechanism.
- Several material and maintenance questions remain unanswered: Khelavaster [[./thread#^c33718698|Khelavaster]] asks about volatile emissions from bacterial breakdown in activated carbon, and agumonkey [[./thread#^c33725643|agumonkey]] asks about regenerating carbon by pyrolysis. DoingIsLearning [[./thread#^c33716446|DoingIsLearning]] asks whether decor emissions involve flame retardants; nerdawson [[./thread#^c33717186|nerdawson]] suggests particleboard, followed by a question about its glues from DoingIsLearning [[./thread#^c33717244|DoingIsLearning]]. None supplies chemical identification, emission rates or safe reuse evidence.
