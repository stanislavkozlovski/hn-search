# [Gas stoves are more hazardous than we’ve been led to believe (2020)](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=31630946)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants debate claims that gas stoves pose substantial health risks, questioning the strength and scope of the evidence and whether cooking emissions, rather than the fuel alone, explain some exposure. The discussion covers ventilation, recirculating versus externally vented hoods, filters, building codes, and the challenges renters face. Gas and induction cooking are compared for heat control, cooking practices, electrical requirements, and outage resilience. A related exchange considers the efficiency of electric kettles and other appliances, power generation losses, and tradeoffs in household electrification.
Tags: gas stoves, indoor air, cooking emissions, ventilation, induction, energy efficiency

## Relevance

Comments cover indoor air impacts of gas stoves and cooking, CO2 and PM2.5 readings, ventilation and filtration, and firsthand monitoring of headaches, sleep, and pollutant spikes. They preserve disagreement about risk, the role of cooking itself, and whether opening windows is appropriate when outdoor air is polluted. ## Arguments

- tootie [[./thread#^c31632019|tootie]] argues for reducing pollution at its source: induction removes the need to burn gas indoors and reduces dependence on occupants remembering mitigation that may be unavailable. Their description of induction as 'foolproof' concerns the gas-burning component; the same comment acknowledges pollution from high-temperature cooking.
- Changing fuel does not eliminate cooking emissions. zeroonetwothree [[./thread#^c31631610|zeroonetwothree]] attributes substantial particulate production to heating food generally, while poutine [[./thread#^c31641262|poutine]] reports particle spikes during cooking with a portable induction plate. tootie [[./thread#^c31632019|tootie]] therefore advocates strong extraction or outdoor cooking for high heat and deep frying.
- A fan's exhaust destination matters. maccard [[./thread#^c31632294|maccard]] says recirculating units with maintained filters can reduce smells but still require opening a window; stefan\_ [[./thread#^c31631731|stefan\_]] describes units that simply return air through a coarse filter. These comments distinguish odor management and recirculation from exchanging indoor air.
- poutine [[./thread#^c31631982|poutine]] describes ventilation as necessary in a tightly sealed home and says a hood helps without eliminating elevated CO2. In a follow-up, poutine [[./thread#^c31641235|poutine]] argues that typical low-flow fresh-air systems cannot adequately handle short cooking-related spikes. Their proposed improvements combine induction, a larger hood, and greater ventilation capacity.
- Filtration offers another response when outdoor air is polluted. Replying to julianeon's claim that preventing indoor emissions is the only solution, kurthr [[./thread#^c31633010|kurthr]] recommends a box-fan filter assembly and reports successful use during wildfires. This supports considering filtration alongside source reduction, although the comment supplies no measured removal rate.
- Maintenance affects the practicality of extraction and recirculation. tpxl [[./thread#^c31632235|tpxl]] suggests wiping units and replacing filters approximately every six months; cdtwigg [[./thread#^c31632293|cdtwigg]] emphasizes removing accumulated oil to prevent a fire hazard and mentions dishwasher-safe filters. These are maintenance recommendations, not measurements of pollutant removal.
- maccard [[./thread#^c31632420|maccard]], responding to was\_a\_dev's question about extractors and CO monitors [[./thread#^c31631919|was\_a\_dev]], distinguishes a carbon-monoxide alarm from an air-quality monitor. The presence of a CO alarm does not answer this discussion's questions about CO2, particles, or other cooking pollutants.

## Counterarguments

- zeroonetwothree [[./thread#^c31631610|zeroonetwothree]] considers the gas-stove threat overstated, citing their own review of unspecified data and arguing that ordinary ventilation makes risk very low. taeric [[./thread#^c31632227|taeric]] similarly allows that the headline may be technically true while exaggerating readers' perceived danger. Neither provides quantitative risk estimates in the supplied comments.
- The proposed precautions are not universally available or beneficial. Responding to a claim that developing countries usually have chimneys, 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c31633202|5e92cb50239222b]] describes severe pollution from neighboring combustion sources. julianeon [[./thread#^c31631772|julianeon]] says opening windows would introduce pollution during substantial portions of the year, while renter i80and [[./thread#^c31631692|i80and]] lacks control over whether the installed fan actually exhausts outdoors.
- ineedasername [[./thread#^c31631798|ineedasername]] questions whether gas cooking itself explains reported asthma associations, proposing that urban density and proximity to industrial pollution could confound them. This is a plausible alternative explanation raised by a commenter, not a demonstrated correction to a supplied study.
- Claims that particular foods require a gas stove receive concrete challenges. Against riddleronroof's assertion that rotis and charred eggplant require fire [[./thread#^c31631922|riddleronroof]], deepdmistry [[./thread#^c31632306|deepdmistry]] reports years of making rotis on induction, and tootie [[./thread#^c31632493|tootie]] reports charring food with an electric broiler. oneoff786 [[./thread#^c31632807|oneoff786]] also distinguishes outdoor barbecue equipment from the indoor-exposure question.
- Outage resilience creates a competing practical concern, but the heating discussion contains an important distinction. hoppyhoppy2 [[./thread#^c31631657|hoppyhoppy2]] proposes repurposing a cooking stove as emergency heat; melolife [[./thread#^c31631829|melolife]] directly objects because of ventilation requirements and water-vapor production. Accounts of useful gas furnaces or fireplaces do not establish that prolonged stove use for room heating is acceptable.

## Assumptions

- The low-risk argument assumes effective, usable mitigation. zeroonetwothree [[./thread#^c31631610|zeroonetwothree]] recommends a window or vent fan, but i80and [[./thread#^c31631692|i80and]] describes a rental without effective outdoor exhaust, and julianeon [[./thread#^c31631772|julianeon]] describes polluted outdoor air. The argument cannot be transferred unchanged across those circumstances.
- riddleronroof [[./thread#^c31631922|riddleronroof]] attributes certain emissions to pan coatings and invokes the long history of cooking with fire as reassurance. The comment supplies neither chemical measurements nor comparative health evidence establishing that pans explain the gas-related concern or that longstanding use demonstrates safety.
- VirusNewbie [[./thread#^c31632641|VirusNewbie]] assumes a large great room with 11-foot ceilings and a running fan should experience fewer problems than a tiny kitchen with an old stove. Havoc [[./thread#^c31634237|Havoc]] challenges the sufficiency of indoor space without outside ventilation. Neither supplies measurements comparing room volume, air exchange, cooking duration, or exposure.
- Two broader risk comparisons remain rhetorical. xnx [[./thread#^c31631580|xnx]] predicts that concern about VOCs, PM2.5, and high CO2 will become comparable to concern about smoking. norvvryo [[./thread#^c31633637|norvvryo]] guesses that chronic stove-related risk is orders of magnitude below commuting by car. Neither comparison is supported here by exposure measurements or comparative health data.

## Conditions

- Outdoor pollution changes the window-opening calculation. julianeon [[./thread#^c31631772|julianeon]] reports bad air in their part of California for roughly one tenth to one quarter of the year. In an unnamed developing country, 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c31633202|5e92cb50239222b]] reports outdoor pollution around 300 µg/m³ amid burning of coal, wood, plastic, rubber, and bitumen. The latter figure has no specified pollutant fraction or measurement method and should not be relabeled as PM2.5.
- Climate and seasonal comfort constrain ventilation. i80and [[./thread#^c31631692|i80and]] says opening a window substantially is impractical for much of the year. In an apartment-heating discussion, dontbenebby [[./thread#^c31631696|dontbenebby]] contrasts the difficulty of opening windows in Pittsburgh with the Bay Area. Neither account quantifies the resulting exposure or heating penalty.
- Control over housing and spending affects available improvements. poutine [[./thread#^c31631982|poutine]] can specify induction and larger ventilation systems in a new house, whereas renter i80and [[./thread#^c31631692|i80and]] cannot choose the existing exhaust arrangement. kurthr [[./thread#^c31633010|kurthr]] offers a much cheaper intervention with an approximately \$40 box-fan filter estimate; that is the commenter's tentative price, not a verified cost or performance comparison.
- Electrical capacity can complicate alternatives to gas. 7speter [[./thread#^c31631742|7speter]] raises upgrade costs; fomine3 [[./thread#^c31637128|fomine3]] gives older Japanese apartments with 30A/100V service and a 12A kettle as an example where simultaneous loads can trip the breaker. JshWright [[./thread#^c31686730|JshWright]] clarifies that their earlier faulty-wiring concern excluded other loads. tootie [[./thread#^c31631974|tootie]] suggests a slower, lower-power kettle, which addresses that appliance rather than establishing feasibility for a full induction range.
- Gas availability and housing patterns vary geographically. ineedasername [[./thread#^c31631798|ineedasername]] reports gas predominating within approximately 30 miles of their suburban location and more electric appliances farther away. xtracto [[./thread#^c31633648|xtracto]] says gas stoves are widespread in Mexico. These local claims caution against generalizing ajross's assertion that older urban housing tends to be electric and gas cooktops are high-end [[./thread#^c31631808|ajross]]; none supplies population-level housing data.
- Electricity reliability affects willingness to change appliances. backtoyoujim [[./thread#^c31631889|backtoyoujim]] reports roughly a dozen outages a year and wants substantial battery backup before abandoning gas. philips [[./thread#^c31631628|philips]] notes that many North American gas furnaces still need electric blowers, and Mountain\_Skies [[./thread#^c31631646|Mountain\_Skies]] notes that some gas appliances require electricity. Resilience therefore depends on the particular appliance and backup arrangement.

## Lived experiences

- poutine [[./thread#^c31631982|poutine]] reports years of monitoring several locations in a tightly sealed home shared with a spouse; both have allergies and poor sleep. They associate CO2 above 1000 ppm with headaches and poor sleep and work to keep occupied bedrooms below approximately 700 ppm. They describe substantial gas-cooktop effects, partial help from the hood, reliance on an HRV, and occasional window opening. These are household observations and personal targets, not established clinical thresholds.
- Asked for a nongas comparison, poutine [[./thread#^c31641262|poutine]] says they use a portable induction plate for dishes such as hot pot, explicitly acknowledges lacking an apples-to-apples comparison, and says cooking spikes 'pm2' without influencing CO2. poutine [[./thread#^c31631982|poutine]] plans induction and larger ventilation equipment in the next house; the comments do not report outcomes from that completed installation.
- Several commenters report gas stoves with recirculating exhaust: i80and [[./thread#^c31632068|i80and]] confirms this in their rental; emacsen [[./thread#^c31632326|emacsen]] describes it in a relatively recent NYC building, believed to date from 2008; and tssva [[./thread#^c31634310|tssva]] reports it in two recently built houses. dmtroyer [[./thread#^c31631994|dmtroyer]] describes an unvented 1970s stove with multiple pilot lights that remains warm. These accounts span different building ages but do not establish national prevalence.
- rascul [[./thread#^c31631704|rascul]], who later identifies working on houses for a living [[./thread#^c31637333|rascul]], reports seeing exhaust routed into attics and identifies moisture damage as a downside. They say mobile homes usually vent outside in their experience and that recirculating units are less common locally, often appearing in older houses or poor renovations. Their local pattern differs from other commenters' reports.
- asveikau [[./thread#^c31632195|asveikau]] describes repeatedly encountering old, dirty fans that appear ineffective and doubts that occupants maintain them. mastax [[./thread#^c31632838|mastax]], replying about recirculating microwave vents, describes sticky oil accumulating on cabinets. These are observations about maintenance and deposited grease, not measurements of airborne PM2.5 or gaseous pollutants.
- kurthr [[./thread#^c31633010|kurthr]] reports using a box-fan filter assembly during severe wildfires with 'excellent effect.' No before-and-after concentrations, sensor details, symptom changes, or distinction between smoke odor and particle reduction accompany that assessment.
- achenatx [[./thread#^c31631701|achenatx]] reports that natural gas remained available during about five days without electricity in the Texas freeze, allowing cooking and operation of a gas furnace with a small generator powering its blower. Separately, chomp [[./thread#^c31631790|chomp]] says a family of four stayed relatively warm around a gas fireplace. These are firsthand comfort and resilience accounts; neither supplies indoor-air measurements.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The magnitude and causes of health risk remain unsettled within this source. zeroonetwothree [[./thread#^c31631610|zeroonetwothree]] asserts low risk, poutine [[./thread#^c31631982|poutine]] reports headaches and poor sleep associated with higher CO2, and ineedasername [[./thread#^c31631798|ineedasername]] raises confounding in asthma attribution. No study methods, adjusted effect sizes, controlled sleep assessment, cognitive testing, or measured asthma risk are supplied. The accounts do not establish whether CO2 itself caused the reported symptoms.
- Pollutant-specific coverage is limited. xnx [[./thread#^c31631580|xnx]] explicitly mentions PM2.5 but supplies no measurements; poutine [[./thread#^c31641262|poutine]] reports unquantified 'pm2' spikes; and 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c31633202|5e92cb50239222b]] supplies an outdoor mass concentration without identifying its particle fraction. No supplied comment reports NO2 measurements or NO2-specific effects. Traffic exposure appears only as norvvryo's speculative commuting comparison [[./thread#^c31633637|norvvryo]], without roadside or in-car measurements.
- Sensor reliability and exposure comparability cannot be assessed. poutine [[./thread#^c31631982|poutine]] provides CO2 values without instrument models, calibration information, raw readings, or detailed measurement protocols. Glench [[./thread#^c31634694|Glench]] links to another person's kitchen measurements said to rival nearby wildfires, but reproduces no numerical results or methods. That is a secondhand measurement claim, insufficient here to compare pollutants, peaks, durations, or cumulative exposure.
- The discussion does not demonstrate a solution that simultaneously maintains low bedroom CO2 and excludes polluted outdoor air. poutine [[./thread#^c31631982|poutine]] sometimes needs windows despite mechanical ventilation; julianeon [[./thread#^c31631772|julianeon]] needs to avoid importing outdoor pollution; kurthr [[./thread#^c31633010|kurthr]] reports successful wildfire filtration without gas measurements. The filter account does not establish CO2 or NO2 removal, and the comments provide no measured comparison of these competing ventilation needs.
- The legality and prevalence of ductless gas-stove installations remain disputed. cplusplusfellow [[./thread#^c31631788|cplusplusfellow]] claims they would violate code in almost every US city; i80and [[./thread#^c31632068|i80and]] suspects grandfathering. tssva [[./thread#^c31634461|tssva]] reports finding a ductless-hood exception in section M1503.1 of the 2018 International Mechanical Code, with state differences. In B.C., construction worker 2ICofafireteam [[./thread#^c31632916|2ICofafireteam]] says their microwave-vent installations must be properly vented to obtain occupancy. These reports do not establish the rules applicable to every dwelling discussed.
- The microwave-vent disagreement becomes partly a disagreement about which hardware is being described. ChoGGi [[./thread#^c31653465|ChoGGi]] describes ducting through cabinets and says the units are designed for exterior exhaust. Spivak [[./thread#^c31716796|Spivak]] eventually clarifies that they mean a different arrangement whose front outlet blows upward outside the cabinet doors. The exchange does not justify treating every microwave hood as either inherently unventable or already effective, and supplies no capture-efficiency measurements.
- Cooking-performance preferences remain contested. alar44 [[./thread#^c31637289|alar44]] favors steady gas heat and criticizes electric cycling and induction overheating; legerdemain [[./thread#^c31634069|legerdemain]] questions whether proposed electric gadgets suit stir-fries and omelets. deepdmistry's induction-roti experience [[./thread#^c31632306|deepdmistry]] establishes that at least some claimed flame requirements are avoidable, while morsch [[./thread#^c31632521|morsch]] accepts oven-broiled eggplant but expects flavor differences from flame charring. These accounts support task-specific tradeoffs rather than universal claims about cooks' preferences.
