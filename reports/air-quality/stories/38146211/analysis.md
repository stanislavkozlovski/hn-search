# [Delhi air pollution spikes to 100 times WHO health limit](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=38146211)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion responds to severe Delhi air pollution with accounts of its effects on daily life and debate about agricultural stubble burning as a source. Participants compare AQI scales and question how claims such as multiples of health limits are calculated, while others criticize costly smog towers. The thread explores alternatives to burning crop residue, including no-till practices and their tradeoffs, and discusses why people and companies remain in the region.
Tags: Delhi, air pollution, crop burning, AQI, agriculture

## Relevance

The discussion includes firsthand reports of burning eyes, breathing difficulty, coughing, and aftertaste during severe Delhi smog, plus accounts of moving away due to exposure. Replies discuss seasonal burning and question how the reported AQI compares with health thresholds. ## Arguments

- The accounts support treating outdoor pollution as a substantial health and comfort burden: ghoomketu [[./thread#^c38149515|ghoomketu]] reports eye irritation, thesaintlives [[./thread#^c38150162|thesaintlives]] describes coughing and restrictions on running, and parakalan [[./thread#^c38153188|parakalan]] describes a difficult visit despite ordinarily having no respiratory problems. These are symptom reports without clinical or pollutant-specific verification.
- 8bitsrule [[./thread#^c38147390|8bitsrule]] attributes the pollution to recurring agricultural burning. In the ensuing crop-residue discussion, whalesalad [[./thread#^c38151932|whalesalad]] describes tilling and a growing no-till movement in the US, while pohuing [[./thread#^c38150390|pohuing]] reports plowing residues under in Germany. These comments suggest alternatives to burning but provide no evidence of their feasibility or exposure benefits around Delhi.
- manishsharan [[./thread#^c38152708|manishsharan]], replying to the agricultural-burning attribution, proposes labeling rice by province so Delhi consumers could boycott Punjab rice. This is a proposed economic route to reducing pollution, with no implementation or effectiveness evidence.
- Relocation emerges as a practical response for some individuals: ghoomketu [[./thread#^c38149515|ghoomketu]] reports being much happier elsewhere in India, and piku [[./thread#^c38156783|piku]] says they left Delhi for Manali after roughly five years. Neither supplies measurements comparing exposure before and after moving.

## Counterarguments

- Tade0 [[./thread#^c38151699|Tade0]] rejects ghoomketu’s [[./thread#^c38149515|ghoomketu]] suggestion that rapidly developing eye irritation might have been psychological, citing the noticeable taste of winter smog in their own region. This offers a corroborating sensory account, but cannot establish the cause of another person’s symptoms.
- Pigalowda [[./thread#^c38151215|Pigalowda]] questions the usefulness of large outdoor cleaning projects, quoting a description of two 80-foot smog towers costing more than \$2 million each and reportedly judged largely ineffective by scientists. Their additional skepticism about tower size is opinion; the supplied comment contains no study methods or measured exposure reductions.
- MisterSandman [[./thread#^c38158368|MisterSandman]] directly challenges ganeshkrishnan’s [[./thread#^c38155492|ganeshkrishnan]] response that tech companies are in Gurgaon rather than Delhi: they report Gurgaon having essentially the same AQI, sometimes worse. The suburban location therefore does not establish cleaner air, although no dated readings accompany this rebuttal.

## Assumptions

- Pollutant identity remains speculative. thesaintlives [[./thread#^c38150162|thesaintlives]] guesses that “nitrous compounds” caused coughing; this does not establish NO2 exposure. Tade0’s [[./thread#^c38151699|Tade0]] description of “PMs of every size” likewise provides no PM2.5 concentration or particle-size measurement.
- The rice boycott proposed by manishsharan [[./thread#^c38152708|manishsharan]] assumes usable provenance labels, sufficient purchasing influence, and changes in farming practices in response to consumer pressure. The assertion that this would make the problem easy to solve is unsupported by results in the discussion.
- RetroTechie [[./thread#^c38181143|RetroTechie]], answering questions about why farmers do not simply till residues under, explicitly guesses that burning is an easy, quick way to clear fields. Their explanations involving machinery access, soil disruption and remaining crop material are proposed mechanisms, not firsthand evidence about the farmers responsible for Delhi’s pollution.

## Conditions

- The ability to escape exposure depends on employment and income. ghoomketu [[./thread#^c38149515|ghoomketu]] credits having a job that does not require a fixed location; piku [[./thread#^c38156783|piku]] reports earning about one-fifth of their Delhi income after moving to Manali. These accounts show markedly different financial costs of relocation.
- Agricultural alternatives have practical constraints. RetroTechie [[./thread#^c38181143|RetroTechie]] says tilling requires work and potentially unavailable or unaffordable machinery, while no-till leaves residues that can obstruct planting or regrow. They also argue that tilling damages beneficial soil structure, complicating the choice among alternatives to burning.
- Timing and geography limit comparisons. parakalan [[./thread#^c38153188|parakalan]] describes difficulty during a February visit, which they identify as outside peak pollution; ghoomketu [[./thread#^c38149515|ghoomketu]] anticipates worsening around Diwali; Tade0 [[./thread#^c38151699|Tade0]] describes unusually clean conditions after polluted winters in an unnamed region. These are different settings and periods, without comparable measurements.
- ganeshkrishnan [[./thread#^c38155492|ganeshkrishnan]] offers a business-history explanation for company concentration in Gurgaon, attributing it to permission for women to work night shifts with employer-provided safety arrangements and associated call-center growth. This is a claimed reason for employment geography, not evidence that the location offers lower exposure.

## Lived experiences

- ghoomketu [[./thread#^c38149515|ghoomketu]] describes living in Delhi as a nightmare, with constantly watery eyes and burning eyes within hours of arriving on a later visit. Their friends’ breathing difficulties are reported secondhand. They also recall fire brigades spraying trees and describe greater happiness after moving to an unspecified part of India.
- piku [[./thread#^c38156783|piku]] reports living in Delhi for about five years before deciding the conditions were intolerable and moving to Manali, accepting substantially lower pay. They do not describe a particular diagnosis or measured improvement.
- thesaintlives [[./thread#^c38150162|thesaintlives]] recalls parts of Delhi where going outside brought persistent coughing and describes approximately 4 a.m. as the only workable time to run. Early runs introduced another problem—packs of dogs—illustrating a practical tradeoff without establishing that the air was measurably safer then.
- parakalan [[./thread#^c38153188|parakalan]] reports having no usual breathing difficulties, sinusitis or allergies, yet finding a February visit very difficult. The comment establishes their perceived contrast with ordinary health, but supplies neither specific exposure readings nor a medical assessment.
- Tade0 [[./thread#^c38151699|Tade0]] contrasts an unusually clean year with previous winters in their region, describing heavy smog as leaving an actual aftertaste. This is a sensory observation without instrument readings or an identified location.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The headline’s numerical comparison remains unresolved. wodenokoto [[./thread#^c38151939|wodenokoto]] quotes a reported AQI of 500 and disputes “100 times” by comparing it with the upper “Good” index value of 50, yielding ten. wodenokoto [[./thread#^c38158717|wodenokoto]] subsequently adds higher app categories, including “hazardous.” These index comparisons do not supply the pollutant concentration, WHO reference value or averaging period needed to substantiate either claimed multiple of a health limit.
- Measurement quality cannot be assessed. wodenokoto [[./thread#^c38151939|wodenokoto]] supplies quoted reporting, and wodenokoto [[./thread#^c38158717|wodenokoto]] supplies app category descriptions, rather than personal sensor measurements. Together with the speculative pollutant attribution from thesaintlives [[./thread#^c38150162|thesaintlives]], this leaves PM2.5, NO2 and CO2 concentrations, sensor calibration, and indoor-versus-outdoor exposure unestablished.
- The health evidence is limited to reported symptoms and comfort. ghoomketu [[./thread#^c38149515|ghoomketu]] questions their own interpretation, while Tade0 [[./thread#^c38151699|Tade0]] disputes that doubt through a separate anecdote. Their exchange does not resolve causality, and this source unit supplies no cognition or sleep observations, clinical testing, or quantified long-term outcomes.
- The practical evidence does not resolve household ventilation choices. ghoomketu [[./thread#^c38149515|ghoomketu]] discusses relocation and municipal spraying, while Pigalowda [[./thread#^c38151215|Pigalowda]] discusses spraying and outdoor towers; neither supplies window-opening, cooking, home-filtration or ventilation comparisons. Whether opening windows improves or worsens a particular indoor exposure remains unanswered. The burning attribution from 8bitsrule [[./thread#^c38147390|8bitsrule]] concerns agriculture; traffic-specific and wildfire-smoke experiences are not supplied.
