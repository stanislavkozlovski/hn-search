# [Smallest particulate matter air quality sensor for ultra-compact IoT devices](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44698733)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion centers on how Bosch’s miniature fanless particle sensor estimates particulate concentration, including its optical sensing method, airflow variability, cleanliness, humidity and fog effects, accuracy, and integration needs. Participants weigh claims of technical novelty against marketing, and consider tradeoffs in size, power, cost, clear field of view, and potential uses in wearables, phones, bikes, and home monitoring. The thread also discusses sensor comparisons and maintenance, and why people collect indoor air data, from personal awareness to alerts and ventilation or filtration control.
Tags: air-quality sensors, particulate matter, sensor accuracy, miniaturization, indoor air quality, ventilation, wearables

## Relevance

Discussion focuses on a compact PM2.5 sensor, how it measures particles without a fan, uncertainty about accuracy and airflow sensitivity, and humidity and fog effects. It also includes firsthand experience comparing sensors and using readings to guide ventilation and filtration. ## Arguments

- Monitoring can guide useful manual actions without HVAC automation. pppone checks PM when cooking and before running [[./thread#^c44702099|pppone]]; shawabawa3 uses CO2 alerts to prompt opening windows [[./thread#^c44701570|shawabawa3]]. These accounts answer the claim that collecting residential air-quality data necessarily produces no action, although they do not establish health benefits.
- Ventilation and filtration can be combined. Implicated describes a CO2-controlled fresh-air intake passing through a 5-inch MERV 14 filter, intended to limit unnecessary admission of summer humidity and winter cold [[./thread#^c44702390|Implicated]]. This is an example of controlling ventilation demand while filtering incoming air, rather than evidence that particle filtration itself lowers CO2.
- Cooking ventilation depends on replacement airflow. macNchz reports that cracking a window for makeup air made the range hood much more effective [[./thread#^c44701813|macNchz]]. This supports a specific reason to open a window during cooking; the account supplies no pollutant concentrations or quantified improvement.
- The fanless measurement principle has a plausible technical explanation. dist-epoch quotes manufacturer specifications describing a laser focal region approximately 5 mm above the lens and calculations using particle counts, relative velocity, and sampled air volume [[./thread#^c44699492|dist-epoch]]. hakonjdjohnsen interprets this as estimating airflow optically instead of imposing a known flow with a fan [[./thread#^c44701092|hakonjdjohnsen]]. These are specifications and interpretation, not independent validation.
- Miniaturization could enable more convenient personal monitoring. ahaucnx identifies size and lower energy consumption as advantages [[./thread#^c44700047|ahaucnx]]. arnejenssen suggests phones or watches [[./thread#^c44699198|arnejenssen]], and dzhiurgis suggests laptops [[./thread#^c44704536|dzhiurgis]]. shagie reports parental interest in monitoring changing conditions while riding e-bikes and quotes claims of silent operation for the PurpleAir PIXEL [[./thread#^c44701194|shagie]]. These are proposed uses and product claims, not demonstrated exposure reductions.

## Counterarguments

- quickthrowman questions monitoring that produces no response, arguing that cooking, candles, hairdryers, and smoke predictably affect readings and that airflow correction can matter more than measurement [[./thread#^c44701192|quickthrowman]]. The reply ancestry materially limits this criticism: Implicated supplies an actual automated installation [[./thread#^c44702390|Implicated]], which quickthrowman subsequently welcomes [[./thread#^c44727366|quickthrowman]]. The exchange does not sustain a claim that residential users generally do nothing with their readings.
- Opening windows or increasing outdoor-air intake is not an unconditional improvement. quickthrowman identifies outdoor wildfire smoke entering through an HVAC intake as a source of elevated readings [[./thread#^c44701192|quickthrowman]], while macNchz reports using a DIY purifier during wildfire smoke [[./thread#^c44701813|macNchz]]. The thread therefore contains reasons both to ventilate and to filter, but no measured window-opening comparison during smoke.
- Smaller sensors can impose application and accuracy constraints. ahaucnx says the Bosch sensor needs a clear field of view, potentially restricting wearable use, and cites experience with environmental compensation requirements in the miniature SCD4x CO2 sensor [[./thread#^c44700047|ahaucnx]]. The CO2 example is an analogy supporting caution; it is not a test showing that the Bosch PM sensor is inaccurate.
- Humidity and fog can undermine interpretation of optical PM readings. Catbert59 says fog is detected as particles and distorts results [[./thread#^c44699861|Catbert59]]. pppone distinguishes correcting high-humidity trends from handling condensation, which they say requires drying or ignoring the data [[./thread#^c44699583|pppone]]. Consequently, an elevated reading in these conditions need not straightforwardly represent the pollution exposure the user wants to assess.
- The smallest sensing element is not necessarily the smallest finished monitor. progbits objects to enclosure and thermal-integration requirements [[./thread#^c44699778|progbits]]. joshvm corrects the claim that a separate lens is required: the lens is integrated, with a protective optical window recommended, and a SparkFun module already provides thermal provisions [[./thread#^c44700104|joshvm]]. progbits accepts that correction [[./thread#^c44700304|progbits]]. Packaging remains a consideration, but the withdrawn lens objection should not be repeated as fact.
- Phone integration may not yield convenient instantaneous readings. progbits argues that useful measurements require averaging over time, potentially requiring the user to hold a phone out for minutes [[./thread#^c44699805|progbits]]. mjs raises waterproofing concerns [[./thread#^c44699360|mjs]], while progbits proposes camera-assembly integration as a solution [[./thread#^c44699805|progbits]]. Neither provides a tested phone implementation or measured sampling-time requirement.

## Assumptions

- Fanless concentration estimates depend on obtaining a useful estimate of sampled air volume. hakonjdjohnsen explicitly assumes some natural air movement [[./thread#^c44701092|hakonjdjohnsen]]. ethan\_smith attributes fanless sensing generally to thermal convection [[./thread#^c44700562|ethan\_smith]], and TimByte proposes a similar explanation [[./thread#^c44699908|TimByte]]; f1shy counters that heat output is too small for meaningful convection [[./thread#^c44700190|f1shy]]. The thread does not establish which explanation applies to this device.
- Both strong confidence and strong dismissal outrun the supplied tests. felixg3 treats Bosch's reputation as assurance of substantial innovation and clinical readiness [[./thread#^c44724125|felixg3]]; ImaCake calls the device marketing without substance [[./thread#^c44699707|ImaCake]], and f1shy makes similar accusations [[./thread#^c44700196|f1shy]] [[./thread#^c44700187|f1shy]]. rurban says fan-based alternatives are better while explicitly saying they did not use this sensor [[./thread#^c44703168|rurban]]. These are judgments, not comparative measurements.
- svilches identifies themselves as an insider and says the device uses novel hardware, while requesting release of more technical information [[./thread#^c44709300|svilches]]. That testimony challenges a software-only interpretation but does not establish accuracy. Likewise, TimByte's description of the device as working reliably appears without a supporting test account [[./thread#^c44699893|TimByte]].
- Long-term optical cleanliness is an unverified operating assumption. trailbits wonders whether inverted mounting would help and notes maintenance requirements in other fanless designs [[./thread#^c44699470|trailbits]]. dist-epoch infers from the optical cover that highly contaminated environments may be unsuitable [[./thread#^c44699502|dist-epoch]]. Neither reports testing these propositions on the Bosch device.
- The usefulness of a CO2-triggered response assumes a suitable sensor, threshold, and source of replacement air. Implicated describes threshold control without giving the concentration or calibration method [[./thread#^c44702390|Implicated]]; shawabawa3 likewise leaves the alert threshold unspecified [[./thread#^c44701570|shawabawa3]]. These accounts demonstrate responses to readings, not validated thresholds for cognitive impairment or health protection.
- Broader monitoring is assumed to encourage cleaner air, but that outcome is not demonstrated. ck2 hopes smartphone sensors would increase concern about air quality [[./thread#^c44703175|ck2]], and alliao expresses an aspiration for cleaner shared indoor air [[./thread#^c44699335|alliao]]. Neither supplies population exposure measurements or evidence of resulting behavior change.
- The title concerns physical sensor size, not demonstrated detection of smaller particles, as SideburnsOfDoom explicitly clarifies [[./thread#^c44700016|SideburnsOfDoom]]. smingo's approximate 20 × 5 × 5 mm dimensions and description of a nearby optical sensing region address packaging, not a newly established particle-size detection limit [[./thread#^c44699511|smingo]].

## Conditions

- Cost assessments differ. mjs reports approximately €30 for one sensor and speculates that volume pricing might be about half that, later pointing to retailer links [[./thread#^c44699360|mjs]] [[./thread#^c44704789|mjs]]. user\_7832 considers that potentially attractive based on remembered Honeywell pricing [[./thread#^c44700274|user\_7832]]. mrlonglong cites £250 for an Airthings View Plus [[./thread#^c44700778|mrlonglong]]. These concern different products and levels of integration, so they do not establish a like-for-like value comparison.
- Housing and installed equipment change the available interventions. shawabawa3's 1930s UK house has no HVAC system to automate, making window alerts useful [[./thread#^c44701570|shawabawa3]]. macNchz reports little CO2 accumulation with two people working in a drafty 120-year-old house [[./thread#^c44701813|macNchz]]. Neither account establishes what happens in tighter homes or represents housing generally.
- Seasonal outdoor conditions affect willingness to ventilate. Implicated reports that continuous exhaust incurred heating or cooling losses, while very hot or cold conditions prompted window closure and subsequently poor perceived indoor air quality [[./thread#^c44702390|Implicated]]. Their filtered, intermittent intake addresses this local comfort and energy tradeoff; no savings or humidity measurements are supplied.
- Professional pressure balancing is an additional intervention with a financial and infrastructure requirement. quickthrowman recommends paying a testing and balancing contractor to adjust an HVAC system for positive pressure [[./thread#^c44701192|quickthrowman]], then acknowledges that maintaining positive pressure is tricky and suggests pressure-based fan or damper control [[./thread#^c44727366|quickthrowman]]. No price, achieved pressure, or measured particle reduction is reported.

## Lived experiences

- pppone reports running multiple Sensirion SPS30 sensors for years, with heavily used and new units continuing to agree within their specified error [[./thread#^c44699551|pppone]]. After bobmcnamara asks about the reference [[./thread#^c44701423|bobmcnamara]], pppone reports colocating many units with government reference stations and finding relatively good performance, emphasizing agreement between sensors [[./thread#^c44702118|pppone]]. This is firsthand comparative experience, but no concentrations, error statistics, or reference-station conditions are provided.
- Implicated built a cardboard proof-of-concept intake and filter enclosure in a kitchen window. Their CO2 control runs the intake for an hour when a threshold is exceeded, stops if the reading has fallen sufficiently, and includes a five-minute presence-triggered pause. They also report realizing that their previous exhaust arrangement created undesirable negative pressure [[./thread#^c44702390|Implicated]]. These are concrete implementation and operating experiences without a quantified before-and-after exposure assessment.
- macNchz bought an Awair in 2020 to investigate CO2 during shared home-office work. They report using it to confirm DIY purifier effectiveness during wildfire smoke, recognize stale air and cooking-related problems, improve range-hood makeup air, change candle-wick and extinguishing habits, and understand temperature and humidity comfort better. The experience also encouraged consideration of induction instead of gas [[./thread#^c44701813|macNchz]]. The reported findings are qualitative, with no numerical pollutant series or controlled comparisons.
- shawabawa3 reports an operating CO2 alert that prompts opening windows in their UK home [[./thread#^c44701570|shawabawa3]]. pppone reports using PM information for cooking and running decisions and says they previously ran Open Seneca [[./thread#^c44702099|pppone]]. Neither supplies a measured personal health, performance, or sleep outcome.
- ahaucnx reports possessing a Bosch sample that had not yet been tested [[./thread#^c44700047|ahaucnx]]. They separately describe experience with tens of thousands of Plantower units passing through a test chamber, enabling performance adjustments, while acknowledging insufficient experience to compare SEN5x confidently [[./thread#^c44704715|ahaucnx]]. The extensive testing experience concerns Plantower, not evidence that the Bosch sample has passed equivalent testing.
- quickthrowman identifies commercial building-automation installation management as their professional experience. They describe CO/NO monitoring used to activate exhaust and makeup air where vehicles operate indoors, such as parking structures and drive-in warehouses, and report that most commercial systems they discuss lack broad IAQ sensing [[./thread#^c44701192|quickthrowman]]. This is a practitioner account, not a supplied building survey or measurement of personal traffic exposure.
- Liftyee reports experimenting with air-quality measurement without yet having a daily-use setup [[./thread#^c44701772|Liftyee]]. westurner reports buying a 10-in-1 monitor whose USB-C connection does not support USB-C PD [[./thread#^c44702175|westurner]]. These establish experimentation and a device-usability limitation, not pollutant accuracy or successful exposure reduction.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Independent accuracy and durability evidence for the Bosch sensor remains missing. trailbits asks for comparison with reference instruments [[./thread#^c44699686|trailbits]], ck2 wants independent technical reviews [[./thread#^c44703175|ck2]], and ahaucnx has not tested the available sample [[./thread#^c44700047|ahaucnx]]. pppone's question about Bosch maintenance is also unanswered [[./thread#^c44699551|pppone]]. Performance under variable airflow, contamination, and prolonged use therefore remains unsettled.
- Fog mitigation is proposed rather than demonstrated. Catbert59 suggests dew-point-based exclusion of problematic readings [[./thread#^c44699559|Catbert59]]. HPsquared proposes radiant heating near the sensing region [[./thread#^c44699856|HPsquared]] and clarifies that fog consists of suspended condensed droplets that such heating might remove [[./thread#^c44700068|HPsquared]], responding to Catbert59's distinction between condensation and airborne fog detection [[./thread#^c44699861|Catbert59]]. TimByte reports seeing no humidity-compensation explanation in the article [[./thread#^c44699918|TimByte]]. No experiment resolves effectiveness or measurement distortion.
- The discussion does not establish an operating rule for when to open windows. macNchz reports benefits for stale air and cooking exhaust [[./thread#^c44701813|macNchz]]; Implicated describes weather and pressure complications [[./thread#^c44702390|Implicated]]; quickthrowman identifies wildfire-smoke entry through outdoor-air intake [[./thread#^c44701192|quickthrowman]]. There are no paired indoor/outdoor readings, smoke thresholds, or comparative trials identifying the best ventilation and filtration balance.
- There is no firsthand NO2 measurement in this part. macNchz's preference for a future induction stove does not identify which pollutant drove that judgment [[./thread#^c44701813|macNchz]]. quickthrowman's commercial-monitoring account explicitly names CO/NO, which should not be silently relabeled NO2 [[./thread#^c44701192|quickthrowman]]. shagie's e-bike monitoring example concerns an intended use, not measured roadside exposure [[./thread#^c44701194|shagie]].
- Health, cognition, and sleep effects remain unestablished by this source unit. macNchz discusses thermal comfort and practical observations [[./thread#^c44701813|macNchz]], and Implicated describes humidity, cold, and perceived indoor-air deterioration [[./thread#^c44702390|Implicated]]. Neither supplies symptom changes, cognitive testing, sleep outcomes, or numerical PM2.5/CO2 exposure-response data. No health-study results are presented, and felixg3's clinical-readiness assertion is unsupported by such evidence [[./thread#^c44724125|felixg3]].
- Several product and use-case questions receive no substantive resolution. benbojangles asks what miniaturization enables beyond existing sensors [[./thread#^c44702601|benbojangles]]. Liftyee requests a SEN5x-versus-Plantower comparison [[./thread#^c44701772|Liftyee]], but ahaucnx acknowledges limited SEN5x experience [[./thread#^c44704715|ahaucnx]]. noahjk's smart-home-device question [[./thread#^c44702315|noahjk]] receives a clarification request rather than an assessment [[./thread#^c44703445|ahaucnx]]. westurner's question about broad sensor coverage, including the stated limitation of PM sensing for radon, likewise supplies no validated multipollutant comparison [[./thread#^c44702175|westurner]].
