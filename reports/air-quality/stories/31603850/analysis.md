# [What a gas stove ban means for restaurants](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=31603850)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion of what restrictions on gas appliances in new Los Angeles construction could mean for restaurants and professional kitchens. Participants consider whether induction and other electric equipment can match gas for responsiveness, high heat, wok cooking, and specialized techniques, while noting potential benefits such as lower kitchen temperatures, easier cleaning, and improved indoor air quality. Korean barbecue, charcoal, propane, portable burners, and commercial induction equipment feature as alternatives or exceptions. The debate broadens to emissions accounting, methane leakage, climate policy, gas infrastructure, electricity generation, cost, grid capacity, and whether regulation or market incentives are the better path to electrification.
Tags: restaurants, commercial kitchens, gas stoves, induction cooking, professional cooking, Korean barbecue, climate policy, methane emissions, energy infrastructure, electrification

## Relevance

Although framed around restaurants and gas-stove rules, comments directly address indoor air pollution from gas combustion and cooking, including NO2, particulates, possible health effects, ventilation, and kitchen heat and comfort. The discussion includes a personal account of a leaking stove and references to research, while also disputing the scale of health risks and the effectiveness of available alternatives. It offers little direct measured data, and several claims are uncertain or contested. ## Arguments

- Removing indoor combustion is presented as an air-quality improvement independently of the climate debate. derriz [[./thread#^c31614946|derriz]] emphasizes avoiding combustion byproducts in breathing air, including fine particulates; bsder [[./thread#^c31616433|bsder]] argues that these emissions become more consequential in tightly sealed homes. These are exposure arguments, not measurements of pollutant concentrations or demonstrated health improvements.
- The health case includes claims about research with different levels of confidence. lkbm [[./thread#^c31611746|lkbm]] cites secondary coverage reporting higher asthma rates in households with gas stoves. dynm [[./thread#^c31613068|dynm]] estimates that lifelong gas-stove use could plausibly shorten life expectancy by a couple of months through NO2 exposure, explicitly describing high uncertainty. Neither comment supplies the underlying study methods or household exposure measurements.
- Reducing unwanted heat is a practical reason to switch. KingMachiavelli [[./thread#^c31612499|KingMachiavelli]] argues that induction should improve kitchen comfort and reduce cooling demand because less heat escapes around cookware. m0llusk [[./thread#^c31611194|m0llusk]] additionally identifies water-vapor condensation from gas combustion as a problem. The cooling savings and humidity effects are asserted without measured before-and-after results.
- Better extraction is proposed alongside appliance replacement. danShumway [[./thread#^c31611331|danShumway]] considers the indoor-air evidence sufficient to influence personal housing choices, while explicitly leaving open whether improved ventilation could justify retaining gas. hnov [[./thread#^c31613175|hnov]] treats real range extraction as an important housing feature and would accept induction, distinguishing it from resistive electric cooking.
- Where combustion occurs and where exhaust goes matter to exposure. Kirby64 [[./thread#^c31612094|Kirby64]] distinguishes a furnace exhausting outdoors from a stove releasing fumes indoors; amluto [[./thread#^c31606085|amluto]] argues that power plants can control NOx emissions. However, tomerv [[./thread#^c31612978|tomerv]] notes that restaurant exhaust must also avoid becoming a nuisance to surrounding people. Moving emissions outside is therefore not presented as eliminating every exposure pathway.
- The exposure argument extends beyond the person choosing the stove. JamesBarney [[./thread#^c31612822|JamesBarney]] asks whether an activity harms people who cannot consent, and danShumway [[./thread#^c31613181|danShumway]] applies this concern to children, other household members, renters, and future occupants. Their argument for housing standards remains conditional on gas-stove air pollution causing meaningful harm.

## Counterarguments

- The thread contains explicit disagreement about health risk. Answering Ekaros's particulate question [[./thread#^c31611940|Ekaros]], macspoofing [[./thread#^c31612081|macspoofing]] calls gas cooking sootless and says there are no health concerns, while qualifying that all cooking produces emissions and acknowledging other gas-related hazards. ryukafalz [[./thread#^c31613139|ryukafalz]] challenges that assurance and links contrary health coverage. Neither side supplies direct measurements in this exchange; the soot assertion does not establish the absence of NO2 exposure.
- Electrification does not remove emissions from food and cooking oil. hristov [[./thread#^c31611927|hristov]] suggests that electric kitchens could save money by dispensing with smoke-removal infrastructure. Direct replies from colin\_mccabe [[./thread#^c31613318|colin\_mccabe]], tomerv [[./thread#^c31612978|tomerv]], and ska [[./thread#^c31614548|ska]] reject this: frying and heating food still produce particles, smoke, vaporized oils, and steam. Consequently, eliminating burner combustion does not establish that a kitchen can dispense with extraction.
- Some commenters dispute the priority of the risk rather than demonstrating its absence. \_\_turbobrew\_\_ [[./thread#^c31613785|\_\_turbobrew\_\_]] treats dynm's proposed lifespan loss as a relatively minor concern compared with diet and exercise. smm11 [[./thread#^c31612874|smm11]] expresses willingness to accept the risk despite parental warnings. These are judgments about priorities and acceptable risk, not comparative health measurements.
- Giving up gas can involve practical cooking compromises. Chef marak830 [[./thread#^c31614698|marak830]] reports losing heat input while lifting pans during rapid preparation of several dishes. cthalupa [[./thread#^c31604558|cthalupa]] values the responsiveness of copper cookware over a butane flame; their later clarification [[./thread#^c31640548|cthalupa]] says induction can still produce good sauces, but copper and gas make some adjustments easier. These accounts qualify claims that switching entails no meaningful inconvenience.
- Alternative combustible fuels are not established clean-air substitutes. joshe [[./thread#^c31614479|joshe]] proposes propane and, with good ventilation, charcoal. cthalupa [[./thread#^c31640548|cthalupa]] explicitly acknowledges indoor air-quality problems with propane and butane. Avoiding a piped natural-gas connection therefore does not, by itself, resolve the indoor combustion issue.
- The wood-fuel exchange exposes a distinction between customer and worker exposure. Hello71 [[./thread#^c31613807|Hello71]] initially assumes particulate emissions acceptable in a dining room would be minor. jrockway [[./thread#^c31614687|jrockway]] counters with occupational concerns and a recalled comparison between daily indoor wood cooking and smoking two packs of cigarettes. Hello71 [[./thread#^c31614791|Hello71]] then acknowledges the concern, while suggesting restaurants may have better ventilation and fuel. Neither the cigarette equivalence nor the reassurance about short customer visits is substantiated here.
- Recovering heat from kitchen exhaust has its own proposed tradeoff. Responding to stcredzero's heat-recovery idea [[./thread#^c31613037|stcredzero]], hn\_go\_brrrrr [[./thread#^c31617760|hn\_go\_brrrrr]] warns that oily exhaust could foul a heat exchanger or heat pump, reducing efficiency or damaging equipment. This is an engineering objection, not a reported installation test.

## Assumptions

- Building airtightness is an important but unquantified premise. bsder [[./thread#^c31616433|bsder]] argues that drafty homes previously diluted stove emissions more effectively than tightly sealed modern homes. The comment supplies no air-exchange rates, building comparisons, or evidence that a particular older home has acceptably low exposure.
- The ventilation objection depends on what ventilation actually exists. colin\_mccabe [[./thread#^c31613481|colin\_mccabe]] questions evaluating cooking without ventilation, but agentdrtran [[./thread#^c31613644|agentdrtran]] replies that their homes have only had recirculating hoods with neglected filters. The exchange challenges an assumption of routine outdoor extraction; it does not establish the effectiveness of either system.
- Comparisons must distinguish induction from resistive electric cooking. throwaway743 [[./thread#^c31616438|throwaway743]] acknowledges having no induction experience after criticizing electric responsiveness. daniel-cussen [[./thread#^c31613559|daniel-cussen]] similarly retracts an electric-cooking criticism after learning the discussion concerns induction. Their original complaints should not be treated as firsthand evidence about induction's suitability as an exposure-reduction option.
- Odor and perceived heat are qualitative observations, not pollutant-specific measurements. Melatonic [[./thread#^c31614647|Melatonic]] reports smelling gas, while carabiner [[./thread#^c31612948|carabiner]] describes clothing needing changing after charcoal barbecue. These observations establish experiences of smell and contamination, but do not quantify PM2.5, NO2, CO2, or inhaled dose.
- Transferring a lifetime risk estimate between users requires assumptions about frequency and duration. dynm [[./thread#^c31613068|dynm]] discusses lifelong gas-stove use; cthalupa [[./thread#^c31640548|cthalupa]] describes occasional indoor butane use alongside mostly induction cooking; jrockway [[./thread#^c31614687|jrockway]] raises prolonged occupational exposure. These are different exposure patterns, and the supplied discussion does not quantify how their risks compare.
- Outdoor emissions totals are not interchangeable with personal exposure. wildrhythms [[./thread#^c31612128|wildrhythms]] cites a claim that cruise ships produce ten times more pollution than European vehicles. gruez [[./thread#^c31614381|gruez]] objects that the comparison concerns particular pollutants with different regulation and emission locations. This exchange supplies neither pollutant concentrations nor measurements of residents', passengers', or commuters' exposure.

## Conditions

- Operating costs depend on local tariffs and the whole kitchen. KingMachiavelli [[./thread#^c31612499|KingMachiavelli]] quotes a restaurant's \$500–700 monthly gas bill and \$1,200 electricity bill, then speculates that gas-related cooling demand explains part of the latter. These are not the commenter's measurements or an isolated stove comparison. Mikeb85 [[./thread#^c31612973|Mikeb85]] argues that North American gas is inexpensive, while chimeracoder [[./thread#^c31613494|chimeracoder]] makes induction's eventual savings conditional on local electricity and gas prices.
- Equipment price and quality constrain the switch. kkfx [[./thread#^c31612557|kkfx]] describes €200–300 induction units with poor low-power behavior, whereas linsomniac [[./thread#^c31611765|linsomniac]] describes a roughly \$3,300 full range and links a demonstration of steadier operation. These are different products and prices reported in 2022. corrral [[./thread#^c31613236|corrral]] additionally raises purchase, repair, and durability concerns; no comprehensive lifetime-cost comparison is supplied.
- Existing electrical infrastructure matters. KingMachiavelli [[./thread#^c31612499|KingMachiavelli]] distinguishes limited-power portable units from installed electric ranges. saxonww [[./thread#^c31614199|saxonww]] warns that older US homes may need panel upgrades, while PaulDavisThe1st [[./thread#^c31615172|PaulDavisThe1st]] notes that homes already equipped for resistive electric ranges often have substantial electrical provision. Retrofit difficulty cannot be inferred from the label 'electric' alone.
- Housing tenure and billing can limit options or savings. ryukafalz [[./thread#^c31612037|ryukafalz]] uses portable induction because renting prevents installing a replacement stove. adrian\_b [[./thread#^c31612245|adrian\_b]] reports ceasing gas-stove use while still paying shared, unmetered condominium gas charges. Changing cooking equipment need not produce an immediately corresponding reduction in the household gas bill.
- Reliability concerns vary geographically. grogenaut [[./thread#^c31616980|grogenaut]] values multiple energy sources because Pacific Northwest windstorms interrupt electricity. briandear [[./thread#^c31612321|briandear]] raises hurricane-related outages and describes a natural-gas backup generator. By contrast, cecilpl2 [[./thread#^c31612482|cecilpl2]] reports never experiencing an outage longer than a few hours. These accounts explain differing preferences without establishing how to manage cooking emissions during an outage.
- The intervention under discussion has a limited scope. hristov [[./thread#^c31611927|hristov]] says the Los Angeles proposal concerns new construction, rather than requiring existing restaurants to replace their equipment immediately. Costs and exposure changes from a voluntary retrofit therefore should not be conflated with the effects of that proposed rule.

## Lived experiences

- Melatonic [[./thread#^c31614647|Melatonic]] reports intermittent gas odor in an apartment, attributes continuous leakage to fittings they believed were improperly installed, and describes considerable heat from three continuously burning pilot lights. They also report changing their pilot-light use. This is a firsthand account of odor, inspection, and heat, with no instrumented leak measurement, pollutant concentration, or demonstrated health effect.
- Ventilation experiences conflict directly. agentdrtran [[./thread#^c31613644|agentdrtran]] says they have never lived with a stove hood exhausting outdoors. Replying to that account, clawlor [[./thread#^c31613790|clawlor]] says most of their homes had outdoor extraction, while acknowledging experience with recirculating systems and suggesting regional variation on the US East Coast. gwbas1c [[./thread#^c31616081|gwbas1c]] separately describes a powerful Viking hood that seemed to remove everything. None supplies measured capture efficiency.
- SenHeng [[./thread#^c31605106|SenHeng]] describes Japanese barbecue restaurants switching to electric hot plates as more comfortable because hot air no longer rushes over the diner's face or heats their wrist. wheels [[./thread#^c31614583|wheels]], who previously worked in kitchens, emphasizes the quality-of-life value of lower kitchen temperatures and reports observing induction in high-end open kitchens. These are qualitative accounts of comfort, not measured changes in air pollution.
- linsomniac [[./thread#^c31611459|linsomniac]] prefers their induction range to an in-law's expensive gas range, reporting less heat reaching the kitchen and cookware handles, while acknowledging loss of heating when lifting or tipping a pan. crooked-v [[./thread#^c31614657|crooked-v]] separately reports the whole kitchen noticeably warming during gas pan-searing. Neither provides thermometer readings or a controlled comparison.
- cthalupa [[./thread#^c31640548|cthalupa]] describes a mixed approach: most cooking on induction, occasional indoor butane for particular cookware, and propane outdoors. In another reply, cthalupa [[./thread#^c31640099|cthalupa]] reports using a 270,000-BTU propane burner outside partly to avoid putting that much heat into the house. Their acceptance of infrequent indoor exposure is a personal risk judgment, not a measured demonstration of safety.
- Chef marak830 [[./thread#^c31614698|marak830]] describes managing five or six dishes at once and finding induction's loss of heating during pan lifting disruptive. In a later reply, marak830 [[./thread#^c31617983|marak830]] accepts that chefs could change technique, albeit with frustration. This is a qualified account of operational difficulty rather than a claim that adaptation is impossible.
- Switching can require adaptation while producing perceived benefits. jeramey [[./thread#^c31613285|jeramey]] reports replacing copper or aluminum cookware with carbon steel, modifying technique, and experiencing no further high-temperature frying flare-ups, at the cost of more pan maintenance. peace4all [[./thread#^c31618171|peace4all]] reports switching partly because of perceived air and heat risks. Neither reports a measured respiratory-health improvement.
- Reduced kitchen heat does not mean an induction surface remains harmlessly cool. sleightofmind [[./thread#^c31615484|sleightofmind]] reports firsthand experience of burning fingers on induction glass, while still preferring induction and considering it safer than gas. This qualifies cecilpl2's categorical claim of no burn risk [[./thread#^c31611805|cecilpl2]].
- pirate787 [[./thread#^c31618952|pirate787]] reports that a 36-year-old chef friend has stage-four lung cancer and wonders whether workplace particulate exposure contributed. This is a personal account about another person's illness, not an established occupational diagnosis: no exposure history, measurements, or causal assessment is supplied.
- Charcoal cooking has sensory costs alongside its appeal. carabiner [[./thread#^c31612948|carabiner]] describes needing to change clothes after visiting restaurants with tabletop charcoal grills. karmicthreat [[./thread#^c31612812|karmicthreat]] nevertheless prefers the gas or charcoal barbecue experience to electric versions. These accounts preserve differing preferences without quantifying their associated exposure.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The magnitude of health benefit from replacing gas rather than improving extraction remains unresolved. dynm [[./thread#^c31613068|dynm]] offers an explicitly uncertain NO2-related lifespan estimate; colin\_mccabe [[./thread#^c31613481|colin\_mccabe]] questions the ventilation scenario; danShumway [[./thread#^c31612494|danShumway]] says more research is needed to judge prevented harm against intervention costs. The supplied material cannot settle that comparison.
- The actual protection provided by hoods and filters is unknown here. agentdrtran [[./thread#^c31613644|agentdrtran]] describes neglected recirculating filters, while gwbas1c [[./thread#^c31616081|gwbas1c]] perceives strong outdoor extraction as effective. Together with ska's reminder that food itself emits smoke and oily vapor [[./thread#^c31614548|ska]], these accounts identify relevant mechanisms but supply no measured removal efficiencies or remaining concentrations.
- Furnace leakage versus stove exposure is an unanswered question, not a finding. Following Kirby64's explanation of outdoor furnace exhaust [[./thread#^c31612094|Kirby64]], wolverine876 [[./thread#^c31635187|wolverine876]] asks whether a furnace's larger gas throughput could create greater leakage and whether basement leakage reaches occupied rooms. They explicitly label their numerical example as made up.
- Commercial cooking equivalence remains disputed. russdill [[./thread#^c31612912|russdill]] reports seeing 15–20-kW induction wok equipment online, while marak830 [[./thread#^c31614698|marak830]] emphasizes heating during pan movement and cthalupa [[./thread#^c31640548|cthalupa]] distinguishes flame-dependent wok effects from ordinary temperature control. High advertised power alone does not resolve those experiential objections, and no restaurant comparison jointly measures cooking results, comfort, and air quality.
- There are no supplied PM2.5, NO2, or indoor CO2 concentration series or air-sensor reliability tests. derriz [[./thread#^c31614946|derriz]] mentions fine particulates without particle-size measurements; dynm [[./thread#^c31613068|dynm]] supplies a health estimate rather than room readings. kkfx's thermometer and power-meter discussion [[./thread#^c31607098|kkfx]] and linsomniac's linked demonstration [[./thread#^c31611765|linsomniac]] concern cooktop behavior, not air monitoring.
- The health discussion does not establish cognitive or sleep effects, or measured symptom improvement after an intervention. lkbm [[./thread#^c31611746|lkbm]] reports an asthma claim from secondary coverage, while pirate787 [[./thread#^c31618952|pirate787]] describes a friend's illness. Neither provides evidence about cognition, sleep, or the effect of indoor CO2 in this source unit.
- This part cannot establish when opening windows helps or worsens exposure. The substantive ventilation accounts from clawlor [[./thread#^c31613790|clawlor]] and gwbas1c [[./thread#^c31616081|gwbas1c]] concern extraction systems, without window trials or paired indoor/outdoor readings. The transport-pollution exchange discussed by gruez [[./thread#^c31614381|gruez]] supplies no personal traffic-exposure measurements, and there are no wildfire-smoke exposure or filtration accounts here.
