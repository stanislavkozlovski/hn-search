# [What to Know Before Buying an Air Purifier to Clear Wildfire Smoke](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=15540981)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

The thread focuses on choosing air purifiers for wildfire smoke and evaluating claims about HEPA filtration, carbon media, UV, ionization, ozone, replacement filters, noise, airflow, and maintenance. Participants question whether advertised room-size ratings and certifications are useful, and discuss the difference between particle filtration and gas removal. Practical options include commercial units, furnace filters, box fans, and water-based air washers, with debate about airflow, filter loading, and how well devices handle smoke-related compounds. Some share personal experiences with products, costs, and performance.
Tags: wildfire smoke, air purifiers, HEPA, activated carbon, filter efficiency, airflow, consumer products

## Relevance

The discussion centers on reducing wildfire smoke indoors with filtration, including firsthand reports of cleaner bedrooms during hazardous outdoor conditions and a child sensitive to smoke. It compares HEPA, carbon, and DIY filters while noting airflow, replacement costs, noise, and uncertainty about marketing claims and performance. ## Arguments

- dandare [[./thread#^c15559917|dandare]] argues for choosing purifiers by true HEPA filtration, replacement-filter costs and air volume processed per unit time, rather than advertised floor area. They consider a prefilter useful for keeping hair and bugs out of the main filter. These are purchasing recommendations, without accompanying performance measurements.
- Responding to stinos’s concern about repeatedly filtering the same air [[./thread#^c15557677|stinos]], dmlorenzetti [[./thread#^c15558606|dmlorenzetti]] argues that occupants, thermal plumes, breathing and the purifier’s airflow mix occupied areas. Less-mixed spaces may remain reservoirs that delay concentration changes. subwayclub [[./thread#^c15563257|subwayclub]] suggests additional room fans, but supplies no test of their benefit.
- todayiamme [[./thread#^c15557493|todayiamme]] argues that activated-carbon prefilters could complement particle filtration by removing volatile compounds. They explicitly acknowledge uncertainty about practical effectiveness; their supporting reference concerns PAH removal from water, which does not establish performance in a household air purifier.
- aaron695 [[./thread#^c15558655|aaron695]] proposes a potential health case for purifiers in inner-city homes, perhaps homes generally, based on concern about small particles. However, they ask for proper exposure figures and location-specific life-extension estimates rather than supplying evidence that universal installation would help.

## Counterarguments

- clumsysmurf [[./thread#^c15557282|clumsysmurf]] raises a limitation of treating wildfire smoke solely as a particle-filtration problem: they cite a report linking the fire-emitted compound retene to DNA damage and lung-cell death, and doubt that HEPA removes PAHs generally. This is a report-based hazard claim and a filtration concern, not a measured household exposure or demonstrated health outcome.
- Against optimism about carbon prefilters, clumsysmurf [[./thread#^c15557636|clumsysmurf]] quotes the article’s claim that at least five pounds of activated charcoal is needed for a statistically significant effect. dandare [[./thread#^c15559917|dandare]] similarly doubts thin carbon films or fibres. CyberDildonics [[./thread#^c15559520|CyberDildonics]] responds that large carbon filters exist, but the product link supplies no performance results within this discussion.
- contingencies [[./thread#^c15559070|contingencies]] challenges tpkj’s warning that carbon can release pollutants. tpkj [[./thread#^c15559879|tpkj]] then quotes a manufacturer’s explanation about saturated carbon releasing collected gases; contingencies [[./thread#^c15560020|contingencies]] counters that maintenance and replacement are necessary for every filtration system and that carbon is useful and inexpensive. Neither supplies measurements resolving the claimed release risk.
- dovdovdov [[./thread#^c15557425|dovdovdov]] argues that wildfire smoke constitutes extreme use and could require almost daily filter changes, even with industrial equipment. No loading measurements, exposure duration or replacement criteria support that broad claim, so it remains a competing assertion about capacity.
- In the box-fan discussion, mmagin [[./thread#^c15561004|mmagin]] warns that restrictive furnace filters create backpressure and substantially reduce airflow. This challenges judging a DIY purifier by its filter medium alone, although no airflow measurements are provided.
- trentmb [[./thread#^c15559302|trentmb]] suggests plants as a cheaper alternative, citing the NASA Clean Air Study. namlem [[./thread#^c15559427|namlem]] replies that pollutant removal is much slower than with a filter, making the suggestion less applicable to an acute smoke or painting episode. Neither provides room-scale removal measurements.
- dandare [[./thread#^c15559917|dandare]] questions maintenance-free claims, association certificates, UV disinfection without wavelength/dose/exposure information, and ionization or ozone without emissions data. These concerns support demanding specifications, but the comment does not establish that every criticized product is ineffective or hazardous.

## Assumptions

- jaggederest’s inability to appreciably detect smoke indoors [[./thread#^c15557472|jaggederest]] and tpkj’s belief that a purifier helped during painting [[./thread#^c15558856|tpkj]] are sensory assessments. Treating either as proof of low PM2.5, removal of all harmful smoke components or improved health would require evidence absent from their accounts.
- mitchtbaum [[./thread#^c15559538|mitchtbaum]] claims water-based air washers can outperform HEPA, avoid a lower particle-size limit and maintain efficiency without filter clogging. They also suggest UV-C for antimicrobial treatment. No comparative tests, collection rates or microbial-control measurements substantiate these claims in the supplied discussion.
- nextos [[./thread#^c15558212|nextos]] claims superior filtration for their Coway model and describes IQAir as filtering down to approximately 0.1 microns; mmagin [[./thread#^c15560972|mmagin]] describes some premium furnace filters as roughly equivalent to HEPA. Neither supplies testing. Likewise, rahimnathwani’s observation that aftermarket filters appear to contain similar materials [[./thread#^c15558363|rahimnathwani]] does not establish equivalent filtration.
- tpkj [[./thread#^c15558856|tpkj]] reports being told their chosen purifier is used in government cleanup operations and speculates that affiliate incentives explain its omission from a review. These are hearsay and an attribution of motive, respectively; neither independently establishes efficacy or review bias.

## Conditions

- Outdoor conditions matter: jaggederest [[./thread#^c15557472|jaggederest]] describes intensely smoky outdoor air alongside much less detectable smoke in filtered rooms. dmlorenzetti [[./thread#^c15558606|dmlorenzetti]] worries about outdoor smoke entering homes, particularly in affected parts of California. Together these suggest that opening windows could worsen smoke exposure, but that is an inference: no open-versus-closed-window trial or demonstrated ventilation benefit appears here.
- Costs are substantial and product-specific in these 2017 accounts. jaggederest [[./thread#^c15557472|jaggederest]] considers approximately \$120–\$180 annually worthwhile; clumsysmurf [[./thread#^c15557801|clumsysmurf]] finds both IQAir equipment and filters expensive. nextos [[./thread#^c15558212|nextos]] recommends Coway for tighter budgets and claims low energy use, without supplying electricity measurements or a complete cost comparison.
- Replacement economics and existing equipment change the options. dandare [[./thread#^c15559917|dandare]] compares proprietary filters to printer cartridges, while legulere [[./thread#^c15562513|legulere]] questions why a fan and filters cost so much. mmagin [[./thread#^c15560972|mmagin]] suggests premium furnace filters and fan-only operation for homes with forced-air systems, which they personally lack; their claimed savings and adequacy are not tested here.
- Noise can constrain usable filtration settings. mmagin [[./thread#^c15560972|mmagin]] recommends larger units operated slowly for quietness and longer filter life. rahimnathwani [[./thread#^c15558363|rahimnathwani]] says cheaper machines they tried became too loud at useful speeds. These experiences favor comparing airflow at tolerable noise levels, rather than purchase price alone.
- Water-based alternatives introduce another comfort and equipment tradeoff: mitchtbaum [[./thread#^c15559538|mitchtbaum]] says evaporation from air washers or indoor water features may require a dehumidifier. No humidity readings, operating costs or geographic limits are given.

## Lived experiences

- jaggederest [[./thread#^c15557472|jaggederest]] reports running a high-quality bedroom filter during a September smoke episode: smoke was scarcely detectable in that room and nearby rooms, while going outside felt like entering a cigar bar. They recommend IQAir from experience; their favorable mention of Austin Air is explicitly hearsay. No particle readings accompany the account.
- linsomniac [[./thread#^c15559462|linsomniac]] bought Coway units for three bedrooms because their son has lung problems and sensitivity to dust and smoke. They report quiet normal operation and automatic fan increases, sometimes triggered by someone entering the room. This demonstrates observed sensor-responsive behavior, but provides neither sensor calibration nor measured improvement in the son’s health.
- tpkj [[./thread#^c15558856|tpkj]], who describes strong sensitivity to smells, bought an expensive EnviroKlenz unit for a residential painting project. They believe it helped and do not regret the purchase, while explicitly saying it was not a complete solution. No pollutant concentrations or symptom measurements are supplied.
- IQAir noise experiences conflict. clumsysmurf [[./thread#^c15557801|clumsysmurf]] reports years of ownership with rattling at certain settings, attributed to motor resonance. rahimnathwani [[./thread#^c15558363|rahimnathwani]] reports no rattling from five HealthPro 250 units across most settings and values their airflow-to-noise balance. These are different households and machines, without standardized acoustic comparisons.
- goldenkey [[./thread#^c15558512|goldenkey]], replying to the Austin Air recommendation, reports a loose three-inch screw destroying a new unit’s fan on first use and the manufacturer referring them to Walmart rather than arranging repair or replacement. This supports a specific reliability and support complaint; it does not establish a brand-wide failure rate.
- nextos [[./thread#^c15558212|nextos]] personally owns a Coway 1008-DH and recommends it as a budget option. rbcgerard [[./thread#^c15558594|rbcgerard]] reports owning two Vornado HEPA units over the years and finding them very effective. Neither gives quantified pollutant reductions or health outcomes.
- mmagin [[./thread#^c15560972|mmagin]] describes using purifiers for pollen, owning an older Kenmore-branded Whirlpool model and discontinued 3M units, and appreciating a separate replaceable dust prefilter. Their comments about newer manufacturing quality are suggestions they have encountered, rather than a reported comparison of old and new units.
- Responding to gyrgtyn’s question about a window-mounted box fan and furnace filter [[./thread#^c15559201|gyrgtyn]], CyberDildonics [[./thread#^c15559425|CyberDildonics]] says they have used that kind of setup successfully as a general filter. The reply does not establish that their own setup was window-mounted, tested against wildfire smoke or evaluated with particle measurements.
- dandare [[./thread#^c15559917|dandare]] reports abandoning a planned purifier purchase after researching the market and becoming distrustful of its claims. This is firsthand evidence about purchasing difficulty and lost confidence, rather than an unsuccessful filtration trial.

## Unresolved disagreements

- This source unit contains no numerical PM2.5, NO2 or CO2 concentrations, independent sensor validation, traffic-exposure measurements or cooking-emissions tests. linsomniac’s automatic particulate-sensor behavior and mention of an older purifier used during kitchen remodeling [[./thread#^c15559462|linsomniac]] do not fill those gaps; aaron695’s inner-city proposal [[./thread#^c15558655|aaron695]] supplies no traffic measurements.
- Health benefits remain unquantified. aaron695 [[./thread#^c15558655|aaron695]] explicitly asks how much life extension purifiers provide and where; linsomniac [[./thread#^c15559462|linsomniac]] reports an existing respiratory vulnerability without a treatment outcome. This part also supplies no firsthand cognition or sleep outcomes. Reported comfort benefits therefore cannot establish those broader effects.
- The necessary filtration standard remains disputed. dandare [[./thread#^c15559917|dandare]] strongly favors true HEPA, whereas contingencies [[./thread#^c15559070|contingencies]] calls home HEPA excessive on the premise that organic fires mainly produce larger particles. CyberDildonics [[./thread#^c15559425|CyberDildonics]] distinguishes furnace filters from HEPA, while mmagin [[./thread#^c15560972|mmagin]] considers certain premium furnace filters comparable. Different filter grades and absent smoke-size or efficiency measurements prevent resolution; nextos [[./thread#^c15558212|nextos]] requests independent testing of very small particles.
- Carbon’s practical limits remain unsettled. SippinLean [[./thread#^c15559109|SippinLean]] asks which pollutants it allegedly releases, and graedus [[./thread#^c15559068|graedus]] asks how often the EnviroKlenz filter needs replacement. Neither receives a specific answer in the supplied replies; tpkj’s manufacturer quotation [[./thread#^c15559879|tpkj]] describes a general saturation mechanism without identifying measured emissions or a service interval.
- Actual room protection cannot be inferred from nominal air cycling alone. stinos [[./thread#^c15557677|stinos]] asks whether all room air reaches the filter. dmlorenzetti [[./thread#^c15558606|dmlorenzetti]] offers a mixing explanation but worries that the quoted four-to-five cycles per hour could be comparable to outdoor-air infiltration in affected California houses, explicitly calling this a gut feeling requiring modeling. No measured room distribution, infiltration rate or smoke-removal curve resolves the capacity question.
