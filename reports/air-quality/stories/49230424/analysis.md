# [Switching to electric stoves can dramatically cut indoor air pollution (2025)](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49230424)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants debate evidence that switching from gas to electric cooking can reduce indoor pollution, including how much risk depends on ventilation, range-hood performance, household cooking habits, and the study’s assumptions. Many compare gas with induction and resistive electric stoves, arguing over heat response, temperature control, power, cookware, cooking techniques, safety, and user interfaces. Other threads discuss costs and electrical infrastructure, the role of utilities and advocacy groups, and whether health findings are being dismissed or overstated for political reasons.
Tags: gas stoves, electric stoves, induction cooking, indoor air pollution, ventilation, public health, cooking performance, energy infrastructure, climate policy, research debate

## Relevance

The discussion directly addresses gas-stove NO2 exposure, asthma effects, and the roles and limitations of range hoods and ventilation. It includes reported measurements and study claims alongside conflicting cooking experiences and tradeoffs between gas and electric or induction stoves. ## Arguments

- its-summertime [[./thread#^c49232093|its-summertime]] quotes a study estimating US long-term total residential NO2 exposure at about 10 ppbv for gas-stove households, about 18 ppbv or more for the highest-use 5\%, and about 8 ppbv attributable to outdoor sources for electric-stove households. The quoted study attributes no additional NO2 to electric stoves. These are study estimates, not the commenter's household measurements.
- The estimated benefit varies substantially with cooking intensity: its-summertime [[./thread#^c49232093|its-summertime]] quotes additional stove-related NO2 exposure of 0.03, 1.7, and 10.3 ppbv at the 5th, 50th, and 95th percentiles of fuel consumption, respectively. Corresponding 95\% confidence intervals are 0.01–0.07, 0.8–3.5, and 4.8–21.7 ppbv. These figures support particularly substantial reductions for heavy users, with considerable uncertainty around the estimates.
- gpm [[./thread#^c49232270|gpm]] describes replacing gas stoves with electric ones as effective for reducing asthma symptoms. In a related reply, gpm [[./thread#^c49235841|gpm]] says the study involved people with asthma and reports that installing hoods over existing gas stoves in 15 homes did not significantly change NO2. This is a commenter’s account of research, with no clinical effect sizes or firsthand health outcomes supplied.
- Electrification leaves emissions from the cooking process itself: xnx [[./thread#^c49232296|xnx]] points to oil droplets and argues that outdoor exhaust remains essential. bob1029 [[./thread#^c49241231|bob1029]] similarly emphasizes deep-frying fumes regardless of how the oil is heated. Their argument distinguishes removing combustion emissions from removing emissions generated by food and oil.
- Comfort provides a separate reason to switch: kibwen [[./thread#^c49233594|kibwen]] argues that induction releases less waste heat into the kitchen, making simultaneous use of several burners more tolerable. btreecat [[./thread#^c49240929|btreecat]] likewise emphasizes excess heat, indoor pollution, and CO2 when comparing powerful gas burners with induction; neither supplies quantitative temperature or CO2 measurements.
- Ventilation quality and actual operation matter alongside stove choice. expedition32 [[./thread#^c49232337|expedition32]] contrasts professional exhaust with domestic kitchens, particularly kitchens opening into living rooms. pkulak [[./thread#^c49232652|pkulak]] reports rarely seeing domestic hoods switched on, while ryan\_lane [[./thread#^c49239278|ryan\_lane]] warns that many US hoods recirculate indoors. These are observations and generalizations from commenters, not measured prevalence estimates.

## Counterarguments

- hellisothers [[./thread#^c49234176|hellisothers]] argues that the pollution findings depend on little or no ventilation and dismisses their relevance where a hood or good airflow to a window exists. acdha [[./thread#^c49238749|acdha]] directly responds that the researchers reportedly prioritize smaller, poorly ventilated homes: ventilation dependence can identify where intervention matters rather than invalidate the concern.
- Some owners regard outdoor exhaust as sufficient mitigation. Forgeties79 [[./thread#^c49232127|Forgeties79]] recalls a study suggesting roughly 90\% or greater pollution reduction, explicitly qualifying this as memory. christoph [[./thread#^c49232301|christoph]] dismisses concern about their gas cooking because they have a large outside-venting hood. Neither reports measured capture efficiency or residual exposure in their own kitchen.
- Spooky23 [[./thread#^c49232125|Spooky23]] doubts the health claims because of funding interests and argues that a causal stove effect should produce comparable suburban asthma clusters. foco\_tubi [[./thread#^c49232304|foco\_tubi]] counters that urban–suburban comparisons contain numerous confounders and cannot exclude a smaller contribution from gas stoves. Forgeties79 [[./thread#^c49233023|Forgeties79]] argues that funding context does not establish whether the health findings are wrong.
- sm-silversight [[./thread#^c49231995|sm-silversight]] explicitly values open-flame cooking enough to accept possible health costs, describing daily cooking and perceived precision as quality-of-life benefits. mint5 [[./thread#^c49232066|mint5]] challenges whether that tradeoff is informed when its health consequences remain unspecified. This is a disagreement about acceptable risk as well as cooking performance.
- chomp [[./thread#^c49232209|chomp]] identifies direct-flame tortillas, pepper charring, and particular cookware as reasons to retain gas, later emphasizing high-output flame techniques [[./thread#^c49233858|chomp]]. PhotonHunter [[./thread#^c49244218|PhotonHunter]] similarly values heating pan sides and proposes a mixed cooking setup with substantial exhaust. These arguments concern particular techniques; they do not dispute that combustion creates an additional exposure source.
- Dissatisfaction with conventional electric cooking should be distinguished from induction performance. preg\_match [[./thread#^c49240060|preg\_match]] reports very slow heating and cooling on a resistive cooktop, while pornel [[./thread#^c49232246|pornel]] argues that induction provides rapid response and better efficiency. A recommendation to replace gas with an unspecified electric appliance can therefore conceal meaningful usability differences.

## Assumptions

- Different pollutants cannot be treated as interchangeable evidence. micromacrofoot [[./thread#^c49232346|micromacrofoot]] discusses PM2.5 monitoring, its-summertime [[./thread#^c49232093|its-summertime]] quotes NO2 estimates, and btreecat [[./thread#^c49240929|btreecat]] mentions CO2. The supplied comments do not establish a relationship that would let a particle reading quantify either gas, or show that a reduction in NO2 entails an equivalent reduction in cooking particles.
- Categorical safety language exceeds the evidence supplied. PeterWhittaker [[./thread#^c49231966|PeterWhittaker]] celebrates induction as having none of the asthma or benzene, and tyjen [[./thread#^c49231920|tyjen]] describes an electric indoor setup as having zero chance of carbon monoxide poisoning. Neither supplies exposure measurements or health follow-up establishing those absolutes; xnx [[./thread#^c49232296|xnx]] explicitly notes that cooking still emits pollutants.
- Personal relevance is sometimes inferred from age and cooking frequency. minetest2048 [[./thread#^c49238513|minetest2048]] considers the issue less relevant because they are a single adult who cooks infrequently. The comment supplies no evidence that adults are unaffected or any exposure threshold below which risk disappears; its-summertime’s quoted estimates [[./thread#^c49232093|its-summertime]] support variation with fuel consumption.
- Appliance sensors are not evidence of air-monitor reliability. comrade1234 [[./thread#^c49232419|comrade1234]] describes temperature sensing and frying presets, while LargoLasskhyfv [[./thread#^c49251928|LargoLasskhyfv]] describes using an LED-bar 'E-smog-meter' to investigate induction cycling. Neither measures PM2.5, NO2, or CO2. micromacrofoot’s air-quality account [[./thread#^c49232346|micromacrofoot]] identifies no monitor model, calibration, or validation procedure.
- Comparisons with gas heating assume correctly functioning exhaust separation. alex43578 [[./thread#^c49232033|alex43578]] distinguishes a properly vented heating system from an open kitchen flame, and rsynnott [[./thread#^c49240796|rsynnott]] says a gas boiler should not exhaust indoors. fhdkweig [[./thread#^c49232179|fhdkweig]] describes combustion isolated inside metal tubes, but the supplied discussion contains no inspection or measurements establishing that leakage is impossible in every installation.

## Conditions

- The numerical NO2 estimates are geographically specific. its-summertime [[./thread#^c49232195|its-summertime]] questions their portability to countries where people use electric kettles despite cooking on gas, because boiling water on the hob contributes to the modeled fuel consumption. Differences in cooking habits can therefore change the relevance of the quoted US estimates.
- Housing affects the practical case for intervention. acdha [[./thread#^c49238749|acdha]] says the researchers prioritize smaller, poorly ventilated units; expedition32 [[./thread#^c49232337|expedition32]] particularly recommends induction where the kitchen shares living space. ryan\_lane [[./thread#^c49239278|ryan\_lane]] and rsynnott [[./thread#^c49242190|rsynnott]] suggest that recirculating or absent extraction is a relevant US housing condition, without providing representative installation data.
- Rental and electrical constraints limit appliance choices. syntaxing [[./thread#^c49232013|syntaxing]] reports being restricted to 120V portable induction units as a renter and finding their hum objectionable. LargoLasskhyfv [[./thread#^c49275980|LargoLasskhyfv]] considers standard US outlet power inadequate for preferred performance, while amluto [[./thread#^c49232148|amluto]] identifies 120V options with battery assistance or sophisticated temperature control. Their comments do not establish a universal minimum wattage for satisfactory cooking.
- Control availability varies by market and appliance type. mort96 [[./thread#^c49232074|mort96]] describes touch controls as ubiquitous in Norway, and Maxion [[./thread#^c49232176|Maxion]] makes a similar Nordic observation. In the US, ericpauley [[./thread#^c49232080|ericpauley]] reports knobs widely available on resistive ranges, while tass [[./thread#^c49232121|tass]] personally bought a relatively inexpensive LG induction range with knobs. Neither regional experience establishes worldwide availability.
- Existing kitchen dimensions can make switching or correcting a disappointing purchase expensive. cassianoleal [[./thread#^c49242044|cassianoleal]] selected a discounted induction range partly because it fitted the available space, then explained that replacing its 100 cm format could require changing cupboards and substantial granite work [[./thread#^c49244180|cassianoleal]]. Appliance recommendations must account for these installation costs.
- Energy affordability informs resistance, although the bills do not measure stove costs. Spooky23 [[./thread#^c49232125|Spooky23]] reports a 40\% electricity-delivery increase and a \$300 monthly spring bill in New York, then reports their parents paying \$700 monthly in winter for an 1,100-square-foot home [[./thread#^c49244519|Spooky23]]. No cooking-specific consumption or gas-to-induction operating-cost comparison is supplied.
- Resilience and local resources change preferences. chomp [[./thread#^c49232209|chomp]] values a gas burner that works during hurricane-season outages. On a Swedish countryside farm, hagbard\_c [[./thread#^c49237462|hagbard\_c]] values available wood, cooking during outages, and simultaneous house heating, while acknowledging that their setup is unsuitable for densely populated areas. These circumstances explain preferences without establishing low personal smoke exposure.

## Lived experiences

- Responding to stevenkkim’s question about residual pollution with an operating outdoor-exhaust hood [[./thread#^c49232249|stevenkkim]], micromacrofoot [[./thread#^c49232346|micromacrofoot]] recommends a PM2.5 meter and reports pollution lingering for a couple of hours in their experience. They also claim daily cooking without a hood can leave pollution all day unless several windows are opened. No numerical concentrations, monitor details, or documented before-and-after series are supplied.
- analog31 [[./thread#^c49232278|analog31]], describing forty years of daily cooking, recalls their children’s student tenements having gas stoves and no ventilation. They now enjoy an LG induction stove with knobs and easier cleaning. The account identifies an actual housing experience and a successful appliance change, but reports no diagnoses or pollutant measurements.
- The claim that serious cooks necessarily prefer gas encounters conflicting experience. lightedman [[./thread#^c49232229|lightedman]] describes nearly thirty years since starting professionally in a Chinese kitchen and praises induction’s speed and control. syntaxing [[./thread#^c49232025|syntaxing]] cooks daily on induction and uses an outdoor gas stove for wok cooking. Conversely, hellisothers [[./thread#^c49234193|hellisothers]] reports that their chef wife strongly prefers gas after years using induction.
- kevincox [[./thread#^c49236979|kevincox]] reports finding cookware handles and nearby objects much hotter when returning to gas, including accidental burns, and prefers induction’s cooler surroundings. minetest2048 [[./thread#^c49238513|minetest2048]] similarly reports favoring an induction burner over an available gas stove because of power and reduced waste heat. These are comfort and usability accounts, without measured room temperatures.
- Controls can create practical friction or satisfaction. jMyles [[./thread#^c49232159|jMyles]] reports oil making touch controls unusable and potentially leaving the stove stuck on high; mort96 [[./thread#^c49232060|mort96]] describes wet-finger control problems and burned cookware. kevincox [[./thread#^c49236934|kevincox]], however, deliberately chose good touch controls and values cleaning the flat surface. These accounts concern particular interfaces, not an inherent requirement of induction.
- Induction performance varies across reported setups. PhotonHunter [[./thread#^c49244218|PhotonHunter]] says a small coil warped a 2 mm carbon-steel pan on first use and describes moving to larger-coil appliances. cassianoleal [[./thread#^c49248468|cassianoleal]] reports persistent cast-iron hot spots even after twenty minutes. acdha [[./thread#^c49238675|acdha]] reports satisfactory simmering and appropriately sized coils on a lower-middle-priced unit. No comparable pollutant measurements accompany these contrasting experiences.
- Noise and cookware compatibility can impose additional costs. pkulak [[./thread#^c49232694|pkulak]] reports replacing pans that buzzed on a Breville Control Freak despite being quiet on an inexpensive induction range. Their explanation involving different power modulation is explicitly a guess. This supplements syntaxing’s report of objectionable portable-unit hum [[./thread#^c49232013|syntaxing]] as an acoustic comfort tradeoff.
- ryan\_lane [[./thread#^c49239229|ryan\_lane]] reports that their Japanese gas cooktop senses temperature, detects overheating, and maintains a selected temperature, showing that these features are not exclusive to induction. Nevertheless, they miss their former induction stove and plan to install induction in a new house. No air-quality comparison is reported.
- adrian\_b [[./thread#^c49233570|adrian\_b]] describes preparing food from raw ingredients using a microwave and leaving their gas stove unused for approximately seven years. They value repeatable power-and-time sequences and reduced attention during cooking. This is another reported way of avoiding routine indoor gas use, with no measured exposure or health outcome.
- hagbard\_c [[./thread#^c49237462|hagbard\_c]] reports cooking almost daily for a family of four on a wood-burning stove, praising its power, versatility, and reliability. Their electric stove largely stores cookware, and induction is occasionally used outdoors. The account contains no indoor particle measurements, exhaust description, or health assessment, so satisfaction cannot establish smoke safety.

## Unresolved disagreements

- How much protection does a properly installed and consistently operated domestic hood provide? Forgeties79’s recalled reduction of roughly 90\% [[./thread#^c49232127|Forgeties79]] contrasts with micromacrofoot’s residual-pollution account [[./thread#^c49232346|micromacrofoot]] and Schiendelman’s claim that front-burner emissions often escape capture [[./thread#^c49235695|Schiendelman]]. 6DM’s question about starting ventilation before cooking and continuing afterward [[./thread#^c49232218|6DM]] receives no measured comparison.
- Why did the hood intervention reportedly fail to lower NO2? gpm [[./thread#^c49235841|gpm]] suggests nonuse or poor performance, favoring nonuse, but the supplied comment does not establish either explanation. btreecat’s warning about recirculating hoods [[./thread#^c49240971|btreecat]] identifies another possible limitation without demonstrating that it applied to those study homes. Installation, operation, capture, and exhaust destination remain distinct unresolved factors.
- When opening windows helps or worsens exposure is not established here. micromacrofoot [[./thread#^c49232346|micromacrofoot]] advocates opening several windows to clear cooking pollution, while its-summertime [[./thread#^c49232093|its-summertime]] quotes a substantial outdoor contribution to residential NO2. This part provides no paired indoor/outdoor measurements, traffic-exposure accounts, wildfire-smoke experiences, or tests of window opening under polluted outdoor conditions. It also supplies no particle-filter or gas-filter performance trials.
- The size and distribution of health benefits remain incompletely documented in the supplied material. gpm [[./thread#^c49232270|gpm]] asserts asthma improvement, while Spooky23 [[./thread#^c49232125|Spooky23]] disputes the causal interpretation and foco\_tubi [[./thread#^c49232304|foco\_tubi]] challenges the confounding in that objection. No commenter provides firsthand before-and-after asthma outcomes, and the quoted NO2 estimates alone do not establish an individual household’s clinical benefit.
- CO2, cognition, and sleep receive almost no evidentiary coverage. btreecat [[./thread#^c49240929|btreecat]] raises CO2 as a concern about gas cooking, but this part supplies no CO2 concentrations, sensor comparisons, or associated cognitive or sleep observations. micromacrofoot’s limited PM2.5 account [[./thread#^c49232346|micromacrofoot]] also leaves particle concentrations and monitor reliability unresolved.
- Whether induction preserves particular valued cooking techniques remains disputed. chomp [[./thread#^c49233759|chomp]] rejects cast iron as an equivalent substitute for direct flame, whereas shimman [[./thread#^c49237251|shimman]] reports regularly charring food that way. cmclaughlin [[./thread#^c49239328|cmclaughlin]] says their Bosch allows pan lifting and tilting, countering hellisothers’ objection [[./thread#^c49234193|hellisothers]]. These differences remain dependent on technique, cookware, and appliance behavior.
- Affordable induction quality and durability are also unsettled. PhotonHunter [[./thread#^c49235507|PhotonHunter]] raises cycling, small coils, and longevity concerns; LargoLasskhyfv [[./thread#^c49251928|LargoLasskhyfv]] reports a roughly €25 unit still working after fifteen years with different behavior. Subsequent discussion does not identify a verified comparable low-cost US product, and PhotonHunter [[./thread#^c49311049|PhotonHunter]] disputes the suggested IKEA example. Neither account supports a universal judgment about induction reliability.
