# [The US stops sharing air quality data from embassies worldwide](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43274821)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion responds to the end of public sharing of air-quality readings collected at US embassies, while the monitors reportedly continue operating. Participants describe the value of independent measurements for public awareness and policy, including accounts of data challenging official pollution reporting in China and India. They debate the likely cost and purpose of the sharing network, whether the change reflects budget cuts, administrative decisions, ideology, or a deliberate policy choice, and whether embassies should provide this service. Broader threads address data transparency, scientific trust, international sovereignty and soft power, government spending and oversight, public infrastructure versus private provision, and the consequences of withdrawing US services for international trust and influence. The comments also branch into US politics and government cuts, including disagreements about their rationale and effects.
Tags: air-quality monitoring, data transparency, public information, US embassies, China, India, international relations, government spending, public infrastructure, science policy

## Relevance

The discussion centers on air-quality monitoring and PM2.5 readings, with firsthand accounts of living in Beijing, using readings to decide whether to go outside, and leaving because of children’s exposure. Comments compare official and embassy data, note differing AQI scales and thresholds, and discuss the availability and limitations of consumer sensors. It is primarily about monitoring and public data rather than mitigation. ## Arguments

- Timely readings can guide personal exposure decisions. viceconsole [[./thread#^c43276805|viceconsole]] used both State Department and host-country AQI reports to decide whether to spend time outdoors; seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c43286489|seanmcdirmid]] checked readings before winter walks in Beijing. These accounts demonstrate practical use, although neither quantifies the exposure avoided.
- Independent measurements may make pollution harder to dismiss. defrost [[./thread#^c43275718|defrost]] quotes news reports attributing improved disclosure and air quality in China partly to embassy PM2.5 reporting. Beijing resident insane\_dreamer [[./thread#^c43276734|insane\_dreamer]] strongly supports that interpretation. cloudbonsai [[./thread#^c43278110|cloudbonsai]] proposes a feedback mechanism—visible measurements motivate action—but illustrates it with electricity consumption, not an air-quality intervention study.
- A trusted source can matter beyond the availability of inexpensive sensors. willvarfar [[./thread#^c43278743|willvarfar]] emphasizes confidence in official US numbers, while l33tman [[./thread#^c43278438|l33tman]] points to Americans visiting or working in China who need usable exposure information. These are arguments about credibility and accessibility, not demonstrations of measurement accuracy.
- Stopping public reporting differs from stopping measurement. adamiscool8 [[./thread#^c43275376|adamiscool8]] quotes a statement directing embassies to keep monitors running, with sharing potentially restored later. Retained recordings would not preserve the immediate public access used for outdoor decisions by viceconsole [[./thread#^c43276805|viceconsole]].

## Counterarguments

- Embassy stations may be less indispensable now that alternatives exist. refurb [[./thread#^c43276125|refurb]] reports seeing numerous stations on IQAir, including seven in Yangon; maxglute [[./thread#^c43277102|maxglute]] claims purifier-based AQI and PM2.5 monitoring is widespread in Chinese cities. Replying directly to refurb, ImaCake [[./thread#^c43276289|ImaCake]] agrees the loss may be mostly symbolic but cautions that stations are not equivalent. Station counts alone do not establish comparable quality or coverage.
- The claim that embassy reporting caused China's improvements is contested. maxglute [[./thread#^c43277354|maxglute]] says factory relocation, construction-dust controls, vehicle-registration restrictions and stronger emissions standards were already underway before the 2008 Olympics. This offers an alternative explanation involving domestic action, but the comment supplies no measurements isolating those policies' contributions.
- Visible smog may already establish that a problem exists. genewitch [[./thread#^c43276657|genewitch]] challenges the assumption that residents needed American numbers to notice polluted air. diggan [[./thread#^c43277650|diggan]] responds with a quoted news account crediting contradictory numerical reporting with prompting action. Recognizing pollution, quantifying its severity and changing policy are different claims; the exchange does not establish which depended on embassy data.
- On travel advice specifically, mytailorisrich [[./thread#^c43278594|mytailorisrich]] argues that a general warning about high pollution is sufficient. mrweasel [[./thread#^c43278946|mrweasel]] replies that someone accustomed to rural France or Scandinavia may not understand Beijing's severity without more information, and notes that Danish advice links to measurements. This disagreement concerns the usefulness of detailed reporting, not whether pollution exists.
- Cheap consumer hardware does not establish the cost or reliability of an institutional monitoring service. mc32 [[./thread#^c43276002|mc32]] raises calibration and software-maintenance requirements. viceconsole [[./thread#^c43276805|viceconsole]] acknowledges that consumer equipment and its networking may be unsuitable for an embassy, while arguing that professional installations could still be substantially cheaper than the estimates discussed.

## Assumptions

- For story 43274821, part 0, the substantive exposure evidence concerns outdoor AQI/PM2.5 and access to readings, exemplified by viceconsole [[./thread#^c43276805|viceconsole]] and insane\_dreamer [[./thread#^c43282722|insane\_dreamer]]. This unit supplies no substantive NO2 or CO2 measurements, cognition or sleep results, measured comfort effects, cooking-exposure accounts, wildfire-smoke experiences or firsthand traffic-exposure measurements. Those portions of the approved question cannot be answered from this unit.
- The numerical anecdotes are recollections without raw logs, instrument details or averaging periods. seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c43286489|seanmcdirmid]] describes a winter reading as '200 2.5 pmi' within an AQI discussion; insane\_dreamer [[./thread#^c43282722|insane\_dreamer]] explicitly uses micrograms per cubic metre. Treating these as directly comparable concentrations would require assumptions the comments do not support.
- Attributing disagreement between stations entirely to dishonest reporting assumes comparable locations, times and measurement methods. seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c43277167|seanmcdirmid]] remembers authorities arguing that the embassy's Liangmaqiao area was unusually polluted and dismisses that explanation. The comment provides no simultaneous measurements testing the spatial explanation.
- The broader sensing-data controversy does not demonstrate manipulation of embassy PM2.5 readings. jandrewrogers [[./thread#^c43276236|jandrewrogers]] alleges US data editing and censorship, then acknowledges that some editing serves legitimate data-cleaning purposes [[./thread#^c43276604|jandrewrogers]]. defrost [[./thread#^c43276590|defrost]] distinguishes removing sensor errors from concealing data; mturmon [[./thread#^c43283823|mturmon]] disputes the allegation's breadth based on GNSS work. These examples concern other sensing systems, not a validated finding about the air-quality feed.
- The effectiveness of the household responses remains an assumption. insane\_dreamer [[./thread#^c43282722|insane\_dreamer]] describes closing windows and staying indoors during severe outdoor pollution, but supplies no paired indoor/outdoor readings. YetAnotherNick [[./thread#^c43277377|YetAnotherNick]] describes reliable purifier telemetry without reporting pollutant reductions. Neither establishes how much filtration or window closure reduced exposure, or when opening windows would improve rather than worsen indoor conditions.

## Conditions

- Health labels and concentration limits require context. tellarin [[./thread#^c43277569|tellarin]] argues that China's scale communicates less severe health implications. homebrewer [[./thread#^c43281746|homebrewer]] claims that up to 70 µg/m³ is treated as safe in China but extreme under EU categories, and contrasts this with a WHO annual recommendation of 5 µg/m³. These are commenter-supplied claims; the 70 figure lacks an averaging period. Differences in labels are also distinct from falsifying measured concentrations.
- Dependence on an independent source varies geographically. iamshs [[./thread#^c43275820|iamshs]] calls embassy data especially reliable in Delhi and alleges that local monitors were sprayed with water to improve their immediate surroundings. The comment provides no firsthand documentation or comparative readings. Conversely, refurb [[./thread#^c43276125|refurb]] reports several alternative stations in Yangon. Neither account establishes conditions throughout its country.
- Quoted equipment prices concern different products and services. EVa5I7bHFq9mnYK [[./thread#^c43278585|EVa5I7bHFq9mnYK]] claims a PM2.5 device costs \$5; seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c43277167|seanmcdirmid]] mentions \$90 consumer monitors. schiffern [[./thread#^c43277448|schiffern]] reports that embassies appear to use pairs of Met One BAM-1020 instruments costing about \$40,000 each. viceconsole [[./thread#^c43276805|viceconsole]] repeats subreddit estimates of \$180,000–\$250,000 per installed post plus contractor maintenance; these are not personal procurement records or verified invoices.
- Total program expenditure and savings from withholding an existing feed are different quantities. genewitch [[./thread#^c43276207|genewitch]] invokes a \$15 million cost; epistasis [[./thread#^c43282648|epistasis]] says that figure belongs to the EPA analysis platform rather than adding embassy data. adamiscool8 [[./thread#^c43276151|adamiscool8]] describes DOSAir as using existing AirNow infrastructure. None supplies an audited recurring cost for public embassy reporting, so claims of either negligible costs or large savings remain unverified.

## Lived experiences

- viceconsole [[./thread#^c43276805|viceconsole]] reports developing asthma for the first time during a two-year consular posting in an unnamed country with severe pollution, with effects persisting after departure. They attribute this to exposure and used two countries' AQI reports to plan outdoor activity. This is a firsthand health account, without clinical documentation, measured dose or identification of the responsible pollutant.
- insane\_dreamer [[./thread#^c43286413|insane\_dreamer]] recalls somewhat better Beijing air by their 2017 departure than in the early 2010s: severe days remained, but seemed less frequent near Beida. In a follow-up, insane\_dreamer [[./thread#^c43287132|insane\_dreamer]] says protecting young children from the air was a reason for leaving, despite that relative improvement.
- seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c43286489|seanmcdirmid]] lived in Chaoyang and worked near PKU, perceived little difference between those areas, and remembers winter pollution continuing to constrain evening walks. Pregnancy and concern about a baby's exposure prompted departure in August 2016. seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c43277167|seanmcdirmid]] distinguishes that personal experience from later reports that Beijing had become much cleaner.
- insane\_dreamer [[./thread#^c43282722|insane\_dreamer]] recalls treating concentrations below 150 µg/m³ as a 'good day' and below 50 as 'amazing'; precautions such as closing windows began above 250, and they stayed indoors above roughly 400–500. This illustrates adaptation to a highly polluted baseline. These were personal historical thresholds, not evidence of safety or demonstrated protection indoors.
- ukoki [[./thread#^c43277349|ukoki]] remembers living in Beijing when the embassy's Twitter account reported a value above its measurable range, explicitly identifying the wording as a paraphrase. The recollection conveys an exceptional episode but supplies neither a concentration nor enough information to distinguish an instrument limit from a reporting limit.
- YetAnotherNick [[./thread#^c43277377|YetAnotherNick]] reports that a \$150 air purifier collected data and synchronized with the cloud successfully for four years. This is firsthand evidence of operational durability and convenient reporting, not validation of sensor accuracy, filtration performance or health improvement.
- thaumasiotes [[./thread#^c43295524|thaumasiotes]] recounts a Shanghai friend's description of a workplace colleague who strongly disliked America but appreciated its air-quality numbers. This is a firsthand recollection of a conversation containing a secondhand workplace account. maxglute [[./thread#^c43298669|maxglute]] challenges its representativeness; it cannot establish nationwide attitudes.

## Unresolved disagreements

- How much did embassy disclosure contribute to cleaner air? insane\_dreamer [[./thread#^c43276734|insane\_dreamer]] attributes substantial policy impact to it, supported by reporting quoted by defrost [[./thread#^c43275718|defrost]]. maxglute [[./thread#^c43277354|maxglute]] emphasizes earlier domestic pollution-control efforts. The supplied material cannot separate the contribution of public pressure from existing policies or quantify resulting exposure and health benefits.
- How much had Beijing improved by 2016–2017, and how much did neighborhoods differ? insane\_dreamer [[./thread#^c43286413|insane\_dreamer]] remembers fewer severe days and stresses location; seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c43286489|seanmcdirmid]] recalls persistently bad winters and doubts a major Haidian–Chaoyang difference. Different departure dates, seasons, locations and pregnancy-related attention limit direct comparison. No shared time series resolves the disagreement.
- Can other stations adequately replace the embassy reference? refurb [[./thread#^c43276125|refurb]] emphasizes plentiful alternatives, while ImaCake [[./thread#^c43276289|ImaCake]] cautions about unequal stations and willvarfar [[./thread#^c43278743|willvarfar]] emphasizes trust. The discussion provides no comparative calibration results, matched readings or evidence that alternative feeds offer equivalent independence, continuity and local relevance.
- What does maintaining public access actually cost? viceconsole [[./thread#^c43276805|viceconsole]] argues for a less expensive professional implementation; defrost [[./thread#^c43276071|defrost]] expects modest maintenance based on experience with other instrumentation. DidYaWipe [[./thread#^c43285247|DidYaWipe]] argues that equipment costs should not be counted as savings when operation continues, while aqueueaqueue [[./thread#^c43278115|aqueueaqueue]] stresses human troubleshooting costs beyond transmission. A service budget separating equipment, maintenance and publication would be needed to resolve these competing estimates.
