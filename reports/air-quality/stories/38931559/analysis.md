# [Contra Wirecutter on the IKEA air purifier (2022)](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=38931559)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

The discussion evaluates a critique of Wirecutter’s review of an IKEA air purifier. Participants debate the technical meaning of HEPA ratings and whether filter efficiency or clean-air delivery rate matters more in a room purifier, while sharing experiences with IKEA, Coway, and DIY Corsi–Rosenthal-style units. Practical topics include airflow, noise, room coverage, filter cost and replacement, sensor reliability, smart-home integration, and controlling dust, smoke, allergens, and odors. A separate extended thread disputes the critique’s factual accuracy and polemical tone, and questions Wirecutter’s review methods, affiliate links, and editorial independence.
Tags: air purifiers, HEPA filters, product reviews, CADR, DIY filtration, affiliate marketing

## Relevance

Comments compare purifier performance, filtration rates, noise and cost, and report experiences with allergies, congestion, traffic pollution, wildfire smoke, cooking, and indoor particles. The discussion also covers sensor limitations and the tradeoff between filtration and fresh-air ventilation, including CO2. ## Arguments

- For recirculating room purifiers, makomk [[./thread#^c38944324|makomk]] argues that airflow through the filter matters more than a small difference between 99.5\% and 99.95\% single-pass efficiency, because much room air has not yet reached the filter. jerlam [[./thread#^c38945651|jerlam]] therefore favors clean air delivery rate (CADR), which combines airflow and filtration efficiency, and cites high-airflow MERV-13 Corsi–Rosenthal boxes as an alternative to HEPA-rated machines.
- Ventilation is presented as necessary for managing accumulated CO2 in occupied rooms. BHSPitMonkey [[./thread#^c38949302|BHSPitMonkey]] recommends mechanical ventilation with heat or energy recovery for sealed buildings; timc3 [[./thread#^c38949315|timc3]] advocates properly sized air-handling units that recover heat and can filter incoming air. christiangenco [[./thread#^c38949038|christiangenco]] also proposes a window-installed ERV for CO2 and VOC control, without supplying performance measurements in this discussion.
- Monitoring can reveal sources and encourage practical changes even when absolute accuracy is uncertain. greggsy [[./thread#^c38946787|greggsy]] emphasizes observing changes associated with windows, household activities, volatile substances, dust and pollen; imp0cat [[./thread#^c38949740|imp0cat]] describes a CO2 sensor as a reminder to ventilate. These are arguments for learning a home's patterns, rather than treating every displayed number as a precise exposure assessment.
- Standard furnace filters and separate fans offer a potentially inexpensive route to substantial particle filtration. grobbyy [[./thread#^c38933012|grobbyy]] argues for spending on a capable, quiet fan rather than proprietary filters; rapind [[./thread#^c38946409|rapind]] describes a four-filter MERV-13 box. Their proposals concern practical particle removal, not demonstrated removal of every gaseous pollutant.
- Several replies favor addressing the source and matching the intervention to the problem. callalex [[./thread#^c38949716|callalex]] suggests investigating dryer ventilation, HVAC leakage or building leakage when basement dust requires daily cleaning. For dust-mite allergens, amluto [[./thread#^c38953638|amluto]] recommends bedding encasements, washing, vacuum filtration and humidity management alongside air filtration. These are proposed explanations and interventions, not diagnoses of the commenters' homes.
- A purifier's usable operating setting matters more than performance that makes occupants switch it off: lathiat [[./thread#^c38935010|lathiat]] makes this point explicitly, and genman [[./thread#^c38933975|genman]] prioritizes actual operating noise. Control convenience may also help: whazor [[./thread#^c38944419|whazor]] describes smart-socket control of the simpler IKEA unit, while Mister\_Snuggles [[./thread#^c38943789|Mister\_Snuggles]] values STARKVIND's Zigbee measurements and controls.
- Activated carbon is valued for odor and comfort improvements. mschuster91 [[./thread#^c38945377|mschuster91]] reports markedly reduced cooking and cigarette smells with the STARKVIND carbon filter, while liminalsunset [[./thread#^c38949326|liminalsunset]] describes continued odor reduction from homemade filters containing more carbon. These observations support an odor-control use case, without establishing removal of particular hazardous gases.

## Counterarguments

- Adding air conditioning does not necessarily provide ventilation. Gare [[./thread#^c38948947|Gare]], directly replying to kettleballroll's suggestion that AC might solve rising CO2, says most European home AC systems do not supply fresh air. The relevant distinction in this exchange is outdoor-air exchange versus cooling or recirculation.
- Low particle readings do not establish that all relevant exposures are controlled. londons\_explore [[./thread#^c38943728|londons\_explore]] expects fan-and-filter arrangements to have little effect on VOCs, NOx or CO, despite helping PM2.5 and PM10. mac-mc [[./thread#^c38945280|mac-mc]] similarly says proximity to highways can remain associated with adverse health statistics even when a household PM monitor shows little concern; that is a claim about studies, not evidence supplied here.
- Consumer measurements receive substantial skepticism. KennyBlanken [[./thread#^c38944602|KennyBlanken]] considers many inexpensive particle sensors useful only for relative measurements; bborud [[./thread#^c38949798|bborud]] criticizes inexpensive particle sensors, some HVAC sensors and CO2 cross-sensitivity. Neither supplies a model-by-model validation dataset, so these warnings should not become a demonstrated verdict on every consumer sensor or sensing technology.
- A large displayed spike is not itself a toxicity measurement. bennyg [[./thread#^c38949060|bennyg]] asks whether a deodorant burst is harmful or merely raises the readings; theshrike79 [[./thread#^c38949738|theshrike79]] replies that particle sensing does not identify composition. wasmitnetzen [[./thread#^c38949804|wasmitnetzen]] suggests alcohol-containing deodorants may involve comparatively less harmful VOCs, but offers no exposure-specific risk assessment.
- Quiet operation can come at the expense of sufficient cleaning. Marsymars [[./thread#^c38946792|Marsymars]] reports inadequate wildfire protection at low speed despite five oversized purifiers. turtlebits [[./thread#^c38944921|turtlebits]] objects that the small IKEA unit's stated low-speed coverage is only 85 square feet, below their smallest room's 110 square feet. Aurornis [[./thread#^c38945449|Aurornis]] counters that longer operation can still help when outdoor-air replacement is limited.
- Prioritizing CADR does not make filter standards meaningless. croes [[./thread#^c38943539|croes]] distinguishes H13 HEPA from lower EPA classes; schiffern [[./thread#^c38944272|schiffern]] explains the competing 99.95\% and 99.97\% figures as international versus US standards. CharlesW [[./thread#^c38945045|CharlesW]] argues that distinguishing genuinely compliant filters from branding such as HEPASilent has value, while expressly declining to equate the branding distinction with better overall purifier performance.
- thadk's hypothesis that overloaded filters released dust-mite allergens back into the room is challenged by amluto [[./thread#^c38958636|amluto]], who says ordinary filters should not behave that way and proposes airflow placement as another explanation. amluto also admits having once left protective plastic on an IKEA filter, illustrating a setup failure without establishing that thadk made the same mistake.
- CPAP filtration should not be equated with HEPA room filtration. TylerE [[./thread#^c38946482|TylerE]] describes basic cellulose filters intended mainly for larger debris. mook [[./thread#^c38949179|mook]] raises Philips Respironics foam and formaldehyde concerns and mentions the manufacturer's claim that emissions were below limits; the comment provides no measurements with which to resolve that device-specific dispute.
- Adding UV is not presented as an automatic improvement. Responding to froglets' concern about household infections [[./thread#^c38947052|froglets]], Toutouxc [[./thread#^c38949358|Toutouxc]] argues that effective irradiation requires sufficient exposure time and that filtration already removes captured particles from circulation. No microbial-removal or inactivation measurements are supplied to compare configurations.

## Assumptions

- The evidence is confined to story 38931559, part 0: individual comments, anecdotes, specifications and descriptions of outside material. For example, mac-mc [[./thread#^c38945280|mac-mc]] refers to highway-health studies and CharlesW [[./thread#^c38944881|CharlesW]] describes a sleep-noise review, but neither underlying source is supplied for independent examination. These comments do not establish population-wide HN agreement.
- Comparing sensors requires comparable placement and conditions. Mister\_Snuggles [[./thread#^c38944455|Mister\_Snuggles]] clarifies that the apparently disagreeing IKEA sensor was downstairs while the purifiers were upstairs, and that a colocated comparison was only planned. timenova [[./thread#^c38944150|timenova]] offers proximity to purifier outlets as an explicitly untested explanation; KennyBlanken [[./thread#^c38944629|KennyBlanken]] proposes humidity and different particle sources as additional explanations.
- The numerical displays cannot all be treated as equivalent exposure measurements. wkat4242 [[./thread#^c38947352|wkat4242]] gives VOC and PM2.5 readings without units; theshrike79 [[./thread#^c38949738|theshrike79]] likewise gives unitless PM2.5-display values. jfim [[./thread#^c38947101|jfim]] reports AQI, whereas wayfinder [[./thread#^c38950134|wayfinder]] reports CO2 in ppm. Converting these into comparable concentrations, doses or health risks would require information absent here.
- Room-cleaning arguments assume particular source rates, outdoor-air infiltration, mixing and operating duration. Aurornis [[./thread#^c38945449|Aurornis]] explicitly conditions success on limited replacement by outside air. SamBam [[./thread#^c38947045|SamBam]] clarifies that the proposed four air changes per hour for allergies refers to equivalent filtered-air changes, not necessarily replacing indoor air with outdoor air every fifteen minutes.
- Successful filter substitution assumes that the adapted assembly preserves an adequate seal and useful filtration. liminalsunset [[./thread#^c38949288|liminalsunset]] describes cardboard sealing and rudimentary anemometer measurements after fitting an IKEA filter into a Coway. That supports a report about airflow and fit, but does not independently establish particle-capture efficiency or the modified machine's CADR.
- Symptom improvements remain observational. wincy's report about their wife's asthma [[./thread#^c38945704|wincy]] and poidos's report of reduced allergies and morning congestion [[./thread#^c38943710|poidos]] lack controlled comparisons. thadk [[./thread#^c38957534|thadk]] uses several simultaneous interventions, including cleaning, ventilation, filtration and medication, making the contribution of any single measure difficult to isolate.
- bborud's building observations [[./thread#^c38949798|bborud]] were recalled from work emphasizing relative changes with inexpensive sensors. The management meeting room was also large and usually no more than one-quarter occupied, confounding comparisons with other rooms. The suggested explanation of evening basement readings through HVAC backflow and CO2 pooling is not independently demonstrated by the reported measurements.

## Conditions

- Replacement-filter prices strongly influence the value calculation. liminalsunset [[./thread#^c38949288|liminalsunset]] quotes \$14.99 CAD for STARKVIND filters and about \$7 for thinner FÖRNUFTIG filters, explicitly noting geographic price variation. timenova [[./thread#^c38944120|timenova]] identifies inexpensive replacements as the major advantage for prolonged use in a polluted city. These are prices reported around January 2024, not current quotations.
- Availability can undermine nominally cheap replacements. Although liminalsunset [[./thread#^c38949288|liminalsunset]] praises IKEA's broad availability, elabajaba [[./thread#^c38948709|elabajaba]] reports being unable to order STARKVIND filters online in Canada and living inconveniently far from a store. They recommend designing a DIY purifier around locally obtainable filters.
- DIY costs include consumables and fan longevity. rapind [[./thread#^c38946409|rapind]] quotes a roughly \$20 box fan; Bloating [[./thread#^c38945491|Bloating]] quotes about \$16.50 each for four 20-by-20-by-2-inch filters. genewitch [[./thread#^c38951369|genewitch]] reports roughly \$30 washable filters and box fans lasting perhaps one or two years in their experience. These are different configurations, without a matched lifetime-cost or filtration-performance comparison.
- Carbon-filter economics depend heavily on use. liminalsunset [[./thread#^c38949326|liminalsunset]] contrasts a typical six-month replacement recommendation with roughly one month of effectiveness for similar filters under heavy use; homemade filters with two to three times the carbon developed odd smells after two to three months. Doxin [[./thread#^c38949814|Doxin]] rejects monthly precautionary replacement and cannot readily rely on smell because of a poor sense of smell.
- Climate and building construction complicate ventilation. wayfinder [[./thread#^c38950134|wayfinder]] keeps a bedroom window open even in cold US weather to manage CO2. thadk [[./thread#^c38957534|thadk]] says lowering humidity below 50\% in an older building in a rainy climate can require substantial electricity, while sealing the building introduces other air-quality concerns. The energy requirement is their assessment, not a supplied metered comparison.
- Outdoor air is not interchangeable across locations or seasons. timenova [[./thread#^c38944120|timenova]] reports substantial city pollution for two-thirds of the year; poidos [[./thread#^c38943710|poidos]] lives beside a busy road; sundvor [[./thread#^c38949570|sundvor]] prepares for Melbourne bushfire smoke after a severe previous episode. thadk [[./thread#^c38957534|thadk]] prefers fresh air particularly outside pollen season. These circumstances limit blanket advice to open windows.
- Filtered ventilation also has financial and seasonal constraints. Marsymars [[./thread#^c38957582|Marsymars]] wants a filtered inward-blowing window fan controlled by outdoor temperature, useful for months of the year in their location, but regards an available product as expensive and questions after-sales support. This is a proposed solution, not a tested installation.
- Lower noise may justify a higher purchase price, but specifications need comparable operating conditions. CharlesW [[./thread#^c38943922|CharlesW]] quotes minimum levels of 28 dB for FÖRNUFTIG and 24 dB for the Coway Mighty; imp0cat [[./thread#^c38948550|imp0cat]] quotes 17–49 dB for a more expensive Electrolux/AEG model versus an IKEA maximum of 60 dB. These quoted figures do not establish equal clean-air delivery at those settings.
- Features add another budget tradeoff. Toutouxc [[./thread#^c38949310|Toutouxc]] says the smart IKEA model costs about twice as much, while the cheaper model retains a mechanical fan-speed selection and can therefore work with a switched outlet. wildzzz [[./thread#^c38956082|wildzzz]] argues that useful buying comparisons should match either budget or required performance, rather than simply reward the strongest machine regardless of price.

## Lived experiences

- kettleballroll [[./thread#^c38948929|kettleballroll]] reports unexpectedly rapid CO2 accumulation in a newly constructed European home, with brief window opening insufficient to maintain the desired level. In a US home they describe as not particularly well sealed, wayfinder [[./thread#^c38950134|wayfinder]] reports an Aranet4 reading increasing by 600 ppm in 30 minutes and approaching 2000 ppm by morning unless the bedroom window remains open. Neither reports measured cognitive or sleep changes alongside those readings.
- From commercial-building experiments, bborud [[./thread#^c38949798|bborud]] recalls most measured rooms failing to remain below 1000 ppm and occupied meeting rooms often exceeding 1200–1300 ppm. A larger, lightly occupied management room remained around 600–800 ppm. Basement sensors reportedly saturated overnight after HVAC shutdown around 18:00 and recovered after morning operation resumed; the saturation concentration and independent confirmation are unspecified.
- wkat4242 [[./thread#^c38947352|wkat4242]] reports a VOC display rising from about 200 to 20000 after cleaning a washing basket with a little alcohol, remaining elevated for roughly a day despite no noticeable smell. They also report that burning a candle increased PM2.5 and that the smoke after extinguishing it produced a peak around 50 from a usual reading near 1. Units and calibration are not provided.
- imp0cat [[./thread#^c38948493|imp0cat]] nearly stopped using candles after observing their purifier's particle and VOC monitoring, and reports that a deodorant spray briefly triggers strong purifier activity. In the reply chain questioning what such spikes mean, theshrike79 [[./thread#^c38949738|theshrike79]] reports an ultrasonic humidifier raising a PM2.5 display from 1–2 to 200–250. Neither observation establishes the toxicity of the detected material.
- Low-speed wildfire performance differs sharply between homes. Marsymars [[./thread#^c38946792|Marsymars]], using an Airthings sensor in a 1970s house, reports that five oversized purifiers on low barely accelerated clearance of indoor emissions and could not keep wildfire PM2.5 acceptable. jfim [[./thread#^c38947101|jfim]] reports two units rated for about 350 square feet each maintaining AQI at zero or single digits during wildfires in a 1500-square-foot house built in 2008.
- poidos [[./thread#^c38943710|poidos]] reports allergies and morning congestion becoming nearly absent after using a Sqair continuously with a carbon filter in a condo beside a very busy road. They want a second unit because the stated coverage is 430 square feet versus a 730-square-foot condo. No before-and-after particle readings or separation of carbon versus particle-filter effects is supplied.
- wincy [[./thread#^c38945704|wincy]] reports substantial improvement in their wife's asthma after adopting box fans and filters, purchasing four filters together for a claimed 50\% bulk discount. Bloating [[./thread#^c38945491|Bloating]] reports DIY filtration working well during post-construction cleanup and seeming to help during wildfire smoke. Both are favorable firsthand accounts without quantified exposure or clinical outcomes.
- Experiences with the small IKEA purifier conflict. timenova [[./thread#^c38944120|timenova]] describes it as effective in a city with prolonged pollution. thadk [[./thread#^c38943948|thadk]] found it insufficient for dust-mite allergy relief compared with similarly sized Coway equipment and disliked cleaning the prefilter. In follow-up, thadk [[./thread#^c38957534|thadk]] reports already using extensive cleaning precautions and relying on filtration particularly during exercise, symptomatic periods and pollen season; the reason for the performance difference remains unmeasured.
- For cooking, mschuster91 [[./thread#^c38945377|mschuster91]] reports that STARKVIND with carbon prevented food smells reaching an adjacent room while frying and chopping onions, also reduced cigarette smells near an open window, and collected cat fur. sundvor [[./thread#^c38949570|sundvor]] reports Samsung AX90 units automatically ramping from quiet operation to high airflow during cooking. These are odor and device-response observations, not measurements of cooking-related NO2 or health effects.
- liminalsunset [[./thread#^c38949288|liminalsunset]] fitted a STARKVIND filter into a Coway Mighty using cardboard to seal the size difference. They report acceptable airflow from rudimentary anemometer testing and approximately 10\% airflow loss after a year or more of continuous use, assessed around replacement. No corresponding particle-removal measurement is given.
- Noise preferences vary. firecall [[./thread#^c38944534|firecall]] finds their STARKVIND table intolerable above the first setting without noise-canceling headphones. elabajaba [[./thread#^c38948709|elabajaba]] built a computer-fan purifier and reports better acoustic comfort, especially with selected premium fans. Conversely, karaterobot [[./thread#^c38943293|karaterobot]] enjoys purifier background noise and notices its absence after a power interruption.
- poulsbohemian [[./thread#^c38948413|poulsbohemian]] reports finding CPAP filtration beneficial despite its limitations. TylerE [[./thread#^c38948527|TylerE]], describing persistent allergies and long-term CPAP use, attributes a major additional benefit to humidification: avoiding winter nosebleeds that had occurred about three times weekly. This account specifically distinguishes humidification comfort from the machine's usual sleep-related benefit.
- A low fine-particle display can coexist with a troublesome coarse-dust problem. stavros [[./thread#^c38946018|stavros]] reports an IKEA sensor consistently around 5–6 μg/m³ while cat hair remains airborne, and later reports a failed makeshift filter attempt [[./thread#^c38946288|stavros]]. heeen2 [[./thread#^c38949473|heeen2]] separately describes basement lint-like dust requiring daily vacuuming, without identifying its source or supplying particle measurements.
- chx [[./thread#^c38943396|chx]] reports an amateur comparison in which the Dyson Zone appeared substantially weaker than a CleanSpace Halo, although the Dyson looked less conspicuous. After throwup238 [[./thread#^c38943698|throwup238]] argues that seal and adequate filtered airflow are critical, chx [[./thread#^c38944385|chx]] decides to retain the Halo. No test method, exposure reduction or protective-factor measurements are supplied.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The CO2 readings do not settle cognitive thresholds or sleep effects. kettleballroll [[./thread#^c38948929|kettleballroll]] invokes an 800 ppm public-building norm and possible cognitive effects, but supplies no study or cognitive testing. wayfinder [[./thread#^c38950134|wayfinder]] demonstrates a personally observed overnight ventilation problem, not a measured causal relationship between those concentrations and sleep quality. The discussion does not validate a universal healthy/unhealthy cutoff.
- NO2-specific evidence is missing. londons\_explore [[./thread#^c38943728|londons\_explore]] mentions limited removal of NOx generally, and mac-mc [[./thread#^c38945280|mac-mc]] invokes highway-health associations without pollutant-specific measurements. 7thaccount [[./thread#^c38947351|7thaccount]] assumes those associations reflect insufficient household filtration, but provides no supporting comparison. The unit cannot establish whether particle filtration eliminates traffic-related risk or how cooking changes NO2 exposure.
- Absolute sensor reliability remains unresolved. Mister\_Snuggles [[./thread#^c38944455|Mister\_Snuggles]] never supplies the planned colocated comparison, and bborud [[./thread#^c38949798|bborud]] gives no reference-instrument validation for the building experiments. KennyBlanken's humidity explanation [[./thread#^c38944629|KennyBlanken]] is plausible within the discussion but untested there. Large trends may motivate investigation without resolving their precise magnitude or composition.
- The health significance of household aerosol spikes is unsettled. bennyg's deodorant question [[./thread#^c38949060|bennyg]] receives no dose-based answer. amluto [[./thread#^c38953212|amluto]] proposes that ultrasonic humidifiers aerosolize salts and potentially bacteria, while imp0cat [[./thread#^c39030338|imp0cat]] recommends distilled water or an evaporative design and warns about tap-water dust. No supplied experiment measures resulting health effects or compares those alternatives under controlled conditions.
- The thread does not establish a universally superior filter grade, purifier or quiet setting. makomk [[./thread#^c38944324|makomk]] emphasizes CADR over small efficiency differences, while BarbaryCoast [[./thread#^c38948703|BarbaryCoast]] insists the significance of H13 versus E12 depends on application and environment. The opposing wildfire accounts from Marsymars [[./thread#^c38946792|Marsymars]] and jfim [[./thread#^c38947101|jfim]] reinforce that building infiltration, operating speed and pollution load prevent a simple product-wide conclusion.
- Filter loading and replacement failure modes remain disputed. DannyBee [[./thread#^c38945220|DannyBee]] argues that rated efficiency can depend on loading; KennyBlanken [[./thread#^c38944961|KennyBlanken]] emphasizes falling box-fan airflow as resistance rises. thadk [[./thread#^c38957534|thadk]] describes filters becoming worse than nothing after one to five months in their home, but amluto [[./thread#^c38958636|amluto]] disputes ordinary-filter re-emission as the mechanism. No controlled particle or airflow series resolves that disagreement.
- White noise and sleep are contested rather than settled by an appeal to science. staplers [[./thread#^c38943359|staplers]] claims white noise is proven to degrade sleep and focus unless masking worse noise. CharlesW [[./thread#^c38944881|CharlesW]] disputes that characterization, describing a review of 38 studies in which one suggested more disrupted sleep and further research was requested. karaterobot's preference for purifier noise [[./thread#^c38943293|karaterobot]] establishes individual comfort, not a general sleep benefit.
- There is no measured universal window-opening strategy. wayfinder [[./thread#^c38950134|wayfinder]] reports needing sustained opening for CO2, while mac-mc [[./thread#^c38945280|mac-mc]] describes competing ventilation, filtration and humidity goals near traffic, and thadk [[./thread#^c38957534|thadk]] qualifies a preference for fresh air by pollen season. Filtered mechanical ventilation is proposed, but this unit supplies no paired indoor/outdoor measurements or comprehensive comparison establishing the best operating schedule.
- Outdoor protection beyond the home remains lightly evidenced. jononomo [[./thread#^c38950605|jononomo]] asks whether building-sized outdoor filters could improve the air people breathe, without receiving feasibility or exposure data here. chx's wearable-device comparison [[./thread#^c38943396|chx]] likewise leaves actual inhaled exposure reduction uncertain. Neither establishes a scalable or quantitatively validated solution for outdoor exposure.
