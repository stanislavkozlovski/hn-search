# [Ikea Vindriktning Air Quality Sensor Review and Accuracy](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=29458404)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion weighs the IKEA Vindriktning’s low price and easy-to-read indicator against its accuracy, calibration, and generous “good” threshold. Participants compare it with commercial and DIY monitors, debate how reliably inexpensive sensors measure particulate matter, and share ways to connect the device to home dashboards. Other threads cover ventilation and air purifiers, sensor maintenance, accessibility and aesthetics, and whether consumer-friendly indicators should prioritize avoiding false alarms or warning about lower pollution levels.
Tags: air-quality sensors, PM2.5, measurement accuracy, DIY electronics, ventilation, air purifiers, consumer products

## Relevance

The review discussion is directly about PM2.5 monitoring and measurement reliability, including comparisons with reference instruments, calibration concerns, low-concentration limits, device failures, and differences between sensors. Firsthand accounts describe cooking-related particle spikes, wildfire smoke and filtration, ventilation and window use, and new-home VOC concerns. Comments also discuss CO2 as a ventilation proxy and possible effects on cognition and stuffiness, though many health interpretations are claims or personal observations rather than controlled evidence. ## Arguments

- Indoor sources can matter even where outdoor air seems clean. Someone1234 [[./thread#^c29460098|Someone1234]] reports an oven’s leftover pizza cheese triggering a yellow indication, and kccqzy [[./thread#^c29464600|kccqzy]] traced high kitchen PM2.5 readings to accumulated oven oil. These accounts support monitoring cooking and appliance cleanliness rather than inferring indoor conditions from neighborhood air quality.
- Cheap monitors can make previously invisible changes actionable. Someone1234 [[./thread#^c29459751|Someone1234]] values the Vindriktning’s low price, finished enclosure, and simple display; Foivos [[./thread#^c29459783|Foivos]] considers rough information better than having no monitor. This is an argument for accessible trend detection, not demonstrated accuracy or proof that a green light means healthy air.
- Numeric logging can make interventions easier to assess. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c29459929|ahaucnx]] describes adding an ESP8266 to retrieve readings hidden behind the traffic-light display; jve [[./thread#^c29461551|jve]] wants those readings to evaluate a purifier. pnathan [[./thread#^c29463494|pnathan]] already logs a Dylos through a serial connection and prefers particle measurements to a composite AQI label.
- The accounts support both controlling indoor sources and filtering particles, with effectiveness depending on the situation. kccqzy [[./thread#^c29464600|kccqzy]] found a kitchen window or dedicated range hood more effective than purchased purifiers for oven emissions. kfarr [[./thread#^c29464575|kfarr]] reports using an indoor PurpleAir to observe filters working during outdoor wildfire smoke. Neither supplies a controlled comparison or numerical removal rate.
- CO2 monitoring addresses a different question from particle monitoring. Animats [[./thread#^c29464672|Animats]] notes that the IKEA device does not measure CO2. mpalczewski [[./thread#^c29466708|mpalczewski]] argues that CO2 indicates accumulated exhaled air and inadequate ventilation, connecting it to infection exposure, stuffiness, and thinking or working comfortably. These are explanatory claims, not measured infection or cognitive outcomes in this thread.
- Several commenters argue that avoiding conspicuously bad air is insufficient for long-term protection. colechristensen [[./thread#^c29467055|colechristensen]] distinguishes acute warnings for sensitive groups from possible chronic effects at lower concentrations, while dognotdog [[./thread#^c29461398|dognotdog]] claims particulate health effects have no threshold. For CO2, eb0la [[./thread#^c29467567|eb0la]] cites a review to argue for staying below 1000 ppm in offices and schools. The supplied comments do not reproduce study methods or results that establish these claims.

## Counterarguments

- A reassuring color can conceal substantial uncertainty. FPGAhacker [[./thread#^c29467219|FPGAhacker]] quotes IKEA’s green range as 0–35 μg/m³, amber as 36–85, and red as 86 or higher. namdnay [[./thread#^c29460727|namdnay]] quotes the review’s finding that the IKEA readings were about 65\% of two comparison sensors’ readings; accountofme [[./thread#^c29469522|accountofme]] cites a ±20 μg/m³ specification. These are separate concerns about classification, relative bias, and stated accuracy, rather than interchangeable measures of error.
- Some defend permissive thresholds as a way to prevent constant alarms. srg0 [[./thread#^c29460862|srg0]] suggests reserving warnings for problems the device can identify confidently, explicitly saying green need not mean good air. djanogo [[./thread#^c29462876|djanogo]] worries that perpetual orange or red would discourage new users. stdbrouw [[./thread#^c29460222|stdbrouw]] also invokes larger particles and cleaner outdoor exposure to defend the cutoff. These are proposed rationales, not evidence that the resulting exposure is safe.
- Good correlation does not establish accurate exposure measurements across conditions. DannyBee [[./thread#^c29460741|DannyBee]] warns that sensors performing well against references can perform poorly in field evaluations. dognotdog [[./thread#^c29461398|dognotdog]] describes disagreement even among expensive instruments around 0–20 μg/m³, citing timing, particle composition and size, optical noise, temperature, and aging. jiveturkey [[./thread#^c29464469|jiveturkey]] adds that enclosure and assembly make accuracy a property of the finished device, limiting claims based only on a shared sensor module.
- The review’s commercial context was contested. jansan [[./thread#^c29460890|jansan]] distrusts its conclusions because the writer sells competing products; ahaucnx [[./thread#^c29459992|ahaucnx]] does promote an AirGradient build. colechristensen [[./thread#^c29461080|colechristensen]] responds that the permissive green range can be criticized independently of that affiliation. The conflict of interest warrants scrutiny but does not itself settle the numerical criticism.
- DIY affordability does not necessarily mean consumer accessibility. Someone1234 [[./thread#^c29460073|Someone1234]] defines accessibility as buying, plugging in, and understanding a finished product without soldering or printing an enclosure. mellavora [[./thread#^c29461939|mellavora]] replies that assembled AirGradient units exist, while zahma [[./thread#^c29465739|zahma]] describes a community build requiring neither soldering nor 3D printing. These alternatives reduce different barriers; they do not make every DIY recommendation equally accessible.
- A particle purifier cannot be assumed to solve every indoor-air problem. fma [[./thread#^c29463714|fma]] argues that ventilation is necessary for new-house off-gassing and categorically rejects filtration as a fix. GekkePrutser [[./thread#^c29468891|GekkePrutser]] counters that activated-carbon filters are sold for formaldehyde, while explicitly being unsure of their effectiveness and reporting only odor reduction. Neither establishes formaldehyde removal performance.
- Some product criticism was corrected within its reply chain. gonehome [[./thread#^c29462343|gonehome]] says Awair lacks useful AQI and PM2.5 output; nitrogen [[./thread#^c29466288|nitrogen]] explains where both appear, and gonehome [[./thread#^c29467353|gonehome]] acknowledges that their experience may concern an older version. Separately, 404mm [[./thread#^c29465342|404mm]] recalls favorable controlled testing but cannot provide the specific report. The feature dispute therefore has a correction, while the claimed validation remains unavailable here.
- Display accessibility has more than one dimension. FPGAhacker [[./thread#^c29467219|FPGAhacker]] objects to red, amber, and green for users with common color-vision deficiencies. Spivak [[./thread#^c29467933|Spivak]] replies that different illuminated positions also encode the state. That response addresses distinguishability, but does not resolve whether the categories communicate exposure accurately.

## Assumptions

- AQI values, particle concentrations, and health guideline averages must be distinguished. dahart [[./thread#^c29461653|dahart]] initially compares AirNow’s numerical AQI colors directly with IKEA’s concentration thresholds; ahaucnx [[./thread#^c29462554|ahaucnx]] explains that, in the discussion’s contemporary US scale, AQI 50 corresponded to roughly 12 μg/m³, and dahart [[./thread#^c29462825|dahart]] accepts the correction. pnathan [[./thread#^c29463494|pnathan]] additionally notes that AQI formulas differ between countries. Comparing matching colors or numbers alone assumes equivalence that the thread explicitly disproves.
- A device labeled CO2 must actually measure that gas if its ppm reading is to support a CO2-specific conclusion. numpad0 [[./thread#^c29460143|numpad0]] criticizes estimated eCO2 derived from other chemical responses. When GekkePrutser [[./thread#^c29468851|GekkePrutser]] raises that concern about the Senseair S8, ahaucnx [[./thread#^c29472139|ahaucnx]] identifies it as an NDIR CO2 sensor. That assurance concerns the named component, not every inexpensive device marketed as a CO2 meter.
- An apparently plausible baseline or a response to smoke does not independently establish accuracy. tdrdt [[./thread#^c29460186|tdrdt]] treats an outdoor reading near 400 ppm as reassurance about a cheap meter. AdrianoKF [[./thread#^c29523264|AdrianoKF]], responding to solomonb’s challenge [[./thread#^c29464967|solomonb]], clarifies that their successful IKEA modification had only been checked with cooking and smoke. Both demonstrate some operational behavior, with no reference calibration supplied.
- Interpreting fluctuating readings assumes suitable averaging, placement, and sampling. genewitch [[./thread#^c29464319|genewitch]] says their monitors recommend averages rather than instantaneous values, and suggests that purifier airflow might disturb dust. jve [[./thread#^c29464766|jve]] considers that explanation plausible but provides no test distinguishing dust resuspension from ongoing emissions, inadequate filtration, or sensor behavior.
- Comparing a local PM reading with an area’s AQI assumes comparable locations, times, and pollutants. dheera [[./thread#^c29466028|dheera]] reports zero readings despite an AQI of 90; zahma [[./thread#^c29470182|zahma]] diagnoses a likely malfunction, but neither establishes a colocated reference comparison. Given pnathan’s discussion of differing AQI calculations [[./thread#^c29463494|pnathan]], the discrepancy is evidence worth investigating rather than a calibrated error measurement.
- Health and comfort interpretations require causal assumptions beyond the reported readings. colechristensen [[./thread#^c29461028|colechristensen]] experienced apartment CO2 buildup but describes cognitive effects as expected, without reporting a cognitive test. dirtyid [[./thread#^c29460821|dirtyid]] says filtration seems to help allergies, without controlled symptom tracking. These accounts cannot isolate CO2 itself, particles, other correlated pollutants, or unrelated changes as the cause.

## Conditions

- Price strongly affects whether monitoring happens at all, and these are prices reported in 2021. LeanderK [[./thread#^c29459952|LeanderK]], living in a small city away from a major road, would spend only €10–15 because poor air quality was uncertain. 404mm [[./thread#^c29460035|404mm]] finds roughly \$300 per Awair difficult for multiple rooms; micheljansen [[./thread#^c29460053|micheljansen]] contrasts a \$200 entry PurpleAir with IKEA’s much lower price. bobiny [[./thread#^c29460320|bobiny]] describes a CO2-focused Netatmo configuration costing about \$200 initially and \$86 per additional room module.
- DIY economics depend on components, assembly, location, and delivery. mellavora [[./thread#^c29461588|mellavora]] lists an approximately \$50 AirGradient component set including a \$13.89 PM sensor and \$28 CO2 sensor. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c29460224|ahaucnx]] recommends a nonconnected SmartMi PM2.5 monitor costing about \$30 in Asia, and later estimates variable shipping of roughly two to three weeks for kits [[./thread#^c29461196|ahaucnx]]. These are not equivalent delivered, assembled products.
- Filter ownership has recurring costs. ortusdux [[./thread#^c29461861|ortusdux]] bought two Winix purifiers on sale for \$100 each, with two years of filters included and an estimated \$30 per unit annually afterward. genewitch [[./thread#^c29464319|genewitch]] uses three roughly \$15 filters in a humid forest setting; they become dark after about two months, although monthly replacement would be preferred if cheaper.
- Opening windows depends on the outdoor pollution context. genewitch [[./thread#^c29463833|genewitch]] reports using windows and exhaust fans to cycle indoor air, while kfarr [[./thread#^c29464575|kfarr]] describes smoky outdoor air during Bay Area wildfires. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c29460334|ahaucnx]] emphasizes that some locations have unhealthy outdoor air much of the year. Together these imply a conditional tradeoff: ventilation may dilute indoor CO2 or emissions while importing particles. No commenter supplies a controlled window-open versus window-closed exposure comparison.
- Building size, ventilation, and equipment capacity affect transferability. colechristensen [[./thread#^c29461028|colechristensen]] finds CO2 accumulation difficult to avoid in a modern apartment without outdoor-air ventilation or much more space. hedora [[./thread#^c29461909|hedora]] reports a purifier whose automatic fan response was too weak for its setting and estimates, without measured savings, that better central-blower control could cut fire-season electricity use by 20–30\%. colechristensen [[./thread#^c29467015|colechristensen]] also warns that highly restrictive furnace filters may reduce airflow.
- Local environmental conditions can change both exposure and maintenance. genewitch [[./thread#^c29464319|genewitch]] describes pollen, dust, pets, leaky windows, open screens, and year-round humidity. fma [[./thread#^c29463714|fma]] attributes changing off-gassing to humidity and season, without supplying a measurement series. These circumstances limit direct comparisons with an apartment in a cleaner or drier setting.
- Community-map coverage is geographically uneven. zahma [[./thread#^c29470137|zahma]] explains that sensor.community displays volunteers’ sensors rather than aggregating every available monitoring source. rplnt [[./thread#^c29473132|rplnt]] responds that this does not explain the particularly sharp Czech-border pattern they observed. A coverage gap therefore cannot be read as evidence of clean air, and its specific geographic cause remains unresolved.

## Lived experiences

- blacksmith\_tb [[./thread#^c29462975|blacksmith\_tb]] placed three Vindriktning units outdoors during wildfire smoke when reported outdoor air quality was around 150, with the scale unspecified in the comment. Two changed from yellow to red while one stayed green. This is a direct comparison between units suggesting inconsistent behavior, although no reference instrument or equilibration protocol is described.
- kccqzy [[./thread#^c29464600|kccqzy]] reports suspiciously high kitchen PM2.5 on a Temtop, traced to oil buildup inside the oven. In that household, both cheap and expensive purifiers were less effective than opening the kitchen window or using a dedicated range hood. Someone1234 [[./thread#^c29460098|Someone1234]] independently reports an oven-residue event triggering yellow, and GatorD42 [[./thread#^c29461050|GatorD42]] uses an AirVisual specifically to detect cooking emissions. None gives a concentration time series.
- jve [[./thread#^c29461551|jve]] reports that an IKEA monitor could remain red for hours with a purifier nearby, with similarly confusing changes without it. In a follow-up, jve [[./thread#^c29464766|jve]] says cooking rapidly turns it red and that recovery takes hours; the purifier was only about two weeks old. This documents a frustrating monitoring experience, not a demonstrated purifier failure or measured removal efficiency.
- kfarr [[./thread#^c29464575|kfarr]] uses an indoor PurpleAir in the Bay Area and was surprised by how often cooking degraded its indication. The same monitor appeared to show indoor filtration working when outside air was smoky from wildfires. The commenter considers it worth the cost, but provides neither paired indoor/outdoor concentrations nor a health outcome.
- genewitch [[./thread#^c29463833|genewitch]] reports using box-fan filters alongside a mini-split system and opening several windows with exhaust fans when monitors indicate high CO2 or another air-quality problem. They say cycling the air takes about 20 minutes. No before-and-after concentrations are supplied, and the statement that the former furnace offered no air-quality management concerns that particular home.
- tdrdt [[./thread#^c29460186|tdrdt]] reports approximately 400 ppm outdoors, 480 ppm indoors with good ventilation, and a temporary 2000 ppm reading after flatulence on a roughly \$30 Simr meter. Those are firsthand displayed values, not validated CO2 concentrations. Separately, genewitch [[./thread#^c29464211|genewitch]] says exposure to pure CO2 also increased their devices’ CO, VOC, and formaldehyde indications, which they interpret as false cross-responses.
- fma [[./thread#^c29463714|fma]] describes a new house with new flooring, paint, cabinets, and carpeting, and readings characterized as off the charts. Asked how they measured this, fma [[./thread#^c29467268|fma]] names a standalone detector and a Foobot, then says they generically measure VOCs. This supports a report of concerning sensor readings and odors; it does not establish a formaldehyde-specific concentration or the asserted comparison with workplace enforcement limits.
- dirtyid [[./thread#^c29460821|dirtyid]] says a Xiaomi purifier seems to help allergies and that its high-air-quality alarm caught forgotten cooking or burning pots. dylanz [[./thread#^c29463485|dylanz]] reports that an Oransi EJ120 removed a household smell within days and made the air feel clean. GekkePrutser [[./thread#^c29468891|GekkePrutser]] also notices odor capture with carbon filtration. These are subjective health or comfort observations, not quantified particle or gas-removal tests.
- prashnts [[./thread#^c29460416|prashnts]] reports that a monitor near a smoking area initially turned red during smoking and returned to green after roughly ten minutes, but later remained red for weeks. Cleaning its fabric-covered intake helped only temporarily. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c29460472|ahaucnx]] describes dust on optics as a possible cause and says compressed air sometimes helps temporarily; skybrian [[./thread#^c29462999|skybrian]] reports occasional improvement after rebooting a Temtop. Persistent high readings therefore have unresolved environmental and device explanations.
- dheera repeats the same PMSA003I experience in two comments [[./thread#^c29466028|dheera]] [[./thread#^c29466021|dheera]]: readings stayed at zero when reported local AQI was below about 200, including around 90. This is one repeated account, not two independent failures. In reply, zahma [[./thread#^c29470182|zahma]] reports that their own inexpensive sensor worked for several years before failing, possibly because of moisture; its model and the failure diagnosis remain unspecified.
- AdrianoKF [[./thread#^c29464949|AdrianoKF]] successfully integrated a modified Vindriktning with Home Assistant but found the fan’s approximately ten-second on/off cycling distracting in a quiet room. Their later clarification [[./thread#^c29523264|AdrianoKF]] limits 'working great' to modification and integration, with only informal smoke and cooking checks. gedy [[./thread#^c29468350|gedy]] suggests continuous operation from a 3.3 V pin for quieter running, without supplying measurement validation after the change.
- ahaucnx [[./thread#^c29460024|ahaucnx]] reports getting the most stable and accurate readings from the Plantower 5003 among tested Plantower variants, and seeing no significant advantage from more expensive Sensirion modules; no protocol or numerical results accompany that comparison. geerlingguy [[./thread#^c29463237|geerlingguy]] endorses AirGradient’s simplicity and decent reliability, while stavros [[./thread#^c29460562|stavros]] reports an ESP8266-connected sensor working for months. These are useful builder experiences, with limited evidence about absolute accuracy.
- simonebrunozzi [[./thread#^c29461021|simonebrunozzi]] bought a refurbished PurpleAir during an intense California fire season and thought it worked well, but found it expensive, bulky, and insufficiently easy for ordinary users. samsolomon [[./thread#^c29468429|samsolomon]] bought two Airthings View Plus monitors after basement radon mitigation instead of paying \$300 annually for offered continuous monitoring; after about a month, they liked the charts and reports but could not judge long-term performance. Neither account independently validates the devices.

## Unresolved disagreements

- How much inaccuracy is acceptable in a cheap warning device remains disputed. Foivos [[./thread#^c29459783|Foivos]] values rough monitoring, while blacksmith\_tb [[./thread#^c29462975|blacksmith\_tb]] would prefer false positives to a wildfire-related false negative. NamTaf [[./thread#^c29467685|NamTaf]] rejects softening warnings merely because users might dislike discovering a problem. The discussion does not establish thresholds that simultaneously minimize missed exposure, nuisance alarms, and misleading reassurance.
- There is no single established best monitor across pollutants and conditions. hedora [[./thread#^c29462323|hedora]] interprets the linked evaluations as favoring only PurpleAir for PM2.5 and finding none of the tested consumer gas sensors useful. DannyBee [[./thread#^c29462650|DannyBee]] adds Atmotube Pro and Elitech for the relevant particle measurements, discusses correlations above 0.9, and regards above 0.8 as strong for inexpensive devices. intrasight [[./thread#^c29460868|intrasight]] instead favors Dylos. These are commenters’ readings and recommendations, not independently examined reports here; correlation also leaves absolute bias unresolved.
- Whether inexpensive modifications preserve or improve accuracy is unanswered. newman314 [[./thread#^c29465751|newman314]] proposes a compensating factor for the IKEA sensor, zibzab [[./thread#^c29465461|zibzab]] asks whether extracted readings bypass software calibration, and formerly\_proven [[./thread#^c29463279|formerly\_proven]] asks what PurpleAir adds beyond an off-the-shelf Plantower module. None receives evidence establishing a transferable correction or equivalence between bare modules and finished monitors.
- The strongest remedy for a given home remains source- and pollutant-dependent. kccqzy [[./thread#^c29464600|kccqzy]] favors kitchen ventilation for their cooking problem, kfarr [[./thread#^c29464575|kfarr]] sees benefits from filtration during outdoor smoke, and jve [[./thread#^c29461551|jve]] cannot tell how much their purifier helps. The comments do not quantify the best balance of source removal, exhaust, window opening, filtration capacity, and operating time.
- Gas-specific measurement and treatment remain particularly uncertain. fma’s later description of generic VOC detectors [[./thread#^c29467268|fma]] limits the formaldehyde conclusion, while GekkePrutser [[./thread#^c29468891|GekkePrutser]] explicitly lacks evidence for the formaldehyde performance of carbon filters. genewitch’s reported cross-responses [[./thread#^c29464211|genewitch]] further complicate interpretation. Odor reduction, a VOC score, and a chemical-specific exposure measurement are not shown to be interchangeable.
- Outdoor durability and several product choices remain unvalidated in the supplied discussion. pingec [[./thread#^c29464102|pingec]] asks about all-weather monitors without receiving a demonstrated solution; zahma [[./thread#^c29470182|zahma]] suspects moisture caused a sensor failure. wnevets [[./thread#^c29468043|wnevets]] asks about uHoo and ortusdux [[./thread#^c29460092|ortusdux]] about Amazon’s monitor, but no substantive test results follow those requests.
- Reliable CO2 measurement near the IKEA price remains unresolved. mpalczewski [[./thread#^c29478985|mpalczewski]] says they know of no options at that price, whereas the S8 component alone is listed at \$28 by mellavora [[./thread#^c29461588|mellavora]]. tdrdt’s roughly \$30 meter readings [[./thread#^c29460186|tdrdt]] do not resolve accuracy. The thread also does not separate direct CO2 effects from other consequences of poorly ventilated occupied rooms.
- For story 29458404, part 0, there are no supplied NO2 measurements, quantified traffic-exposure comparisons, or firsthand sleep outcomes. LeanderK [[./thread#^c29459952|LeanderK]] merely describes living away from a major road; colechristensen [[./thread#^c29461028|colechristensen]] expects cognitive effects from experienced CO2 buildup without testing cognition; AdrianoKF [[./thread#^c29464949|AdrianoKF]] reports distracting fan noise rather than sleep effects. The discussion therefore supports a detailed account of monitoring experiences and practical tradeoffs, but leaves those requested health and exposure questions unanswered.
