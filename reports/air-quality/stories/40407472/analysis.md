# [Nitrogen dioxide exposure due to gas and propane combustion by U.S. stoves](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40407472)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion weighs reported nitrogen dioxide exposure from gas and propane stoves against practical reasons people keep gas appliances, including existing wiring, renovation costs, cookware, price, cooking preferences, and resilience during power outages. Participants compare induction and gas cooking, ventilation hoods and open windows, and whether fireplaces or outdoor cooking are safer alternatives. The thread also questions the study’s funding and considers the limits of generalizing from household monitoring and personal experience.
Tags: indoor-air-quality, nitrogen-dioxide, gas-stoves, induction-cooking, ventilation, home-safety, energy-costs, research-funding

## Relevance

Comments address indoor NO2 from gas cooking and practical mitigation with vented range hoods or open windows. People compare gas and induction, describe cooking-related air readings, and note that hood effectiveness depends on capture, placement, and use. Some comments question the study’s funding and the practical costs of switching stoves. ## Arguments

- Induction is presented as a practical alternative to indoor gas combustion. moooo99 [[./thread#^c40407713|moooo99]] reports enjoying a recently purchased induction stove and finding it efficient; kemiller2002 [[./thread#^c40407791|kemiller2002]], drawing on experience with several stove types, argues that induction can replace gas for ordinary cooking. Neither supplies a before-and-after exposure measurement.
- The strongest numerical exposure evidence is a study excerpt reproduced by wffurr [[./thread#^c40407680|wffurr]]: outside-venting hoods reduced hour-averaged kitchen NO2 by 10–70\%, averaging 35\%, in five randomly selected homes. These are reported study measurements, with a small stated sample and substantial variation between homes.
- wffurr [[./thread#^c40407680|wffurr]] also quotes a study estimate that a hood with 75\% capture efficiency, used every time someone cooks, yields 70\% less long-term stove-attributable NO2 exposure than average. The quoted window-opening scenario produced a benefit comparable to average hood use. loeg [[./thread#^c40407921|loeg]] emphasizes that this scenario involved at least four windows opened together.
- Ventilation design is offered as a reason fuel type alone may not determine indoor exposure. gpm [[./thread#^c40407836|gpm]] hypothesizes that chimneys remove wood-combustion gases as well as particles; hagbard\_c [[./thread#^c40421126|hagbard\_c]] describes a warm chimney, a large passive hood, and ample incoming air as the mechanisms keeping combustion products out of their farmhouse.
- greentxt [[./thread#^c40407971|greentxt]] advocates cooking outdoors to reduce household exposure and associated health problems, especially for children. This is a proposed intervention and health claim; the comment supplies no exposure readings or observed health outcomes.

## Counterarguments

- A hood's presence does not establish a particular reduction in NO2. wffurr [[./thread#^c40407680|wffurr]] quotes findings that some outside-venting hoods were ineffective and notes that the study did not specifically evaluate high-CFM models. loeg [[./thread#^c40407921|loeg]] nevertheless argues that partial reductions still demonstrate useful effectiveness.
- Wood cooking is disputed as an alternative: coffeebeqn [[./thread#^c40407799|coffeebeqn]] argues that wood produces substantially more airborne particles than gas, while loeg [[./thread#^c40407896|loeg]] stresses the harm from smoke entering a dwelling. These comments challenge gpm's [[./thread#^c40407836|gpm]] suggestion that chimney exhaust might compensate, particularly for NO2; they provide no comparative pollutant measurements.
- Induction's claimed equivalence to gas encounters cooking-specific objections. asguy [[./thread#^c40407859|asguy]] identifies direct pepper charring, and geoka9 [[./thread#^c40407887|geoka9]] identifies wok cooking. cogman10 [[./thread#^c40407938|cogman10]] reports successfully stir-frying on induction while conceding that gas works better for that task. chewbacha [[./thread#^c40408005|chewbacha]] proposes a broiler for peppers, while ceejayoz [[./thread#^c40407941|ceejayoz]] proposes a blowtorch; these suggestions have no accompanying exposure comparisons.
- The assertion that switching necessarily requires new cookware is directly contested. kareemm [[./thread#^c40407899|kareemm]] replaced none when switching; cogman10 [[./thread#^c40407846|cogman10]] identifies glass, ceramic, copper, and aluminum cookware as potential incompatibilities and mentions adapters. lotsofpulp [[./thread#^c40408105|lotsofpulp]] acknowledges that their earlier understanding of separate induction cookware may be outdated.
- Outage resilience challenges an unconditional recommendation to replace gas. sparrish [[./thread#^c40409684|sparrish]] notes that storm outages can last days; SkyPuncher [[./thread#^c40410084|SkyPuncher]] questions their frequency and proposes emergency cooking equipment. bradknowles [[./thread#^c40412454|bradknowles]], replying within that exchange, stresses that alternatives must already be available when frozen roads and closed stores prevent obtaining them.

## Assumptions

- CFM ratings, visible smoke removal, and absence of odor are treated by some participants as evidence of effective capture, but the discussion does not establish their relationship to measured NO2 or PM2.5. drewcon [[./thread#^c40407903|drewcon]] asserts complete capture beneath a 1200-CFM hood; pests [[./thread#^c40408068|pests]] reports no smoke smell after opening a fireplace flue. Neither reports pollutant instrumentation.
- loeg's [[./thread#^c40407915|loeg]] assertion that exhausting indoor air and drawing in outdoor air 'can't hurt' assumes that the incoming air is suitable. No outdoor pollutant measurements accompany that claim, so its applicability across outdoor air conditions remains untested here.
- loeg [[./thread#^c40407915|loeg]] attributes the article's reported advantage for 3000-square-foot dwellings over 800-square-foot dwellings entirely to dilution. The supplied comment gives no analysis separating dwelling size from ventilation, appliance use, or other household differences.
- Avoiding gas-burner combustion should not be treated as evidence that every cooking-related air problem has been resolved. moooo99 [[./thread#^c40407713|moooo99]] reports satisfaction with induction, while chasebank [[./thread#^c40407658|chasebank]] describes smoke from cast-iron searing; this unit contains no comparison isolating burner emissions from emissions associated with the cooking process.
- greentxt's [[./thread#^c40407971|greentxt]] outdoor-cooking proposal assumes affordable equipment, usable outdoor space, and suitable weather across much of the United States for much of the year. Its broad claims about feasibility and children's health are unsupported by measurements or household outcome data in the comment.

## Conditions

- Electrical conversion costs depend on the existing home. PopAlongKid [[./thread#^c40407805|PopAlongKid]] describes stove fuel as largely fixed during construction; lotsofpulp [[./thread#^c40407773|lotsofpulp]] estimates that adding the required electrical supply could cost \$1,000. kemiller2002 [[./thread#^c40407855|kemiller2002]] reports making the conversion and finding it relatively straightforward, while mentioning possible electrical-panel work. moooo99 [[./thread#^c40407833|moooo99]] contrasts this with their account of suitable wiring already being commonplace in Germany.
- benzible [[./thread#^c40407940|benzible]] cites battery-assisted induction products intended to operate from a standard 120V outlet and avoid an electrical upgrade. This is a reported product capability; the discussion supplies no owner's experience, purchase price, or information about sustained cooking performance.
- Appliance-price comparisons depend on country and product format. germinalphrase [[./thread#^c40407888|germinalphrase]] claims induction ranges cost two to four times as much as gas ranges in the US. Shog9 [[./thread#^c40407932|Shog9]] explains that US buyers commonly purchase a combined range and oven, limiting the relevance of namibj's [[./thread#^c40407854|namibj]] claim about inexpensive individual hobs. These are commenter price assessments rather than a matched market survey.
- Operating costs depend on tariffs and cooking habits. blindriver [[./thread#^c40407935|blindriver]] reports California electricity prices of \$0.42–\$0.53/kWh. citrin\_ru [[./thread#^c40408015|citrin\_ru]] argues that cheaper gas can outweigh induction's efficiency advantage. \_fizz\_buzz\_ [[./thread#^c40408049|\_fizz\_buzz\_]] estimates only 10–15 kWh monthly for cooking, but pandaman [[./thread#^c40410016|pandaman]] disputes that estimate using boiling and prolonged cooking examples. Neither consumption estimate comes from a reported household electricity measurement.
- The cost of acceptable controls and repairs also affects switching. citrin\_ru [[./thread#^c40408015|citrin\_ru]] says the cheapest countertop induction option they found with physical controls cost five times their chosen model, and criticizes low-power pulsing. timthorn [[./thread#^c40407879|timthorn]] describes touch controls as prevalent in the UK. O5vYtytb [[./thread#^c40407961|O5vYtytb]] expects induction repairs to be costly and gas appliances to be simpler, but supplies no reliability dataset.
- Geography changes the importance of backup cooking and heat. giardini [[./thread#^c40409430|giardini]] cites coastal exposure to hurricanes, ice storms, and tornadoes. bradknowles [[./thread#^c40410615|bradknowles]] describes a steep Austin neighborhood that becomes inaccessible during freezes and reports multiple prolonged electricity outages. Those circumstances limit comparisons with households reporting very rare outages.

## Lived experiences

- Induction users describe practical benefits and frustrations, without reporting measured health changes. moooo99 [[./thread#^c40407713|moooo99]] enjoys its efficiency and cooking experience; wffurr [[./thread#^c40407758|wffurr]] appreciates fast heating and toddler-resistant controls, while leaving behavior with small metal objects untested. wzdd [[./thread#^c40408112|wzdd]] prefers induction to gas but reports wet-hand, splash, and control-interface problems.
- chasebank [[./thread#^c40407658|chasebank]] installed a high-CFM hood for smoke from cast-iron searing and finds it highly useful, while explicitly asking whether it also reduces NO2. uranium [[./thread#^c40407886|uranium]] reports that a nominal 900-CFM fan still allows substantial kitchen smoke when searing on a front burner at high fan speed, whereas using a back burner keeps the air visibly clearer even at low speed.
- hagbard\_c [[./thread#^c40421126|hagbard\_c]] cooks and heats with wood in a seventeenth-century farmhouse. They attribute indoor emissions to occasional cold-chimney starts and describe effective draft once the brick chimney warms, a passive hood, substantial incoming air even with windows closed, and sweeping once or twice annually. This is a detailed operational account without pollutant readings.
- pests [[./thread#^c40408068|pests]], replying to warnings about fireplace smoke, reports no smoke smell once the flue is open and describes strong chimney suction. bluedino [[./thread#^c40407707|bluedino]] recalls family cooking with fireplaces and wood stoves and childhood wood heating, but asks how those exposures compare rather than claiming a measured advantage.
- bradknowles [[./thread#^c40410615|bradknowles]] believes a gas stove and fireplace saved them and their wife during the Texas freeze, describing two major freezes in roughly twenty years and blocked escape routes. In the same outage discussion, mulderc [[./thread#^c40477978|mulderc]] reports only one comparable electricity failure in more than forty years. The accounts establish different personal circumstances rather than a common outage frequency.
- SkyPuncher [[./thread#^c40411544|SkyPuncher]] reports losing heat when a rental furnace failed during a Midwestern winter and describes partitioning rooms and wearing layers as ways to retain sufficient warmth. The comment supplies no air-quality measurements evaluating reduced ventilation or the combustion-based backup equipment it also mentions.
- Rental preferences conflict directly. O5vYtytb [[./thread#^c40407883|O5vYtytb]] is remodeling a rental and considers gas an advantage, later arguing that renters do not want to investigate cookware compatibility [[./thread#^c40407922|O5vYtytb]]. mulderc [[./thread#^c40477969|mulderc]] responds with a recent rental search in which they specifically avoided gas and preferred induction.
- Cooking experience also varies with technique and equipment. cogman10 [[./thread#^c40407938|cogman10]] recently stir-fried successfully on induction after longer wok preheating. hagbard\_c [[./thread#^c40420898|hagbard\_c]] draws on Dutch-Indonesian cooking experience across gas, resistance electric, induction, and wood, favoring gas or wood for large batches and reporting eight portions cooked together on a wood stove. Neither account measures the associated air exposure.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The health implications cannot be quantified from this unit. wffurr [[./thread#^c40407680|wffurr]] supplies quoted NO2 exposure findings, greentxt [[./thread#^c40407971|greentxt]] makes broad health claims, and coffeebeqn [[./thread#^c40407799|coffeebeqn]] discusses particles generally. There are no firsthand numerical NO2, PM2.5, or CO2 readings, sensor-validation comparisons, or reported tests of symptoms, cognition, or sleep.
- Opening windows has limited support here: the scenario quoted by wffurr [[./thread#^c40407680|wffurr]] involved several windows, as loeg [[./thread#^c40407921|loeg]] emphasizes. The discussion does not establish the benefit of cracking one window or compare indoor cooking pollution against outdoor traffic emissions or wildfire smoke. It therefore cannot identify when opening windows would worsen exposure, or compare that choice with standalone air filtration.
- The relationship between rated airflow and actual capture remains disputed. drewcon [[./thread#^c40407903|drewcon]] asserts complete capture with 1200 CFM; chasebank [[./thread#^c40407946|chasebank]] questions uranium's 900-CFM rating and claims genuine high-CFM equipment starts around \$2,000. uranium [[./thread#^c40428453|uranium]] replies that theirs was a name-brand showroom purchase costing under \$1,000 about a decade earlier. No installed-airflow or NO2 test resolves the competing claims.
- Chimney effectiveness does not settle the wood-versus-gas exposure comparison raised by bluedino [[./thread#^c40407707|bluedino]]. gpm [[./thread#^c40407836|gpm]] hypothesizes a NO2 advantage from exhaust, loeg [[./thread#^c40407896|loeg]] emphasizes smoke hazards, and hagbard\_c [[./thread#^c40421126|hagbard\_c]] describes favorable draft under particular conditions. The supplied accounts contain no matched measurements across fuels, startup conditions, or stove designs.
- Some suggested transition options remain unverified. cameldrv [[./thread#^c40407898|cameldrv]] thinks mixed induction-and-coil stoves are common, but O5vYtytb [[./thread#^c40407916|O5vYtytb]] asks where such ranges are available in the US and receives no answer in this export. Availability and suitability cannot be inferred from the suggestion alone.
- Study sponsorship is questioned without a demonstrated effect on the results. WaitWaitWha [[./thread#^c40407949|WaitWaitWha]] asks who the listed funder, HT LLC, is; josehill [[./thread#^c40424153|josehill]] identifies it as the High Tide Foundation and notes its advocacy interests. The supplied exchange does not independently verify that identification or establish a methodological problem with the NO2 findings.
