# [You Can Just Print an Air Purifier](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47698067)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion assesses a 3D-printed air purifier design against familiar box-fan filter builds. Participants question airflow through the filter, fan static pressure, noise, power use, and the value of a shroud, while comparing compact case fans with larger fan-and-filter arrangements. They also debate plastic use and microplastics, and one participant is eager to try the design.
Tags: DIY air purifier, 3D printing, air filters, fan performance, microplastics

## Relevance

The comments discuss DIY filtration design, airflow through filter media, fan static pressure, and noise and power tradeoffs. They offer practical suggestions and engineering opinions, but no measured purification results. ## Arguments

- quercusa [[./thread#^c47704367|quercusa]] recommends adding a corner shroud to a filter taped to a box fan, arguing that blade-tip turbulence otherwise causes reverse airflow. They link supporting advice but provide no measurements from their own setup.
- felixgallo [[./thread#^c47702668|felixgallo]] argues that fan ratings without filters can overstate usable filtered airflow. They suspect five nominally 72-CFM case fans in an unsealed, turbulent enclosure are underpowered and suggest a nominally 800-CFM AC Infinity Cloudline S8 directed through a sealed arrangement. This comparison is explicitly intuition. Their accompanying claim that airbrush stations can deliver under 1 linear foot/minute despite ratings above 150 without filters lacks a measurement protocol or explicit firsthand attribution.

## Counterarguments

- Responding to felixgallo’s airflow concerns, jaen [[./thread#^c47702694|jaen]] distinguishes high-airflow case fans from high-static-pressure models: CFM alone does not establish suitability for pushing air through filters. They assume the latter would perform better, without supplying comparative results.
- ninininino [[./thread#^c47705560|ninininino]] challenges the printed plastic housing on exposure grounds, predicting degradation into microplastics and a more toxic environment. The comment supplies no shedding measurements, particle sizes, exposure estimates, or evidence that this would outweigh filtration benefits.
- Replying to the microplastic objection, burnt-resistor [[./thread#^c47709295|burnt-resistor]] asserts that the printing process presents much greater known health risks than subsequent degradation. No supporting exposure or health evidence is supplied; their preferred alternative still includes a printed frame.

## Assumptions

- In reply to quercusa [[./thread#^c47704367|quercusa]], burnt-resistor [[./thread#^c47709221|burnt-resistor]] bets that “this” moves more air per unit of noise and/or power. The referent and comparison are not explicit, and neither efficiency improvement is measured.

## Conditions

- burnt-resistor [[./thread#^c47709295|burnt-resistor]] makes affordability, noise, and filter area central practical considerations: they expect the featured design to be loud and expensive with suitable high-static-pressure fans, and to have less filter area than box-fan alternatives. They prefer two box fans with four or five filters and a printed frame. These are anticipated tradeoffs, with no prices, sound levels, power readings, or geographic context supplied.
- felixgallo [[./thread#^c47702668|felixgallo]] makes performance conditional on filter resistance, filter-face opening, enclosure sealing, and airflow arrangement; jaen [[./thread#^c47702694|jaen]] adds fan type. Their exchange cautions against judging the proposed purifier from nominal airflow ratings alone, but does not establish performance in a particular room.
