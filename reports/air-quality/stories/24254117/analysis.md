# [Realtime California Air Quality](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=24254117)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion shares tools for tracking California air quality and wildfire smoke, including official monitoring maps, community sensors, forecasts, and conversion settings. Participants compare AirNow and PurpleAir data, considering calibration, sensor density, indoor readings, particle characteristics, and local variation. The thread also covers current fire conditions, forecast warnings, fire maps, and prescribed burning. Other topics include the cost and purpose of public sensor networks, building a DIY purifier, and the stresses of living through fires during the pandemic.
Tags: California, wildfires, air-quality-monitoring, PurpleAir, AirNow, PM2.5, smoke-forecasting, DIY-air-filtration

## Relevance

The thread concerns wildfire smoke and local PM2.5 monitoring, including differences between official and crowdsourced sensors, calibration corrections, sampling density, and forecasts. A commenter reports comparing a personal sensor with AirNow during fires, and another describes a low-cost box-fan filter used through multiple fire seasons, including its noise and practical usefulness. ## Arguments

- Dense neighborhood monitoring may make wildfire readings more locally relevant. tjohns [[./thread#^c24254979|tjohns]] recommends PurpleAir when smoke shifts between neighborhoods, while gonehome [[./thread#^c24254947|gonehome]] favors its many owner-installed monitors. These arguments concern spatial coverage; they do not establish each device’s measurement accuracy.
- Comparisons should account for conversion settings and averaging periods. ilyagr [[./thread#^c24254873|ilyagr]] reports getting comparable numbers by selecting PurpleAir’s one-hour averages and LRAPA conversion. karlding [[./thread#^c24254968|karlding]] relays an article’s explanation that an assumed particle density can make uncorrected readings overestimate wood-smoke pollution.
- Separating indoor and outdoor readings can improve interpretation of outdoor conditions. tmpz22 [[./thread#^c24254946|tmpz22]] suggests hiding indoor sensors when comparing PurpleAir with AirNow. mjrpes [[./thread#^c24256098|mjrpes]] reports that bookmarking a configured map preserves the location, indoor-sensor exclusion, and LRAPA setting.
- Combining monitoring with smoke forecasts offers practical situational awareness. ilyagr [[./thread#^c24254873|ilyagr]] recommends AirNow’s combined fire, smoke, and monitor map plus surface-level smoke forecasts. rz2k [[./thread#^c24255085|rz2k]] considers BlueSky the most accurate forecast for their location that week; ilyagr [[./thread#^c24256178|ilyagr]] subsequently found the suggested California forecast helpful. These are user assessments rather than quantified forecast evaluations.
- A homemade filter offers a potentially affordable response when commercial purifiers are expensive or unavailable. Zhenya [[./thread#^c24255503|Zhenya]] describes a box fan with an FPR 10 filter taped to its outlet, with the filter oriented for airflow, and reports successful use over two California fire seasons. The claimed effectiveness is experiential rather than supported by supplied before-and-after measurements.
- Local monitoring could cover more than particles. AirGradient startup representative ahaucnx [[./thread#^c24257683|ahaucnx]] announces an open-source, open-hardware device intended to measure PM2.5, CO2, temperature, and humidity at home or school. This establishes an advertised monitoring option, without providing accuracy tests or exposure results.

## Counterarguments

- Greater sensor density does not settle the reliability comparison. tjohns [[./thread#^c24254979|tjohns]] regards professionally maintained EPA sites as providing the highest-quality measurements, and rz2k [[./thread#^c24255085|rz2k]] reports that AirNow agreed with their own PM2.5 sensor better than PurpleAir or IQAir did. That experience qualifies broad claims that PurpleAir is always preferable.
- Variation between nearby map readings may reflect different measurement environments. tmpz22 [[./thread#^c24254946|tmpz22]] identifies the mixture of indoor and outdoor sensors; unsrsly [[./thread#^c24255132|unsrsly]] speculates that some monitors sit in garages or locations with particular local pollution concerns. The garage explanation is a hypothesis, not a documented investigation of the discrepant sensors.
- A particulate-focused map does not describe every air pollutant. labster [[./thread#^c24255750|labster]] reports being able to obtain ozone information from AirNow but finding no corresponding option on PurpleAir. Their observation limits conclusions drawn from a single air-quality display.
- An offline monitor does not establish that fire destroyed it. Fauntleroy [[./thread#^c24254576|Fauntleroy]] proposes tracking fire through apparently destroyed sensors; anamexis [[./thread#^c24254615|anamexis]] replies that loss of power can also explain their disappearance. Missing readings are therefore ambiguous.
- Preventive burning can itself create residential smoke exposure. In response to coronadisaster’s suggestion of controlled burns [[./thread#^c24254627|coronadisaster]], claydavisss [[./thread#^c24255040|claydavisss]] argues that burns near homes produce substantial smoke even when controlled and can threaten homes if control fails. This identifies an exposure tradeoff without quantifying whether prescribed burning reduces total personal exposure.

## Assumptions

- Applying LRAPA assumes that its correction is appropriate for the particles being measured. karlding [[./thread#^c24254968|karlding]] and gnutun [[./thread#^c24254953|gnutun]] reference the same external explanation; oski [[./thread#^c24254670|oski]] recommends the setting, and aaroninsf [[./thread#^c24265720|aaroninsf]] reports adopting it. These comments demonstrate shared advice and uptake, without supplying independent validation or a study’s methods and results.
- Treating agreement with a personal monitor as accuracy assumes that the personal instrument is a suitable reference. rz2k [[./thread#^c24255085|rz2k]] supplies no calibration or controlled comparison protocol. Likewise, scurvy’s preference for a monitor two houses away [[./thread#^c24255070|scurvy]] assumes that proximity makes it representative; no paired readings establish exposure inside their own home.
- Some descriptions of AirNow’s data sources are explicitly tentative. ilyagr [[./thread#^c24254991|ilyagr]] doubts that low-cost data are used beyond the new map pilot, while jxramos [[./thread#^c24285375|jxramos]] believes the regular interactive map omits some available sensors. Their comments support distinguishing map products, but do not establish every product’s underlying data pipeline.

## Conditions

- The discussion is situated in the August 2020 California wildfires. khuey [[./thread#^c24254557|khuey]] reports that dry thunderstorms triggered fires burning more than 1,500 square miles in under a week. RobLach [[./thread#^c24254613|RobLach]] and coding123 [[./thread#^c24254772|coding123]] describe forecasts of renewed dangerous conditions. The observations should retain that episodic and regional context.
- Location and forecast coverage matter. scurvy [[./thread#^c24255070|scurvy]] reports that a Lamorinda ZIP code returned the Oakland reporting area, despite displaying the correct locality. rz2k [[./thread#^c24255085|rz2k]] cautions that the suggested high-resolution California forecast might miss northern smoke and also supplies a broader regional model. A nearby monitor or appropriately bounded forecast may answer a different question from a city-level report.
- Affordability and availability constrain personal monitoring and filtration. rz2k [[./thread#^c24255085|rz2k]] quotes a price below \$20 for a Plantower PMS7003 sensor, without giving a complete installed-system cost. Zhenya [[./thread#^c24255503|Zhenya]] describes the fan-and-filter option as inexpensive. archarios [[./thread#^c24254859|archarios]] reports a shipping label being created and then cancelled, interpreting that experience as a possible indoor-sensor shortage; stock status is not independently confirmed.
- Paying for a monitor also involves preferences about participation and data sharing. IvyMike [[./thread#^c24256223|IvyMike]] views their purchase as support for citizen science and accepts possible future commercial changes. suzakus [[./thread#^c24255929|suzakus]] reports purchasing a sensor and keeping it private on the map. supernova87a [[./thread#^c24255445|supernova87a]] questions paying while contributing data, then softens the objection [[./thread#^c24257352|supernova87a]]; the proposed data-monetization business model remains unverified.

## Lived experiences

- rz2k [[./thread#^c24255085|rz2k]] reports operating a PM2.5 sensor bought during earlier wildfires and finding its readings more consistent with AirNow than with PurpleAir or IQAir. They also describe a spike when the sensor was above a grill. This is firsthand measurement reporting, but the supplied text contains no numerical particle concentrations or accessible plot data; the grill observation does not establish room-wide or gas-stove exposure.
- krat0sprakhar [[./thread#^c24254926|krat0sprakhar]] reports simultaneous-looking San Francisco displays of roughly 150 on AirNow and 88 on PurpleAir, prompting a reliability question. These are reported AQI values from websites, not a personal measurement of PM2.5 mass concentration.
- scurvy [[./thread#^c24255070|scurvy]] finds PurpleAir more useful because it shows a neighbor’s readings two houses away, whereas AirNow returns Oakland for their Lamorinda search. Their claim of greater accuracy is grounded in local relevance, without a separate instrument comparison.
- Zhenya [[./thread#^c24255503|Zhenya]] reports using the homemade purifier successfully for two fire seasons. Answering ilyagr’s noise question [[./thread#^c24257252|ilyagr]], Zhenya [[./thread#^c24257384|Zhenya]] describes a background hum and says their inexpensive fan is comfortable to sleep beside, while noting that noise depends on the motor. This supports sleep compatibility for that setup, not a measured improvement in sleep quality.
- wmichelin [[./thread#^c24254721|wmichelin]] reports that their partner’s school planned to open if AQI was below the red category while consulting an indoor sensor. They also describe substantial stress despite having some financial security. This is an account of a potentially misleading decision process; it does not establish that reopening occurred or that anyone suffered a measured health outcome.
- tempsy [[./thread#^c24255003|tempsy]] describes the combination of fires and COVID as a personal breaking point in San Francisco. scurvy [[./thread#^c24255185|scurvy]] replies that earlier fires were worse for the city, while novok [[./thread#^c24255527|novok]] emphasizes the added difficulty of being unable to meet comfortably indoors or outdoors during the pandemic. The exchange distinguishes perceived smoke severity from the combined burden of smoke and pandemic restrictions.
- Access to usable information varies. ilyagr [[./thread#^c24254873|ilyagr]] finds AirNow helpful and faster to load than PurpleAir, while also calling AirNow’s map slow. modeless [[./thread#^c24254680|modeless]] reports building an alternative using PurpleAir’s API because frequent checks were too slow. Animats [[./thread#^c24257766|Animats]] reports an over-quota outage for a site in the discussion. These observations concern interface availability and speed, without establishing measurement freshness.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Which service best represents actual local conditions remains unresolved. rz2k [[./thread#^c24255085|rz2k]] favors AirNow based on agreement with a personal sensor; scurvy [[./thread#^c24255070|scurvy]] favors PurpleAir based on proximity. Moreover, krat0sprakhar’s reported PurpleAir value is lower than AirNow’s [[./thread#^c24254926|krat0sprakhar]], whereas karlding’s particle-density explanation predicts excessive uncorrected PurpleAir readings [[./thread#^c24254968|karlding]]. That explanation alone does not resolve the particular discrepancy.
- The meaning of real time is insufficiently specified. tjohns [[./thread#^c24254979|tjohns]] calls both services real time, while ilyagr [[./thread#^c24254873|ilyagr]] obtains agreement using one-hour averages. The thread supplies no controlled comparison of reporting delays, refresh rates, or how quickly either display follows shifting neighborhood smoke.
- The magnitude of filtration benefit is unknown. Zhenya [[./thread#^c24255503|Zhenya]] says the homemade purifier works dramatically well and can protect lungs, but supplies no measured PM2.5 reduction, airflow result, exposure reduction, or health outcome. Zhenya’s motor-dependent noise qualification [[./thread#^c24257384|Zhenya]] also limits generalization about bedroom comfort.
- Health, cognition, and sleep effects are largely unmeasured in this unit. Zhenya [[./thread#^c24255503|Zhenya]] makes a protective-health claim and reports tolerable sleeping-room noise [[./thread#^c24257384|Zhenya]]; tempsy [[./thread#^c24255003|tempsy]] and wmichelin [[./thread#^c24254721|wmichelin]] describe distress amid overlapping crises. There are no supplied cognitive tests, clinical outcomes, or measured sleep changes establishing an air-pollution effect.
- The approved question’s NO2, traffic, ventilation, and window-opening comparisons remain unanswered here. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c24257683|ahaucnx]] advertises CO2 measurement capability but supplies no CO2 readings or effects. Cooking evidence is limited to rz2k’s grill-associated particle spike [[./thread#^c24255085|rz2k]]. No paired indoor/outdoor measurements or window-opening experiments establish when opening windows would improve or worsen exposure.
- The indoor-sensor default remains a contested usability issue with a partial workaround. ericand [[./thread#^c24254690|ericand]] questions its value, and stbtrax [[./thread#^c24254734|stbtrax]] agrees it should be disabled by default. mjrpes [[./thread#^c24256117|mjrpes]] recounts an older promised preference-saving feature and reports a bookmark workaround. The supplied replies do not establish a default change or resolution of the school’s interpretation problem reported by wmichelin [[./thread#^c24254721|wmichelin]].
