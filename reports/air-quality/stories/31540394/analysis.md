# [Reverse engineering Xiaomi air purifier NFC stickers](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=31540394)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion of reverse engineering Xiaomi air-purifier filter NFC stickers and ways to bypass filter-recognition or replacement reminders. Participants compare how different purifier models handle third-party or expired filters, describe reset and automation workarounds, and debate whether filter-life counters reflect actual condition or mostly elapsed time. The thread considers how clogged filters affect airflow, energy use, and performance, and shares experiences with long filter lifetimes in different pollution levels. It also covers DIY purifier designs, filter and fan combinations, activated-carbon options, noise and cost tradeoffs, air-quality monitoring, and personal experiences using purifiers for smoke, allergies, cooking particles, and other indoor air concerns.
Tags: Xiaomi, air-purifiers, NFC, DRM, repairability, filters, air-quality, DIY, home-automation

## Relevance

Although focused on bypassing purifier filter authentication, the comments discuss filter lifespan and performance, PM2.5 monitoring, and purifier effectiveness. Firsthand reports include running filters in highly polluted conditions, allergy and wildfire-smoke relief, and particle increases associated with cooking and opening windows. The discussion offers useful practical accounts but little on NO2 or CO2, and some claims about sensor accuracy and filter replacement are conflicting or uncertain. ## Arguments

- Mechanical filtration is presented as a practical way to reduce particle exposure. dxhdr [[./thread#^c31541314|dxhdr]] recommends comparing particle readings before and after 30 minutes of HEPA filtration in a closed room, while explicitly separating particle removal from demonstrated health benefit. ms4720 [[./thread#^c31543168|ms4720]] makes effectiveness conditional on the pollutants, filter capabilities, personal sensitivity, and how frequently room air passes through the filter.
- Several commenters argue that reducing exposure matters even without immediate symptoms. AlotOfReading [[./thread#^c31542562|AlotOfReading]] asserts an association between childhood pollution exposure and later respiratory disease; legulere [[./thread#^c31541515|legulere]] cites pollution as a major mortality risk; throwaway290 [[./thread#^c31541220|throwaway290]] says no PM2.5 level is considered safe. These are commenters' health claims and references, not studies or independently verified findings supplied in this unit.
- Outdoor conditions alone do not establish indoor exposure. Responding to a question about relatively low Japanese outdoor PM2.5, bjoli [[./thread#^c31544000|bjoli]] identifies frying, candles, and cleaning as indoor sources. fragmede [[./thread#^c31540990|fragmede]] highlights partially burnt food, and legulere [[./thread#^c31541575|legulere]] argues that frying can produce substantial particles even on induction. These comments distinguish cooking emissions from fuel combustion, although their quantitative comparisons are not documented here.
- Someone1234 [[./thread#^c31543553|Someone1234]] advocates HEPA filtration for particles and activated carbon for gases, with a pre-filter primarily protecting the more expensive media. papercrane [[./thread#^c31543862|papercrane]] suggests grow-room carbon filters compatible with inline fans. This is a proposed division of functions; the discussion supplies no measured NO2 removal or other gas-specific performance results.
- Simple fan-and-filter construction is repeatedly proposed as an affordable intervention. input\_sh [[./thread#^c31544341|input\_sh]] argues that the basic mechanism is shared with commercial purifiers, and qgin [[./thread#^c31541478|qgin]] recommends a box-fan design for approaching fire season. hnarn [[./thread#^c31546979|hnarn]] reports reading about an effective construction-fan arrangement, but supplies a secondhand account rather than a personal test.
- Measuring the actual indoor environment is favored over inferring exposure from a road address or outdoor appearance. MengerSponge [[./thread#^c31541703|MengerSponge]] identifies wind and building construction as reasons traffic exposure varies indoors. DragonStrength [[./thread#^c31541431|DragonStrength]] recommends an appropriate monitor and an indoor/outdoor baseline, noting that demonstrating improvement is harder when starting concentrations are already low.
- Several commenters distinguish particle capture from airflow as filters load. Danieru [[./thread#^c31540844|Danieru]] and KennyBlanken [[./thread#^c31541669|KennyBlanken]] argue that loaded media can capture particles more effectively while restricting flow. thaeli [[./thread#^c31542430|thaeli]] adds that fan controls and bypass affect the result. Their shared mechanism suggests evaluating delivered filtration and operating cost, rather than assuming a countdown measures exhausted particle-capture capacity.
- Monitoring and suitable operating settings are presented as useful complements to filtration. deno [[./thread#^c31540802|deno]] proposes judging actual cleaning performance instead of a replacement countdown, and userbinator [[./thread#^c31544168|userbinator]] suggests pressure sensing for filter loading. chmars [[./thread#^c31540791|chmars]] reports automatic speed increases during cooking, while Havoc [[./thread#^c31544774|Havoc]] says such increases have alerted them to a forgotten cooking plate; neither establishes that every purifier's automatic settings are adequate.

## Counterarguments

- Benefits are not established as equally large in every home. CoastalCoder [[./thread#^c31541377|CoastalCoder]] questions extrapolation from wildfire conditions to ordinary conditions, and hammock [[./thread#^c31541424|hammock]] agrees that benefits depend on the environment and the suffering it causes. phphphphp [[./thread#^c31540971|phphphphp]] reports incomplete allergy relief and uncertainty about recommending a purifier to someone without breathing problems.
- The broad claim that indoor pollution is the leading lifespan-reducing factor for otherwise healthy people is explicitly hearsay in jaqalopes [[./thread#^c31541266|jaqalopes]]. Rastonbury [[./thread#^c31541500|Rastonbury]] replies that such claims may concern smoky household stoves in developing countries. Subsequent replies preserve an important qualification: legulere [[./thread#^c31541575|legulere]] identifies particles from induction frying, and Rebelgecko [[./thread#^c31542807|Rebelgecko]] raises gas-stove exposure in developed countries. The thread does not quantify the relative risks of these settings.
- Visible dirt and perceived freshness are unreliable stand-ins for harmful exposure. In response to legulere's identification of gray filter deposits as soot, gruez [[./thread#^c31542380|gruez]] reports gray filters despite mostly zero or low-single-digit PM2.5 readings and suggests ordinary dust. trompetenaccoun [[./thread#^c31541594|trompetenaccoun]] describes experiences of people confusing fog with pollution and clear skies with clean air, although their broader dismissal of perceived improvements as placebo is not demonstrated.
- The claim that filtration is merely placebo unless a home can be isolated from outdoor air is too absolute for the supplied accounts. throwaway290 [[./thread#^c31541220|throwaway290]] makes that claim while recommending closed windows during bad outdoor air. hammock [[./thread#^c31541036|hammock]], however, reports relief in a drafty, smoke-affected apartment. That anecdote supports possible benefit despite leakage, without establishing how much exposure remained.
- Shared fan-and-filter principles do not establish equal practical performance. Against input\_sh's broad equivalence claim [[./thread#^c31544341|input\_sh]], oppositelock [[./thread#^c31541542|oppositelock]] reports that commercial units were quieter, less bulky, and more effective according to their meter than their DIY setup. stavros [[./thread#^c31544242|stavros]] reports a box-fan assembly that moved almost no air. Conversely, haunter's suggestion that a \$1,000 purifier would probably perform much better [[./thread#^c31541051|haunter]] is speculation, not a comparison.
- Continued particle capture does not mean an old filter maintains its original room-cleaning rate. Waterluvian [[./thread#^c31541665|Waterluvian]] emphasizes reduced airflow, and thaeli [[./thread#^c31542430|thaeli]] describes fan-dependent behavior and possible bypass. sschueller [[./thread#^c31542630|sschueller]] speculates about earlier motor failure under restrictive conditions; no failure measurements establish that concern in this discussion.
- A separate purifier may offer less additional benefit when other effective filtration already exists. KennyBlanken [[./thread#^c31541611|KennyBlanken]] recommends suitable central-air filters and regular HEPA-bag vacuuming, while dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c31542674|dzhiurgis]] notes that heat-recovery ventilation often includes filters. These are conditional suggestions; neither they nor imajoredinecon's claim that AC performs approximately the same air-quality function as a purifier [[./thread#^c31541473|imajoredinecon]] establish equivalence for unspecified systems.
- Airborne fine particles and settled pet hair call for different practical approaches. Responding to stavros's concern about cat hair repeatedly reaching food and face [[./thread#^c31545116|stavros]], Gigachad [[./thread#^c31545049|Gigachad]] argues that capturing falling hair would require extreme airflow. gh02t [[./thread#^c31545235|gh02t]] suggests a robot vacuum and reports using one to control husky hair. This offers an alternative for that particular nuisance, not a substitute for measured PM2.5 control.

## Assumptions

- Several numerical reports require unresolved unit assumptions. GekkePrutser [[./thread#^c31541171|GekkePrutser]] and radicality [[./thread#^c31541595|radicality]] label purifier readings as 'ppm'; Havoc [[./thread#^c31544755|Havoc]] gives a reading around 10 without units. These should remain reported display values, not be silently converted into PM2.5 mass concentrations, AQI, or CO2 concentrations.
- Particle counts are assumed to be useful exposure indicators, but they do not identify composition. MengerSponge [[./thread#^c31541703|MengerSponge]] explicitly says the detectors do not perform chemistry, then suggests shower-related particles are probably harmless salts while oven-related particles probably are not. Those composition and hazard judgments are tentative explanations, not chemical analyses supplied with the readings.
- Extending replacement intervals assumes the manufacturer's schedule is conservative for the user's actual loading. chmars [[./thread#^c31540776|chmars]] guesses Xiaomi's recommendation reflects polluted Asian cities and assumes additional months are safe in cleaner air. Havoc [[./thread#^c31544755|Havoc]] makes a similar inference from low readings. Neither establishes a validated extension interval or accounts for every component of a combination filter.
- Rapid symptom changes are attributed to filtration without controlled comparisons. Joeri [[./thread#^c31541134|Joeri]] reports overnight allergy relief, and Danieru [[./thread#^c31544736|Danieru]] links stuffy noses to a child switching off purifiers. These are meaningful personal observations, but other causes are not ruled out. Danieru's separate theory that everyone eventually develops pollen allergies is explicitly a personal theory.
- DIY substitution assumes compatible filters, airflow, and assembly quality. joshvm [[./thread#^c31543640|joshvm]] acknowledges uncertainty about HEPA certification, relies on manufacturer claims for replacement filters, and questions whether cylindrical geometry is better than equal-area flat media. jrochkind1 [[./thread#^c31543718|jrochkind1]] points to available shop-vac filters while also questioning their suitability for the proposed design.
- Using grow-room carbon equipment assumes odor-control experience transfers to the desired gas exposure problem. papercrane [[./thread#^c31543862|papercrane]] describes cannabis odor removal, and Someone1234 [[./thread#^c31543553|Someone1234]] makes a broader claim about harmful gases. The supplied evidence does not establish which gases, concentrations, operating durations, or health-relevant reductions these arrangements address.
- Resetting an indicator is assumed to permit continued use of a still-effective filter; it does not establish restored filtration. alliao [[./thread#^c31545848|alliao]] describes resetting recorded operating hours, and occz [[./thread#^c31541535|occz]] reports successfully installing a device that continually reports full filter health. romseb [[./thread#^c31540685|romseb]] observes that their purifier can run without a filter, illustrating why operation alone cannot demonstrate particle removal.

## Conditions

- Traffic exposure depends on location within a building and outdoor-to-indoor transport. iSloth [[./thread#^c31540900|iSloth]] asks from a home on a UK main road, but supplies no measurements. MengerSponge [[./thread#^c31541703|MengerSponge]] emphasizes prevailing winds and construction; GekkePrutser [[./thread#^c31541171|GekkePrutser]] reports unexpectedly low purifier readings on a second floor beside a busy street with diesel buses. A roadside address does not determine a numerical indoor exposure.
- The accounts span markedly different pollution settings. 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c31542247|5e92cb50239222b]] reports winter PM2.5 averaging approximately 300 µg/m³ in an unspecified location. Danieru [[./thread#^c31544736|Danieru]] describes comparatively clean Tokyo air alongside traffic and severe seasonal pollen. dirtyid [[./thread#^c31574318|dirtyid]] compares Canadian and unspecified Asian-country filter appearances. These contexts limit transfer of maintenance intervals and perceived benefits between homes.
- Wildfire conditions make outdoor air a significant constraint on ventilation. imajoredinecon [[./thread#^c31541473|imajoredinecon]] characterizes unhealthy-to-hazardous smoke as a recurring West Coast problem, while hammock [[./thread#^c31541036|hammock]] supplies a specific smoke-infiltration account. trompetenaccoun [[./thread#^c31541594|trompetenaccoun]] recommends relocation when pollution is persistently severe because home filtration does not protect outdoor activity, but gives no assessment of relocation feasibility or comparative exposure reduction.
- Replacement economics depend on electricity prices as well as filter prices. In Japan, Danieru [[./thread#^c31540844|Danieru]] reports approximately \$30 replacement filters and high-speed operation consuming about four times the electricity of medium speed. They describe an eventual point where replacing a loaded filter becomes cheaper than compensating with higher speed; the proposed different outcome in cheaper-electricity countries is a conditional extrapolation.
- DIY component costs and availability are disputed. kortex [[./thread#^c31544060|kortex]] quotes a few dollars for rectangular filters versus roughly \$30–50 for cylinders and over \$130 for some specialty HEPA cylinders. bsder [[./thread#^c31543491|bsder]] favors locally available rectangular filters and questions duct-fan noise. jrochkind1 [[./thread#^c31543718|jrochkind1]] counters that cylindrical shop-vac filters appear readily available, without resolving suitability or equivalent performance.
- Monitoring and construction budgets influence practical choices. muxneo [[./thread#^c31541668|muxneo]] estimates a DIY design could save \$100–300, without reporting a completed purchase comparison. criddell [[./thread#^c31543091|criddell]] estimates a Dylos would cost about \$25 per reading if used only ten times and asks whether cheaper monitors are adequate. dxhdr [[./thread#^c31543835|dxhdr]] values longer-term hourly monitoring, which implies a different use case from occasional checks.
- Noise, space, replacement-media cost, and serviceability affect sustained use. Mister\_Snuggles [[./thread#^c31544548|Mister\_Snuggles]] contrasts relatively quiet IKEA units, an inexpensive but very noisy litter-box fan, and Bionaire units with costly filters that get louder as they load. Reason077 [[./thread#^c31545062|Reason077]] reports independently replaceable Philips HEPA and carbon filters plus a vacuumable pre-filter. These are concrete ownership differences beyond initial purchase price.

## Lived experiences

- hammock [[./thread#^c31541036|hammock]] describes a drafty San Francisco apartment becoming visibly hazy during wildfires around 2018 and waking with a sore throat. After placing the cheapest Amazon purifier beside the bed, they report the sore throat disappeared after one night. This is a temporal symptom association without particle measurements; hammock [[./thread#^c31541424|hammock]] subsequently limits the benefit claim to local conditions and individual suffering.
- DragonStrength [[./thread#^c31541265|DragonStrength]] reports that one Coway HEPA purifier kept their Bay Area apartment within what they called a 'safe' AQI range during severe fire season, while the hallway smelled strongly of smoke. They identify a Temtop M10 monitor but provide no actual values or threshold definition. teaearlgraycold [[./thread#^c31541321|teaearlgraycold]] separately describes strong satisfaction with a Coway during smoky California conditions, without measurements.
- oppositelock [[./thread#^c31541542|oppositelock]] used a box fan with two 4-inch filters arranged in a triangle through several California fire seasons. They found it effective but loud and bulky, and later judged room-by-room BlueAir 211 units quieter and more effective using an AQI meter; no readings or controlled comparisons are supplied. BasilPH [[./thread#^c31542366|BasilPH]] independently reports building and liking the linked DIY purifier, without numerical performance evidence.
- Joeri [[./thread#^c31541134|Joeri]] reports that a city-dwelling family with persistent coughing, sneezing, and allergies improved overnight after running a Xiaomi purifier in bedrooms for several evening hours. They use a fixed setting because they regard the particulate sensor as mostly useless. The symptom improvement is firsthand; the broader assertion of a known Xiaomi sensor problem is not independently substantiated here.
- Reported relief varies substantially. phphphphp [[./thread#^c31540971|phphphphp]] describes noticeable but incomplete improvement in dust-related breathing problems with a Coway Airmega in London. arinlen [[./thread#^c31541058|arinlen]] says an IKEA Förnuftig at full speed removes their room's dust/pollen-related problems in about a minute. haunter [[./thread#^c31541051|haunter]] reports substantial but incomplete dust reduction in their parents' eighteenth-century house. None supplies numerical exposure or clinical measurements.
- Danieru [[./thread#^c31544736|Danieru]] describes household pollen sensitivity in Japan: their wife is affected seasonally, and they have begun noticing symptoms too. They report recognizing that a child has switched off a purifier only after noses become stuffy. Their household uses induction and does not stir-fry, so this account attributes perceived benefit primarily to pollen and background pollution rather than gas cooking; the causal attribution remains observational.
- ms4720 [[./thread#^c31543168|ms4720]] reports that entering filtered spaces while working in Beijing sometimes felt like a weight had been removed. jaqalopes [[./thread#^c31541266|jaqalopes]] describes odor removal and relief from allergenic dust or breathing irritants more generally, without identifying measured concentrations or a specific comparison. These support perceived comfort benefits in their accounts, not a quantified exposure-response relationship.
- Mister\_Snuggles [[./thread#^c31544548|Mister\_Snuggles]] reports immediate improvement in an asthmatic cat's quality of life after installing purifiers and a motion-activated box fan with a furnace filter near litter boxes, along with improvement in their own health. They specifically find the noisy box-fan arrangement effective for litter dust. No veterinary outcomes, medical measures, or particle readings are supplied.
- radicality [[./thread#^c31541595|radicality]] reports that opening windows to refresh a room and remove CO2 sometimes briefly increases the purifier's displayed 'ppm'. With poor cooking ventilation, frying sometimes pushes that display above 200 before it falls quickly with filtration. This is the clearest account of the ventilation-versus-particle-exposure tradeoff, but the pollutant and units behind the display are unclear and no CO2 measurements are reported.
- bjoli [[./thread#^c31544000|bjoli]] reports that a medium-high-heat stir-fry with poor ventilation can leave pollution above what they describe as harmful levels for hours, without specifying concentrations or equipment. Havoc [[./thread#^c31544774|Havoc]] reports a different cooking observation: automatic purifier speed increases have repeatedly reminded them of a forgotten cooking plate. Neither account quantifies emission rates or establishes a safety threshold.
- GekkePrutser [[./thread#^c31540765|GekkePrutser]] stopped using automatic mode because airflow stayed very low, instead running around 40\% for hay fever and retaining filters for two to three indicated lifetimes. In a later reply, GekkePrutser [[./thread#^c31541171|GekkePrutser]] reports ordinary readings no higher than approximately '10ppm', but over '200ppm' and an immediate fan increase during a small outdoor bin fire, despite no visible indoor smoke and only a slight smell.
- andor [[./thread#^c31542207|andor]] reports that their Xiaomi particulate sensor tracks the city's air-quality network well, while automatic operation barely responds below 50 µg/m³. They instead schedule full-speed operation for 30 or 60 minutes. This separates reported sensor plausibility from dissatisfaction with the control threshold; no correlation statistics, independent indoor reference, or resulting concentration reductions are given.
- chmars [[./thread#^c31540776|chmars]] installed several purifiers in December 2021 in different locations, ran them continuously mostly in automatic mode, and saw every filter reach its indicated end of life on the same day about five months later. The units continued running. The synchronization supports their inference of time-based accounting, although actual filter loading and remaining cleaning capacity were not measured.
- 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c31542247|5e92cb50239222b]] reports continuously using one inexpensive Xiaomi purifier filter for 1.5 years in a location with winter PM2.5 averaging about 300 µg/m³. The filter was nearly black, but DIY PM sensors reportedly showed continued effectiveness without an apparent efficiency difference. No sensor calibration, airflow measurements, or comparison protocol is supplied; increased motor strain is their speculation.
- pawelos [[./thread#^c31540807|pawelos]] reports that a Xiaomi 3H repeatedly displays a red 0\% warning instead of PM2.5, despite the same device's sensor suggesting effective cleaning for several indicated lifetimes. deno [[./thread#^c31540802|deno]] describes a simple 365-day countdown in their unit. tym0 [[./thread#^c31666133|tym0]] reports using a filter for more than a year, but provides no performance measurements. These accounts document indicator behavior and extended use with differing levels of verification.
- Two visual observations caution against treating appearance as a quantitative measure. gruez [[./thread#^c31542380|gruez]] reports gray filters despite winter/spring PM2.5 readings usually at zero or low single digits, with units unspecified. dirtyid [[./thread#^c31574318|dirtyid]] says a filter used for two years in a relatively busy Canadian location looks better than one used for six months in an unspecified Asian country. Neither identifies deposit composition or standardized loading.
- dxhdr [[./thread#^c31543835|dxhdr]] reports owning a Dylos since 2015 and appreciating its hourly sampling mode for observing changes associated with open or closed windows, cooking, and room occupancy. They give no traces or magnitudes and do not specify a universal direction for window-related changes. Their preference for laser over infrared detectors is advice based on their understanding, not comparative results provided here.
- samstave [[./thread#^c31541547|samstave]] reports viewing PurpleAir network readings around 700 near Folsom, California, during fires. This is firsthand observation of a monitoring network, not a measurement inside their own home. Their accompanying suggestion that healthy air is below about 100 receives a direct objection from GekkePrutser [[./thread#^c31545124|GekkePrutser]]; the numerical scale is not explicitly resolved.

## Unresolved disagreements

- This unit provides no NO2 measurements, measured CO2 reduction, or evidence about cognition. Rebelgecko [[./thread#^c31542807|Rebelgecko]] raises gas-stove air quality without identifying NO2, while radicality [[./thread#^c31541595|radicality]] mentions opening windows for CO2 without measuring it. Sleep-related material consists of hammock's waking sore throat [[./thread#^c31541036|hammock]] and azuredragon's reduced nighttime mosquito annoyance [[./thread#^c31542044|azuredragon]], neither of which establishes effects on sleep quality or duration.
- The optimal ventilation strategy remains unresolved. radicality [[./thread#^c31541595|radicality]] reports increased purifier readings when opening windows for fresh air and CO2 removal; throwaway290 [[./thread#^c31541220|throwaway290]] recommends closing windows when outdoor air is bad. dxhdr [[./thread#^c31543835|dxhdr]] observes changes with window position but supplies no paired indoor/outdoor data. There is no simultaneous particle-and-CO2 series that establishes when, how far, or how long to open windows.
- Sensor reliability and automatic-control adequacy remain distinct, contested issues. Joeri [[./thread#^c31541134|Joeri]] calls the Xiaomi sensor mostly useless; andor [[./thread#^c31542207|andor]] finds good agreement with a city network but an excessive automatic threshold; GekkePrutser [[./thread#^c31541171|GekkePrutser]] demonstrates a response to smoke. None supplies calibration data. 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c31542349|5e92cb50239222b]] attributes the threshold to Chinese standards, but that explanation is not verified; criddell's evaluation link [[./thread#^c31544031|criddell]] contains no reproduced test results in the prompt.
- The discussion does not establish a common meaning of 'safe' air. samstave [[./thread#^c31541547|samstave]] suggests a value below about 100, GekkePrutser [[./thread#^c31545124|GekkePrutser]] says healthy levels are much lower, DragonStrength [[./thread#^c31541265|DragonStrength]] reports staying in a 'safe' AQI range, and throwaway290 [[./thread#^c31541220|throwaway290]] rejects any safe PM2.5 level. Unspecified scales and differing uses of safety prevent a defensible numerical reconciliation.
- The replacement point for a loaded filter is not established. VoidWhisperer [[./thread#^c31540700|VoidWhisperer]] questions whether bypassing lifetime tracking defeats useful maintenance, while random3 [[./thread#^c31541604|random3]] asks whether used filters lose efficiency or release particles. Long-use reports such as 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c31542247|5e92cb50239222b]] show reported continued particle reduction, but do not resolve remaining airflow, energy cost, bypass, motor durability, or particle release. Danieru's economic crossover [[./thread#^c31540844|Danieru]] remains household-specific.
- DIY geometry and performance equivalence remain open. joshvm [[./thread#^c31543306|joshvm]] proposes a cylindrical filter as a simpler construction, then explicitly questions whether it outperforms equal-area flat media [[./thread#^c31543640|joshvm]]. The cited low duct-fan noise figure is a claim from the linked design, not their measurement. oppositelock's comparative ownership experience [[./thread#^c31541542|oppositelock]] is informative but cannot isolate filter area, fan behavior, room conditions, or geometry.
- Replacement-lock behavior cannot be generalized across Xiaomi devices. GekkePrutser initially says newer models shut down [[./thread#^c31541143|GekkePrutser]], then attributes that information to a forum and says their own unit does not [[./thread#^c31542540|GekkePrutser]]. throwaway4good reports a 3H operating with a non-Xiaomi filter and only a warning [[./thread#^c31540953|throwaway4good]] [[./thread#^c31542309|throwaway4good]]; Mandatum reports no shutdown in Australia [[./thread#^c31544684|Mandatum]]. EU location and firmware-update explanations remain speculation in throwaway4good [[./thread#^c31542846|throwaway4good]] and GekkePrutser [[./thread#^c31545079|GekkePrutser]].
- Third-party testing and purchase recommendations are contested without resolution. KennyBlanken [[./thread#^c31541669|KennyBlanken]] cites Wirecutter testing in support of aged-filter performance. inferiorhuman [[./thread#^c31542843|inferiorhuman]] alleges ignored reports of filter VOCs and fan failures and personally reports Coway warranty problems; only the warranty experience is explicitly firsthand. aardvarkr [[./thread#^c31542034|aardvarkr]] replies to the affiliate-fee criticism that retailers pay those fees, which does not settle whether the purifier testing or recommendations were sound.
- Claims about nonmechanical purification remain unevaluated. Someone1234 [[./thread#^c31543553|Someone1234]] warns that ionizers and UV features may be ineffective or generate ozone. techwiz137 [[./thread#^c31541298|techwiz137]] asks whether their inexpensive purifier and UV feature actually work, and DragonStrength [[./thread#^c31541431|DragonStrength]] describes distrust arising partly from a family member's fanless UV product. The unit supplies no ozone readings, germ-removal tests, or device-specific evaluation to settle those claims.
