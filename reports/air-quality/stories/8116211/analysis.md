# [Grad Student Rigs Cheap Alternative to \$1,000 Air Purifiers in China](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=8116211)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

The discussion evaluates inexpensive DIY HEPA setups against costly commercial purifiers, with personal reports about measured performance, noise, drafts, room coverage, and living through severe pollution in China. Commenters debate whether the comparison accounts for different particle sizes, filter stages, chemical pollutants, airflow, convenience, and replacement costs. A broad political exchange considers whether China’s pollution illustrates free-market failures, weak enforcement, corruption, or the limits of market-based theories.
Tags: DIY-air-purifiers, HEPA, China, indoor-air-quality, product-costs, free-market-debate

## Relevance

A firsthand account describes using multiple DIY and commercial purifiers in Shanghai, checking performance with a laser particle counter, and reducing drafts to limit polluted-air ingress. The discussion compares noise, cost, particle filtration, prefilters, and the limits of HEPA-only solutions. ## Arguments

- Low-cost particle filtration is presented as technically plausible: devicenull [[./thread#^c8116696|devicenull]] describes HEPA media plus a box fan as common and cheap, while colechristensen [[./thread#^c8118392|colechristensen]] argues that an expensive enclosure need not provide better filter media. His \$130 purifier’s stated 99.97\% capture rating for particles \>0.3 µm is a product specification he reports, not an independently measured room-cleaning result.
- peferron [[./thread#^c8118031|peferron]] recommends combining filtration with reduced infiltration when outdoor air is polluted. After repeatedly checking four inexpensive purifiers with a laser particle counter in Shanghai, he advises sealing drafts and running noisy units in temporarily unoccupied rooms. His account supports a practical strategy for that apartment, without establishing equivalent results in other buildings.
- rahimnathwani [[./thread#^c8118394|rahimnathwani]] argues that a pre-filter is necessary to prevent larger particles from quickly compromising a HEPA filter. Replying, seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c8118994|seanmcdirmid]] describes multiple filtration stages and emphasizes the smaller-particle problem. Neither supplies measurements of loading, replacement intervals, or declining performance.
- omegaworks [[./thread#^c8116345|omegaworks]] frames private clean-air spaces as an unequal response to outdoor pollution: protection depends on being able to afford it while pollution costs remain shared. The comparison between healthcare, private filtration, and cleaning ambient air is a rhetorical economic argument, not a supplied cost calculation.

## Counterarguments

- Replying to colechristensen’s inexpensive-HEPA argument, seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c8118969|seanmcdirmid]] says his Beijing experience favors Blueair: other units he says they tested did not let them sleep during very bad pollution. He emphasizes sufficient airflow for an entire apartment. This challenges comparisons based only on filter-media specifications, but his unspecified tests do not establish that inexpensive units generally fail.
- Premium equipment may offer useful comfort and operating advantages. rahimnathwani [[./thread#^c8117772|rahimnathwani]] values an IQAir unit that he says is quiet enough for bedroom use at full speed and easy to wheel around. zdean [[./thread#^c8117987|zdean]], disclosing employment by a higher-end purifier manufacturer, argues that noise, design, usability, allergies, and chemical sensitivities create different requirements; this is an industry perspective rather than comparative test evidence.
- amanfredi [[./thread#^c8117324|amanfredi]] objects to treating particle removal as the whole purification problem: HEPA addresses particulate contaminants, while some expensive devices include activated charcoal and other chemical filters. This qualifies claims of complete equivalence between products, although the comment provides no contaminant-specific removal results.
- Responding to omegaworks’s economic framing, josephschmoe [[./thread#^c8117034|josephschmoe]] argues that sickness and premature death can make pollution costly even under a narrowly commercial calculation. He also argues that private indoor protection leaves exposure whenever people go outdoors. These are assertions about health and economic consequences, without quantified risks or costs in the discussion.

## Assumptions

- The claim that similar HEPA media imply similar protection assumes comparable airflow, room coverage, pollutant entry, and operating conditions. The exchange between colechristensen [[./thread#^c8118392|colechristensen]] and seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c8118969|seanmcdirmid]] does not establish those conditions; their disagreement concerns complete devices and environments as well as media.
- Visible filter dirt is only indirect evidence. rahimnathwani [[./thread#^c8117772|rahimnathwani]] reports secondhand that acquaintances think the inexpensive kits work because their filters become dirty. That observation does not establish the reduction in airborne fine particles or occupants’ exposure.
- Particle-size and pollution-level terminology cannot be accepted literally throughout the thread. seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c8117827|seanmcdirmid]] uses '2.5 ppm' and '10 ppm' to distinguish particles; colechristensen [[./thread#^c8118392|colechristensen]] and homulilly [[./thread#^c8122388|homulilly]] explicitly challenge those units. The intended particulate distinction should not be converted into a verified concentration measurement.
- The source does not establish a controlled relationship between filtration and health. homulilly [[./thread#^c8117169|homulilly]] describes using a purifier for allergies and dust, while seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c8118969|seanmcdirmid]] attributes being able to sleep on severe-pollution days to Blueair. Neither supplies symptom tracking, measured sleep outcomes, or controls for other explanations.
- Linked material supplies no additional findings within this export. Chromozon [[./thread#^c8116552|Chromozon]] posts a BeijingAir link, goatforce5 [[./thread#^c8118197|goatforce5]] mentions an earlier Consumer Reports discussion of DIY filtration, and \_delirium [[./thread#^c8116718|\_delirium]] links another HN thread. Their comments do not reproduce measurements or study results that can resolve this discussion.

## Conditions

- Geography and season limit comparisons. peferron [[./thread#^c8118031|peferron]] describes polluted outdoor air entering a Shanghai apartment; seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c8118969|seanmcdirmid]] describes Beijing conditions as much worse in winter. The latter calls a reported level of '100' locally 'clean air' and mentions days at '800,' but his challenged terminology prevents treating these as unambiguous PM2.5 concentrations.
- Room coverage affects equipment requirements and expense. seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c8118969|seanmcdirmid]] says colleagues use multiple approximately \$1,000 units to cover an apartment and separate children’s bedrooms. peferron [[./thread#^c8118031|peferron]] uses four inexpensive units. Neither provides apartment dimensions or comparable airflow measurements.
- Prices quoted in this 2014 discussion span roughly \$130 for colechristensen’s device [[./thread#^c8118392|colechristensen]], \$150 for homulilly’s [[./thread#^c8117169|homulilly]], and over \$1,000 for rahimnathwani’s [[./thread#^c8117772|rahimnathwani]]. In response to guelo’s suggestion of Chinese manufacture [[./thread#^c8117228|guelo]], homulilly [[./thread#^c8117244|homulilly]] confirms that origin and calls the higher pricing gouging. Manufacturing location and price differences alone do not establish equivalent performance or profit margins.
- Purchase price is not the only financial consideration. rahimnathwani [[./thread#^c8117772|rahimnathwani]] mentions three replacement filter modules and suspects substantial manufacturer margins, while still finding the convenience worthwhile. hcarvalhoalves [[./thread#^c8118600|hcarvalhoalves]] quotes activated carbon at about \$10/kg retail. Neither supplies enough information to compare complete-device lifetime costs.
- Availability of knowledge may differ by location: devicenull [[./thread#^c8116696|devicenull]] considers fan-plus-HEPA construction familiar, whereas Zikes [[./thread#^c8117133|Zikes]] suggests it may be uncommon in China. This is a tentative explanation of the story’s novelty, not evidence about national adoption rates.

## Lived experiences

- peferron [[./thread#^c8118031|peferron]] reports owning four inexpensive purifiers in Shanghai and repeatedly verifying their effectiveness with a laser particle counter. This is the clearest firsthand instrumental account in the unit, but it includes no before-and-after readings, counter model, calibration information, particle-size channels, or measurement protocol.
- peferron [[./thread#^c8118031|peferron]] describes the units as loud, recommends filtering bedrooms during the day and living space at night, and says he lined his windows with hardware-store foam tape. He had decided to move back to the Bay Area because of both the continuing struggle with pollution and poor international internet connectivity; the comment does not report a completed move or subsequent exposure changes.
- seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c8118969|seanmcdirmid]] reports owning Blueair in Beijing and says it was the only option among those they tested that allowed sleep on extremely polluted days. The account conveys perceived sleep and comfort benefits, but gives neither the competing models nor instrumental results supporting the comparison.
- rahimnathwani [[./thread#^c8117772|rahimnathwani]] reports that his own \$1,000-plus IQAir purifiers can run at full speed in the bedroom at night without unacceptable noise, and that wheels make relocation convenient. His favorable firsthand ownership account is distinct from his acquaintances’ secondhand reports about noisy DIY kits.
- homulilly [[./thread#^c8117169|homulilly]] reports owning a roughly \$150 true-HEPA purifier used for allergies and dust. colechristensen [[./thread#^c8118392|colechristensen]] reports owning a \$130 model and searching unsuccessfully for higher-grade HEPA products outside laboratory equipment. These establish ownership and reported specifications, not measured performance under the Beijing conditions described elsewhere.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The price-versus-performance dispute remains unresolved. colechristensen [[./thread#^c8118392|colechristensen]] sees expensive products largely as costly packaging around comparable media; seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c8118969|seanmcdirmid]] reports a decisive practical advantage for Blueair; peferron [[./thread#^c8118031|peferron]] reports successful inexpensive filtration in Shanghai. Different locations, devices, room conditions, and evidence types prevent either a universal equivalence claim or a universal need for premium equipment.
- Sensor reliability and actual PM2.5 exposure remain uncertain. peferron [[./thread#^c8118031|peferron]] supplies a firsthand account of repeated particle-counter checks but no readings or validation. seanmcdirmid’s severity figures [[./thread#^c8118969|seanmcdirmid]] coexist with the unit confusion challenged by colechristensen [[./thread#^c8118392|colechristensen]]. The thread therefore offers neither a calibrated PM2.5 exposure series nor a sensor-accuracy comparison.
- Chemical filtration is disputed without supporting tests. amanfredi [[./thread#^c8117324|amanfredi]] points to charcoal and other chemical filters; replying, hcarvalhoalves [[./thread#^c8118600|hcarvalhoalves]] claims activated carbon removes only odors and suggests high-end products are more likely to use ionization. Neither establishes removal of particular gases, and the discussion contains no NO2 or CO2 measurements or demonstrated removal results.
- The window-opening tradeoff is only partly addressed. peferron [[./thread#^c8118031|peferron]] identifies incoming polluted air as a problem and recommends sealing drafts, supporting caution about admitting outdoor air in his Shanghai circumstances. He does not compare open versus closed windows or measure ventilation, CO2, or indoor-generated pollution. This account cannot determine when opening windows would improve overall exposure.
- Health evidence is limited to intended allergy relief in homulilly’s account [[./thread#^c8117169|homulilly]], perceived ability to sleep in seanmcdirmid’s account [[./thread#^c8118969|seanmcdirmid]], and broad sickness and mortality claims from josephschmoe [[./thread#^c8117034|josephschmoe]]. No study findings, cognitive testing, or quantified health outcomes are supplied. The unit also provides no firsthand cooking, traffic, or wildfire-smoke exposure comparisons.
- Pollution-source attribution remains speculative. bduerst [[./thread#^c8117061|bduerst]] asks whether government-built coal power plants explain China’s air pollution; acadien [[./thread#^c8117753|acadien]] adds coal-burning fireplaces distributed through a welfare program but explicitly lacks comparative source data. The identical repeated comment by acadien [[./thread#^c8117754|acadien]] adds no independent evidence, and neither account measures personal exposure from those sources.
