# [Updated WHO global air quality guidelines](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=28624831)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion interprets the updated WHO air-quality guidelines, clarifying annual averages, daily limits, interim targets, and how the recommendations relate to familiar AQI scales. Participants debate whether any particulate exposure is safe and whether particle sources differ in harm. They share experiences of severe smog, compare pollution across regions, and discuss the economic and political difficulty of reducing it. The thread also covers sensor recommendations, European standards, domestic heating, industrial emissions, wildfires, and the effects of geography and wind on local air quality.
Tags: WHO-guidelines, air-pollution, PM2.5, PM10, AQI, public-health, air-quality-sensors, policy, regional-comparisons

## Relevance

The discussion interprets WHO PM2.5 and PM10 guideline levels and considers how particulate types and sources affect risk. Firsthand accounts describe throat irritation, voice loss, and difficulty being outdoors during high-AQI episodes, while comments discuss smoke, household burning, outdoor masks, and sensor options. The accounts differ in severity and are not controlled evidence. ## Arguments

- The guideline discussion distinguishes sustained exposure from short peaks. dahart [[./thread#^c28625647|dahart]] reports annual targets of 5 µg/m³ for PM2.5 and 15 µg/m³ for PM10. Answering gruez’s question about averaging [[./thread#^c28632378|gruez]], dahart [[./thread#^c28634794|dahart]] explains that these are annual averages, with separate 99th-percentile daily limits of 15 and 45 µg/m³ respectively. These are not instructions that every instantaneous reading must remain below the annual target.
- A reassuring AQI label need not mean negligible risk. dahart [[./thread#^c28625647|dahart]] maps 5 µg/m³ PM2.5 to approximately AQI 21, within the then-current “good” category, and attributes the tighter WHO recommendations to stronger statistical evidence of harm at lower concentrations. strogonoff [[./thread#^c28627439|strogonoff]] goes further, citing Wikipedia for the claim that particulates appear to have no safe level. These are interpretations of external evidence, not measurements or health studies conducted by the commenters.
- Some participants invoke substantial long-term health burdens. mensetmanusman [[./thread#^c28627772|mensetmanusman]] says researchers have calculated lost life expectancy from repeated exposure, without supplying an estimate or methodology. input\_sh [[./thread#^c28626887|input\_sh]] cites a report estimating approximately 19,000 deaths across Europe over three years attributable to Western Balkan coal plants. The export supplies these claims and links, but not the underlying analyses needed to assess attribution or uncertainty.
- Exposure can depend on where emissions occur, rather than total emitted mass alone. Replying to a comparison with coal plants, 7952 [[./thread#^c28628027|7952]] argues that domestic burners discharge near people and ground level, whereas power stations use tall stacks. MichaelZuo [[./thread#^c28628380|MichaelZuo]] adds an explicitly conjectural distinction between plant filtration and unfiltered fireplaces. This supports examining nearby sources, while leaving their actual contribution to an individual’s exposure unmeasured.
- Suggested improvements address different points in exposure: mensetmanusman [[./thread#^c28627785|mensetmanusman]] recommends normalizing outdoor N95 use in response to winter-smog complaints; rhacker [[./thread#^c28630616|rhacker]] sees increased need for HEPA air cleaning; danw1979 [[./thread#^c28626453|danw1979]] considers replacing a neighbor’s coal heating with a heat pump. These are recommendations or contemplated actions, not comparative trials demonstrating achieved reductions or health benefits.
- Participants describe accessible ways to collect particle data. 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c28627590|5e92cb50239222b]] built an ESP32/PMS5003 monitor, and stavros [[./thread#^c28629007|stavros]] confirms that approach was easy. tzs [[./thread#^c28630518|tzs]] describes obtaining raw SPS30 readings through a USB/serial adapter and sample software, including periodic cleaning; they distinguish UART mass-and-number outputs from I2C mass-only output. This establishes practical data-access options, without establishing accuracy.

## Counterarguments

- Replying directly to LurkingPenguin’s concern about children’s lifelong exposure, Proven [[./thread#^c28626363|Proven]] dismisses the concern, asserting that people exposed as children in earlier decades are not significantly worse off and may outlive Western peers. No cohort, exposure comparison, or supporting analysis is supplied. This is a conflicting population-level assertion, not a demonstrated rebuttal of the reported symptoms or long-term risk.
- The claim that particulate exposure is harmful encounters disagreement over composition. Answering infogulch’s question about natural particles [[./thread#^c28628204|infogulch]], rubatuga [[./thread#^c28632500|rubatuga]] calls aerosolized salts from tap-water humidifiers essentially harmless despite counting as PM2.5. aaron695 [[./thread#^c28635225|aaron695]] directly contradicts that, claiming equal lethality and recommending water filtration. In a separate reply, MichaelZuo [[./thread#^c28628367|MichaelZuo]] identifies asbestos, brake dust, and lead-containing dust as especially harmful, while speculating that most other particles are roughly comparable. None supplies a comparative toxicity analysis.
- mensetmanusman [[./thread#^c28627730|mensetmanusman]] argues that domestic fires emit far less particulate matter than coal plants. Beyond 7952’s proximity objection [[./thread#^c28628027|7952]], legulere [[./thread#^c28651134|legulere]] disputes the implied European source hierarchy, citing an atlas for the claim that traffic, industry, and heating now contribute on the same order of magnitude. Those claims concern different scales and contexts; the thread does not establish a universal ranking of household and industrial sources.
- A claim about regulation does not settle actual local exposure. legulere [[./thread#^c28626021|legulere]] says most European countries banned domestic coal burning decades ago and emphasizes wood heating, cars, and population density. mnd999 [[./thread#^c28626353|mnd999]] replies that enforcement is lax and suburban households still burn coal and logs. danw1979’s account of an upwind coal-burning neighbor [[./thread#^c28626453|danw1979]] illustrates a continuing local source, without establishing how widespread such exceptions are.
- LurkingPenguin [[./thread#^c28629371|LurkingPenguin]] corrects the China-centered framing of mensetmanusman’s reply [[./thread#^c28627772|mensetmanusman]]: their own location is Taiwan, where they identify domestic coal generation, factories, and scooters alongside pollution transported from China. Their response argues against treating Asian exposure as a single-country problem or assigning every polluted day to foreign sources; it does not quantify the respective contributions.
- The proposed explanation that ocean water cleans passing air remains contested. 14 [[./thread#^c28627581|14]] explicitly presents absorption by the ocean as a theory. Dumblydorr [[./thread#^c28628541|Dumblydorr]] doubts it is a major factor and emphasizes distance from pollution sources and prevailing winds instead. tyho69 [[./thread#^c28629469|tyho69]] also emphasizes low local emissions and Atlantic airflow, while noting ozone and sea-salt aerosols rather than describing maritime air as pollutant-free.

## Assumptions

- Using reported ambient AQI as a measure of personal cumulative exposure requires assumptions about averaging periods, time outdoors, and indoor conditions. LurkingPenguin [[./thread#^c28625474|LurkingPenguin]] reports weeks above AQI 100, while rhacker [[./thread#^c28630616|rhacker]] supplies remembered and anticipated California ranges. Neither identifies a personal monitor, calculation method, or exposure record. dahart’s distinction between annual averages and daily limits [[./thread#^c28634794|dahart]] makes those missing time scales consequential.
- The symptom accounts attribute discomfort to polluted air through timing and circumstances. okal [[./thread#^c28625551|okal]] describes nearly fainting while waiting outside in Gurgaon, and spaetzleesser [[./thread#^c28629142|spaetzleesser]] describes leaving Mumbai after two days of intolerable conditions. These are firsthand experiences, but the comments provide no clinical assessment or measurements identifying which pollutant caused a particular symptom.
- Remaining in an air-conditioned room is implicitly treated as refuge in okal’s Gurgaon account [[./thread#^c28625551|okal]]. The comment supplies no indoor particle readings, ventilation rate, or filtration specification. It therefore establishes an avoidance behavior, not proof that air conditioning itself reduced PM2.5 or maintained healthy gas concentrations.
- Owner satisfaction, ease of assembly, and price are imperfect substitutes for sensor validation. jschulenklopper [[./thread#^c28627949|jschulenklopper]] says the Awair Element meets their needs, and fdr [[./thread#^c28630870|fdr]] liked it enough to buy two. goblin89 [[./thread#^c28627475|goblin89]] only presumes that LaserEgg is less reliable than AirVisual and explicitly cannot assess performance. None of these comments demonstrates accuracy or agreement with a reference instrument.
- Any recommendation to open windows would require assumptions about outdoor conditions and the indoor problem being addressed. danw1979 [[./thread#^c28626453|danw1979]] reports choking outdoor coal smoke, while ehnto [[./thread#^c28626830|ehnto]] describes prolonged exposure to remotely transported wildfire smoke. These accounts make cleaner incoming air an unsafe blanket assumption. They do not, however, report a window-opening experiment or measure the resulting indoor exposure.

## Conditions

- Cost and access constrain monitoring choices. In this 2021 discussion, acksal [[./thread#^c28631049|acksal]] reports an Awair price increase from £179 to £299; goblin89 [[./thread#^c28627475|goblin89]] describes LaserEgg as cheaper than AirVisual but says customs in their unspecified country prohibited personal importation of air-quality monitors. snthd [[./thread#^c28627139|snthd]] points to a \$12 IKEA sensor with a Wi-Fi logging modification, explicitly without recommending it. These are historical purchasing reports, not current prices or verified performance comparisons.
- Participants describe economic obstacles to cleaner exposure without establishing that pollution is unavoidable. spaetzleesser [[./thread#^c28629074|spaetzleesser]] criticizes accepting known harm because action is considered expensive. LurkingPenguin [[./thread#^c28629371|LurkingPenguin]] argues that Taiwan’s dependence on manufacturing complicates pollution reduction and links Asian emissions partly to Western consumption. Those are explanations of perceived incentives and constraints, not quantified assessments of cleaner alternatives.
- The reported geography includes substantial cross-border transport. LurkingPenguin [[./thread#^c28629371|LurkingPenguin]] attributes some Malaysian pollution to Indonesian fires and severe seasonal pollution around Chiang Mai to agricultural fires in Myanmar and Laos. These are the commenter’s regional descriptions, not supplied source-apportionment measurements. They also distinguish agricultural burning from industrial emissions, so the discussion’s various smoke sources should not all be labeled wildfire.
- Terrain and season are presented as exposure conditions. input\_sh [[./thread#^c28626887|input\_sh]] describes Sarajevo and Skopje as valleys surrounded by hills or mountains where smog can persist through cold weather without rain or snow. beebeepka [[./thread#^c28625825|beebeepka]] associates winter deterioration with household coal burning and old vehicles. 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c28627578|5e92cb50239222b]] says the Balkan examples remain comparatively mild beside some Asian locations, explicitly grounding that comparison in personal experience rather than a standardized regional analysis.
- Coastal location offers a qualified advantage in this discussion. Tepix [[./thread#^c28627007|Tepix]] calls Ireland’s west coast Europe’s best place for air quality; tyho69 [[./thread#^c28629469|tyho69]] explains low local emissions and Atlantic winds but identifies baseline ozone and elevated sea-salt aerosols. aaron695 [[./thread#^c28635225|aaron695]] proposes enjoying the sea breeze while using filtered air at other times, framing relaxation and lower urban pollution as benefits. Neither the regional ranking nor this comfort–exposure tradeoff is quantified.
- Regulatory ambitions and present exposure are separate questions. eisa01 [[./thread#^c28625676|eisa01]] asks how the WHO recommendations compare with EU guidance. wjnc [[./thread#^c28626008|wjnc]] reports a Dutch pact targeting the previous WHO guidelines by 2030 and predicts much slower compliance with the new ones. This is a reported policy timetable and personal forecast; it supplies neither a numerical EU comparison nor current local concentrations.

## Lived experiences

- LurkingPenguin [[./thread#^c28625474|LurkingPenguin]], later identifying Taiwan as their home [[./thread#^c28629371|LurkingPenguin]], describes weeks with AQI above 100 and high PM2.5, with noticeable physical effects after even an hour outdoors. Their concern about accumulated years of exposure and children’s health is prospective; they do not report a diagnosed chronic condition, individual dose, or measured childhood outcome.
- okal [[./thread#^c28625551|okal]] contrasts living in Nairobi with visiting Gurgaon, where they initially mistook dense smog for clouds and nearly passed out while waiting for a taxi. They mostly stayed in air-conditioned rooms and found a short visit to Delhi noticeably better. Visits to cleaner cities have made air quality a possible reason to relocate. These are place-specific experiences without pollutant measurements.
- nomercy400 [[./thread#^c28625964|nomercy400]] describes AQI 200-plus conditions as producing a stinging, hoarse or raspy sensation. Replying to that description, spaetzleesser [[./thread#^c28629142|spaetzleesser]] says a Mumbai visit became intolerable after two days. 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c28627644|5e92cb50239222b]] reports that people locally can lose their voices for days after one or two hours in winter smog. The last account describes a perceived local pattern, rather than explicitly documenting the commenter’s own medical episode.
- danw1979 [[./thread#^c28626453|danw1979]] reports choking outdoor conditions in winter from one upwind neighbor’s coal fire, in a village of roughly 4,000 people with mains gas since the 1990s. The nuisance is severe enough that they are considering paying for a heat pump and the neighbor’s electricity. The proposal illustrates willingness to bear substantial costs to remove a nearby source, but no replacement or resulting improvement has occurred in the account.
- Responding to the single-neighbor account, 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c28627501|5e92cb50239222b]] describes living among thousands of households burning sulfur-rich coal for 10–11 months annually, sometimes with plastics and tires. Elsewhere, they report small boiler houses with low chimneys emitting black smoke continuously for much of the year [[./thread#^c28628472|5e92cb50239222b]]. Their location is unspecified, and the described absence of emission controls is not accompanied by testing or inspection records.
- ehnto [[./thread#^c28626830|ehnto]] describes the 2020 Australian wildfires changing daily life despite the fires being in another state, thousands of kilometers away. They watched Windy particulate maps and forecasts to time bicycle commutes, with weeks when riding seemed unwise. This is an actual account of using forecast information to manage activity; it does not quantify personal exposure reductions or health outcomes.
- rhacker [[./thread#^c28630616|rhacker]] contrasts remembered early-life AQI around 5–25 with an expected California summer range of 80–500 in approximately the same area. This is a recollection and outlook, not a supplied measurement series or explicit attribution to a particular fire. Separately, Dumblydorr [[./thread#^c28628541|Dumblydorr]] reports East Coast US haze and smog following fires to the west or northwest, supporting their argument about transported pollution.
- There is firsthand experience with monitor ownership and construction, but little measurement output. 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c28627590|5e92cb50239222b]] wrote firmware for an ESP32/PMS5003 setup, and stavros [[./thread#^c28629007|stavros]] shares a configuration after confirming the approach was easy. jschulenklopper [[./thread#^c28627949|jschulenklopper]] and fdr [[./thread#^c28630870|fdr]] express satisfaction with Awair Elements. None provides readings, calibration results, or evidence that monitoring itself improved exposure.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The approved question extends beyond this unit’s evidence. NO2 appears only in dahart’s summary of pollutants covered by WHO guidance [[./thread#^c28634794|dahart]]; no NO2 concentration or health experience is reported. That comment mentions CO, not CO2. There are no supplied CO2 readings, cognition or sleep outcomes, or indoor cooking measurements. okal’s air-conditioned-room account [[./thread#^c28625551|okal]] does not establish indoor air chemistry, and the heating-source discussion cannot substitute for cooking or ventilation evidence.
- Particle composition remains a substantive disagreement. infogulch [[./thread#^c28628204|infogulch]] asks whether natural pollen, bacteria, and forest particles remain harmful as other pollution falls. rubatuga’s harmless-salt assertion [[./thread#^c28632500|rubatuga]], aaron695’s equal-lethality assertion [[./thread#^c28635225|aaron695]], and MichaelZuo’s mixture of specific hazards and broad equivalence speculation [[./thread#^c28628367|MichaelZuo]] do not resolve the question. The thread supplies no matched-dose comparison of natural aerosols, humidifier emissions, combustion smoke, and traffic particles.
- Immediate discomfort, chronic disease, and longevity are not reconciled by these comments. LurkingPenguin [[./thread#^c28625474|LurkingPenguin]] reports short-term effects and fears accumulated harm; Proven [[./thread#^c28626363|Proven]] counters with unsupported longevity comparisons; mensetmanusman [[./thread#^c28627772|mensetmanusman]] invokes unspecified statistical estimates. The supplied material cannot determine an individual’s lost life expectancy or explain why people with similar reported exposure might experience different symptoms.
- Sensor reliability and data provenance remain largely untested. goblin89 [[./thread#^c28627475|goblin89]] supplies only a presumed LaserEgg/AirVisual comparison and a pointer to AirGradient. tzs [[./thread#^c28630518|tzs]] explains interfaces and software rather than benchmarking accuracy. MrsPeaches [[./thread#^c28627108|MrsPeaches]] assumes Airly may share data, while meteo-jeff [[./thread#^c28628398|meteo-jeff]] describes a future CAMS-based API. Neither proposed data sharing nor a planned API constitutes an observed personal measurement or validation result.
- The practical interventions lack measured comparisons. Outdoor N95 use is recommended by mensetmanusman [[./thread#^c28627785|mensetmanusman]], HEPA cleaning is suggested by rhacker [[./thread#^c28630616|rhacker]], and a heat-pump replacement is contemplated by danw1979 [[./thread#^c28626453|danw1979]], but no achieved reductions, operating costs, or comfort outcomes are supplied. Opening windows might admit the outdoor smoke described by danw1979 and ehnto [[./thread#^c28626830|ehnto]]; this unit does not establish when ventilation would instead relieve an indoor buildup enough to improve overall exposure.
- Geographic rankings remain incomplete. hendry [[./thread#^c28627165|hendry]] asks about Singapore, and blago [[./thread#^c28630129|blago]] supplies a historical-data link without reproducing readings. Tepix’s favorable ranking of western Ireland [[./thread#^c28627007|Tepix]] is not accompanied by comparative measurements, although tyho69 [[./thread#^c28629469|tyho69]] offers a qualified atmospheric explanation. These accounts identify potentially important local conditions, but do not establish uniform air quality across countries or a population-wide HN consensus.
