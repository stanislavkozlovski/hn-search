# [Air quality monitors: paying more does not get you more accuracy](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=36234790)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

The discussion examines evidence that expensive consumer air-quality monitors may use the same low-cost particle sensors as cheaper devices, and asks what price does or does not indicate about accuracy. Participants discuss sensor testing, calibration, environmental effects, aging, limits in detecting ultrafine particles, and the distinction between useful trends and reliable absolute readings. They compare PM and CO2 sensors, consumer and research instruments, and DIY options such as AirGradient, ESPHome, and IKEA-based projects; other topics include device reliability, sensor networks, open licensing, and possible uses for local pollution monitoring.
Tags: air quality monitors, sensor accuracy, PM2.5, CO2, calibration, DIY electronics, open hardware

## Relevance

The discussion focuses on air-monitor accuracy and limitations, including PM2.5 sensor comparisons, CO2 calibration and drift, and unreliable estimated CO2 readings. Firsthand reports describe cooking spikes, wildfire monitoring, ventilation responses, sensor failures, and the value of relative trends versus exact readings. ## Arguments

- Price is a weak standalone guide to consumer PM-monitor accuracy in these accounts. Article author and AirGradient maintainer ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36235954|ahaucnx]] reports finding a sub-US\$20 PM module inside a monitor costing over US\$1,000. AceJohnny2 [[./thread#^c36237252|AceJohnny2]] similarly observes that multiple consumer products wrap the same Plantower PMS5003. This supports checking the underlying technology and validation rather than assuming a premium price guarantees better measurements.
- Relative changes can inform household actions even when absolute accuracy is uncertain. crazygringo [[./thread#^c36243996|crazygringo]] illustrates this with CO2 readings falling from 1200 to 500 and PM2.5 changing from 2 to 25 and back, arguing that even approximately 20\% error may be tolerable for learning when to ventilate. These are illustrative examples and a judgment about personal use, not a documented measurement experiment or health threshold.
- Measuring CO2 directly is materially different from estimating it through another pollutant. mnw21cam [[./thread#^c36240326|mnw21cam]] distinguishes direct CO2 sensors from devices that infer CO2 from VOCs; GuB-42 [[./thread#^c36261051|GuB-42]] warns that some very cheap products extrapolate numerical readings from nonspecific air sensors. Their argument concerns what is actually measured, beyond whether a display responds to changing conditions.
- Monitoring should match the exposure being investigated. For a pottery studio, ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36239839|ahaucnx]] recommends PM and VOC measurements, with CO2 as an indication of ventilation. The same comment explains that AirGradient's outdoor kit emphasizes dual PM modules, whereas the indoor kit adds CO2 and optional TVOC sensing; the products therefore answer different questions.
- Reference comparisons across operating conditions are proposed as a stronger accuracy test. Leo\_Germond [[./thread#^c36238543|Leo\_Germond]] recommends placing multiple sensors around a certified reference and following their performance across age, temperature, wind, pollution levels, and measurement duration. This addresses more than whether a device produces plausible-looking numbers.
- PM mass alone may miss health-relevant distinctions between particle populations. dognotdog [[./thread#^c36240865|dognotdog]] argues that particle count, deposited surface area, and size-dependent penetration into lungs or cells can tell a different story from total mass. These are technical claims about potential health mechanisms, not firsthand clinical findings or independently assessed study results in this export.

## Counterarguments

- An inexpensive sensing element does not establish that the finished instrument's additional cost is worthless. joshvm [[./thread#^c36241304|joshvm]] points to firmware, calibration, and traceability as potentially valuable expenses. In a separate exchange, fuzzehchat [[./thread#^c36239323|fuzzehchat]] objects that cheap and expensive sensor modules differ, while AlfeG [[./thread#^c36239407|AlfeG]] clarifies that the article compares consumer devices rather than bare modules.
- The interpretation of reference-test correlations remains contested. dognotdog [[./thread#^c36236274|dognotdog]] argues that R² against an imperfect reference can mislead and does not capture important sensor deficiencies. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36236821|ahaucnx]] replies that AQMD uses BAM and Grimm reference instruments and checks agreement between them first. That reply explains a safeguard, but the supplied discussion does not settle absolute bias, performance outside the tested conditions, or ultrafine-particle coverage.
- Claims about inexpensive PM monitoring should not automatically extend to every gas sensor. aulin [[./thread#^c36237675|aulin]] describes laser-scattering PM measurement as difficult but somewhat manageable, while calling electrochemical measurement of toxic gases at typical ambient concentrations much harder. Conversely, ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36237239|ahaucnx]] praises SenseAir NDIR CO2 modules. These are technology-specific assessments, not evidence that all inexpensive air measurements are equally reliable or unreliable.
- Unconditional window-opening advice overlooks competing exposures and comfort. Avlin67 [[./thread#^c36239143|Avlin67]] recommends opening windows as much as possible, whereas pawelduda [[./thread#^c36240357|pawelduda]] reports difficulty controlling indoor CO2 during winter because outdoor air is cold and polluted. crazygringo [[./thread#^c36243996|crazygringo]] explicitly allows that current outdoor conditions may call for closing windows. The useful action depends on both indoor accumulation and outdoor conditions.
- A VOC response can be relevant to pollution while still being misleading as a CO2 estimate. Responding to michaelcampbell's question about whether VOCs matter regardless of source [[./thread#^c36251395|michaelcampbell]], ImaCake [[./thread#^c36306898|ImaCake]] argues that urban vehicle emissions can overwhelm the signal intended to represent CO2 from occupants. ImaCake's expectation that suburban or rural settings avoid this problem is not backed by measurements here.
- Reliability depends on processing and firmware as well as sensor hardware. synack [[./thread#^c36236033|synack]] notes that inexpensive PM modules internally filter their raw measurements, affecting interpretation. noja [[./thread#^c36239223|noja]] reports being satisfied with a Qing Ping Lite until a firmware update bricked it. Neither component price nor initial responsiveness captures these operational limitations.

## Assumptions

- Outdoor-baseline calibration assumes that the air encountered actually represents the baseline programmed into the monitor. Rebelgecko [[./thread#^c36238259|Rebelgecko]] describes devices that treat their lowest recent reading as ambient outdoor CO2 and warns that a fixed baseline can become outdated. This assumption can fail when the device never encounters suitable reference air.
- A response to breath, an open window, or additional occupants demonstrates responsiveness without establishing absolute accuracy. ioulian [[./thread#^c36238461|ioulian]] explicitly has only one CO2 device and no accuracy comparison; pawelduda [[./thread#^c36240357|pawelduda]] likewise reports plausible responses without a reference instrument. Their observations support practical usefulness more strongly than a numerical accuracy claim.
- Agreement or averaging assumes that errors are sufficiently independent and cancel. plett [[./thread#^c36238782|plett]] treats agreement between two IKEA sensors as reassuring, and nabla9 [[./thread#^c36238375|nabla9]] recommends averaging two or three cheap monitors. Neither supplies a reference comparison showing improved accuracy; AceJohnny2's observation of shared sensor modules [[./thread#^c36237252|AceJohnny2]] also leaves shared errors possible.
- The preferred CO2 action levels in this discussion are personal operating choices. ioulian [[./thread#^c36238461|ioulian]] wants to distinguish approximately 400 ppm from levels above 1000 ppm to decide when to open a window. EVa5I7bHFq9mnYK [[./thread#^c36239147|EVa5I7bHFq9mnYK]] would accept a few coarse concentration categories. Neither comment establishes those categories as clinical safety boundaries or cognition and sleep thresholds.
- Claims based on linked evaluations remain commenters' accounts of those evaluations. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36235991|ahaucnx]] describes ongoing outdoor-monitor co-location testing on four continents and favorable preliminary results, while Johnny555 [[./thread#^c36237094|Johnny555]] quotes published correlation figures. The underlying datasets and complete methods are not supplied, and the manufacturer's ongoing testing does not by itself establish performance in every environment.

## Conditions

- Reported 2023 DIY costs are substantially below many finished monitors: ioulian [[./thread#^c36238461|ioulian]] quotes about US\$26 for a SenseAir CO2 sensor plus US\$5 for an ESP32, and alexk307 [[./thread#^c36242279|alexk307]] reports an SDS011/ESP8266 setup costing under US\$40. Assembly ability matters: ARandomerDude [[./thread#^c36237153|ARandomerDude]] cautions that many buyers lack electronics knowledge, while wintermutestwin [[./thread#^c36243779|wintermutestwin]] found a presoldered AirGradient kit easy to assemble and appreciated its lack of a required cloud connection.
- Higher prices sometimes purchase additional measurements or convenience. athollywood [[./thread#^c36236232|athollywood]] chose a more expensive monitor to obtain CO2 readings; flanbiscuit [[./thread#^c36242081|flanbiscuit]] paid US\$299 for an Airthings View Plus because it included radon sensing. gfd [[./thread#^c36236543|gfd]] notes that an AirGradient kit also includes CO2, while victor106 [[./thread#^c36242981|victor106]] points out that the recommended Aranet4 lacks PM2.5. These are differences in coverage, not demonstrated accuracy rankings.
- Public-network usefulness is geographically uneven. bullen [[./thread#^c36238714|bullen]] recommends Sensor.Community and wind information, but asdff [[./thread#^c36238751|asdff]] considers that sensor map less useful outside central Europe and recommends PurpleAir instead. These are commenters' assessments of coverage, not evidence that a distant network reading represents conditions at a particular home.
- Calibration and durability may depend on the environment. tuatoru [[./thread#^c36236283|tuatoru]] identifies humidity, temperature, aerosol composition, integration time, and possibly pressure as calibration complications. Asked about outdoor pollen exposure by gh02t [[./thread#^c36242129|gh02t]], ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36242198|ahaucnx]] reports variable Plantower lifetimes and insect ingress, but explicitly has no pollen experience.
- Compact packaging can compromise temperature measurements. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36239824|ahaucnx]] says a smaller enclosure made it difficult to separate the temperature sensor from heat-producing components. In response to ilyt's proposed fixed correction [[./thread#^c36240056|ilyt]], ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36240168|ahaucnx]] says compensation also depends on absolute temperature and room airflow. This is a design tradeoff, not proof that temperature errors imply corresponding PM or CO2 errors.
- More specific chemical investigation can cost considerably more than a basic sensor. Drawing on a similar building problem, dementik [[./thread#^c36239655|dementik]] describes local laboratory air-sampling kits costing approximately €200, with detailed VOC analysis and separate mold or material tests. No particular laboratory result or successfully identified source is reported.
- The required confidence depends on the intended use. kingsloi [[./thread#^c36242369|kingsloi]] wants a rough picture of family exposure and accepts some uncertainty. toomuchtodo [[./thread#^c36235912|toomuchtodo]] instead proposes an affordable urban and suburban sensor network to establish a baseline for opposing industrial rezoning, potentially through litigation. The latter is a proposed application; the discussion supplies neither completed findings nor evidence of their legal sufficiency.

## Lived experiences

- sarabveer [[./thread#^c36244005|sarabveer]] reports a substantial disagreement among home monitors during very poor air quality in the US Northeast: a SEN55 read approximately 200 µg/m³ while two Plantower-based AirGradient units read approximately 100 µg/m³. A second SEN55 was planned as a check. No reference instrument or controlled placement comparison is supplied, so the observation establishes disagreement rather than which reading was correct.
- Havoc [[./thread#^c36238557|Havoc]] bought both a BME680-based CO2-equivalent setup and an SCD41 direct CO2 sensor. They describe a sizable difference in absolute values, with the equivalent reading showing only a vaguely useful trend when opening a window. No numerical comparison or independent reference is given.
- ioulian [[./thread#^c36238461|ioulian]] reports that a DIY SenseAir CO2 sensor rises when breathed on and falls to approximately 400 ppm near a window. They use it to distinguish lower from elevated concentrations, but explicitly have not performed the available outdoor calibration and cannot establish accuracy from owning one unit.
- pawelduda [[./thread#^c36240357|pawelduda]] reports that an Airthings CO2 sensor underwent a week of self-calibration and responded plausibly to indoor/outdoor placement, open windows, occupants in an unventilated room, exercise, and cooking. There was no comparison instrument. Their practical finding was that maintaining lower indoor CO2 required more ventilation than expected, especially during cold, polluted winter conditions.
- newman314 [[./thread#^c36236430|newman314]] reports returning several Vitalight monitors because their readings varied widely and keeping an Aranet4. A different Aranet4 owner, electroly [[./thread#^c36258677|electroly]], reports an apparent error of roughly 1000 ppm despite factory-calibration labeling, followed by much more plausible readings after outdoor exposure. Neither account provides an independent concentration reference, and together they qualify simple brand-based assurances.
- plett [[./thread#^c36238782|plett]] reports agreement between two IKEA Vindriktning PM2.5 sensors integrated into Home Assistant. Replying directly, jve [[./thread#^c36240302|jve]] calls their absolute measurements highly inaccurate while accepting their usefulness for trends; jve also reports that a kitchen unit became stuck at maximum PM2.5 and suspects cooking grease contaminated it. The contamination mechanism is an inference, not a demonstrated diagnosis.
- bullen [[./thread#^c36238714|bullen]] observed particle spikes while frying food and associated polluted air with wind coming from the south. Their Pimoroni monitor failed after six months. The account supplies neither particle concentrations nor the cooking fuel, and the proposed future use of ionization is not a tested intervention.
- ruph123 [[./thread#^c36237421|ruph123]] reports frequent incorrect CO2 readings from an AirGradient DIY kit. CommanderData [[./thread#^c36237789|CommanderData]] separately reports a soldered unit sometimes reading zero after extended operation and sometimes exceeding the module's stated range. CommanderData considered replacing the module, but no replacement outcome is reported.
- amatecha [[./thread#^c36237078|amatecha]] initially describes three AirGradient units as apparently accurate because of agreement with nearby weather stations and online services. After 2rsf asks about CO2 reference data [[./thread#^c36238538|2rsf]], amatecha [[./thread#^c36265184|amatecha]] clarifies that the station comparison was mainly for temperature and that they had not found CO2 data. The initial statement therefore cannot serve as independent CO2 validation.
- vbezhenar [[./thread#^c36238250|vbezhenar]] reports an air monitor's temperature reading becoming approximately five degrees too high after several dozen minutes. Leo\_Germond [[./thread#^c36238477|Leo\_Germond]] and Wolfenstein98k [[./thread#^c36238493|Wolfenstein98k]] suggest internal heating as an explanation, but the supplied discussion contains no confirmed diagnosis or successful correction.
- Johnny555 [[./thread#^c36237094|Johnny555]] keeps a PurpleAir PA-II outdoors and consults it frequently during wildfire season. They are reassured by quoted evaluation results of R² greater than 0.96 for PM1.0 and greater than 0.93 for PM2.5. Those figures are reported reference-test correlations, not the commenter's own calibration or a measurement of health benefit.
- kingsloi [[./thread#^c36242369|kingsloi]] has monitored air in Gary, Indiana, using a PurpleAir, then an AQMesh pod, then a Sensit RAMP. Because only one operated at a time, they could not compare the instruments directly. Indoors they use a Canairi CO2 monitor and a Dyson purifier, but report no pollutant reduction or health outcome attributable to purification.
- BestGuess [[./thread#^c36266997|BestGuess]] reports having asthma and repeatedly encountering neighboring cigarette smoke in supposedly smoke-free apartments. They seek evidence identifying both cigarette smoke and its source apartment to avoid another move, and describe lease termination as the available remedy without proof. This is a firsthand exposure and housing account, not measured PM2.5 exposure or a documented change in asthma severity.
- supermatt [[./thread#^c36238582|supermatt]] describes a partner's pottery studio becoming visibly smoggy during kiln firing and taking time to clear afterward; the studio is unoccupied during firing. After receiving monitoring suggestions, supermatt [[./thread#^c36253068|supermatt]] reports ordering a monitor. No subsequent concentrations, ventilation improvement, or exposure outcome are supplied.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The discussion does not establish a universal price-to-accuracy relationship or resolve the SEN55/Plantower discrepancy. reassembled [[./thread#^c36239359|reassembled]] owns both sensor types but has not compared their accuracy; ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36239813|ahaucnx]] favors Plantower partly because its behavior is better researched. sarabveer's divergent readings [[./thread#^c36244005|sarabveer]] remain without a reference-based resolution.
- The AirGradient fault reports have a proposed explanation and claimed fix, but no confirmed recovery from the reporting users. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36237491|ahaucnx]] says hardware and firmware were improved by version 4.2 and later identifies software-serial and command bugs [[./thread#^c36237801|ahaucnx]]. \_zoltan\_ [[./thread#^c36237619|\_zoltan\_]] says the linked issue remains open; rgovostes [[./thread#^c36237752|rgovostes]] disputes that, saying it had been closed for over six months. Issue status and a maintainer's fix claim do not demonstrate that ruph123's reported problem [[./thread#^c36237421|ruph123]] was resolved.
- Affordable ultrafine-particle measurement remains unanswered. thenickdude [[./thread#^c36239538|thenickdude]] asks for accessible PM0.1 sensing or cheaper condensation particle counters. dognotdog [[./thread#^c36240865|dognotdog]] describes optical difficulty below approximately 0.3 microns and the limitations of mass-based measurements. No tested inexpensive alternative or quantified personal ultrafine exposure is supplied.
- Health, cognition, and sleep effects cannot be established from this part. BestGuess [[./thread#^c36266997|BestGuess]] supplies an asthma and smoke-exposure context, while dognotdog [[./thread#^c36240865|dognotdog]] supplies mechanistic particle-health claims. The CO2 accounts from ioulian [[./thread#^c36238461|ioulian]] and pawelduda [[./thread#^c36240357|pawelduda]] describe instrument behavior and ventilation difficulty, without measured cognitive performance, sleep outcomes, or health improvements.
- NO2 exposure is particularly underdocumented. reassembled [[./thread#^c36239359|reassembled]] lists a NOx channel in the SEN55, but provides no NO2-specific readings or validation. bullen's frying observations [[./thread#^c36238714|bullen]] do not identify fuel or gaseous emissions. Related questions about CO sensitivity also remain untested: chaxor [[./thread#^c36242679|chaxor]] asks about low-level detection, and StrictDabbler [[./thread#^c36242833|StrictDabbler]] attributes limited availability to certification and liability without supplying a regulatory analysis.
- The practical comparison between ventilation and filtration is incomplete. kingsloi [[./thread#^c36242369|kingsloi]] reports purifier ownership without before-and-after measurements, and bullen [[./thread#^c36238714|bullen]] mentions ionization only as a possible future action. Together with pawelduda's winter tradeoff [[./thread#^c36240357|pawelduda]], these accounts identify competing concerns but do not establish an optimal window-opening schedule, filtration performance during wildfire smoke, or resulting health benefits.
- Identifying a pollutant source remains distinct from detecting an elevated aggregate reading. BestGuess's request to prove cigarette smoke came from a particular apartment [[./thread#^c36266997|BestGuess]] receives no demonstrated solution. dementik [[./thread#^c36239655|dementik]] describes laboratory and material-testing options for chemical investigation, but supplies no evidence that they solve that specific attribution problem.
- Outdoor CO2 baselines remain uncertain for several commenters. bombcar [[./thread#^c36236398|bombcar]] suspects elevated outdoor CO2 from a high indoor reading but has no outdoor proof. 2rsf [[./thread#^c36238538|2rsf]] cannot find suitable Swedish station data, and amatecha [[./thread#^c36265184|amatecha]] also lacks nearby CO2 references. The supplied accounts cannot distinguish elevated outdoor background from indoor accumulation or instrument error in those cases.
