# [Germany's inefficient love affair with open windows (2021)](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=38819249)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants debate Germany’s practice of opening windows for ventilation, including its effects on heat loss, humidity, mold prevention, and comfort. The discussion contrasts brief shock ventilation with leaving windows open, and questions whether insulation is the problem or whether buildings need better mechanical ventilation and heat recovery. Residents share rental expectations and experiences, while others discuss window design, airtight construction, and retrofit constraints. A practical branch compares HRV and ERV systems, filters, and standalone purifiers for managing fresh air and smoke exposure.
Tags: ventilation, Germany, indoor humidity, mold, energy efficiency, HRV, building design

## Relevance

The discussion compares open-window ventilation with heat-recovery systems, filtration, and airtightness, including firsthand reports of smoke, headaches, and indoor comfort. It also describes tradeoffs around humidity, mold, energy use, and filtering outdoor air. ## Arguments

- PreInternet01 [[./thread#^c38819619|PreInternet01]] argues that airtight construction can coexist with continuous fresh-air ventilation: balanced ventilation manages CO2 and moisture while recovering heat, and operable windows provide additional comfort. \_aavaa\_ [[./thread#^c38820576|\_aavaa\_]] and blacksmith\_tb [[./thread#^c38820862|blacksmith\_tb]] likewise propose heat recovery to reduce the energy cost of exchanging air. These are explanations of building systems, not measured results from particular homes.
- RGamma [[./thread#^c38819699|RGamma]] recommends decentralized ventilation with an enthalpy exchanger in frequently occupied rooms, especially bedrooms. The proposed advantages include addressing winter dryness, adding particle filtration, and optionally controlling ventilation through CO2 or humidity sensors. Summer bypass cooling is mentioned as another feature, without measured cooling or air-quality outcomes.
- Responding to RGamma's filter recommendation, amluto [[./thread#^c38819999|amluto]] distinguishes outdoor-air intake from recirculation: a filter removing only half the incoming particles can still admit substantial pollution. The example of 50 μg/m³ outdoors becoming 25 μg/m³ indoors through the intake is illustrative arithmetic, not a reported household measurement. The recommendation is a large, low-velocity filter with at least 95\% efficiency and a prefilter.
- For recirculating purifiers, amluto [[./thread#^c38829420|amluto]] instead emphasizes contaminant-removal rate: concentration multiplied by airflow and filtration efficiency. A somewhat less efficient filter can be attractive if lower resistance permits more airflow per unit of electricity. This explains the preference for IKEA's approach without contradicting the demand for stronger single-pass intake filtration; no comparative product test is supplied.
- rad\_gruchalski [[./thread#^c38819488|rad\_gruchalski]] advocates brief window opening to remove moisture while avoiding excessive cooling of window surrounds. In a separate reply chain, volemo [[./thread#^c38824980|volemo]] describes shock ventilation's intended mechanism as exchanging air without substantially cooling walls. Both support short airing periods, but neither provides humidity, surface-temperature, or mold measurements.

## Counterarguments

- KolmogorovComp [[./thread#^c38819415|KolmogorovComp]] challenges the article's quoted suggestion of reducing insulation to accommodate ventilation preferences. lxgr [[./thread#^c38820446|lxgr]] argues that heat transfer and air exchange can be managed separately through counterflow heat exchangers. Their objection supports retaining insulation while providing deliberate ventilation.
- Within the discussion questioning whether repeated airing is worthwhile, imtringued [[./thread#^c38823665|imtringued]] argues that mold depends on moisture meeting cold walls and that excessive ventilation can worsen wall cooling; insulation and keeping surfaces warm are presented as the reliable solution. volemo [[./thread#^c38824980|volemo]] replies that brief shock ventilation is specifically intended to avoid that cooling. The disagreement concerns ventilation's sufficiency and implementation, not whether moisture matters.
- bradknowles [[./thread#^c38851743|bradknowles]] counters amluto's criticism of HRV/ERV filters by treating them as prefilters upstream of HVAC-mounted HEPA filtration. That response assumes a particular system configuration. RGamma's original recommendation concerns decentralized room ventilation [[./thread#^c38819699|RGamma]], so downstream HVAC filtration cannot be assumed to resolve the concern in that setting.
- Slartie [[./thread#^c38819597|Slartie]] finds prescribed window-opening schedules burdensome and prolonged partial opening expensive in winter. lm28469 [[./thread#^c38820596|lm28469]] argues that briefly operating windows is feasible compared with time spent on entertainment; kaashif [[./thread#^c38820829|kaashif]] replies that available time does not establish the value of a menial task whose effectiveness seems doubtful. This is a disagreement about effort and perceived benefit, without measured adherence or effectiveness.

## Assumptions

- PreInternet01's account [[./thread#^c38819619|PreInternet01]] assumes a modern building with balanced ventilation, substantial temperature-controlled building mass, and well-insulated operable windows. The claims that the ventilation already prevents mold and that opening windows for periods measured in hours has little energy impact are not accompanied by measurements. They should not automatically be applied to older or differently equipped buildings.
- rad\_gruchalski [[./thread#^c38819488|rad\_gruchalski]] presents 12°C at window sills as a mold-relevant threshold and associates warmer interiors with more moisture. No humidity readings, moisture-source information, or surface-temperature observations substantiate those statements in this unit; the discussion does not establish a universal temperature cutoff.
- 6R1M0R4CL3 [[./thread#^c38841308|6R1M0R4CL3]] infers that frequent German home ventilation is beneficial from admiration for German engineering and intelligence. That is an appeal to national reputation, not exposure or health evidence. The contrasting routines reported by hasoleju [[./thread#^c38819559|hasoleju]] and Slartie [[./thread#^c38819597|Slartie]] also caution against treating the discussion as evidence of uniform German behavior.

## Conditions

- Opening windows has competing implications in these accounts: it may dilute indoor CO2 or moisture, as discussed by PreInternet01 [[./thread#^c38819619|PreInternet01]] and rad\_gruchalski [[./thread#^c38819488|rad\_gruchalski]], while admitting outdoor pollution where RGamma reports fireplace smoke [[./thread#^c38820745|RGamma]]. The resulting inference is conditional: outdoor air quality matters alongside indoor ventilation needs. Nobody supplies a measured open-window versus closed-window comparison during smoke exposure.
- Building age and retrofit feasibility constrain the options. PreInternet01 [[./thread#^c38819619|PreInternet01]] and RGamma [[./thread#^c38819699|RGamma]] describe retrofitting comprehensive or central ventilation as difficult. worklaptopacct [[./thread#^c38820729|worklaptopacct]] reports a daily five-minute airing clause in a German apartment building dating from around 1900, but doubts its enforceability. Slartie [[./thread#^c38819597|Slartie]] disputes the contractual enforceability of precise schedules, while taejo [[./thread#^c38823595|taejo]] distinguishes landlord heating obligations from tenants' responsibility to avoid damage.
- Stronger intake filtration has financial and physical requirements. amluto [[./thread#^c38819999|amluto]] proposes a filter costing several hundred dollars, with roughly 150 cfm through a 24-by-24-inch housing to limit resistance. RGamma [[./thread#^c38820745|RGamma]] worries that an added filter could push the small ComfoSpot50 outside its specifications and finds particle counters expensive; after seeing an inline option, RGamma [[./thread#^c38821001|RGamma]] says fitting it would be very difficult.
- RGamma's purchasing constraints [[./thread#^c38827819|RGamma]] include a crowded room of approximately 20 m², the price and size of IQAir and Allermed devices, and local unavailability of the Levoit EverestAir. RGamma later considers IKEA attractive for energy use and filter price, while treating future filter availability as less certain [[./thread#^c38834329|RGamma]]. These are household-specific constraints and expectations, not demonstrated lifetime-cost comparisons.
- Window ventilation also affects practical comfort. hasoleju [[./thread#^c38819559|hasoleju]] describes floor-height windows as easier to access without furniture or plants obstructing them. wrp [[./thread#^c38824167|wrp]] relays a friend's complaint about fruit flies entering an unscreened German apartment; this is secondhand. lxgr [[./thread#^c38820446|lxgr]] discusses handling, noise, and thermal-insulation tradeoffs among window designs, while db48x [[./thread#^c38821610|db48x]] notes that tilt-and-turn windows are available outside Germany.

## Lived experiences

- shankr [[./thread#^c38821317|shankr]] reports terrible winter headaches in Germany but no such problems when staying in France with closed windows, heating, and modern ventilation. The commenter identifies ventilation as the apparent difference. This is a firsthand symptom comparison, but no pollutant measurements or controlled comparison establish the cause or justify a country-wide conclusion.
- amluto [[./thread#^c38819999|amluto]] reports testing European HRV/ERV filters with a handheld particle counter and finding performance as poor as expected from their specifications. This is the clearest firsthand instrumental observation in the unit, but it remains qualitative: no raw counts, measured PM2.5 mass concentrations, instrument model, calibration information, or test protocol are given.
- RGamma [[./thread#^c38820745|RGamma]] reports substantial, foul-smelling exposure attributed to nearby fireplaces and unsuitable wood, despite using the best available filters for the device, allegedly rated ePM1 70\%. The exposure description is sensory rather than quantified. Borrowing a particle counter to gather evidence for the municipality is a proposed next step, not completed monitoring.
- The filtration upgrades have no reported outcome yet. amluto [[./thread#^c38820905|amluto]] bought an inline HEPA housing for a Zehnder installation but had not finished connecting it. RGamma [[./thread#^c38827819|RGamma]] ordered a Levoit Vital 200S Pro and hoped it was suitable. Neither account demonstrates a completed reduction in particles, symptoms, or odor; amluto's separate IKEA recommendation [[./thread#^c38827252|amluto]] is explicitly an opinion.
- Slartie [[./thread#^c38819597|Slartie]] reports usually keeping windows closed, except after bathroom showers, rather than following repeated daily airing instructions. hasoleju [[./thread#^c38819559|hasoleju]] describes airing every room each morning. These contrasting accounts illustrate actual routines, but neither includes exposure or health measurements.
- MarkusWandel [[./thread#^c38820324|MarkusWandel]] recalls sleeping with an open window while growing up in Germany and reports German visitors insisting on the same arrangement. The account conveys a strong sleep-related preference and habit, expressed partly through satire about HVAC and HEPA-filtered interiors. It does not demonstrate improved sleep duration, sleep quality, or overnight CO2 levels.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The appropriate duration and energy cost of airing remain unsettled. Slartie [[./thread#^c38819597|Slartie]] regards prolonged partial opening as costly, rad\_gruchalski [[./thread#^c38819488|rad\_gruchalski]] warns against cooling window surrounds, and PreInternet01 [[./thread#^c38819619|PreInternet01]] claims that even periods measured in hours have little efficiency impact in the described modern building. Different building assumptions may explain part of the disagreement, but there are no energy or temperature measurements to resolve it.
- The practical filtration comparison remains incomplete. amluto [[./thread#^c38819999|amluto]] supplies qualitative particle-counter observations and later a theoretical airflow–efficiency argument [[./thread#^c38829420|amluto]]; RGamma [[./thread#^c38827819|RGamma]] supplies a purchase decision rather than operating results. The unit does not establish sensor accuracy, achieved room concentrations, comparative clean-air delivery, filter longevity, or which retrofit preserves adequate ventilation airflow.
- Health and sleep causality remain uncertain. shankr's headache account [[./thread#^c38821317|shankr]] does not identify CO2, particles, humidity, or another cause, and MarkusWandel's open-window sleep preference [[./thread#^c38820324|MarkusWandel]] contains no objective sleep assessment. No cognition measurements or study findings are supplied, so the discussion cannot establish cognitive or sleep benefits from any particular intervention.
- The pollutant coverage is narrower than the approved question: PreInternet01 [[./thread#^c38819619|PreInternet01]] identifies CO2 management as a ventilation purpose but gives no ppm readings; amluto [[./thread#^c38819999|amluto]] discusses fine-particle filtration without reporting measured PM2.5 concentrations. No supplied account measures NO2 or demonstrates that a particle purifier controls either gas. RGamma's smoke account concerns fireplaces [[./thread#^c38820745|RGamma]]; cooking, traffic, and wildfire-specific exposure experiences are absent from this source unit.
