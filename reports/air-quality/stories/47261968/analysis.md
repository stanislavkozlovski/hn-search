# [Rising carbon dioxide levels now detected in human blood](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47261968)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion ranges from possible health and cognitive effects of rising atmospheric and indoor CO₂ to questions about the study’s methods, confounding factors, and interpretation of blood measurements. Participants debate ventilation, CO₂ monitors, air pollution, and whether plants or indoor scrubbers can meaningfully change exposure. Much of the thread turns to climate policy, fossil-fuel dependence, clean-energy deployment, political responsibility, and the balance between economic growth, consumption, and environmental limits.
Tags: carbon dioxide, health research, indoor air, climate change, clean energy, politics, economic growth

## Relevance

Although much of the discussion is about climate policy, comments address indoor CO2 levels, ventilation, and possible cognitive effects. These include a firsthand school account of sustained elevated readings and a home CO2-monitoring account; other claims about cognition and rising blood CO2 are debated and not established by personal measurements in the thread. ## Arguments

- gcanyon [[./thread#^c47262527|gcanyon]] links research to argue that higher CO2 impairs cognition. kibitzor [[./thread#^c47262713|kibitzor]] reports a claim of more than 20\% worse cognitive performance for a 400 ppm increase, while acknowledging exceptions for focused activity and information search at some concentrations. These are commenters' descriptions of linked research; the supplied text does not establish a corresponding loss of IQ or uniform impairment across tasks.
- pocksuppet [[./thread#^c47262915|pocksuppet]] argues that chronic exposure above normal CO2 levels could reduce cognitive ability and increase irritability even below acute toxicity. The comment supplies neither an exposure threshold nor measurements supporting those outcomes.
- zug\_zug [[./thread#^c47262610|zug\_zug]] cites atmospheric CO2 around 428 ppm and worries about indirect health effects as it rises. hxorr [[./thread#^c47263066|hxorr]] argues that spending more time indoors and closing windows for heating or air-conditioning efficiency compounds exposure. These are background concentration claims and an explanation of potential exposure, rather than paired personal indoor/outdoor measurements.
- chneu [[./thread#^c47263465|chneu]] cites research on wildfire smoke and cognition, describing the effects as substantial. This is a claim about a study, not a firsthand smoke-exposure account, and the assertion that it affects every person and animal exceeds the evidence reproduced here. Replying in that smoke branch, piloto\_ciego [[./thread#^c47285372|piloto\_ciego]] says indoor levels are higher without specifying the pollutant or supplying measurements.
- squeegmeister [[./thread#^c47265205|squeegmeister]] recommends opening windows to address CO2 and presents HRV/ERV ventilation as a more energy-efficient alternative. WorldMaker [[./thread#^c47263943|WorldMaker]] describes window opening, fan circulation and possible automation as common home interventions. Neither supplies measured reductions or separates the results of outdoor air exchange from circulation within a room.
- RobGR [[./thread#^c47263137|RobGR]] proposes an indoor CO2 scrubber using commercial lime and discusses aquarium scrubbers as a possible analogue. This is a contemplated intervention, with consumable replacement and regeneration limitations, rather than a completed device or demonstrated improvement in air quality.

## Counterarguments

- AlexandrB [[./thread#^c47262965|AlexandrB]] challenges the parent thread's suffocation rhetoric by contrasting approximately 450 ppm outdoors with meeting rooms that can reach 1500 ppm or more. Those figures are presented as general examples, not personal measurements. johnboiles [[./thread#^c47262987|johnboiles]] disagrees with the reassurance but provides no supporting mechanism or data; the exchange does not settle subtler effects below acute danger.
- boringg [[./thread#^c47262913|boringg]] questions the claimed greater-than-20\% cognitive effect and asks for a before/after account of fixing the home discussed in the linked article. This identifies missing intervention evidence in the discussion, rather than providing a contradictory experiment.
- eitau\_1 [[./thread#^c47263282|eitau\_1]] questions direct CO2 causation because, in their account, resting lung concentrations already exceed 10000 ppm. They suggest that indoor CO2 might instead correlate with other compounds emitted by occupants that impair performance. The physiological comparison and alternative explanation are hypotheses; neither is tested in the supplied exchange.
- alexk307 [[./thread#^c47263992|alexk307]] challenges the blood-study interpretation, saying NHANES instruments changed at least three times during 1999–2020 and that serum bicarbonate has other determinants, including health conditions associated with an approximately 40\% increase in the number of obese Americans. These are unverified methodological objections within the comment, but they directly contest treating bicarbonate trends as a specific measure of atmospheric CO2 exposure.
- bethekidyouwant [[./thread#^c47264184|bethekidyouwant]] asks how the blood findings distinguish rising outdoor CO2 from spending longer inside better-sealed buildings. This offers an alternative exposure explanation that the supplied discussion does not resolve.
- squeegmeister [[./thread#^c47265205|squeegmeister]] says houseplants make too little difference to be worthwhile. gcanyon [[./thread#^c47271056|gcanyon]] supports that objection with an explicitly remembered, currently unlocated video: ordinary plants proved inadequate, a 55-gallon algae drum failed to offset one person's CO2, and roughly three drums with lighting and mixers still produced unstable control. The approximately 2000 ppm figure belongs to the recalled experiment; the video's danger terminology is not a validated threshold.
- bob1029 [[./thread#^c47263375|bob1029]] invokes physiological regulation and suggests exercise might partially offset rising CO2 effects. thesz [[./thread#^c47265073|thesz]] describes exercise-related changes in blood CO2 and ventilation and recommends more training. Neither reports an experiment showing that exercise mitigates effects from ordinary indoor or outdoor CO2 exposure.

## Assumptions

- Moving from cognitive task scores to broad intellectual or societal decline requires additional assumptions. api [[./thread#^c47263518|api]] expresses concern about losing IQ and focus; piloto\_ciego [[./thread#^c47263389|piloto\_ciego]] hypothesizes widespread deterioration in planning and decisions, speculating about roughly 400 ppm outdoors and indoor concentrations exceeding 1000 ppm. piloto\_ciego [[./thread#^c47285365|piloto\_ciego]] later invokes a reversed Flynn effect, but supplies no analysis establishing CO2 as its cause or showing approximately equal effects across people.
- CO2's role as a ventilation indicator is distinct from its proposed direct toxicity. Karawebnetwork [[./thread#^c47263189|Karawebnetwork]] explicitly says the school installed sensors as an indirect airflow measure. Attributing associated learning or cognitive problems specifically to CO2 would require distinguishing it from the other occupant-related exposures proposed by eitau\_1 [[./thread#^c47263282|eitau\_1]].
- lenerdenator [[./thread#^c47262700|lenerdenator]] compares rising ambient exposure with CO2 poisoning and later cites hypercapnia symptoms including headaches, daytime sluggishness, disorientation, confusion, paranoia, depression and seizures [[./thread#^c47266312|lenerdenator]]. Applying that symptom list to the outdoor or indoor concentrations discussed assumes a dose and physiological connection that the supplied comments do not establish.
- Interpreting NoSalt's approximately 450 ppm reading [[./thread#^c47263261|NoSalt]] as accurate and representative assumes adequate sensor performance and sampling. NoSalt gives no model, sensing method, calibration, reference comparison, placement or measurement duration, leaving reliability and coverage of occupied periods uncertain.
- RobGR [[./thread#^c47263137|RobGR]] explicitly questions the assumption that near-zero indoor CO2 would necessarily be beneficial, citing blood-pH regulation as a reason for caution. The comment establishes neither harm from near-zero exposure nor an optimal indoor concentration.

## Conditions

- Karawebnetwork [[./thread#^c47263189|Karawebnetwork]] places the school account in a small, low-income town, in an old building sealed tightly against winter cold and lacking proper HVAC. Those building and financial circumstances matter to feasible improvements; the account identifies no country, retrofit budget or comparable schools.
- hxorr [[./thread#^c47263066|hxorr]] describes a practical conflict between ventilation and retaining heated or cooled air. squeegmeister [[./thread#^c47265205|squeegmeister]] proposes HRV/ERV as a more efficient option, but provides no installation cost, energy-use comparison or comfort measurements for a particular climate or building.
- WorldMaker [[./thread#^c47263943|WorldMaker]] notes that monitoring and automated interventions require willingness to invest. RobGR [[./thread#^c47263137|RobGR]] identifies replenishment of scrubber material as an ongoing burden and says the aquarium products considered do not appear regenerable. No purchase prices, running costs or maintenance intervals are supplied.
- In a broader energy debate, slibhb [[./thread#^c47263619|slibhb]] makes a directly relevant household-exposure argument: more expensive heating and cooling could encourage wood burning, which they say worsens air quality. This is a conditional affordability warning, without household observations or particulate measurements.

## Lived experiences

- NoSalt [[./thread#^c47263261|NoSalt]] reports recently buying a small Amazon CO2 detector for home and work and seeing readings around 450 ppm. This is a firsthand numerical air measurement, without accompanying symptoms, device validation or a ventilation experiment.
- Karawebnetwork [[./thread#^c47263189|Karawebnetwork]] reports that sensors at a friend's children's high school showed around 1200 ppm throughout winter, sometimes higher. This is secondhand reporting of measurements. Decades of similar exposure and children being mislabeled as underperforming are the commenter's inferences, not measured historical exposure or demonstrated educational effects.
- fullstop [[./thread#^c47263808|fullstop]], replying to the school account, remembers yawning during compulsory Catholic-school services and now suspects elevated CO2 rather than simply boredom. The yawning is firsthand; its attribution to CO2 is retrospective and unsupported by contemporaneous measurements.
- johnboiles [[./thread#^c47263033|johnboiles]] says their office acquired a CO2 canary, but reports no readings or effects. WorldMaker [[./thread#^c47263943|WorldMaker]] describes friends who maintain home monitoring and pager alerts; that is a secondhand account of monitoring practices, without numerical results or demonstrated benefits.
- OutOfHere [[./thread#^c47263760|OutOfHere]], responding to reassurance about bodily regulation, says they witnessed problems they associate with higher bicarbonate, reduced extracellular potassium and heart-rate spikes. The report gives no ambient CO2 measurement or sufficient clinical context to connect that experience to ordinary indoor exposure or evaluate the proposed exercise remedy.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The magnitude, causality and duration of cognitive effects remain unsettled within this source unit. kibitzor [[./thread#^c47262713|kibitzor]] presents a large quantitative effect, boringg [[./thread#^c47262913|boringg]] challenges its evidentiary support, and eitau\_1 [[./thread#^c47263282|eitau\_1]] proposes correlated occupant emissions as an alternative cause. The supplied text cannot resolve effects on particular tasks, chronic exposure, individual susceptibility or everyday functioning.
- The connection between outdoor CO2, indoor exposure and blood biomarkers remains unresolved. throawayonthe [[./thread#^c47262678|throawayonthe]] asks whether blood CO2 reflects the moment of sampling or long-term environment, and whether dense or industrial settings matter. alexk307 [[./thread#^c47263992|alexk307]] raises measurement and medical confounding, while bethekidyouwant [[./thread#^c47264184|bethekidyouwant]] raises changing time spent in sealed buildings. No supplied answer separates these possibilities.
- Houseplant effectiveness remains an explicit disagreement. WorldMaker [[./thread#^c47266290|WorldMaker]] argues that plants could lower average or baseline home CO2 over months or years even if they cannot suppress spikes. squeegmeister [[./thread#^c47265205|squeegmeister]] considers their effect too small, and gcanyon [[./thread#^c47271056|gcanyon]] recounts an unsuccessful plant/algae experiment. There are no comparable long-term measurements resolving those claims.
- The window-opening tradeoff is only partly addressed. squeegmeister [[./thread#^c47265205|squeegmeister]] recommends ventilation for CO2, while chneu [[./thread#^c47263465|chneu]] raises smoke-related cognitive concerns. No account compares simultaneous indoor/outdoor CO2 and particles, or measurements before and after opening windows during smoke. Consequently, this part does not establish when outdoor pollution would outweigh a CO2 benefit.
- Coverage of the approved question is uneven: this part supplies no PM2.5 or NO2 measurements, cooking- or traffic-exposure experiments, filtration performance results, or direct sleep-quality outcomes. chneu [[./thread#^c47263465|chneu]] discusses wildfire research without measured personal exposure; RobGR [[./thread#^c47263137|RobGR]] mentions HEPA filters only as a potential customer-market comparison; fullstop [[./thread#^c47263808|fullstop]] describes yawning rather than measured sleep disruption. Those gaps prevent conclusions about these pollutants, interventions and outcomes from this source unit.
