# [Ask HN: How do I deal with high levels of formaldehyde in my apartment?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=31873745)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

A tenant asks how to respond to an air monitor detecting elevated formaldehyde, alongside a cat’s asthma and weak responses from the landlord and HOA. Replies discuss possible sources such as furnishings, flooring, appliances, insulation, and household products; they differ on how concerning the readings are and suggest professional sampling and ventilation options.
Tags: formaldehyde, indoor air quality, tenant rights, air monitoring, health

## Relevance

This is a firsthand indoor-air account with monitor readings, a reported increase at night and with air conditioning, and a decrease when windows are opened. The tenant links the concern to a cat’s asthma, while replies discuss possible building-material sources, uncertainty in the readings, and professional sampling. It concerns formaldehyde rather than the question’s highlighted pollutants, but directly addresses indoor exposure, measurement, ventilation, comfort, and health concerns. ## Arguments

- Ventilation has some firsthand support for lowering the reported indoor reading: beginnercounter [[./thread#^c31874541|beginnercounter]] says keeping every window wide open holds the monitor below 150 ppb, compared with a reported 300 ppb. This supports a possible practical improvement in this apartment, without establishing measurement accuracy or overall exposure reduction.
- Commenters propose indoor materials as possible sources. goethes\_kind [[./thread#^c31874077|goethes\_kind]] points to glue in particleboard furniture or walls; aurizon [[./thread#^c31874079|aurizon]] discusses urea-formaldehyde foam insulation and certain plastics, including possible renewed emissions during summer heating. These are source hypotheses, not findings from an inspection.
- Responding specifically to the tenant's difficulty ventilating in desert heat, aurizon [[./thread#^c31910492|aurizon]] proposes a counter-current air exchanger. The claimed benefit is fresh-air exchange with only '5-8\% heat import'; the thread contains no installation, performance measurement, or confirmation of that figure.
- SpikeDad [[./thread#^c31887176|SpikeDad]] recommends certified professional air sampling to interpret the readings and support a complaint. This addresses the gap between a consumer monitor alarm and evidence about the apartment, although the assertion that professional sampling is the 'only way' to obtain evidence is not demonstrated.

## Counterarguments

- The seriousness of the reported concentration is disputed. aurizon [[./thread#^c31874079|aurizon]] calls the levels low and suggests something else may be in the air. beginnercounter [[./thread#^c31874541|beginnercounter]] explicitly questions whether 300 ppb is low, while scantis [[./thread#^c31876136|scantis]] offers an approximately 100 ppb German residential benchmark. The thread does not reconcile these assessments.
- Ordinary building-material emissions are not the only explanation raised. aritmo [[./thread#^c31873814|aritmo]] recalls someone else's account involving another substance and apartments built on inadequately cleaned industrial or military land, then supplies a discussion link [[./thread#^c31874003|aritmo]]. This is a secondhand analogy, with no evidence that the Las Vegas apartment has that history.
- scantis [[./thread#^c31876136|scantis]] combines concern about thresholds with reassurance that monitoring the situation will hopefully be sufficient. Neither that reassurance nor aurizon's low-level characterization [[./thread#^c31874079|aurizon]] is supported here by a verified exposure assessment or a reported health outcome.

## Assumptions

- The proposed material sources depend on unverified building details. beginnercounter [[./thread#^c31874541|beginnercounter]] reports recent vinyl flooring and new appliances, tentatively dates the building to the 1990s, and does not know whether foam insulation was used. Those facts do not establish that the insulation or particleboard discussed by aurizon [[./thread#^c31874079|aurizon]] and goethes\_kind [[./thread#^c31874077|goethes\_kind]] caused the readings.
- scantis [[./thread#^c31876136|scantis]] tentatively gives German workplace and residential values of approximately 370 and 100 ppb, explicitly qualifying the recollection. Their legal status, averaging periods, and applicability to this Nevada rental are not supplied. Trouble\_007 [[./thread#^c31873997|Trouble\_007]] provides an OSHA search link rather than an actual evaluated exposure limit.
- scantis [[./thread#^c31876136|scantis]] also suggests candles and cleaning agents as possible sources, mentions endogenous formaldehyde production, and speculates about urine testing. The tenant does not report those activities or a test result; these suggestions do not establish either the source or the safety of the apartment's air.

## Conditions

- Las Vegas heat limits sustained window ventilation. beginnercounter [[./thread#^c31874541|beginnercounter]] says maintaining lower readings requires all windows wide open and is difficult for long in the desert heat. The practical tradeoff is between the reported reduction and thermal comfort; no cooling-cost measurements are provided.
- Professional assessment introduces a financial and access constraint: SpikeDad [[./thread#^c31887176|SpikeDad]] says certified sampling costs money, while beginnercounter [[./thread#^c31892682|beginnercounter]] explicitly says they are willing to pay but asks which kind of professional to hire. The supplied discussion contains no answer, price, or completed assessment.
- The opening post describes an unresponsive landlord and HOA, making this a tenant problem as well as an exposure concern. beginnercounter's uncertainty about insulation [[./thread#^c31874541|beginnercounter]] and request for help finding a professional [[./thread#^c31892682|beginnercounter]] show that access to building information and actionable expertise remains limited.

## Lived experiences

- beginnercounter [[./thread#^c31874541|beginnercounter]] reiterates the 300 ppb concern and reports keeping readings below 150 ppb with all windows open. The opening post additionally reports roughly 300 ppb at night, 70 ppb with all windows open, and an increase to around 100 ppb when using air conditioning. These are self-reported monitor outputs under incompletely described conditions, not a controlled comparison or professionally confirmed concentrations.
- beginnercounter [[./thread#^c31874541|beginnercounter]] reports that vinyl flooring and appliances were added immediately before moving in. This supplies a concrete renovation history, but there are no before-and-after readings, identified emitting products, or results from removing a suspected source.
- The opening post says the tenant bought the monitor after their cat developed asthma with daily attacks. beginnercounter's follow-up [[./thread#^c31874541|beginnercounter]] reports ventilation and readings, but no change in the cat's symptoms. The account establishes the motivation for investigating air quality, not that formaldehyde caused the asthma.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Whether the readings accurately represent formaldehyde remains unresolved. No calibration, independent comparison, sensor-specific reliability assessment, or professional sampling result is supplied. SpikeDad [[./thread#^c31887176|SpikeDad]] recommends verification, and beginnercounter [[./thread#^c31892682|beginnercounter]] is still asking how to obtain it.
- The source and appropriate response remain unsettled: aurizon [[./thread#^c31874079|aurizon]] proposes insulation or plastics while calling the levels low; goethes\_kind [[./thread#^c31874077|goethes\_kind]] suggests particleboard glue; scantis [[./thread#^c31876136|scantis]] raises other sources and a lower residential benchmark. No inspection or intervention outcome adjudicates these explanations or establishes an applicable safety threshold.
- Opening windows reportedly lowers this monitor's reading, but there are no outdoor measurements establishing whether it improves total personal exposure. beginnercounter [[./thread#^c31874541|beginnercounter]] documents the heat penalty; aurizon's exchanger suggestion [[./thread#^c31910492|aurizon]] remains untested. The account cannot establish whether outdoor pollutants would make window opening harmful, or whether air conditioning itself is an emission source.
- This source unit does not supply PM2.5, NO2, or CO2 measurements, filtration results, or experiences with cooking, traffic, or wildfire smoke. Its firsthand measurement discussion concerns formaldehyde [[./thread#^c31874541|beginnercounter]]. It also provides no human health, cognition, or sleep outcomes; higher nighttime readings should not be treated as evidence of impaired sleep.
