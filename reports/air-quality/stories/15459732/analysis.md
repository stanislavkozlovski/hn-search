# [Wildfire smoke spreads across the bay](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=15459732)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Commenters share observations of wildfire smoke spreading across the Bay Area and point to satellite imagery, forecast tools, air-quality resources, and evacuation information. They discuss smoke exposure and practical precautions such as staying indoors, using filters or respirators, and finding public buildings with filtration, while weighing short-term risks for healthy people. The thread also touches on housing preparedness, controlled burns, fire ecology, regional wildfire impacts, and pollution in India and the Pacific Northwest.
Tags: wildfire-smoke, Bay-Area, air-quality, health-protection, fire-management, smoke-forecasting

## Relevance

The discussion reports firsthand wildfire-smoke exposure, including visible ash, smoke odor, and poor visibility, and compares staying indoors, filtration, respirators, and going to filtered public buildings. Participants differ on the long-term significance of short-term exposure, and note the tradeoff between keeping windows shut and overheating. ## Arguments

- pmoriarty [[./thread#^c15460109|pmoriarty]] argues that wildfire smoke warrants reducing outdoor exposure through working from home, staying indoors, deliveries, and portable air purifiers. These are protective recommendations; the comment supplies no measurements demonstrating their effectiveness in this episode.
- gopalv [[./thread#^c15459980|gopalv]] reports that some San Francisco Public Library locations have air filters and that the mayor encouraged people experiencing breathing trouble to shelter there. This identifies a shared indoor refuge, but provides no filtration specifications or indoor air measurements.
- Responding to a request for respirator recommendations, 24gttghh [[./thread#^c15461067|24gttghh]] recommends P100 filters, quotes 99.97\% particulate reduction, and says N95 filters also work for smoke but clog sooner in heavy smoke. The linked guidance and product specifications are claims about protection, not firsthand measurements of exposure reduction.
- Several commenters recommend tracking smoke geographically: Oompa [[./thread#^c15460571|Oompa]] points to a southward-moving plume, Scaevolus [[./thread#^c15460089|Scaevolus]] links satellite imagery and daily satellite layers, jlev [[./thread#^c15460205|jlev]] recommends an AirFire visualization, and MiguelVieira [[./thread#^c15460902|MiguelVieira]] recommends NOAA's near-surface smoke model. These resources concern smoke distribution; the supplied comments contain no numerical readings from them.
- kumarski [[./thread#^c15460323|kumarski]] connects the smoke with coal pollution remembered from India and cites a news article for an estimated 0.5 million annual deaths from particulates. justboxing [[./thread#^c15460390|justboxing]] adds trash burning in Delhi despite fines. These comments broaden the discussion to persistent combustion pollution, without establishing the health consequences of a short Bay Area wildfire exposure.
- jimmywanger [[./thread#^c15463220|jimmywanger]] argues that fire suppression, accumulated underbrush, and development in fire-prone areas contribute to severe fires, advocating controlled burns and vegetation clearance. This is a proposed approach to fire prevention; the comment does not compare personal smoke exposure from controlled burns versus uncontrolled wildfires.

## Counterarguments

- in\_cahoots [[./thread#^c15460505|in\_cahoots]] directly challenges the recommendation to work from home: offices may have better filtration and pressure control than typical homes. This makes the preferred shelter building-dependent, although no particular office or home is measured.
- drdrey [[./thread#^c15460169|drdrey]] and timcederman [[./thread#^c15460145|timcederman]] challenge delivery-based avoidance because it transfers outdoor exposure to delivery workers. Their objection concerns who bears the risk, rather than whether deliveries reduce the customer's exposure.
- oh\_sigh [[./thread#^c15460290|oh\_sigh]] argues that otherwise healthy people who are neither very young, elderly, nor pregnant should expect annoyance and acute effects rather than long-term damage. l4yao [[./thread#^c15460688|l4yao]] supports a narrower, approximately one-week version by recalling a guest on KQED Forum. These are an assertion and a secondhand expert account, not documented health outcomes or reproduced study results.
- ProfessorLayton [[./thread#^c15460376|ProfessorLayton]] reports smoke-like odor inside an East Bay home, while kingnothing [[./thread#^c15460464|kingnothing]] claims similar conditions across San Francisco apartments. These accounts complicate assurances about simply staying indoors, but do not establish whether indoor concentrations were lower than outdoor concentrations.
- In reply to smoke-avoidance advice, pvaldes [[./thread#^c15464771|pvaldes]] asserts that white forest-fire smoke is mostly water. The comment supplies no composition measurements or reasoning that establishes the remaining exposure as harmless.

## Assumptions

- The shelter-at-home recommendation from pmoriarty [[./thread#^c15460109|pmoriarty]] assumes that the available home meaningfully reduces exposure. gdubs [[./thread#^c15462596|gdubs]] describes local housing as poorly insulated and designed for mild weather, but neither comment measures smoke infiltration or indoor filtration performance; insulation quality alone is not an infiltration measurement.
- The reassuring health claims depend on population and duration limits: oh\_sigh [[./thread#^c15460290|oh\_sigh]] excludes several potentially vulnerable groups, and l4yao [[./thread#^c15460688|l4yao]] discusses about one week. Extending these claims to children, medically vulnerable people, or the summer-long conditions described by craftyguy [[./thread#^c15460408|craftyguy]] would exceed their stated scope.
- Odor, ash, sky color, and visibility are observations of smoke conditions, not measurements of PM2.5 concentration. nicolashahn [[./thread#^c15460676|nicolashahn]] reports deposited ash and campfire odor; sizzzzlerz [[./thread#^c15461054|sizzzzlerz]] reports obscured mountains. The latter's comparison with photographs of roughly ten-meter visibility in China is not a measured Bay Area visibility result.
- Broad geographic statements remain individual assertions: kingnothing [[./thread#^c15460464|kingnothing]] says essentially every San Francisco apartment is affected, while terrabytes [[./thread#^c15462154|terrabytes]] describes the city as exceptionally smoky. These comments do not establish apartment-wide prevalence or population-wide agreement.
- Source attribution needs care: ProfessorLayton's barbecue comparison [[./thread#^c15460376|ProfessorLayton]] describes wildfire-like odor entering a home, not a cooking experiment. bobsil1's report of smoke on 280 [[./thread#^c15461023|bobsil1]] describes highway conditions during the wildfire episode, without identifying or measuring traffic-generated pollution.

## Conditions

- The window tradeoff varies with weather and neighborhood. In reply to a Southern California overheating concern, gnicholas [[./thread#^c15460904|gnicholas]] says closing windows with AC off had remained manageable locally, while forecasting temperatures in the mid-80s near Palo Alto and possibly the 90s in the East Bay. gnopgnip [[./thread#^c15461035|gnopgnip]] similarly emphasizes neighborhood differences and approximately 70-degree weather that day versus 80 on Sunday. These are conditions reported during the October 2017 episode, not a universal window strategy.
- Housing affects practical options: gdubs [[./thread#^c15462596|gdubs]] describes Los Altos homes as poorly suited to both smoke events and unusual summer heat, while expressing concern for young children. gopalv's library refuge suggestion [[./thread#^c15459980|gopalv]] applies specifically to locations reported to have filters; the thread does not establish equivalent refuge availability elsewhere.
- Avoidance depends on resources and work arrangements. pmoriarty's recommendations [[./thread#^c15460109|pmoriarty]] require access to remote work, deliveries, or a purifier. pmorici [[./thread#^c15460384|pmorici]] quotes about \$30 for a respirator in 2017, while 24gttghh [[./thread#^c15461067|24gttghh]] notes that filters become expensive. No household cost comparison or evidence of affordability is supplied.
- Smoke information is time-sensitive and can be difficult to navigate. greglindahl [[./thread#^c15460433|greglindahl]] corrects jlev's AirFire link [[./thread#^c15460205|jlev]] because it points to a run from two days earlier. MiguelVieira [[./thread#^c15460902|MiguelVieira]] specifies the near-surface layer and the seven-hour difference between displayed Z-time and Pacific Daylight Time; 24gttghh [[./thread#^c15460962|24gttghh]] describes the tools as useful but overwhelming.
- The India reply chain distinguishes electricity generation from heating: usefulcat [[./thread#^c15460629|usefulcat]] suggests coal-fired power plants, and kumarski [[./thread#^c15483801|kumarski]] confirms that interpretation. Separately, bdamm [[./thread#^c15461407|bdamm]] emphasizes India's climatic diversity and explicitly lacks Delhi-specific heating knowledge. The exchange does not support treating coal pollution as solely a cold-weather household-heating issue.

## Lived experiences

- nicolashahn [[./thread#^c15460676|nicolashahn]], in San Francisco's Bernal Heights, reports brushing ash off a motorcycle before work and smelling a campfire whenever going outside over several days. terrabytes [[./thread#^c15462154|terrabytes]] also reports unusually smoky conditions in San Francisco. Neither supplies pollutant readings or a personal health outcome.
- gdubs [[./thread#^c15462596|gdubs]], in Los Altos, reports campfire odor and a thick orange sky the previous night, with some improvement the following evening. The comment expresses concern for young children and recalls difficult summer heat, rather than reporting a diagnosed smoke-related illness.
- sizzzzlerz [[./thread#^c15461054|sizzzzlerz]], living and working in the South Bay, reports barely seeing nearby mountains during the worse day; the next morning looked clearer but still smelled smoky. bobsil1 [[./thread#^c15461023|bobsil1]] likewise reports hills beside 280 becoming difficult to see. These accounts distinguish partial visual improvement from disappearance of smoke odor.
- ProfessorLayton [[./thread#^c15460376|ProfessorLayton]] reports an East Bay home smelling as though windows had been left open during a barbecue; the analogy does not establish whether the windows actually were open. Replying to that indoor-smoke account, craftyguy [[./thread#^c15460408|craftyguy]] describes similar conditions in the Pacific Northwest for much of the summer.
- aidenn0 [[./thread#^c15460649|aidenn0]] describes a recurring Southern California dilemma during wildfires accompanying Santa Ana conditions: closing windows can cause overheating, while opening them admits smoky outdoor air. This is an account of competing comfort and exposure concerns, without temperature or particle measurements from the home.
- Animats [[./thread#^c15460588|Animats]] relays a friend's photograph from a downtown San Jose bank. After nostrademons [[./thread#^c15461138|nostrademons]] reports that the image is inaccessible, Animats [[./thread#^c15461453|Animats]] describes bank buildings beneath a reddish-brown sky at noon. This is a secondhand visual account; the photograph itself is not available in the supplied evidence.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The health-risk disagreement remains unresolved: pmoriarty [[./thread#^c15460109|pmoriarty]] urges substantial avoidance, whereas oh\_sigh [[./thread#^c15460290|oh\_sigh]] and l4yao [[./thread#^c15460688|l4yao]] minimize lasting harm for healthy people after brief exposure. No exposure concentrations, individual follow-up, or study results are supplied to adjudicate that disagreement. kumarski's annual particulate-mortality claim for India [[./thread#^c15460323|kumarski]] concerns a different exposure context.
- The thread cannot quantify PM2.5 exposure or establish sensor reliability. Scaevolus [[./thread#^c15460089|Scaevolus]] and MiguelVieira [[./thread#^c15460902|MiguelVieira]] offer imagery and model resources, while gdubs [[./thread#^c15462596|gdubs]] recommends following air-quality reports, but the supplied text contains no PM2.5 concentrations, AQI readings, instrument descriptions, calibration checks, or paired indoor/outdoor measurements.
- The most effective protection remains untested here. in\_cahoots [[./thread#^c15460505|in\_cahoots]] favors potentially better-filtered offices, pmoriarty [[./thread#^c15460109|pmoriarty]] favors staying home and obtaining a purifier, and gopalv [[./thread#^c15459980|gopalv]] identifies filtered libraries. There are no comparative exposure measurements, including commuting exposure. Likewise, 24gttghh's P100/N95 performance and clogging claims [[./thread#^c15461067|24gttghh]] are not tested by a reported user experience.
- Opening windows is discussed as a way to relieve heat that can also admit wildfire smoke, principally by aidenn0 [[./thread#^c15460649|aidenn0]]. gnicholas [[./thread#^c15460904|gnicholas]] reports conditions where keeping them closed remained tolerable. There is no measured example establishing when opening windows improves overall indoor air quality, and no CO2 or NO2 evidence to evaluate additional ventilation tradeoffs.
- Effects on cognition and sleep remain unaddressed, and no commenter supplies a personal clinical outcome attributable to this smoke episode. The concrete observations concern ash and odor from nicolashahn [[./thread#^c15460676|nicolashahn]], visibility from sizzzzlerz [[./thread#^c15461054|sizzzzlerz]], and thermal discomfort from aidenn0 [[./thread#^c15460649|aidenn0]]. The record also contains no quantified cooking-emission or traffic-emission exposure; ProfessorLayton's barbecue analogy [[./thread#^c15460376|ProfessorLayton]] and bobsil1's highway observation [[./thread#^c15461023|bobsil1]] cannot fill those gaps.
