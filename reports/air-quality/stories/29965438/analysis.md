# [UK schools receive 300k CO2 monitors in gov initiative to reduce COVID19 spread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=29965438)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants discuss a government rollout of portable CO₂ monitors in UK schools as a way to identify poor ventilation and reduce respiratory-virus transmission. They examine sensor calibration, whether readings can lead to practical action in older classrooms, the limited number of air-cleaning units, and whether monitors are being ignored. A related debate weighs CO₂ as a ventilation proxy against particulate monitoring and the tradeoffs of opening windows where outdoor air is polluted or cold. Comments also clarify the rollout’s geographic scope and consider fixed sensors and demand-controlled ventilation.
Tags: schools, CO₂ monitors, ventilation, COVID-19, airborne transmission, sensor calibration, air filtration, public policy

## Relevance

The discussion covers CO2 monitoring and calibration in schools, using readings as a ventilation indicator, and reports of high classroom readings. It weighs opening windows against outdoor PM2.5, cold, and inadequate ventilation, and discusses filtration and sensor reliability. ## Arguments

- Daishiman [[./thread#^c29969625|Daishiman]] supports CO2 monitoring as a proxy for air circulation and cites longstanding scientific support for ventilation reducing respiratory transmission. AlbertoGP [[./thread#^c29965572|AlbertoGP]] quotes the article linking lower CO2 with learning and cognitive performance. These are claims about scientific evidence, not measured health or learning improvements reported in this discussion.
- Besticle [[./thread#^c29966000|Besticle]] argues that CO2 alone gives an incomplete picture of indoor air quality and proposes adding PM2.5 monitoring; Besticle [[./thread#^c29966612|Besticle]] subsequently recommends measuring both to balance ventilation against particle exposure. This treats the two measurements as complementary indicators.
- yourusername [[./thread#^c29966040|yourusername]] identifies filtering incoming air as a way to avoid importing urban PM2.5 when increasing ventilation. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c29966077|ahaucnx]] reports growing use of positive-pressure systems in polluted parts of Asia. Neither supplies a measured comparison of these systems with window ventilation or classroom purifiers.
- janekm [[./thread#^c29966541|janekm]] considers getting windows opened a worthwhile initial improvement and suggests fans could increase air exchange. giantg2 [[./thread#^c29966016|giantg2]] proposes increasing outdoor-air exchange and system capacity through changes to airflow and ducts. These are proposed interventions, with no before-and-after results supplied.
- ahaucnx [[./thread#^c29966029|ahaucnx]] advocates permanent CO2 sensors connected to demand-controlled ventilation, claiming potential energy savings while accommodating differences in classroom size, pupil age and activity. AlbertoGP [[./thread#^c29965572|AlbertoGP]] separately favors fixed monitors because portable devices might be put away after the pandemic.
- Youden [[./thread#^c29966091|Youden]] favors equipment supporting manual fresh-air calibration because automatic baseline assumptions can fail in continuously occupied or poorly ventilated buildings. ysleepy [[./thread#^c29966806|ysleepy]] notes that the MH-Z19 can be switched to manual calibration, so its default behavior need not determine its usefulness.
- ahaucnx [[./thread#^c29965988|ahaucnx]] presents an open-source WiFi monitor using a Senseair S8 NDIR sensor and a large colored display as a practical school project. bloopernova [[./thread#^c29965927|bloopernova]] similarly plans window-opening alerts from home measurements. These illustrate ways to make readings actionable, without demonstrating resulting exposure or health improvements.

## Counterarguments

- ahaucnx [[./thread#^c29966077|ahaucnx]] describes a direct tradeoff in polluted locations: opening windows lowers CO2 but raises PM2.5, while keeping windows closed with purifiers can produce the reverse. toyg [[./thread#^c29966246|toyg]] therefore questions whether monitoring that prompts window opening could worsen exposure in English cities; that broad geographic concern is not supported by local measurements in the comment.
- giantg2 [[./thread#^c29966269|giantg2]] argues that monitors have little practical value where schools cannot change ventilation, and speculates that knowing about poor conditions could add stress. This responds to reports of persistently high readings despite open doors and windows, rather than establishing that monitoring is generally ineffective.
- yourusername [[./thread#^c29966040|yourusername]] illustrates the ambiguity of a PM2.5 reading with a hypothetical 30 µg/m³ concentration that could have different sources, including a neighboring wood heater. In reply to the proposal for PM monitoring, parkingrift [[./thread#^c29966226|parkingrift]] cites a study reportedly finding 90\% loss of viral infectivity within as little as five minutes and doubts its benefit. Besticle [[./thread#^c29966604|Besticle]] answers that benefits could extend to seasonal illness and absenteeism; neither side supplies classroom outcome data.
- giantg2 [[./thread#^c29965985|giantg2]] favors monitoring during ventilation design and testing rather than permanent allocation. Replying to demand-control proposals, giantg2 [[./thread#^c29966214|giantg2]] distinguishes a new installation from the actual portable rollout, whose monitors cannot control existing systems. Rickthedick [[./thread#^c29972427|Rickthedick]] asks whether portable equipment and frequent inspections could be more economical, but offers no comparison.
- ysleepy [[./thread#^c29966806|ysleepy]] disputes the practical severity of automatic-calibration errors, guessing an offset around 100 ppm might matter little when measuring around 1,500 ppm. This qualifies Youden's [[./thread#^c29966091|Youden]] stronger warning about unreliable absolute measurements, but does not establish that errors are always small.
- giantg2 [[./thread#^c29965985|giantg2]] claims mask CO2 above 20,000 ppm would negate classroom cognitive benefits, citing a linked study. netizen-936824 [[./thread#^c29966012|netizen-936824]] adds concern about all-day use. vertnerd [[./thread#^c29966785|vertnerd]] challenges whether the study measured trapped exhaled gas rather than the average concentration actually inhaled. The dispute concerns measurement interpretation, not supplied measurements of pupils' inhaled doses.
- chrisco255 [[./thread#^c29968612|chrisco255]] [[./thread#^c29968837|chrisco255]] dismisses monitoring as ineffective because infection is supposedly unavoidable and argues that precautions create fear. Daishiman [[./thread#^c29969625|Daishiman]] directly rejects this by invoking established ventilation benefits. The dismissal and claimed psychological harms are opinions here, without supporting exposure or outcome measurements.
- pasabagi [[./thread#^c29965920|pasabagi]] [[./thread#^c29968737|pasabagi]] considers 7,000–8,000 air cleaners inadequate relative to the number of schools and presumed poorly ventilated classrooms. OJFord [[./thread#^c29968183|OJFord]] counters that targeting rooms where simple ventilation fails could make a limited allocation useful. Neither provides a needs assessment establishing the appropriate quantity.

## Assumptions

- Automatic baseline calibration assumes periodic exposure to approximately outdoor CO2. Youden [[./thread#^c29966091|Youden]] describes a common daily fresh-air assumption; ahaucnx [[./thread#^c29966299|ahaucnx]] contrasts the MH-Z19's reported 24-hour schedule with the S8's eight-day schedule. The suggested advantage of eight days depends on weekends actually reducing occupancy and allowing indoor concentrations to approach ambient levels.
- Manual calibration also requires a suitable reference environment and correct operation. janekm [[./thread#^c29966512|janekm]] makes the S8 calibration procedure conditional on reaching approximately 400 ppm, while noneeeed [[./thread#^c29966352|noneeeed]] explicitly assumes school staff will calibrate devices properly. Neither establishes that outdoor conditions at every installation provide the intended reference.
- The proposed benefits of monitoring assume that an alert leads to an effective intervention. janekm [[./thread#^c29966541|janekm]] expects value from prompting window opening, whereas Resinderate [[./thread#^c29965895|Resinderate]] describes staff exhausting that option while readings remain high. Detecting a problem and having the means to remedy it are separate requirements.
- giantg2's [[./thread#^c29966985|giantg2]] revised mask argument assumes adult tidal volumes of 400–500 ml, mask space of roughly 20 ml or more, room CO2 of at least about 600 ppm, and different proportions for children. The resulting estimate above 1,000 ppm is a calculation based on these assumptions. The cited target below 800 ppm is the commenter's account of the article, not a threshold established by measurements in this thread.
- Interpreting a persistent red display assumes known alarm thresholds, valid calibration and sufficient time for readings to respond. The accounts from 656565656565 [[./thread#^c29965914|656565656565]] do not specify those details; raverbashing [[./thread#^c29965994|raverbashing]] raises response delay as a possible explanation. A color alone cannot quantify the reported exposure.

## Conditions

- Outdoor pollution changes the practical choice. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c29966077|ahaucnx]] discusses purifier-equipped classrooms and positive-pressure systems in polluted Asian locations; OJFord [[./thread#^c29968183|OJFord]] suggests prioritizing London over rural schools. These are contextual arguments, not measurements establishing that every urban school has worse outdoor air or that rural air is consistently clean.
- UK winter conditions constrain window ventilation. JamesMcMinn [[./thread#^c29966028|JamesMcMinn]], relaying conversations with teachers, describes freezing, windy conditions as uncomfortable and often insufficiently effective. giantg2 [[./thread#^c29966553|giantg2]] questions whether heating could keep up with a window fan. Youden [[./thread#^c29966091|Youden]] also identifies winter window closure as a threat to automatic-calibration assumptions.
- Building infrastructure limits feasible improvements. JamesMcMinn [[./thread#^c29966061|JamesMcMinn]] says few UK schools have HVAC; jordanrobinson [[./thread#^c29967269|jordanrobinson]] gives an explicitly tentative estimate below 1\%. r\_hoods\_ghost [[./thread#^c29967324|r\_hoods\_ghost]] separately discusses the temperate climate, limited air-conditioning provision and difficulty retrofitting older schools. These comments provide no building survey or installation-cost evidence.
- The rollout's geography is contested. nmeofthestate [[./thread#^c29966486|nmeofthestate]] points out that the Department for Education does not cover the entire UK. egeek [[./thread#^c29966805|egeek]] interprets the story as concerning England while describing separate initiatives in Northern Ireland and Scotland. Consequently, a UK-wide denominator may not match the reported allocation.
- Coverage estimates depend on school types and counting assumptions. andylynch [[./thread#^c29966268|andylynch]] cites roughly 302,000 English classes; mavhc [[./thread#^c29967017|mavhc]] calculates about 335,000 for England and extrapolates about 402,000 UK-wide. iso1631 [[./thread#^c29967118|iso1631]] suggests roughly 277,000 after excluding private schools and assuming larger classes, before specialist rooms. jamessb [[./thread#^c29966047|jamessb]] also challenges ten classrooms per school using secondary-school organization. These estimates do not establish an exact classroom inventory.
- Funding expectations conflict. pjc50 [[./thread#^c29966469|pjc50]] considers HVAC upgrades outside centrally provided budgets. nicoburns [[./thread#^c29966371|nicoburns]] hopes the pandemic's cost could justify investment, while cjrp [[./thread#^c29966418|cjrp]] expects that cost to reduce school funding. No actual project budget, savings calculation or approved retrofit program is supplied.
- The financing debate also uses competing premises: lbriner [[./thread#^c29966820|lbriner]] frames school improvements as requiring taxes or cuts elsewhere, while neilwilson [[./thread#^c29967665|neilwilson]] emphasizes available labor and alternative uses of resources. These are arguments about financing renovations, not evidence of their air-quality performance or cost effectiveness.

## Lived experiences

- bloopernova [[./thread#^c29965927|bloopernova]] reports home-office CO2 around 400 ppm at night and above 900 ppm during the day, using a hobby monitoring setup connected to a Raspberry Pi and InfluxDB. They plan ventilation alerts and note that their back-to-back townhouse has windows on only one side. No calibration details, subsequent ventilation results or symptoms are reported.
- jve [[./thread#^c29967621|jve]] checks a monitor behind their laptop and reports 600 ppm, describing the ventilation as good but humidity as very low. This is a contemporaneous CO2 reading and a reported comfort-related tradeoff; no humidity measurement or causal test is provided.
- vertnerd [[./thread#^c29977021|vertnerd]] reports measuring their teaching classroom at the start of the 2020–21 school year and finding it easily reached 1,000 ppm. vertnerd [[./thread#^c29966785|vertnerd]] also reports wearing a KN95 throughout the day. Their classroom measurement does not measure CO2 inside a mask or establish a cognitive effect.
- ysleepy [[./thread#^c29966806|ysleepy]] reports configuring an MH-Z19 for manual calibration and still obtaining apparently reasonable outdoor readings after several months. This supports the feasibility of changing its calibration mode, but the comment supplies neither numerical outdoor results nor comparison with a reference instrument.
- 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c29966420|5e92cb50239222b]] reports being unable to open a window for about a month because of extreme outdoor pollution. Their S8 displayed 1,500–2,000 ppm, which they believed corresponded to nearer 4,000 ppm, then fell to zero and jumped to approximately 65,000 ppm when exposed to fresh air. It reportedly remained there after two weeks in a room with an open window. The claimed true concentration lacks an explained independent reference.
- ahaucnx [[./thread#^c29965988|ahaucnx]] reports good experience with the Senseair S8. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c29966029|ahaucnx]] also says their classroom data vary substantially with room size, student age and activity. These are experience-based observations, but no dataset, numerical variation or accuracy comparison is included.
- Resinderate [[./thread#^c29965895|Resinderate]] relays their teacher partner's account of high readings continuing after doors and windows were opened. 656565656565 [[./thread#^c29965914|656565656565]] reports several academies with constantly red rooms under those conditions. JamesMcMinn [[./thread#^c29966028|JamesMcMinn]] describes similar concerns from conversations with teachers. These are field reports, including explicitly secondhand accounts, without numerical readings, durations or airflow measurements.
- rand49an [[./thread#^c29966332|rand49an]] reports seeing fixed CO2 monitors in almost every classroom during visits to new UK schools, including before COVID; the suggestion that this is a departmental requirement is their assumption. egeek [[./thread#^c29966805|egeek]] reports monitors arriving at their children's Northern Ireland schools. These observations establish local presence or delivery, not nationwide coverage, sustained use or effectiveness.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The discussion does not establish how much lowering classroom CO2 improves cognition or health, or separate effects of CO2 itself from other changes accompanying ventilation. AlbertoGP [[./thread#^c29965572|AlbertoGP]] reports an article's learning claim, while Besticle [[./thread#^c29966604|Besticle]] predicts reduced illness and absenteeism. Neither supplies measured outcomes, and no firsthand account documents a before-and-after cognitive or health change.
- The connection between readings and infectious exposure remains unquantified. yalogin [[./thread#^c29967190|yalogin]] asks about airborne transmission and viral concentration through HVAC; 70rd [[./thread#^c29967224|70rd]] supplies an external explainer rather than an in-thread dose analysis. parkingrift's [[./thread#^c29966226|parkingrift]] reported infectivity-decay result likewise comes without a classroom exposure assessment or demonstrated effect on transmission.
- No paired indoor/outdoor PM2.5 and CO2 measurements establish when opening windows produces the better overall exposure in these settings. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c29966077|ahaucnx]] supplies the tradeoff concept. In the separate exchange, noneeeed's [[./thread#^c29966352|noneeeed]] calibration response does not resolve toyg's [[./thread#^c29966246|toyg]] concern about polluted outdoor air.
- Persistently red classrooms could reflect inadequate air exchange, delayed readings or calibration problems; the supplied accounts cannot distinguish them. 656565656565 [[./thread#^c29965914|656565656565]], raverbashing [[./thread#^c29965994|raverbashing]] and Youden [[./thread#^c29966091|Youden]] provide the respective observations or possibilities. Suggested fans and HVAC changes have no reported comparative trial here.
- The malfunctioning S8's cause and recovery remain unknown. nemetroid [[./thread#^c29966679|nemetroid]] suggests integer underflow because the output is near the 16-bit limit; ahaucnx [[./thread#^c29966569|ahaucnx]] suggests communication or code trouble and says the value exceeds the measurement range. janekm [[./thread#^c29966512|janekm]] proposes forced manual calibration using the calibration pin in approximately 400 ppm air. No successful fix is reported by 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c29966420|5e92cb50239222b]], and the 65,000 ppm display is not credible evidence of that actual room concentration.
- The mask dispute remains unresolved: vertnerd [[./thread#^c29966785|vertnerd]] questions whether sampled mask-space gas represents inhaled exposure, while giantg2 [[./thread#^c29966985|giantg2]] substitutes a mixing calculation predicting a smaller elevation. vertnerd [[./thread#^c29977021|vertnerd]] allows that masks could make exposure somewhat worse but rejects equating the situation with sustained exposure to the original much higher figure. No supplied breath-resolved measurement settles the magnitude or cognitive consequence.
- The discussion does not determine whether targeted cleaners, frequent portable inspections or permanent monitoring offer the best allocation. pasabagi [[./thread#^c29968737|pasabagi]] disputes the sufficiency of the cleaner rollout; OJFord [[./thread#^c29968183|OJFord]] defends targeting; Rickthedick [[./thread#^c29972427|Rickthedick]] proposes inspections; AlbertoGP [[./thread#^c29965572|AlbertoGP]] worries about devices being abandoned. Comparative costs, room-level needs and follow-up usage are absent.
- This source unit contains no substantive measurements or accounts about NO2, sleep, cooking emissions, traffic-specific exposure or wildfire smoke. Its numerical firsthand observations concern CO2, as in bloopernova [[./thread#^c29965927|bloopernova]], jve [[./thread#^c29967621|jve]] and vertnerd [[./thread#^c29977021|vertnerd]]. The wood-heater PM2.5 example from yourusername [[./thread#^c29966040|yourusername]] is hypothetical, so it should not be presented as a measured smoke event.
