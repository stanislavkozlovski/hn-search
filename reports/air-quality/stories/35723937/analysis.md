# [Ask HN: What is the best air quality monitor purchasable on Amazon (no DIY)?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=35723937)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants share recommendations for consumer air quality monitors, including devices that measure combinations of CO2, particulate matter, and radon. They compare setup, sensor coverage, and smart home integration, while one asks whether the intended use is indoor or outdoor monitoring and what pollutants matter.
Tags: air quality monitors, CO2, radon, particulate matter, consumer devices

## Relevance

Users discuss air monitors that measure PM2.5 and CO2, including a firsthand report that CO2 rises quickly in a closed office and falls when a window is opened, with tiredness attributed to the levels. [[./thread#^c35729970|jtap]] [[./thread#^c35744909|ATMLOTTOBEER]] [[./thread#^c35759148|bradknowles]]

## Arguments

- jtap [[./thread#^c35729970|jtap]] reports that displayed CO2 rises surprisingly quickly with the basement-office door closed and falls quickly after opening a window. This supports ventilation as a practical response to the buildup observed in that particular room.
- bradknowles [[./thread#^c35759148|bradknowles]], replying to jtap, favors the Airthings View Plus because it is among the few devices they have found combining PM2.5, CO2, and radon measurement. They also cite a Home Performance YouTube recommendation and the presenter’s home use; these are purchasing reasons, not firsthand validation of accuracy.
- jononor [[./thread#^c35729794|jononor]] asks whether monitoring is intended for indoors or outdoors and what the buyer wants to measure. Those unanswered questions limit any claim that one monitor is universally best.

## Counterarguments

- Responding to ATMLOTTOBEER’s report of using the IQAir AirVisual Pro [[./thread#^c35744909|ATMLOTTOBEER]], bradknowles [[./thread#^c35759152|bradknowles]] says they also own it and consider it good, but prefer the Airthings View Plus’s broader measurement coverage. Their judgment that IQAir is less good is not supported here by comparative accuracy measurements.
- jtap [[./thread#^c35729970|jtap]] describes practical friction with Airthings: selecting the correct app and completing setup took roughly an hour, and the app wanted about a week of readings before showing some information. However, the device screen displayed current values, so the delay did not prevent all immediate observation.

## Assumptions

- jtap [[./thread#^c35729970|jtap]] believes elevated CO2 explains their tiredness, based on the recorded history and time spent in the office. This is a personal causal interpretation of an observed association; the comment provides no concentrations, controlled comparison, or assessment of alternative explanations.
- ATMLOTTOBEER [[./thread#^c35744909|ATMLOTTOBEER]] links an IQAir laboratory evaluation but supplies none of its findings. The supplied discussion therefore cannot establish the device’s accuracy from that link, just as bradknowles’s preference for broader sensor coverage [[./thread#^c35759148|bradknowles]] does not establish measurement reliability.

## Conditions

- jtap’s experience [[./thread#^c35729970|jtap]] concerns a home office in a walkout basement with accessible windows. Window ventilation was feasible there, but outdoor pollutant levels, weather, and location are unreported. The observed CO2 decrease alone cannot establish whether opening that window improved total exposure, particularly under traffic pollution or wildfire smoke.

## Lived experiences

- jtap [[./thread#^c35729970|jtap]] bought an Airthings 2960 View Plus because of concern about radon and other basement-office exposures. They report that radon and other readings were not concerning to them, while CO2 emerged as the perceived problem. They are satisfied and considering another unit for the ground floor; no numerical readings or basis for judging the other pollutants acceptable are supplied.
- tw8345 [[./thread#^c35743820|tw8345]] reports buying a pre-soldered AirGradient with software already loaded and getting it working by plugging the ICs into place and powering it on. This is firsthand evidence about setup convenience, although it still involved assembly despite the story’s request for a no-DIY monitor; no air-quality results or sensor checks are reported.
- QuinnyPig [[./thread#^c35733585|QuinnyPig]] bought an Awair primarily for Home Assistant integration and likes it. Their account supports integration as a practical purchasing consideration, but provides no exposure measurements, reliability assessment, or health outcome.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The discussion does not establish a comparative winner: ATMLOTTOBEER [[./thread#^c35744909|ATMLOTTOBEER]] reports using IQAir, while bradknowles [[./thread#^c35759152|bradknowles]] offers a qualified preference for Airthings without a documented side-by-side test. jononor’s questions about intended setting and monitoring goals [[./thread#^c35729794|jononor]] remain unanswered.
- The only reported symptom is jtap’s tiredness [[./thread#^c35729970|jtap]]. There are no supplied sleep observations, cognitive tests, clinical outcomes, or systematic comfort assessments, so those parts of the approved question remain unresolved in this source unit.
- PM2.5 appears as a claimed Airthings capability in bradknowles’s comment [[./thread#^c35759148|bradknowles]], rather than as an actual reported concentration. Alongside jtap’s qualitative CO2 observations [[./thread#^c35729970|jtap]], the discussion supplies no NO2 measurements, quantitative exposure comparisons, or firsthand filtration, cooking, traffic, or wildfire-smoke experiments. It therefore cannot resolve ventilation-versus-filtration tradeoffs or when outdoor pollution would outweigh the benefit of lowering indoor CO2.
