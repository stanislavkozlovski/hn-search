# [Potted plants do not improve indoor air quality](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=21466299)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion questions whether potted plants meaningfully improve indoor air quality and whether the headline overstates the study’s findings. Participants distinguish general air cleaning from removing specific VOCs, and point out that detectable effects may require very large numbers of plants. A branch compares ordinary houseplants with a NASA design that moves air through activated carbon and plant roots, explaining why the latter is not equivalent to placing plants around a home. The conversation also touches on plant care, mold, and a humorous misreading of the title.
Tags: indoor air quality, houseplants, VOC removal, air filtration, research interpretation, NASA study

## Relevance

The discussion evaluates whether houseplants meaningfully clean indoor air, distinguishes plant effects from engineered filtration, and questions how large the practical benefit is. Most of the claims concern VOC removal rather than PM2.5, NO2, or CO2. ## Arguments

- Ordinary houseplants appear weak as practical air cleaners in commenters' accounts of the research. mooman219 [[./thread#^c21467067|mooman219]] interprets the abstract of a meta-study as finding extremely small improvements; replying, dwd [[./thread#^c21469102|dwd]] claims meaningful VOC removal requires 10–1000 plants per square metre. Neither supplies firsthand pollutant measurements or enough study detail to verify that range.
- Results from a combined filtration system cannot establish the effectiveness of ordinary potted plants alone. Answering BasicObject's apparent contradiction with NASA research [[./thread#^c21468977|BasicObject]], gabetax [[./thread#^c21469618|gabetax]] quotes a design that moves contaminated air through activated carbon serving as the plants' growing medium. BasicObject [[./thread#^c21479756|BasicObject]] acknowledges previously overlooking the filter. The quoted excerpt does not isolate the plants' contribution from the carbon filter.

## Counterarguments

- The headline may overstate the conclusion. 22c [[./thread#^c21470322|22c]], explicitly lacking full article access, distinguishes CADR from the broader, less clearly defined concept of air quality and proposes 'potted plants do not make good air filters.' This challenges the scope of the headline without demonstrating substantial practical benefits.
- A claimed physiological benefit need not contradict weak pollutant removal. Responding to pkaye's video reference [[./thread#^c21468934|pkaye]], m0llusk [[./thread#^c21471558|m0llusk]] says Meattle's primary result was increased occupants' blood oxygen, requiring many plants and extensive care. This is a commenter-reported research claim, not a firsthand health measurement, and does not establish effects on cognition, sleep, or symptoms.

## Assumptions

- Attributing a benefit specifically to plants requires separating their effects from maintenance and other interventions. m0llusk [[./thread#^c21471558|m0llusk]] notes frequent dust cleaning and suggests it might contribute to the reported results; the comment provides no comparison that resolves this attribution.
- Possible harm from potting soil remains a hypothesis. cellular [[./thread#^c21470841|cellular]] suspects mold and microbes could be detrimental but reports no exposure measurements, symptoms, or comparison with a plant-free room.
- Linked material cannot supply evidence beyond what is reproduced here. smoovb [[./thread#^c21470076|smoovb]] points to additional studies and microscopic images without describing findings; pkaye [[./thread#^c21468934|pkaye]] supplies a video challenge without measurements. These references do not independently substantiate benefits or harms within this source.
