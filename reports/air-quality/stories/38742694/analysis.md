# [DIY ventilation heat exchanger (2010)](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=38742694)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

A technical exchange about DIY heat-recovery ventilation, especially compact and window-mounted designs for improving indoor air while retaining heating or cooling energy. Topics include recuperators versus regenerators, airflow and efficiency, materials and fabrication, humidity recovery, condensation, frost, mold, climate differences, installation constraints, commercial alternatives, and whether DIY systems can be reliable and economical.
Tags: DIY-ventilation, heat-recovery, indoor-air-quality, HVAC, energy-efficiency, humidity, building-science

## Relevance

The thread discusses ventilation and heat-recovery options to control indoor CO2 and stuffiness, alongside practical tradeoffs such as energy use, humidity, condensation, mold, and outdoor conditions. It includes firsthand design and performance reports as well as disagreement about CO2-related cognitive effects. ## Arguments

- walterbell presents heat-recovery ventilation as a way to ventilate a winter home office without losing as much heat, motivated partly by concerns about CO2 and cognition; this is a proposed benefit, not a measured cognitive improvement [[./thread#^c38743181|walterbell]]. ClumsyPilot argues that very airtight construction requires deliberate ventilation to retain both efficiency and adequate air exchange [[./thread#^c38745444|ClumsyPilot]].
- jorvi supports the CO2–cognition concern with personal symptoms, a claimed Dutch classroom target below 950 ppm, and a claim to have found at least five supportive studies, some also concerning PM2.5. Neither those studies nor measurements from the symptomatic rooms are supplied [[./thread#^c38746893|jorvi]] [[./thread#^c38747570|jorvi]] [[./thread#^c38752772|jorvi]].
- schiffern argues for arranging airflow around the house, particularly extracting from bathrooms and kitchens, rather than merely exchanging air near one opening [[./thread#^c38743885|schiffern]]. In the cooking discussion, schiffern interprets mrspuratic's improvement from running intake fans alongside an extracting hood as addressing inadequate replacement air for the hood [[./thread#^c38744469|schiffern]].
- Open\_erv explains that reversing-flow regenerators can recover some moisture when outgoing air deposits condensation that subsequently evaporates into incoming air; adsorbents may extend moisture recovery beyond those condensation conditions [[./thread#^c38745943|Open\_erv]] [[./thread#^c38746150|Open\_erv]]. This addresses the desire for ventilation that does not excessively dry winter interiors expressed by jnsaff2 [[./thread#^c38751026|jnsaff2]].
- walterbell promotes both window ventilation units and quiet MERV13 Corsi–Rosenthal filters, quoting reporting about a five-air-changes-per-hour CDC recommendation and possible benefits concerning infection, allergens, and wildfire smoke. These are attributed guidance and benefit claims, not filtration measurements or health outcomes from this discussion [[./thread#^c38751217|walterbell]].
- geon and briHass argue that correctly positioned and sized vents can distribute air without objectionable drafts or noise, answering notyoutube's concern that combining heating with ventilation could make a house windy [[./thread#^c38744806|geon]] [[./thread#^c38744974|briHass]] [[./thread#^c38742909|notyoutube]].
- schiffern recommends checking CO2 and/or performing a blower-door test before buying an ERV. This provides a proposed diagnostic starting point, although the accompanying claim that most buildings are already sufficiently leaky is not demonstrated with measurements here [[./thread#^c38743885|schiffern]].

## Counterarguments

- sarchertech disputes the large cognitive effects attributed to ordinary indoor CO2 concentrations, contrasting a claimed 50\% reduction at 1,400 ppm with submarine experience and a claimed study involving 40,000 ppm for two weeks without measurable decline [[./thread#^c38746070|sarchertech]]. They also describe a 2020 academic meta-analysis as containing at least one negative result for every positive one. These remain accounts of outside research, not independently assessable findings within the export [[./thread#^c38753239|sarchertech]].
- Replies challenge attributing the benefit of opening a window specifically to CO2: cameronh90 points to humidity and a cold breeze, maxerickson asks about the temperature change, and refurb suggests placebo effects [[./thread#^c38747702|cameronh90]] [[./thread#^c38747609|maxerickson]] [[./thread#^c38750428|refurb]]. These are plausible alternative explanations raised by commenters; none was tested in jorvi's room.
- jnsaff2 and schiffern refer to the same external builder's mold problems, with schiffern identifying a humid climate as relevant; these are not two independent firsthand failures [[./thread#^c38743111|jnsaff2]] [[./thread#^c38743093|schiffern]]. Answering zug\_zug's question about comparison with an open window, aifooh7Keew6xoo proposes that condensation and deposited material on exchanger surfaces provide a concentrated growth site. No microbial measurements accompany that explanation [[./thread#^c38744498|zug\_zug]] [[./thread#^c38747246|aifooh7Keew6xoo]].
- leoedin argues that ventilation can increase indoor humidity when outside air contains more moisture, limiting the usefulness of plain heat recovery in some seasons [[./thread#^c38747378|leoedin]]. Conversely, jnsaff2's winter account shows that additional ventilation can conflict with comfort when indoor air is already too dry [[./thread#^c38751026|jnsaff2]].
- samstave suggests UV treatment against airborne organisms, but schneems questions whether duct exposure lasts long enough and warns that some devices produce irritants such as ozone; schneems explicitly recalls an analysis limited to COVID [[./thread#^c38745477|samstave]] [[./thread#^c38745873|schneems]]. hinkley proposes that suppressing growth on the exchanger itself might still help, without supplying results [[./thread#^c38751166|hinkley]].
- schiffern criticizes inexpensive reversing units for low airflow, inadequate pressure balancing, lack of continuous extraction from wet rooms, and potentially exhausting recently admitted fresh air [[./thread#^c38743885|schiffern]]. Open\_erv separately claims that typical units achieve around 70\% efficiency at medium flow, below their advertised performance, and describes surface-area, airflow-resistance, fan-noise, and ownership-cost tradeoffs [[./thread#^c38753345|Open\_erv]] [[./thread#^c38750170|Open\_erv]].
- 0wis cautions that low DIY cost does not establish reliability or suitability for the available space [[./thread#^c38742874|0wis]]. userbinator counters that competent DIY can outperform poor local professional work, while Open\_erv says both original design work and commercial long-term reliability can be problematic. None supplies a systematic failure-rate comparison [[./thread#^c38743115|userbinator]] [[./thread#^c38746009|Open\_erv]].

## Assumptions

- Interpreting jorvi's clearer thinking after opening a window as a direct CO2 effect assumes that CO2 changed sufficiently and that temperature, humidity, other pollutants, and expectations did not explain the improvement. The account establishes a perceived benefit from ventilation, without isolating its cause [[./thread#^c38746893|jorvi]] [[./thread#^c38747702|cameronh90]] [[./thread#^c38750428|refurb]].
- The cognitive dispute involves uncertain extrapolations: sarchertech moves from reported experimental effect sizes to expected effects on standardized tests and astronaut performance, while jorvi invokes classroom drowsiness and a regulatory target. Neither reasoning establishes a universal impairment threshold or demonstrates that the populations and tasks are comparable [[./thread#^c38747208|sarchertech]] [[./thread#^c38747570|jorvi]].
- Heat-recovery efficiency, airflow, humidity recovery, and pollutant exposure are separate outcomes. Open\_erv's reported sensible efficiency and airflow, and mrspuratic's manufacturer's 'up to 90\%' rating, do not establish corresponding reductions in CO2, PM2.5, NO2, or illness [[./thread#^c38745943|Open\_erv]] [[./thread#^c38744246|mrspuratic]].
- The humidity advice depends on outdoor moisture content, indoor temperature, and whether cooling produces condensation. jnsaff2's contrasting outdoor and indoor relative-humidity figures illustrate why outdoor relative humidity alone is insufficient; mnw21cam's regional dew-point and cooling claims are explanatory estimates, not measurements of every climate or house [[./thread#^c38751026|jnsaff2]] [[./thread#^c38752678|mnw21cam]].
- Extending favorable window-opening experiences to every location assumes that incoming outdoor air is suitable. jorvi provides no simultaneous outdoor pollution measurements, and walterbell's quoted discussion of wildfire-smoke protection does not establish that unfiltered outdoor ventilation would help during a smoke episode [[./thread#^c38746893|jorvi]] [[./thread#^c38751217|walterbell]].
- Air movement within a building should not be treated as demonstrated fresh-air exchange. dclowd9901 proposes circulating air between floors for thermal comfort, but reports neither an installed system nor pollutant removal; schiffern's ventilation argument additionally depends on where replacement air enters and exhaust leaves [[./thread#^c38749407|dclowd9901]] [[./thread#^c38743885|schiffern]].

## Conditions

- Rental restrictions and available space constrain installation. TheWoolRug asks about a window adaptation because wall penetrations may be prohibited, and andbberger proposes connecting a Panasonic unit through an insulated portable-AC window manifold [[./thread#^c38744430|TheWoolRug]] [[./thread#^c38744489|andbberger]]. Open\_erv reports that a friend's landlord rejected a completed window unit, showing that even a window-based design may face permission barriers [[./thread#^c38745943|Open\_erv]].
- Prices are discussion-era examples, not verified current quotations: walterbell mentions commercial units starting around \$1,000, a roughly \$500 Panasonic option, a discontinued \$150 window unit, and alternatives around \$300 or \$700-plus [[./thread#^c38743181|walterbell]] [[./thread#^c38744741|walterbell]]. Open\_erv cites approximately CAD 1,800 for a Lunos unit delivering 25 CFM, linking affordability to achievable ventilation [[./thread#^c38745943|Open\_erv]].
- Climate changes the moisture-control problem. leoedin considers plain HRV suitable when outside air is drier; mnw21cam argues that UK and some high-altitude equatorial conditions may be more accommodating than many inland US or European climates [[./thread#^c38747378|leoedin]] [[./thread#^c38752678|mnw21cam]]. These are regional assessments, with jnsaff2 providing a contrasting account of excessive winter dryness [[./thread#^c38751026|jnsaff2]].
- Local energy prices affect whether heat recovery makes sustained ventilation affordable. kragen poses a hypothetical electricity price of \$0.02/kWh with free daytime power; spockz reports Dutch fixed-contract prices of €0.30–0.40/kWh after tax. nine\_k adds that limited winter sunlight weakens assumptions of nearly free solar-powered heating [[./thread#^c38742992|kragen]] [[./thread#^c38743914|spockz]] [[./thread#^c38748811|nine\_k]].
- Open\_erv argues that heating and cooling degree days, including latent heat, are relevant to ventilation savings, estimating cooling-dominated savings in Austin and roughly 100 times more winter than summer savings in Ottawa. These are the developer's calculations or estimates, not household billing results supplied in the thread [[./thread#^c38746095|Open\_erv]].
- DIY cost includes equipment and labor: Open\_erv considers owning a printer important, says outsourced printing is expensive, and estimates several days of assembly work even with an existing design [[./thread#^c38746009|Open\_erv]]. Building requirements also matter: mrspuratic says their renovation must satisfy ventilation requirements to qualify for energy-upgrade grants [[./thread#^c38744246|mrspuratic]].

## Lived experiences

- jorvi reports mental fog clearing within minutes after opening an afternoon window in a study/work room, and headaches after spending much of a day in musty rooms. This is firsthand symptom reporting without CO2 readings, blinded comparisons, or controls for temperature and humidity [[./thread#^c38746893|jorvi]].
- jnsaff2 reports winter outdoor conditions around −10°C and 50–80\% relative humidity, compared with approximately 20\% indoors, which they find uncomfortably dry. They want heat and moisture recovery together and say windows are open much of the summer; the humidity measurement method is unspecified [[./thread#^c38751026|jnsaff2]].
- mrspuratic installed paired reversing units across a mostly open-plan 40 m² ground floor and found that running both as intakes helped while the cooking hood extracted air [[./thread#^c38744246|mrspuratic]]. They clarify that this cooking mode provides no heat recovery; normal operation alternates flow. Their units have an outward installation slope and humidity-triggered purging, but no cooking-pollutant concentrations, fuel type, or health outcomes are reported [[./thread#^c38745046|mrspuratic]].
- doctorhandshake installed a Panasonic ERV for about 500 square feet plus a separate bathroom exhaust fan at a similar airflow. They subsequently thought locating the ERV extraction in the bathroom could have simplified moisture control. This is an installation experience and retrospective design judgment, without before-and-after humidity or exposure data [[./thread#^c38744761|doctorhandshake]].
- aifooh7Keew6xoo reports successful rental ventilation experiments using fitted boards in sliding-door tracks to carry ducts and other connections. The practical tradeoff was a narrower door opening and some discreet screw holes; no measured indoor-air improvement is claimed [[./thread#^c38747542|aifooh7Keew6xoo]].
- Open\_erv reports building two window units and describes a printed regenerator delivering about 45 CFM at approximately 80\% sensible efficiency [[./thread#^c38745943|Open\_erv]]. A later correction specifies 3.25 mm H2O pressure for the 45 CFM result. These are the developer's reported equipment results, without a supplied calibration procedure or independent replication [[./thread#^c38777218|Open\_erv]].
- Open\_erv says a silica-gel experiment on a thermal wheel produced favorable results measured with AHT10 sensors. They provide no numerical before-and-after readings and extend the idea to zeolite as a potentially longer-lasting adsorbent. This is firsthand humidity/heat-exchange experimentation, not CO2 or particle-sensor validation [[./thread#^c38746150|Open\_erv]].

## Unresolved disagreements

- The direct cognitive effect of ordinary indoor CO2 remains contested. jorvi treats several positive studies as confirmation, whereas sarchertech describes numerous negative studies and mixed academic evidence [[./thread#^c38752772|jorvi]] [[./thread#^c38753204|sarchertech]]. dotancohen questions apparent institutional bias, and JoshGG requests reputable publications; the exported discussion does not supply enough study methods to resolve those objections [[./thread#^c38751787|dotancohen]] [[./thread#^c38746171|JoshGG]].
- There is no supplied validation of CO2, PM2.5, or NO2 sensors, nor a firsthand concentration series for those pollutants. schiffern recommends CO2 testing, TheWoolRug mentions a proposed CO2-responsive unit, and Open\_erv reports AHT10-based exchanger experiments; none establishes pollutant-sensor accuracy, drift, calibration, or reliable exposure thresholds [[./thread#^c38743885|schiffern]] [[./thread#^c38744430|TheWoolRug]] [[./thread#^c38746150|Open\_erv]].
- The approved question extends beyond this unit's evidence: PM2.5 appears in jorvi's claim about studies, and wildfire smoke appears in material quoted by walterbell, but there are no firsthand traffic, NO2, or wildfire exposure comparisons [[./thread#^c38752772|jorvi]] [[./thread#^c38751217|walterbell]]. No air-related sleep outcome is reported: mrspuratic's 'night mode' is an equipment specification, while porkbeer's four-hour-sleep remark challenges trust in military evidence rather than describing an air-quality effect on sleep [[./thread#^c38744246|mrspuratic]] [[./thread#^c38746630|porkbeer]].
- Opening windows has a favorable subjective account from jorvi, but the discussion does not determine when that benefit outweighs outdoor pollution, humidity, thermal discomfort, or heating costs. leoedin supplies a reason ventilation can worsen humidity, while walterbell's wildfire reference supplies no window-open versus window-closed exposure measurements [[./thread#^c38746893|jorvi]] [[./thread#^c38747378|leoedin]] [[./thread#^c38751217|walterbell]].
- Mold prevention is not settled by choosing commercial equipment. keep\_reading recommends an Aprilaire unit to avoid mold, but persolb asks how it addresses condensation and later notes its moisture transfer [[./thread#^c38744492|keep\_reading]] [[./thread#^c38755476|persolb]]. thadk highlights a claim about filtering a dehumidifier, while schneems advocates separate ventilation, dehumidification, and HVAC systems; no comparative mold or health results establish which arrangement is sufficient [[./thread#^c38745103|thadk]] [[./thread#^c38745401|schneems]].
- The disagreement over regenerator moisture recovery becomes more conditional through replies: schiffern initially says there is none, Open\_erv describes condensation and re-evaporation, and kragen asks whether adsorbents permit exchange without condensation, which Open\_erv confirms [[./thread#^c38743885|schiffern]] [[./thread#^c38746150|Open\_erv]] [[./thread#^c38753374|kragen]] [[./thread#^c38777165|Open\_erv]]. Achieved moisture transfer, durability, and possible adsorbent carryover remain unquantified.
- Centralized versus decentralized ventilation remains a design tradeoff. schiffern favors centralized automatic pressure balancing; Open\_erv argues that inexpensive airflow sensors and control can regulate decentralized units, while acknowledging that strong winds can overwhelm their fans and describing a storm-closing valve. Neither provides a comparative field trial covering air quality, noise, electricity use, and ventilation during storms [[./thread#^c38764504|schiffern]] [[./thread#^c38777193|Open\_erv]].
- Long-term efficiency and payback remain uncertain. Open\_erv questions commercial ratings and lifetime economics, while kragen challenges assumptions about cooling versus heating savings [[./thread#^c38750170|Open\_erv]] [[./thread#^c38753308|kragen]]. Proposed branching and checkerboard exchanger designs from kragen and schiffern are engineering hypotheses, without demonstrated household exposure improvements or lifecycle savings in this source [[./thread#^c38753349|kragen]] [[./thread#^c38766871|schiffern]].
