# [Low Cost CO2 Sensors Comparison: Photo-Acoustic vs. NDIR](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=35261115)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants compare photoacoustic and NDIR CO2 sensors, focusing on accuracy, drift, calibration practices, operating conditions, power, size, and price. The thread also distinguishes true CO2 measurement from inexpensive VOC-based estimated readings, and discusses the limited uses and drift of TVOC sensors. Practical experiences with bedroom and office CO2 readings lead to discussion of ventilation, opening windows, and possible home automation. Other comments explore how photoacoustic sensing works and whether CO2 monitoring could be integrated into phones or shared through public maps.
Tags: CO2 sensors, photoacoustic sensing, NDIR, calibration, sensor drift, ventilation, TVOC

## Relevance

Comments directly discuss CO2 sensor accuracy, calibration, drift, and whether VOC-based estimates represent actual CO2. Firsthand accounts report high bedroom and office readings, headaches and sleep-related ventilation concerns, and using open windows or considering mechanical ventilation, with cost and cold-weather tradeoffs. ## Arguments

- CO2 monitoring can reveal ventilation problems that occupants otherwise miss. teekert [[./thread#^c35287778|teekert]] treats CO2 as a ventilation indicator relevant to occupied rooms, cooking and moisture; gambiting [[./thread#^c35285851|gambiting]] describes using readings to change office and bedroom ventilation. These accounts support monitoring as a practical prompt to act, without establishing that CO2 measures every indoor pollutant.
- VOC-derived eCO2 should not be treated as a direct CO2 measurement. wkat4242 [[./thread#^c35284469|wkat4242]] argues that alcohol can sharply raise a VOC reading without changing actual CO2. mnw21cam [[./thread#^c35288164|mnw21cam]] reinforces this with a secondhand account of a video demonstration in which a cheap detector responded to alcohol but poorly to a CO2 stream; that demonstration is described, not independently available in this source.
- wkat4242 [[./thread#^c35284469|wkat4242]] favors NDIR for purportedly lower drift and reference-gas arrangements that help compensate for environmental effects, while allowing that photoacoustic technology may improve. Their follow-up [[./thread#^c35286343|wkat4242]] attributes the long-term stability claim partly to an industrial user's experience; their own home sensors regularly receive fresh air.
- The photoacoustic explanation concerns direct physical sensing: mnw21cam [[./thread#^c35288132|mnw21cam]] attributes wavelength-specific absorption to quantum processes and sound generation to heating, then explains that the acoustic frequency follows the switched infrared illumination [[./thread#^c35288576|mnw21cam]]. This explains the proposed mechanism, without supplying an accuracy validation.
- ahaucnx [[./thread#^c35285265|ahaucnx]], identifying themselves as the article's author, recommends TVOC sensors for detecting changes such as cleaning-chemical or paint-container emissions, rather than determining specific concentrations. They also report that Sensirion TVOC sensors respond quickly to chemicals [[./thread#^c35285577|ahaucnx]]; the suggested applications are not documented exposure incidents in this thread.
- illegalsmile [[./thread#^c35289048|illegalsmile]] argues that inexpensive sensors can be useful for household decisions despite falling short of research-grade performance. Drawing on instrumentation work and kit building, they propose local calibration and response correction, but provide no calibration dataset or quantified residual error.
- mschuster91 [[./thread#^c35286012|mschuster91]] proposes heat-recovery ventilation to reduce the heating penalty of outdoor air, or forced air exchange with an adjacent room and sound damping. These are suggested improvements to the cold-window problem, with no reported installation or before-and-after measurements here.

## Counterarguments

- Matching trends do not establish accurate concentrations. unwind [[./thread#^c35286212|unwind]] describes two technologies tracking peaks and valleys together while reporting different amplitudes, leaving them unsure which to trust. Their suggestion that the higher-peaking sensor would be safer is a preference under uncertainty, not evidence that it measures more accurately.
- NDIR does not have uniformly demonstrated stability in these accounts. alwayslikethis [[./thread#^c35284777|alwayslikethis]] reports an MH-Z19 baseline falling to roughly 180–200 ppm, challenging wkat4242's general stability claim. Replies propose automatic-calibration error, but the affected owner's settings and the cause are never confirmed.
- Price alone is a weak reliability rule. bsmth [[./thread#^c35285707|bsmth]] recalls an informal view that sensors below €50 were worthless after poor cheap-sensor experiences; illegalsmile [[./thread#^c35289048|illegalsmile]] describes useful possibilities with approximately \$35 NDIR modules. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c35285977|ahaucnx]] claims their kit uses modules found in expensive monitors, but this is a seller's assertion rather than an independent accuracy result.
- TVOC outputs can become misleading even when their intended role is detecting changes. dws [[./thread#^c35285401|dws]] reports a steady increase over 12 hours in an empty room with a window cracked, disputing a simple leak or outgassing explanation. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c35285577|ahaucnx]] explicitly lacks experience with that sensor model, so their positive experience with Sensirion does not resolve this failure.
- The cognitive-risk interpretation of the article's bedroom graph is disputed. haram\_masala [[./thread#^c35284385|haram\_masala]] thinks it reaches impairment-associated levels; MBCook [[./thread#^c35284577|MBCook]] says it appears to remain below 1,000 ppm and recalls concern starting nearer 1,500 ppm. Neither supplies a supporting study, and the graph itself is absent from the supplied export.
- A roughly 400 ppm ambient reference is not a universal lower bound for every enclosure or sensor output. Responding to bsder's question [[./thread#^c35285372|bsder]], gambiting [[./thread#^c35285867|gambiting]] reports SCD41 readings below 400 and above 5,000 ppm, with reduced accuracy outside the stated range. Robotbeat [[./thread#^c35286005|Robotbeat]] [[./thread#^c35293494|Robotbeat]] raises fresh concrete and sealed greenhouses as possible low-CO2 environments; these are contextual counterexamples, not documented personal exposure measurements.

## Assumptions

- Automatic baseline calibration assumes the sensor periodically encounters an appropriate fresh-air reference. ysleepy [[./thread#^c35287450|ysleepy]] suggests minimum tracking toward approximately 400 ppm could miscalibrate a continuously occupied enclosure; wkat4242 [[./thread#^c35292505|wkat4242]] agrees. This is a proposed explanation for the reported MH-Z19 drift, not a demonstrated diagnosis or a universal instruction to disable calibration.
- Attributing symptoms specifically to CO2 assumes that other changes accompanying ventilation do not explain the improvement. teekert [[./thread#^c35287778|teekert]] reports headache relief after opening windows, and gambiting [[./thread#^c35285851|gambiting]] retrospectively links sleepiness and reduced focus to high readings. Neither account isolates CO2 from other environmental changes.
- Reliability judgments assume correct preparation, calibration and environmental compensation. bsmth [[./thread#^c35285707|bsmth]] says they initially misunderstood their cheap sensors' conditioning requirements; nimish [[./thread#^c35284342|nimish]] stresses calibration for an Infineon alternative; numpad0 [[./thread#^c35286974|numpad0]] describes temperature-related measurement problems. The thread does not independently establish a common setup procedure across these different devices.
- Ventilation proposals assume the incoming air improves the relevant exposure. mschuster91's adjacent-room option [[./thread#^c35286012|mschuster91]] presumes that room offers useful replacement air, while teekert's window-opening practice [[./thread#^c35287778|teekert]] does not include outdoor pollutant measurements. The discussion supplies no basis for assuming every outdoor-air exchange improves overall air quality.

## Conditions

- Cold weather, occupancy and privacy shape the practical tradeoff. teekert [[./thread#^c35287778|teekert]] was sharing a bedroom with children during house reconstruction and closing it against the cold. gambiting [[./thread#^c35285851|gambiting]] wanted a closed office door for privacy but found that maintaining ventilation meant an open window and discomfort in winter. Neither specifies outdoor pollution conditions.
- A complete portable monitor can be affordable for spot checks but expensive for every room. teekert [[./thread#^c35287778|teekert]] paid about €220 for an Aranet4, wanted multiple devices for automated ventilation, and considered that price prohibitive at room-by-room scale. Their expectation of more than a year on a standard battery is prospective, not a measured battery-life result.
- Quoted component prices are substantially lower than the complete-monitor price, but they represent different purchases. unwind [[./thread#^c35286212|unwind]] reports approximately \$20 sensors in the comparison; illegalsmile [[./thread#^c35289048|illegalsmile]] mentions \$35 NDIR; Havoc [[./thread#^c35290132|Havoc]] plans to try an approximately \$17 SCD41 from AliExpress. snvzz [[./thread#^c35285940|snvzz]] questions the apparent accuracy-to-price ranking. These comments do not establish equivalent finished-device cost, provenance or performance.
- Retrofit affordability depends on ownership, installation and tolerance for noise. mschuster91 [[./thread#^c35286012|mschuster91]] frames structural ventilation changes as an option for homeowners and quotes a €20 bathroom fan, an indoor exchange below \$100, or a €525 heat-exchange fan [[./thread#^c35286344|mschuster91]]. These are proposed equipment-level options, without a demonstrated installed price.
- gambiting [[./thread#^c35286880|gambiting]] does not feel comfortable installing ventilation themselves and cites an actual air-conditioning purchase costing £500 for hardware plus £1,000 installation. Their expectation that a ventilation fan would also cost at least £1,000 installed is an extrapolation, but their lack of that spare budget is the stated reason for continuing with windows.

## Lived experiences

- teekert [[./thread#^c35287778|teekert]] reports an Aranet4 reaching 2,500 ppm on the first night in a crowded, closed bedroom. They opened windows and say their recurring morning headaches subsequently disappeared, with cold nostrils as the comfort cost. This is a firsthand reading and symptom account, without controlled attribution or measured sleep quality.
- gambiting [[./thread#^c35285851|gambiting]] reports their home office quickly exceeding 2,000 and then 3,000 ppm with its door and window closed. They associate prolonged occupancy with sleepiness and reduced focus. They also report bedroom readings above 2,500 ppm overnight with the door closed and stopped closing it; no quantified post-change cognitive or sleep outcome is supplied.
- Replying to teekert, umeshunni [[./thread#^c35292581|umeshunni]] reports that opening windows for 15 minutes can lower CO2 by 400–500 ppm. The comment does not specify the sensor, starting concentration, room size, occupancy or weather, so it does not establish a transferable ventilation rate.
- alwayslikethis [[./thread#^c35284400|alwayslikethis]] [[./thread#^c35284777|alwayslikethis]] reports multiple MH-Z19 sensors drifting downward by more than 50\% over weeks or months, including a baseline around 180–200 ppm. In the direct reply chain, ysleepy [[./thread#^c35287450|ysleepy]] reports little drift from an MH-Z19C over more than a year with automatic calibration disabled, explicitly calling this anecdotal. These involve different model variants and incompletely documented conditions.
- ahaucnx [[./thread#^c35285280|ahaucnx]] reports SenseAir S8 and MH-Z19 monitors giving approximately matching readings with automatic baseline calibration enabled, and some MH-Z19 units running for years without issues. They explicitly lack long-term drift experience without that calibration, limiting what this comparison can resolve.
- dws [[./thread#^c35285188|dws]] describes months of testing sensors they call CSS811, with repeated upward drift to implausible values and recovery after a full reset. Their follow-up [[./thread#^c35285401|dws]] describes a 12-hour increase in an empty, partly ventilated room. No independent chemical measurement establishes what was actually present.
- numpad0 [[./thread#^c35286974|numpad0]] reports that a BME280/CCS811 combination appeared to drift with temperature errors and attributes some interference to the CCS811 heater affecting the temperature sensor. Adding another temperature sensor delayed drift only marginally in their setup.
- GeoAtreides [[./thread#^c35286699|GeoAtreides]] reports nighttime and post-bean spikes in a breath-VOC-equivalent output, which they associate with digestion, and an air-quality output that often closely inversely tracks humidity. These are observations of sensor behavior; they do not establish a harmful chemical concentration or distinguish humidity effects on the sensor from changes in exposure.
- bsmth [[./thread#^c35285707|bsmth]] describes very poor accuracy from cheap gas sensors such as MQ-7 in DIY devices, while acknowledging an initial misunderstanding of conditioning versus warmup. They ordered the article's kit but had not yet reported results. Havoc [[./thread#^c35290132|Havoc]] likewise calls an existing TVOC sensor unusable because of drift and describes an intended SCD41 trial, not a completed comparison.
- illegalsmile [[./thread#^c35289048|illegalsmile]] reports a background in remote environmental instrumentation, building two AirGradient kits, and comparing inexpensive sensors with professional equipment. They judge the inexpensive equipment below research grade but potentially adequate at home; no numerical comparison is provided.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The cause and duration of CO2-sensor drift remain unresolved. alwayslikethis [[./thread#^c35284400|alwayslikethis]] asks whether contamination or declining source brightness limits lifetime; ysleepy [[./thread#^c35287450|ysleepy]] and wkat4242 [[./thread#^c35286343|wkat4242]] propose automatic-baseline problems instead. No follow-up confirms the failing units' calibration settings, tests either explanation or establishes technology-wide longevity.
- The supplied comments cannot determine which sensor technology is most accurate or best value. unwind [[./thread#^c35286212|unwind]] sees agreement in timing but disagreement in magnitude; snvzz [[./thread#^c35285940|snvzz]] questions the price/accuracy ranking; throwaway81523 [[./thread#^c35285018|throwaway81523]] is explicitly uncertain whether photoacoustic sensing uses less power and says they bought NDIR. There is no supplied reference-instrument dataset or measured power comparison.
- Health thresholds and causal effects remain uncertain within this source. haram\_masala [[./thread#^c35284385|haram\_masala]] and MBCook [[./thread#^c35284577|MBCook]] disagree about the cognitive significance of the described graph, while londons\_explore [[./thread#^c35286670|londons\_explore]] proposes a harmful-air warning at 1,500 ppm without substantiation here. teekert [[./thread#^c35287778|teekert]] and gambiting [[./thread#^c35285851|gambiting]] supply symptom associations, but no controlled cognition tests, sleep measurements or evidence establishing a universal safe/unsafe cutoff.
- The usefulness of TVOC sensing in ordinary homes remains contested. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c35285265|ahaucnx]] recommends detecting chemical spikes, while dws [[./thread#^c35285401|dws]] reports unexplained baseline increases and GeoAtreides [[./thread#^c35286699|GeoAtreides]] questions what actionable information the readings add beyond odors and humidity. The discussion does not resolve how reliably a user can distinguish a meaningful emission event from sensor behavior.
- Affordable accuracy below approximately 400 ppm is not demonstrated. Robotbeat [[./thread#^c35293494|Robotbeat]] requests such a sensor for a greenhouse; gambiting [[./thread#^c35293556|gambiting]] tentatively suggests STC31, expresses uncertainty about its percentage output, and reiterates reduced below-range accuracy for SCD40/SCD41. Displaying a low value or reaching zero on an output scale is not evidence supplied here of adequate low-concentration precision.
- Installed ventilation cost remains disputed. gambiting [[./thread#^c35286880|gambiting]] expects substantial professional labor costs based on an air-conditioning installation; mschuster91 [[./thread#^c35288018|mschuster91]] argues that a simple fan requires much less specialized work and estimates roughly half an hour each for drilling and electrical work. No job-specific quote or completed installation settles the disagreement.
- Phone-integrated gas sensing and crowdsourced exposure maps remain proposals. londons\_explore [[./thread#^c35286670|londons\_explore]] suggests CO2 alerts and detection of ozone, CO or NOx using different LEDs; wkat4242 [[./thread#^c35284469|wkat4242]] says current direct CO2 sensors are still too large for phones. Malic [[./thread#^c35288277|Malic]] asks about geographic mapping, and londons\_explore [[./thread#^c35291109|londons\_explore]] identifies absent phone sensors and proprietary interfaces as barriers. No validated deployment or NO2 measurement is supplied.
- This source unit provides no firsthand PM2.5 or NO2 measurements, filtration outcomes, traffic-exposure comparisons or wildfire-smoke accounts. Its window-opening evidence concerns CO2 reductions and cold discomfort, as reported by teekert [[./thread#^c35287778|teekert]] and umeshunni [[./thread#^c35292581|umeshunni]]. It therefore cannot establish when opening windows worsens pollution exposure, or compare filtration with ventilation under polluted outdoor conditions.
