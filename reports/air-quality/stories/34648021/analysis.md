# [I bought a CO2 monitor and it broke me](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=34648021)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

The thread responds to an account of becoming anxious after monitoring indoor CO2, debating how meaningful elevated readings are and whether reported cognitive effects at ordinary indoor levels are well established. Participants discuss monitor accuracy, calibration, sensor types, DIY devices, and the limits of treating CO2 as a stand-in for other pollutants. They compare ways to refresh indoor air, including brief cross-ventilation, keeping windows cracked, fans, filtered fresh-air intakes, and ERV or HRV systems, while weighing heat loss, outdoor pollution, noise, cost, and building layout. Other branches cover whether plants can meaningfully absorb household CO2, the distinction between particle filtration and CO2 removal, air-purifier use, gas appliances and carbon monoxide, and whether monitoring improves health or intensifies anxiety.
Tags: CO2 monitoring, indoor ventilation, air quality sensors, cognitive effects, ERV and HRV, air filtration, calibration, health anxiety

## Relevance

The comments provide firsthand CO2 readings, sensor calibration and accuracy caveats, and practical ventilation experiences, including opening windows and installing ERV systems. They discuss reported effects on concentration, sleep and comfort while presenting conflicting interpretations of whether ordinary indoor CO2 levels directly impair cognition or mainly indicate poor ventilation. Filtration and outdoor particulate exposure, including tradeoffs between cleaner air and increased CO2, are also addressed. ## Arguments

- Monitoring can reveal actionable patterns that sensations alone do not explain. cardanome [[./thread#^c34649406|cardanome]] associates rapid CO2 buildup with talking during online meetings; stuaxo [[./thread#^c34648438|stuaxo]] notices increases when people occupy a small closed room. plorkyeran [[./thread#^c34649830|plorkyeran]] bought a monitor to investigate fluctuating concentration, but does not report a resulting correlation or diagnosis.
- Ventilation and particle filtration address different problems. mindslight [[./thread#^c34648754|mindslight]] explains that a recirculating range hood or filtered box fan will not lower CO2. Answering kayson's question, ahaucnx [[./thread#^c34651343|ahaucnx]] clarifies that occupants produce the CO2; purifiers do not produce it, but keeping rooms closed for effective particle removal can allow it to accumulate.
- Filtered outdoor-air supply offers a way to address high indoor CO2 without admitting outdoor particles untreated. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c34650911|ahaucnx]] reports successful demand-controlled ventilation through high-performance filters in an area with periodically severe pollution. This supports the feasibility of that particular installation, rather than establishing that every home needs the same system.
- Purposeful airflow can outperform simply opening an arbitrary window. mindslight [[./thread#^c34648754|mindslight]] proposes separate openings and a fan; distances [[./thread#^c34652793|distances]] argues for brief cross-ventilation so that walls and furniture retain heat. These are practical arguments for improving air exchange, conditional on the building actually permitting throughflow.
- Heat or energy recovery can make regular ventilation more comfortable and economical. MengerSponge [[./thread#^c34652731|MengerSponge]] identifies commercial HRV and ERV equipment, while zsolt224 [[./thread#^c34652605|zsolt224]] recommends recovering humidity as well as heat. bradknowles [[./thread#^c34653024|bradknowles]] frames ventilation as balancing clean supply air, contaminant exhaust, humidity, and temperature.
- Moving air between rooms can reduce exposure in a heavily occupied room even without removing CO2 from the entire building. leipert [[./thread#^c34648923|leipert]] explains this dilution mechanism, and simondotau [[./thread#^c34648868|simondotau]] reports substantially lower overnight bedroom readings when using ducted AC fan mode.
- Cooking-related source control is another practical option. twawaaay [[./thread#^c34648243|twawaaay]] reports using electric appliances more and gas less after monitoring CO2, and airing afterward when using gas. ladberg [[./thread#^c34648127|ladberg]] reports stove or oven use causing sharp CO2 increases and responds by opening windows fully. Neither provides cooking-related NO2 measurements.
- The case for controlling particles includes both immediate respiratory distress and claimed longer-term effects. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c34651674|ahaucnx]] describes hazardous local pollution and hospitals treating breathing difficulties, and invokes studies of harm at low PM2.5 concentrations and a WHO guideline of 5 µg/m³. Those study and guideline claims are reported by the commenter, not independently evaluated in this source unit.
- CO2 can be useful as a ventilation or shared-air indicator even when its direct physiological effects are disputed. panarky [[./thread#^c34650619|panarky]] frames it as a proxy for potentially pathogen-containing exhaled air. This argument concerns ventilation conditions, not a direct measurement of infectious particles.
- There is a positive case for cognitive and sleep effects, but it combines different kinds of evidence. NelsonMinar [[./thread#^c34650951|NelsonMinar]] claims a study finds a statistically significant cognitive difference between 400 and 600 ppm. eulers\_secret [[./thread#^c34649206|eulers\_secret]] cites reported ISS symptoms at 5000 ppm, including headaches, mental slowness, irritation, and disrupted sleep, while acknowledging uncertainty around effects near 1000 ppm.
- Air-quality concerns need not be justified solely by the leading cause of death. flangola7 [[./thread#^c34654450|flangola7]] argues that traffic risk does not make other preventive measures pointless; xboxnolifes [[./thread#^c34658914|xboxnolifes]] emphasizes prolonged poor health. These replies challenge an either-or framing of pollution protection versus other health priorities.
- Measurements can support automatic, local control. p1mrx [[./thread#^c34650757|p1mrx]] built CO2-triggered ventilation using a bathroom exhaust and smart switch. xolox [[./thread#^c34660485|xolox]] uses local Aranet4 readings in Home Assistant to activate a window-directed fan when temperatures permit; ryansouza [[./thread#^c34662580|ryansouza]] describes retaining historical readings through HomeKit and Prometheus integration.

## Counterarguments

- The low-level CO2 cognition claim is directly contested. sarchertech [[./thread#^c34651244|sarchertech]] says NelsonMinar's linked study uses CO2 as a proxy for ventilation rather than isolating CO2 exposure. In a later methodological reply, sarchertech [[./thread#^c34658142|sarchertech]] argues that positive findings are inconsistently replicated and often concern extreme concentrations. NelsonMinar [[./thread#^c34654982|NelsonMinar]] acknowledges conflicting research. These are competing readings of studies, not experiments supplied here.
- Skepticism about ordinary indoor CO2 does not establish that effects begin only at unconsciousness-level exposure. clairity [[./thread#^c34648980|clairity]] claims real effects require 50,000–100,000 ppm; CommieBobDole [[./thread#^c34651209|CommieBobDole]] challenges those numbers, and deadbeeves [[./thread#^c34651732|deadbeeves]] says the cited study reports loss of consciousness after roughly seven minutes at 10\%. The exchange does not justify treating those concentrations as a harmless boundary.
- Opening windows is not an unconditional air-quality improvement. 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c34653839|5e92cb50239222b]] reports outdoor particle concentrations high enough to overwhelm two purifiers when a window is barely opened. Conversely, robga [[./thread#^c34653188|robga]] challenges the categorical claim that windows must remain closed: partial ventilation can remain compatible with filtration when outdoor pollution and incoming airflow are within the purifier's capacity.
- A universal five-minute airing rule is contradicted by other measured accounts. cardanome [[./thread#^c34650202|cardanome]] reports reliable large reductions, but dathinab [[./thread#^c34678182|dathinab]] reports only a modest reduction despite opening available windows as fully as practical. medler [[./thread#^c34652410|medler]] describes dramatically different results between a windy apartment and a stagnant house. Poor results cannot automatically be attributed to occupant error.
- The V in HVAC does not guarantee outdoor-air exchange. dragonwriter [[./thread#^c34648799|dragonwriter]] expects ventilation from HVAC, but jms703 [[./thread#^c34648858|jms703]] says many residential systems only recirculate. rektide [[./thread#^c34649235|rektide]] distinguishes reducing a room's concentration by mixing from preventing whole-building accumulation; EricE [[./thread#^c34649379|EricE]] offers leaky ductwork as another possible explanation for falling readings.
- The inference that the article's meter must be broken overlooks a reported outdoor check. \_Microft [[./thread#^c34652680|\_Microft]] quotes an outdoor reading of 480 ppm, and godelski [[./thread#^c34652703|godelski]] acknowledges missing it. Nevertheless, psychphysic [[./thread#^c34653436|psychphysic]] cautions that a sensor can read correctly near its calibration point while behaving incorrectly at higher concentrations, so the outdoor check is not complete validation.
- Price alone is an inadequate reliability test. brendangregg [[./thread#^c34648772|brendangregg]] distrusts sub-\$100 meters based on experience, while wkat4242 [[./thread#^c34655745|wkat4242]] describes inexpensive direct-sensing components and an approximately €100 finished device, and hrez [[./thread#^c34651583|hrez]] names a \$60 Sensirion gadget. These reports challenge a blanket price cutoff without proving that all inexpensive devices are accurate.
- A few houseplants are repeatedly challenged as an effective substitute for ventilation. Against suggestions such as TEP\_Kim\_Il\_Sung's [[./thread#^c34653286|TEP\_Kim\_Il\_Sung]], cinntaile [[./thread#^c34653298|cinntaile]], etrautmann [[./thread#^c34651432|etrautmann]], and JohnBooty [[./thread#^c34651836|JohnBooty]] offer estimates requiring hundreds or thousands of plants. jimnotgym [[./thread#^c34653752|jimnotgym]] additionally notes respiration in darkness. The exact estimates vary, but the practical objection is the mismatch between occupant emissions and ordinary houseplant growth.
- The claim that clean filtered air weakens children's immunity is disputed. kerpotgh [[./thread#^c34651105|kerpotgh]] invokes allergies and weak immunity; bawolff [[./thread#^c34651736|bawolff]] responds that the hygiene hypothesis concerns microorganisms, and travisjungroth [[./thread#^c34652521|travisjungroth]] distinguishes proposed benefits of some biological exposures from harmful coal particles. No childhood allergy outcomes from these filtration setups are supplied.
- Routine furnace operation should not be assumed to inject combustion products indoors. Responding to jandrese's hypothesis, kesslern [[./thread#^c34648548|kesslern]] says combustion products should exhaust outside. throw0101c [[./thread#^c34648901|throw0101c]] distinguishes sealed high-efficiency systems from older units vulnerable to back-drafting. Malfunctions remain relevant, but normal operation and failed exhaust separation are different cases.
- Keeping a bedroom door open for ventilation introduces a separate fire-safety issue. flandish [[./thread#^c34648401|flandish]], identifying as a firefighter, argues for closed bedroom doors and smoke/CO detectors. When twawaaay [[./thread#^c34648719|twawaaay]] says smoke could provide earlier warning, qbasic\_forever [[./thread#^c34648983|qbasic\_forever]] replies that detectors should provide the warning before smoke becomes overwhelming.
- More monitoring is not uniformly reassuring or beneficial. bryceacc [[./thread#^c34648662|bryceacc]] reports anxiety from the displayed number without noticing cognitive differences, while thenerdhead [[./thread#^c34666995|thenerdhead]] worries about rumination from continuous health data. That criticism coexists with xyzelement's [[./thread#^c34649130|xyzelement]] account of useful behavior changes and improved comfort; neither experience establishes a universal psychological response.
- Existing CO alarms do not settle the question of lower persistent exposures. ignite [[./thread#^c34659367|ignite]] emphasizes local detector requirements and widespread awareness, but dolni [[./thread#^c34655113|dolni]] reports a furnace leak repeatedly producing a 25 ppm maximum reading without an alarm. dolni's broader claims about chronic effects and alarm standards [[./thread#^c34651828|dolni]] require evidence beyond that household incident.
- Dismissing air pollution because other hazards may kill first is challenged by the affected resident. Gordonjcp [[./thread#^c34652960|Gordonjcp]] calls air-quality equipment a distraction; 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c34653924|5e92cb50239222b]] responds with severe local pollution figures and reported health disparities, then describes concern about developing COPD despite never smoking [[./thread#^c34655321|5e92cb50239222b]]. The anecdote challenges dismissal, while the claimed population statistics remain unverified here.

## Assumptions

- This analysis is limited to story 34648021, part 0. The article itself is absent: its contents are available only through commenters' quotations and interpretations, including godelski [[./thread#^c34652613|godelski]] and \_Microft [[./thread#^c34652680|\_Microft]]. Likewise, sarchertech's literature discussion [[./thread#^c34658142|sarchertech]] is evidence of what the commenter argues, not an independent review of the linked studies.
- Feeling better after ventilation does not isolate CO2 as the cause. godelski [[./thread#^c34652613|godelski]] reports both improved sleep and more stable temperature after changing airflow, while dilippkumar [[./thread#^c34648482|dilippkumar]] associates stuffiness with VOC peaks. These accounts leave temperature, other pollutants, expectations, and ventilation itself as possible contributors.
- CO2 is not a universal clean-air score. antirez [[./thread#^c34652693|antirez]] reports low office CO2 alongside a separate basement radon problem. def\_true\_false [[./thread#^c34661624|def\_true\_false]] explains that filtration can remove particles from previously exhaled air without removing its CO2, weakening a simple equivalence between high CO2 and unfiltered pathogen exposure.
- Outdoor calibration assumes an appropriate outdoor reference. fotta [[./thread#^c34648457|fotta]] recommends 400 ppm, but chris\_va [[./thread#^c34648927|chris\_va]] raises urban CO2 accumulation. After examining the offered links, kolinko [[./thread#^c34664038|kolinko]] accepts possible 25–75\% elevations under particular conditions; chris\_va [[./thread#^c34667515|chris\_va]] qualifies doubling as extreme and a Paris figure as unverified. These comments do not establish one outdoor baseline for every location.
- Automatic baseline calibration depends on the sensor encountering the baseline it assumes. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c34653344|ahaucnx]] describes a seven-day cycle requiring ambient exposure; fotta [[./thread#^c34656418|fotta]] instead disables automatic calibration and uses a one-time outdoor reference. fotta's quoted lifetime drift specification of ±50 ppm [[./thread#^c34656451|fotta]] is a manufacturer claim, not a longitudinal verification reported in the thread.
- Sensor placement can affect what a reading represents. blkhawk [[./thread#^c34651845|blkhawk]] moved one sensor over months and inferred low-level pooling and strong desk-proximity effects, while explicitly acknowledging possible mistaken conclusions. That observation does not establish a room-wide concentration map or validate the proposed mechanism.
- A displayed PM2.5 value near zero is not proof of particle-free air. wkat4242 [[./thread#^c34655770|wkat4242]] reports near-zero readings from both purifier and separate sensor while still seeing dust in flashlight illumination. blacksmith\_tb [[./thread#^c34649009|blacksmith\_tb]] reports disagreement between two particle monitors. Size sensitivity and instrument uncertainty limit what can be inferred from a single displayed metric.
- Purifier-versus-leak calculations depend on a simplified room model. ravel-bar-foo [[./thread#^c34659250|ravel-bar-foo]] estimates roughly 9\% residual outdoor contamination when air replacement is 1\% per minute and removal about 10\% per minute. Applying that estimate assumes comparable mixing, steady flows, and removal behavior; the commenter does not validate it against a particular occupied room.
- Plant-removal calculations require consistent units, room volume, time, lighting, and ongoing emissions. ramchip [[./thread#^c34651737|ramchip]] challenges a ppm-per-pot figure lacking environmental volume, and etrautmann [[./thread#^c34651406|etrautmann]] acknowledges uncertainty about the time basis of another quoted estimate. The discussion's differing plant counts should not be treated as interchangeable measured results.
- Severe disease-related hypoxia is not evidence about ordinary indoor CO2 concentrations. stordoff [[./thread#^c34651896|stordoff]] describes impaired judgment during COVID-associated low oxygen saturation, while aaronmdjones [[./thread#^c34653667|aaronmdjones]] recounts airway obstruction and emergency intubation. Their reply ancestry concerns whether impairment is always self-noticeable, not whether household CO2 caused either episode.
- An intuitively convincing response is weaker than a calibrated performance test. ricksunny [[./thread#^c34653033|ricksunny]] finds increased fan noise during physical activity persuasive because it fits expected CO2 production and controller response, but explicitly distinguishes that feeling from accepting the device's efficacy without further diligence.
- Environmental measurements, regional health comparisons, and personal illness have different evidentiary status. 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c34653924|5e92cb50239222b]] combines pollutant figures with claims of fourfold respiratory illness and shorter life expectancy; the later COPD concern [[./thread#^c34655321|5e92cb50239222b]] is personal testimony. The supplied comments do not establish diagnoses, statistical adjustment, or pollutant-specific causation for those outcomes.

## Conditions

- Outdoor pollution determines whether window opening is a workable improvement. robga [[./thread#^c34654653|robga]] lives in a fairly clean city with troublesome pollen; 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c34653839|5e92cb50239222b]] describes severe outdoor particles and seven to eight cold months annually. brmgb [[./thread#^c34653697|brmgb]] emphasizes traffic exhaust, industrial fumes, and tire residues in urban settings, rather than treating all outdoor particles as ordinary dust or pollen.
- Heating and cooling constraints differ sharply by climate. criddell [[./thread#^c34653655|criddell]] reports that admitting 38°C outdoor air makes maintaining 24°C indoors difficult for hours. distances [[./thread#^c34653887|distances]] describes the opposite situation: no AC, tolerance for warm summer interiors, and a cold-season need for short ventilation in an older apartment without mechanical ventilation.
- Building geometry and exposure matter alongside window size. medler [[./thread#^c34652410|medler]] contrasts a windy sixth-floor apartment with a stagnant house. def\_true\_false [[./thread#^c34661345|def\_true\_false]] finds L- or U-shaped airflow paths harder than straight ones, and dathinab [[./thread#^c34649219|dathinab]] notes that room volume and ceiling height affect both accumulation and how long airing may take.
- The filtered-supply retrofit was not a cheap plug-in intervention. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c34651081|ahaucnx]] reports spending a few thousand US dollars, including extensive sealing of an initially leaky house; the price included blowers, but the installation was highly customized and also used to test the monitors the commenter develops [[./thread#^c34651318|ahaucnx]].
- One apartment installation has relatively modest reported operating costs but ongoing maintenance. EVa5I7bHFq9mnYK [[./thread#^c34652531|EVa5I7bHFq9mnYK]] reports about \$20 monthly winter heating cost after five years of continuous operation. The system averages 40 m³/hour, has a 1400 W maximum heater, receives coarse-filter washing every two months and annual HEPA replacement [[./thread#^c34654515|EVa5I7bHFq9mnYK]]. Those figures do not establish annual costs elsewhere.
- Heat-recovery economics depend on existing costs and installation compatibility. \_spduchamp [[./thread#^c34656240|\_spduchamp]] reports approximately \$1000 for a shed ERV and \$2000 for a house unit. EVa5I7bHFq9mnYK [[./thread#^c34652771|EVa5I7bHFq9mnYK]] says heat recovery would never repay its cost in their situation and questions fitting the proposed arrangement into an apartment with supply and exhaust on opposite sides [[./thread#^c34654541|EVa5I7bHFq9mnYK]].
- Ventilation arrangements and habits vary across housing systems. leipert [[./thread#^c34648997|leipert]] describes frequent German airing and moisture concerns in airtight buildings; kzrdude [[./thread#^c34652409|kzrdude]] has a wall inlet in a 1950s Swedish apartment; t0mas88 [[./thread#^c34652902|t0mas88]] describes Dutch passive inlets with central exhaust or heat recovery. Sharlin [[./thread#^c34653453|Sharlin]] cautions that an apparently passive apartment inlet may belong to a mechanically assisted building system.
- Airtight retrofits require attention to the resulting ventilation and building moisture conditions. jaclaz [[./thread#^c34653042|jaclaz]] reports problems in some Italian energy-saving renovations lacking adequate ventilation. porker [[./thread#^c34652940|porker]] raises spray-foam off-gassing, moisture accumulation, and repair difficulties; these are the commenter's cautions, not measured findings from the nursery retrofit discussed above them.
- Leak detection itself can require time, access, and interpretation. IanOzsvald [[./thread#^c34653121|IanOzsvald]] reports three years of repairs to a 1930s London house using thermal imaging. kolinko [[./thread#^c34652816|kolinko]] finds varying surface emissivity confusing; HybridCurve [[./thread#^c34658304|HybridCurve]] recommends a blower and fogger for direct visual detection instead of inferring leakage from temperature or humidity.
- Monitor purchase price is only part of DIY cost. fotta [[./thread#^c34648457|fotta]] identifies a sensor component costing about one-quarter of an Aranet4, but FloatArtifact [[./thread#^c34653927|FloatArtifact]] finds enclosure, PCB, and \$24–30 shipping costs erase much of the saving. Youden [[./thread#^c34653562|Youden]] additionally describes difficult SCD41 assembly and substantially different battery requirements between sensor designs.
- Cloud dependence is a purchase and usability constraint for some participants. strstr [[./thread#^c34648594|strstr]] discontinued a Netatmo after recognizing that its CO2 history exposed household bedtime patterns. jchw [[./thread#^c34649121|jchw]] and xolox [[./thread#^c34660485|xolox]] describe local Bluetooth/Home Assistant alternatives; their accounts concern particular devices and integrations, not every model sold under those brands.
- Comfort and local noise can prevent otherwise plausible ventilation routines. tayo42 [[./thread#^c34653039|tayo42]] cannot leave a bedroom window open without being awakened by neighbors. EVa5I7bHFq9mnYK [[./thread#^c34652531|EVa5I7bHFq9mnYK]] hears more mechanical noise when physical activity increases ventilation demand, while 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c34653839|5e92cb50239222b]] regards adding many more purifiers as too expensive and noisy.
- Draft preferences also have cultural and bodily contexts. KronisLV [[./thread#^c34652518|KronisLV]] describes Latvian beliefs that drafts cause illness; TomSwirly [[./thread#^c34653869|TomSwirly]] prefers a cold home but needs local heat for working hands. samus [[./thread#^c34704997|samus]] distinguishes brief ventilation drafts from continuous uncomfortable airflow, without resolving claims that drafts cause infections.
- Large-scale building refits face workforce constraints. In response to Terretta's question about missed pandemic-era ventilation upgrades, bobthepanda [[./thread#^c34662108|bobthepanda]] argues that construction continued operating, workers faced infection risks, and existing labor shortages limited additional retrofit capacity.

## Lived experiences

- ahaucnx [[./thread#^c34650911|ahaucnx]] reports that demand-controlled, filtered outdoor-air supply consistently keeps displayed PM2.5 at zero and CO2 below 600 ppm despite periodic severe outdoor pollution. This is a firsthand account of one customized system; the linked charts and article are not included in the supplied evidence.
- 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c34653839|5e92cb50239222b]] runs two Xiaomi purifiers, each with stated CADR around 300–350, often at maximum speed with windows shut and gaps sealed. Reported indoor PM2.5 usually remains 2–5 µg/m³ and can reach 30–40, while outdoor readings are described as hundreds and sometimes thousands. Even a tiny window opening substantially worsens indoor readings.
- 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c34653924|5e92cb50239222b]] reports local NO2 of 150–200 µg/m³ or higher, SO2 spikes around 10 mg/m³, and annual PM2.5 averages above 200 µg/m³. The gas instruments and measurement procedures are not specified. The commenter separately describes concern about developing COPD despite never smoking and personally knowing more cancer victims than traffic victims [[./thread#^c34655321|5e92cb50239222b]].
- robga [[./thread#^c34654653|robga]] uses two purifiers with stated CADR of 400 m³ and four AirGradient monitors. In a relatively clean city with pollen, the commenter deliberately accepts a PM2.5 reading rising from 0 to 6 while ventilation reduces CO2 from 1200 to 600 ppm, explicitly leaving the health wisdom of that exchange unresolved.
- jerlam [[./thread#^c34648666|jerlam]] reports 600–800 ppm with a cracked window in a windy location. strstr [[./thread#^c34648594|strstr]], using electric heating and cooking in the Pacific Northwest, similarly reports 600–800 with a small opening, needing a wider opening when indoor and outdoor temperatures are similar. These are successful local arrangements, not evidence that a small crack works everywhere.
- cardanome [[./thread#^c34650202|cardanome]] reports that five minutes of airing reduces roughly 1500 ppm to 500. dathinab [[./thread#^c34678182|dathinab]] instead reports a five-minute reduction from about 1200 to 1000, and clarifies that the estimated frequent-airing requirement came from earlier measurements plus approximately six hours testing a 55-minute-closed/five-minute-open cycle.
- medler [[./thread#^c34652410|medler]] gets below 500 ppm within two minutes by cracking a sixth-floor apartment window. At the parents' house, multiple open windows can have little effect for hours; an exhaust-directed fan helps. The commenter also identifies recirculating HVAC bringing air from another approximately 1500 ppm wing as an obstacle.
- kuon [[./thread#^c34653358|kuon]] reports that opening two roof windows for about five minutes brings the whole house to roughly 450 ppm, whereas an ordinary window mainly changes readings within a few meters while still cooling the house. The roof-window arrangement is unavailable to many apartment residents and is not used during rain.
- godelski [[./thread#^c34652613|godelski]] reports bedroom readings above 800 ppm, remaining above 600 with the bedroom window open. Cracking a particular window across the apartment as well brings readings near the local approximately 400 ppm baseline. The commenter reports better sleep and steadier temperature, but rejects large claimed cognitive-effect percentages.
- xyzelement [[./thread#^c34649130|xyzelement]] reports that an AirThings made high winter CO2 visible and prompted window opening in a clean-air waterfront suburb, with noticeably improved well-being. In a related reply, xyzelement [[./thread#^c34650581|xyzelement]] describes feeling mentally dull and low in stuffy rooms, while the spouse notices no comparable personal effect.
- bryceacc [[./thread#^c34648662|bryceacc]] originally monitored particles from fires and became preoccupied with lowering CO2 despite colder indoor temperatures. The commenter notices no cognitive difference between approximately 2000 ppm and outdoors, but feels distress at the displayed number; winter visits can produce readings above 2500 without guests expressing concern.
- colechristensen [[./thread#^c34648910|colechristensen]] reports sensing levels above 800 ppm and estimating readings within roughly 25\%, while mentioning impaired nasal breathing after an old injury. Smar [[./thread#^c34658635|Smar]] reports dizziness from around 800 ppm. rtpg [[./thread#^c34653223|rtpg]] reports worse sleep with higher readings and guessing the result before checking. These are subjective associations without controlled exposure tests.
- pgporada [[./thread#^c34650357|pgporada]] reports nursery CO2 well above 1400 ppm overnight after a spray-foam retrofit, falling to an average around 800 after adding an ERV. ericd [[./thread#^c34650935|ericd]] reports rooms rarely exceeding 600 with continuous HVAC circulation and ERV operation, plus fresher-feeling air and more even temperature, but does not know the energy-cost impact.
- simondotau [[./thread#^c34648868|simondotau]] reports overnight bedroom CO2 falling from well above 1500 ppm to below 800 using ducted AC fan mode. jchw [[./thread#^c34649084|jchw]] likewise observes lower readings from circulation alone. Neither account measures whole-house CO2 removal or establishes whether redistribution, leakage, or outdoor-air intake explains the change.
- ladberg [[./thread#^c34648127|ladberg]] reports approximately 2000 ppm after days without window opening and around 600 with a small continuous opening. Stove or oven use causes unspecified high spikes. twawaaay [[./thread#^c34648243|twawaaay]] reports subsequently favoring an electric grill and Instant Pot over gas, but supplies no before-and-after pollutant measurements for that substitution.
- car [[./thread#^c34649571|car]] reports watching a WYND Halo's air-quality readings improve as its connected purifier operated, particularly during severe forest-fire pollution. The account provides neither numerical wildfire PM2.5 reductions nor health outcomes. bryceacc [[./thread#^c34648662|bryceacc]] also identifies fires as the original reason for monitoring particles.
- antirez [[./thread#^c34652693|antirez]] reports office CO2 around 500 ppm with open windows in warm Catania, Sicily, but concerning radon readings in a basement after several days closed. The commenter installed a continuous outside-air supply fan. The office CO2 and basement radon observations concern different spaces, and no numerical radon reduction after installation is given.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The magnitude and cause of cognitive impairment at ordinary indoor CO2 remain unsettled in this unit. NelsonMinar [[./thread#^c34650951|NelsonMinar]] presents a low-concentration effect claim; sarchertech [[./thread#^c34658142|sarchertech]] contests the interpretation and replication evidence; arisAlexis [[./thread#^c34652876|arisAlexis]] rejects a claimed 50\% reduction at 1500 ppm. No controlled study supplied here resolves the threshold, duration, or everyday effect size.
- Individual sensitivity, other indoor conditions, and expectations remain difficult to separate. colechristensen [[./thread#^c34648910|colechristensen]] and Smar [[./thread#^c34658635|Smar]] report symptoms near 800 ppm, whereas bryceacc [[./thread#^c34648662|bryceacc]] notices no cognitive difference near 2000. ianai [[./thread#^c34649847|ianai]] proposes a blinded test in an unfamiliar room, but no completed test is reported.
- There is no established optimum for trading particles against CO2. robga [[./thread#^c34654653|robga]] explicitly questions whether accepting higher PM2.5 to halve CO2 is sensible; 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c34653839|5e92cb50239222b]] describes conditions where that strategy admits much larger particle loads. The comments do not quantify comparative health effects or provide a universal window-opening threshold.
- Sensor calibration advice conflicts even within named products. iudqnolq [[./thread#^c34653829|iudqnolq]] reports Aranet4 instructions discouraging recalibration without factory-quality conditions; bookofjoe [[./thread#^c34659101|bookofjoe]] says it does not need calibration; schmichael [[./thread#^c34694126|schmichael]] cites annual calibration and elevation-related advice. Together with dathinab's reported scaling failures [[./thread#^c34654374|dathinab]] and Youden's contrary deployment experience [[./thread#^c34655773|Youden]], this leaves model-specific reliability unresolved.
- The article's stubborn indoor readings cannot be diagnosed from the discussion. \_Microft [[./thread#^c34652680|\_Microft]] reports the outdoor 480 ppm check; psychphysic [[./thread#^c34653436|psychphysic]] says that does not establish higher-range accuracy; dathinab [[./thread#^c34678182|dathinab]] and medler [[./thread#^c34652410|medler]] demonstrate that genuinely poor air exchange is also plausible. Layout, instrument comparison, and a controlled ventilation test are missing.
- Heat-recovery performance and payback remain installation-specific. jaggederest [[./thread#^c34652941|jaggederest]] claims roughly 90\% recovery is standard and suggests cycling, while EVa5I7bHFq9mnYK [[./thread#^c34652771|EVa5I7bHFq9mnYK]] doubts both financial return and compact-exchanger effectiveness in the existing setup. scotty79 [[./thread#^c34656460|scotty79]] notes that exhaust already occurs but integrating suitable exchangers may be difficult. No matched energy measurements settle the dispute.
- NO2 evidence is sparse: 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c34653924|5e92cb50239222b]] supplies local concentration figures but no gas-monitor method, household intervention results, or NO2-specific health attribution. lstodd's low CO2 readings beside a highway [[./thread#^c34648909|lstodd]] cannot establish low traffic-pollution exposure. Cooking discussions such as twawaaay's [[./thread#^c34648243|twawaaay]] likewise provide no NO2 measurements or removal comparisons.
- Wildfire evidence supports perceived usefulness of filtration but leaves effectiveness largely unquantified. car [[./thread#^c34649571|car]] reports improved readings during forest-fire pollution, and bryceacc [[./thread#^c34648662|bryceacc]] reports monitoring fire-related particles. This part supplies no detailed wildfire exposure series, verified purifier reduction percentage, or comparison of ventilation schedules during smoke events.
- Plant and unconventional indoor CO2-removal proposals lack comparable home-scale validation. 2devnull [[./thread#^c34655998|2devnull]] asserts possible 10–25\% reductions without a method, while JohnBooty [[./thread#^c34651836|JohnBooty]] estimates thousands of ferns and acknowledges uncertain sources. scotty79's oxygen-concentrator experiment [[./thread#^c34656510|scotty79]] lowered a garbage bag's sensor reading, but does not establish occupied-room capacity, operating cost, or a practical home system.
- Sleep-related ventilation has competing constraints without a demonstrated universal solution. twawaaay [[./thread#^c34648243|twawaaay]] favors open doors or windows and a cold bedroom; flandish [[./thread#^c34648401|flandish]] prioritizes closed doors for fire protection; tayo42 [[./thread#^c34653039|tayo42]] cannot tolerate outside noise. The discussion does not establish which arrangement best balances sleep, ventilation, temperature, and safety in a particular home.
- Claims about shared spaces remain thinner than household evidence. Terretta [[./thread#^c34660532|Terretta]] says typical gyms have lower CO2 than office meetings but supplies no readings; readthenotes1 [[./thread#^c34651749|readthenotes1]] proposes leaving meetings above an unspecified threshold. danjc's car observation [[./thread#^c34657195|danjc]] is numerical but lacks occupancy and ventilation details, preventing broader comparison.
- The health benefit attributable to these particular improvements is not measured. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c34651674|ahaucnx]] cites low-PM health research, and 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c34653924|5e92cb50239222b]] reports local disease disparities, but neither supplies a before-and-after health study of the described home intervention. The opposing concern about filtration and childhood immunity from kerpotgh [[./thread#^c34651105|kerpotgh]] is likewise unsupported by outcome data here.
