# [Which houseplants should you buy to purify air? None of them](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=21552101)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants debate whether houseplants can meaningfully clean indoor air, considering the scale of air exchange compared with what plants can remove and whether engineered plants or circulating living walls could help. The thread also compares purifier costs and raises possible drawbacks such as pollen and mold.
Tags: houseplants, indoor air quality, air purifiers, VOCs, air filtration

## Relevance

The comments compare plants with air cleaners, discuss how ventilation limits the effect of plants, and raise practical questions about filter cost and possible pollen or mold tradeoffs. ## Arguments

- maxander [[./thread#^c21555949|maxander]] quotes Gall's categorical warning that a \$30 houseplant will not improve air quality, framed as advice to a low-income family considering a \$400 air cleaner. This is a quoted claim about practical effectiveness, without a household test or exposure measurements.
- Replying to Quequau's question about genetically improving plants [[./thread#^c21552222|Quequau]], notjtrig [[./thread#^c21555805|notjtrig]] argues that plants do purify air, but their small size and a claimed average indoor air replacement time of 80 minutes make their contribution negligible. The distinction is between biological activity and a meaningful room-level effect.
- notjtrig [[./thread#^c21555796|notjtrig]] reports that the paper hypothesizes an indoor living wall with air circulated through plants might work. They separately suggest investigating whether an algae CO2-capture system could reduce VOCs. Both are proposed approaches, without reported performance results.

## Counterarguments

- IXxXI [[./thread#^c21562528|IXxXI]] quotes an account of a 1989 NASA study describing toxin removal through plants' leaves, soil, and roots for space-station purification. This provides a study-based counterpoint to categorical dismissal, but the comment gives no quantified results establishing useful cleaning in ordinary homes.
- Replying directly to maxander's price objection [[./thread#^c21555949|maxander]], SyneRyder [[./thread#^c21556873|SyneRyder]] disputes \$400 as the necessary entry price by citing cheaper retail offerings. That challenges the affordability premise without establishing equivalent filtration performance.
- Replying to notjtrig's living-wall and algae proposals [[./thread#^c21555796|notjtrig]], dredmorbius [[./thread#^c21569909|dredmorbius]] asks whether added pollen and mold could outweigh the cleaning benefit. This is a possible adverse tradeoff, not a report of observed contamination.

## Assumptions

- maxander's suggestion that the purifier market needs disruption rests on describing an air cleaner as usually a fan blowing through activated charcoal [[./thread#^c21555949|maxander]]. The discussion supplies no device specifications, airflow measurements, or pollutant-removal comparisons to substantiate that simplification or establish what a cheaper substitute would accomplish.
- notjtrig's negligible-benefit argument depends on plant scale relative to air exchange [[./thread#^c21555805|notjtrig]]. The 80-minute figure is presented as an average without a source, building characteristics, or firsthand measurement; its applicability to a particular room remains untested.

## Conditions

The low-income household is hypothetical. Prices are expressed in US dollars, but no geographic market or household budget is established.

Neither provides concentrations, removal rates, or a direct comparison under the same conditions.
- Whether engineered plants or actively circulated biological systems could overcome ordinary houseplants' limitations remains open. - This unit supplies no PM2.5 or NO2 measurements, CO2 readings, sensor-reliability comparisons, or reported effects on health, cognition, sleep, or comfort. notjtrig's CO2 reference concerns a speculative treatment system [[./thread#^c21555796|notjtrig]], and IXxXI's NASA reference is a secondhand study claim rather than personal exposure evidence [[./thread#^c21562528|IXxXI]].
