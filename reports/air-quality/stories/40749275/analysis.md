# [Ask HN: What indoor CO2 monitor do you recommend?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40749275)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants recommend and compare indoor CO2 monitors, ranging from standalone commercial devices to DIY sensor boards integrated with home-automation and data-logging systems. They discuss sensor types and calibration, battery life, local versus cloud access, historical graphs, price, reliability, and whether monitoring helps guide ventilation habits.
Tags: CO2 monitors, indoor air quality, NDIR sensors, home automation, data logging, ventilation

## Relevance

Focuses on CO2 monitors and their reliability, including the distinction between direct NDIR measurements and estimated CO2 from VOC sensors. Firsthand accounts describe readings and ventilation responses, with reports of improved alertness but also practical tradeoffs and uncertainty about interpretation. [[./thread#^c40749857|agwa]] [[./thread#^c40749941|zerojames]] [[./thread#^c40750512|amluto]] [[./thread#^c40750680|radicality]] [[./thread#^c40750722|crazygringo]] [[./thread#^c40753555|troydavis]]

## Arguments

- Monitoring can guide ventilation while limiting heating and cooling losses. crazygringo [[./thread#^c40750722|crazygringo]] reports better thinking, mood, and productivity with lower CO2 or fresher air, and uses readings to decide how far to open windows. This is a personal account of usefulness, without controlled cognitive testing.
- Historical readings help identify patterns that an instantaneous display misses. crazygringo [[./thread#^c40750186|crazygringo]] describes CO2 falling during absences, rising sharply with oven use, and approaching an overnight steady state. In response to the suggestion that feelings suffice, danudey [[./thread#^c40751067|danudey]] argues that gradual acclimatization makes trends particularly useful.
- A monitor should measure the intended pollutant. amluto [[./thread#^c40750512|amluto]] recommends actual CO2 sensing, usually NDIR, and warns that some devices estimate a CO2 equivalent from TVOC with variable performance. Their comparison of separate CO2 and TVOC readings showed similar daily patterns but cleaner CO2 data; this is an observation, not a reference calibration.
- Controlled mechanical ventilation offers an intervention beyond monitoring. kkfx [[./thread#^c40751837|kkfx]] advocates exchanging indoor and outdoor air while recovering heat, using passive heat exchangers or active heat-pump systems according to circumstances. forbiddenlake [[./thread#^c40752538|forbiddenlake]] clarifies that VMC is the French initialism for controlled mechanical ventilation. The claimed advantages concern air exchange and thermal comfort; no before-and-after pollutant measurements accompany the recommendation.
- Sensor selection should reflect the exposure being investigated. danudey [[./thread#^c40750949|danudey]] values PM2.5 and PM10 monitoring because of Vancouver wildfire seasons, and proposes particulate sensors in occupied rooms with windows and VOC sensors near cooking or poorly ventilated cleaning areas. These proposed placements are informed preferences, not completed experiments demonstrating optimal placement.

## Counterarguments

- The need for monitoring is disputed. kkfx [[./thread#^c40750872|kkfx]] argues that feelings already provide enough information and prioritizes ventilation equipment. Replying to crazygringo's measurement-guided routine, moooo99 [[./thread#^c40766646|moooo99]] asks whether opening a window briefly once daily would suffice. Neither supplies a CO2 time series demonstrating that the proposed alternative maintains comparable conditions.
- AirGradient ownership exposed limitations that can make readings misleading. troydavis [[./thread#^c40753555|troydavis]] reports a rolling 24-hour VOC baseline that normalizes persistent sources, weekly CO2 calibration to the lowest observed value, and graphing that does not mark potentially baseline-changing reboots. Founder ahaucnx [[./thread#^c40784825|ahaucnx]] acknowledges the concerns and reports new firmware controls for VOC/NOx learning and CO2 calibration periods; the reboot issue remained under investigation.
- Open hardware does not guarantee straightforward operation or trustworthy software presentation. mtlynch [[./thread#^c40750789|mtlynch]] reports missing instructions, difficult source flashing, discarded numerical precision, and contribution work disrupted by an unannounced rewrite, while still saying they would probably buy again. Founder ahaucnx [[./thread#^c40784887|ahaucnx]] attributes problems to an open-source transition. Subsequent replies from mtlynch [[./thread#^c40801790|mtlynch]] and ahaucnx [[./thread#^c40828893|ahaucnx]] establish that flashing documentation still referenced a renamed file.
- Awair satisfaction conflicts with failure reports. mrsirduke [[./thread#^c40749781|mrsirduke]] and crazygringo [[./thread#^c40750186|crazygringo]] describe years of satisfactory use, whereas huevosabio [[./thread#^c40749827|huevosabio]] reports failure after roughly eighteen months. ailurooo [[./thread#^c40849997|ailurooo]] replies with a similar experience but provides no additional detail. These accounts establish differing outcomes, not a failure rate.
- Access and maintenance can undermine otherwise useful instruments. sebazzz [[./thread#^c40752115|sebazzz]] reports that retiring cloud support for an older Awair also removed local API access. radicality [[./thread#^c40750680|radicality]] describes uHoo's raw-data subscription and poor app despite functioning hardware. Replying to hedora's satisfactory integrated system, AtlasBarfed [[./thread#^c40750812|AtlasBarfed]] predicts eventual DIY maintenance; that prediction is broader than the failures actually documented here.

## Assumptions

- The proposed CO2 targets are personal operating preferences, not established health thresholds. crazygringo [[./thread#^c40750722|crazygringo]] opens windows above 650 ppm, opens them wider above 800, and closes them below 550. OutOfHere [[./thread#^c40750005|OutOfHere]] prefers never exceeding 770 ppm. OutOfHere's separate warning about horticultural alarm defaults [[./thread#^c40749972|OutOfHere]] also shows why a device's factory alert should not automatically be treated as a residential recommendation.
- Attributing benefits specifically to CO2 assumes that accompanying changes in air and circumstances are not responsible. crazygringo [[./thread#^c40750722|crazygringo]] explicitly allows that fresher air generally correlates with lower CO2 and identifies sleep or blood sugar as alternative explanations for brain fog. shagie's association between restless sleep and higher readings [[./thread#^c40749888|shagie]] cannot isolate causation. kkfx's description of ventilation as providing more oxygenated air [[./thread#^c40750872|kkfx]] is unsupported by oxygen measurements.
- Automatic baseline correction assumes that the minimum encountered is a valid reference. troydavis [[./thread#^c40753555|troydavis]] says AirGradient treated the weekly minimum as approximately 400 ppm, creating drift if sufficiently fresh air was never encountered. Extending that interval, as founder ahaucnx [[./thread#^c40784825|ahaucnx]] reports, does not itself demonstrate that the reference assumption holds. Likewise, a much longer VOC learning period does not establish an absolute concentration measurement.
- A CO2 reading or simple good/bad indicator cannot be assumed to characterize every exposure discussed. amluto [[./thread#^c40750512|amluto]] distinguishes CO2 from TVOC and NOx sensing, while danudey [[./thread#^c40750949|danudey]] selects particulate monitoring for wildfire concerns. kingsloi's praise for Birdie's easy visual indication [[./thread#^c40750739|kingsloi]] establishes usability, without showing coverage of PM2.5, NO2, or other pollutants.
- The AC advice depends on an unspecified airflow mechanism. OutOfHere [[./thread#^c40750005|OutOfHere]] claims that a slightly open window can preserve most cooling while slowing CO2 buildup, and that Energy Saver mode worsens CO2. Elsewhere, kkfx [[./thread#^c40752841|kkfx]] distinguishes ordinary recirculating air conditioning from outdoor-air exchange. The discussion supplies no installation details or measurements establishing a general relationship between AC operating mode and CO2.

## Conditions

- Outdoor temperature constrains window opening. whalesalad [[./thread#^c40749989|whalesalad]] reports 1500 ppm and anticipates admitting heat when opening windows. crazygringo [[./thread#^c40750722|crazygringo]] similarly balances fresh air against wasted heating or cooling. OutOfHere's window-cracking suggestion [[./thread#^c40750005|OutOfHere]] is a proposed compromise, with no measured cooling loss or ventilation rate.
- Wildfire conditions qualify advice to open windows. danudey [[./thread#^c40750949|danudey]] specifically wants particulate monitoring in Vancouver's windowed living spaces because of wildfire seasons. This suggests a possible tradeoff between reducing indoor CO2 and admitting outdoor smoke, but no window-opening smoke experiment is reported. phinnaeus [[./thread#^c40749800|phinnaeus]] owns indoor and outdoor monitors but supplies neither paired readings nor settled installation positions.
- Prices and advertised savings are disputed and time-specific. agwa [[./thread#^c40749857|agwa]] describes an Aranet promotion as 40\% off; hipadev23 [[./thread#^c40750740|hipadev23]] counters that its longstanding Amazon price was about \$165, making the effective discount closer to 10\%. leemck [[./thread#^c40753759|leemck]] describes an AZ-0001 available below \$200. These are June 2024 purchasing accounts, not current quotations.
- DIY can lower purchase costs while requiring assembly and integration effort. tomek\_ycomb [[./thread#^c40749820|tomek\_ycomb]] reports saving money with an AirGradient kit, and ds\_opseeker [[./thread#^c40750031|ds\_opseeker]] assembled an ESP32 setup from purchased boards and separately ordered components. sofixa [[./thread#^c40750076|sofixa]] describes Apollo Automation as comparatively affordable and entirely local, without supplying a price comparison or accuracy comparison.
- Mechanical ventilation choices depend on the building and climate. kkfx [[./thread#^c40751837|kkfx]] distinguishes passive heat recovery from active conditioning and describes additional heating or cooling needs outside mild conditions. In a follow-up, kkfx [[./thread#^c40752841|kkfx]] identifies duct installation as a retrofit constraint and characterizes distributed wall units as a costlier, less effective alternative. Those comparative claims are not supported by measurements in the export.
- House layout and existing integrations affect practical suitability. hedora [[./thread#^c40750027|hedora]] reports satisfactory Ambient Weather operation but notes that sensors must reach the base station, limiting coverage across buildings. mrweasel [[./thread#^c40750058|mrweasel]] is considering Eve Room for HomeKit compatibility but has not purchased it. sofixa [[./thread#^c40750076|sofixa]] contrasts local Apollo operation with a Netatmo setup requiring a cloud service.

## Lived experiences

- Aranet owners provide substantial usability and longevity testimony, but little metrology. agwa [[./thread#^c40749857|agwa]] owns two, reads them through Python over Bluetooth, and praises the e-ink display and battery life. zerojames [[./thread#^c40749941|zerojames]] reports at least a year on one charge; joeyagreco [[./thread#^c40749988|joeyagreco]] reports three years of ownership. EA-3167 [[./thread#^c40750330|EA-3167]] describes trouble-free use and apparently accurate readings, without a reference comparison.
- Several users demonstrate practical access to historical data. Responding to a cloud-dependency question, agwa [[./thread#^c40751019|agwa]] says the Aranet home version uses Bluetooth and a documented protocol; jdoss [[./thread#^c40750775|jdoss]] reports that the linked Python code worked immediately. throwaranay4933 [[./thread#^c40751438|throwaranay4933]] points to working Linux/Go and Prometheus options. mambo\_giro [[./thread#^c40750534|mambo\_giro]] reports multiple OpenCO2 devices integrated with Homebridge for monitoring and notifications.
- Positive AirGradient experiences coexist with the detailed complaints. phinnaeus [[./thread#^c40749800|phinnaeus]] is satisfied after several months with an indoor/outdoor pair. tomek\_ycomb [[./thread#^c40749820|tomek\_ycomb]] describes giving a unit to a friend whose house uses a wood stove and building another from a kit. ds\_opseeker [[./thread#^c40750031|ds\_opseeker]] reports a successful component-based build. None gives measured wood-smoke concentrations or an intervention result.
- shagie [[./thread#^c40749888|shagie]] reports sometimes waking from restless sleep to a higher CO2 reading, particularly when the house is more closed during winter, and using history to notice changes after cooking. Their kitchen has additional air monitoring, and their Mila purifier/monitor units worked for a period before unspecified problems. The comment gives no sleep intervention, concentration values, or quantified filtration performance.
- radicality [[./thread#^c40750680|radicality]] reports seeing quick changes during gas-stove use and window opening after switching to an SCD41/AtomS3 system with five-second reporting, Home Assistant, InfluxDB, and Grafana. Their previous uHoo reported at one-minute intervals. No concentrations, change magnitudes, NO2 measurements, or independent response-time tests are supplied.
- whalesalad [[./thread#^c40749946|whalesalad]] reports years of satisfactory use of an Autopilot desktop CO2 monitor and subsequently gives a contemporaneous reading of 1500 ppm [[./thread#^c40749989|whalesalad]]. This is a concrete concentration snapshot. The comment expresses an intention to open windows despite the heat, without reporting the resulting concentration.
- leemck [[./thread#^c40753759|leemck]] reports that nearby breathing noticeably influences AZ-0001 readings on a workbench and recommends keeping outdoor measurements at least eight feet from passing people. They also report manual restart requirements after power interruption and abandoning another device because of its humidity measurement. The stated 0–2000 ppm range and 30 or 50 ppm accuracy are product specifications, not demonstrated field accuracy.
- kkfx [[./thread#^c40751837|kkfx]] describes an active ventilation system in a five-year-old Alpine home, reporting roughly 10 kWh/day as sufficient for much of spring and autumn, with a minisplit supplement in summer and a water-to-water heat pump in winter. Their 1–2 kWh/day figure for passive ventilation is a general comparison, not a reported measurement from a second installed system. Neither system is accompanied by CO2 or PM readings.
- Multiple commenters report functioning DIY CO2 systems: huseyinkeles [[./thread#^c40749809|huseyinkeles]] uses MH-Z19, ESP32, ESPHome, and Home Assistant; londons\_explore [[./thread#^c40750165|londons\_explore]] sends MH-Z19 readings to Prometheus; anfractuosity [[./thread#^c40749887|anfractuosity]] uses an SCD30 with a Raspberry Pi and Grafana; ashish01 [[./thread#^c40750956|ashish01]] built two SCD41/ESP32 devices. taf2 [[./thread#^c40749644|taf2]] describes a Pico clock/sensor build. These establish successful projects rather than comparative sensor accuracy.
- Airthings owners emphasize broader monitoring and access. sschueller [[./thread#^c40749812|sschueller]] likes View Plus, including its radon capability; xnx [[./thread#^c40749859|xnx]] praises history and export; sliken [[./thread#^c40752891|sliken]] reports reliable gateway connections from devices across three floors; pawelduda [[./thread#^c40750725|pawelduda]] reports two years with Wave Plus. azamba [[./thread#^c40750012|azamba]] qualifies their endorsement on not needing customer support, without detailing a support incident.
- Other recommendations concern convenience more than measured performance. kingsloi [[./thread#^c40750739|kingsloi]] likes Birdie's conspicuous living-room indication. danudey [[./thread#^c40750949|danudey]] praises Eve Room's display and battery life and owns a Qingping particulate monitor. zeke\_the\_cat [[./thread#^c40750209|zeke\_the\_cat]] calls a GQ Electronics GMC-520 reliable but supplies no CO2-specific sensing method or results. Their inclusion in a CO2 discussion does not itself verify CO2 capability.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The size and cause of health, cognition, mood, and sleep effects remain unresolved. crazygringo [[./thread#^c40750722|crazygringo]] and shagie [[./thread#^c40749888|shagie]] provide subjective associations, without controlled comparisons or clinical outcomes. zefhous [[./thread#^c40752428|zefhous]] links a talk said to cite CO2 and VOC studies, while blankx32 [[./thread#^c40750242|blankx32]] points to another discussion. Neither supplies those underlying findings in this source unit, so they cannot establish effect sizes or thresholds here.
- The discussion does not settle how much ventilation is necessary or whether a sensor materially outperforms a fixed airing routine. moooo99 [[./thread#^c40766646|moooo99]] proposes a few minutes once daily, while crazygringo [[./thread#^c40750722|crazygringo]] adjusts windows several times daily using readings. Room volume, occupancy, air-exchange rates, and matched comparisons are absent; neither routine can be generalized from these accounts.
- NO2 and traffic exposure are not characterized in this unit. amluto [[./thread#^c40750512|amluto]] discusses a separate TVOC/NOx sensor, and founder ahaucnx [[./thread#^c40784825|ahaucnx]] discusses its adjustable learning behavior; neither reports a calibrated NO2 concentration. radicality's gas-stove observations [[./thread#^c40750680|radicality]] concern CO2 monitoring and cannot establish concurrent NO2 or particulate exposure.
- There are no numerical PM2.5 results demonstrating wildfire ingress, cooking-particle exposure, or purifier effectiveness. danudey [[./thread#^c40750949|danudey]] explains why wildfire monitoring matters, phinnaeus [[./thread#^c40749800|phinnaeus]] has indoor/outdoor instruments, and shagie [[./thread#^c40749888|shagie]] has used purifier/monitor combinations. These accounts do not establish how much filtration helps, when opening windows worsens particle exposure, or whether any described purifier changes CO2.
- Comparative sensor accuracy remains largely untested. EA-3167's apparent Aranet accuracy [[./thread#^c40750330|EA-3167]] and leemck's quoted AZ-0001 specifications [[./thread#^c40753759|leemck]] are different kinds of evidence, neither a controlled comparison. radicality's preference for SCD41 over SCD40 [[./thread#^c40751048|radicality]] is not backed by test results, and their faster reporting configuration [[./thread#^c40750680|radicality]] does not by itself demonstrate greater accuracy or faster physical sensor response.
- AirGradient's follow-up resolves some product-history questions while leaving measurement consequences uncertain. Founder ahaucnx [[./thread#^c40784825|ahaucnx]] acknowledges troydavis's concerns [[./thread#^c40753555|troydavis]] and reports implemented configuration options, but the export contains no subsequent user validation that persistent-source behavior or CO2 drift was corrected under the original conditions. Reboot-related baseline continuity was explicitly still being investigated.
