# [Ask HN: Should I stop using air purifiers?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=31790910)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

A person asks whether to stop using three high-maintenance air purifiers after noticing allergy symptoms when filters go unchanged for a short time, and says ventilation and cleaning feel easier. Replies suggest reducing the number of units or running them only when needed, placing filtration near occupied areas, using larger filters, or building a box-fan filter. Others discuss ducted filtration, mold, outdoor allergens, and whether symptoms indicate effective filtration. The exchange includes mixed experiences with DIY designs and the tradeoff between cleaner air and filter upkeep.
Tags: air purifiers, indoor air quality, allergies, filters, ventilation, DIY

## Arguments

- h2odragon [[./thread#^c31791146|h2odragon]] suggests concentrating filtration near the bed or desk and running it during occupancy to reduce maintenance. bckr [[./thread#^c31791096|bckr]] proposes replacing three units with one larger unit or operating them only when air quality is poor. These are suggestions without reported before-and-after results.
- h2odragon [[./thread#^c31791146|h2odragon]] argues that greater filter area and thickness could lengthen replacement intervals and proposes a homemade filter-and-fan assembly. Replying to that suggestion, alister [[./thread#^c31796735|alister]] describes a Corsi–Rosenthal box using four 20×20-inch filters, characterized as HEPA in the comment, and a box fan.
- DantesKite [[./thread#^c31791796|DantesKite]] recommends checking the home's ventilation-system maintenance. moistly [[./thread#^c31793745|moistly]] proposes duct-mounted filtration and supplies an example of an existing installation, making central filtration another option where suitable ductwork exists.
- b20000 [[./thread#^c31791688|b20000]] advocates cleaning and ventilation, with moving as a further possibility. The comment offers no personal measurements or comparison showing when these alternatives would adequately replace filtration.

## Counterarguments

- fuzzfactor [[./thread#^c31800843|fuzzfactor]] argues that some air requires substantial maintenance effort to keep clean. They suggest sensitivity to spent filter media could indicate that filtration has captured substantial contamination; the comment does not establish what caused the reported symptoms.
- The enthusiastic DIY recommendations encounter a contrary firsthand account: replying to DantesKite's suggestion, muzani [[./thread#^c31795285|muzani]] describes their homemade HEPA setup as only moderately useful and not much better than an inexpensive assembled purifier. This limits confidence in alister's [[./thread#^c31796735|alister]] remembered claim of superiority over most commercial units.
- fuzzfactor [[./thread#^c31800843|fuzzfactor]] challenges any blanket preference for outdoor ventilation: outdoor pollen can be better or worse for a particular person than accumulated indoor pollutants. Opening windows therefore has a plausible exposure tradeoff within this discussion, although nobody reports measuring it.

## Assumptions

- h2odragon [[./thread#^c31791146|h2odragon]] conditionally treats the absence of allergy reactions without purifiers as evidence that they provide no benefit. That uses symptoms as the effectiveness criterion; the discussion does not establish whether symptom relief captures all relevant exposure or health effects.
- The opening post's explanation that aging filters re-release particles is an untested causal attribution. h2odragon [[./thread#^c31791146|h2odragon]] and fuzzfactor [[./thread#^c31800843|fuzzfactor]] offer different interpretations of the complaint, but neither reports inspecting the devices, measuring emissions, or establishing the mechanism.
- The favorable DIY evidence is secondhand: DantesKite [[./thread#^c31791796|DantesKite]] recalls a video, and alister [[./thread#^c31796735|alister]] recalls an HN experimental comparison. WalterGR [[./thread#^c31796409|WalterGR]], replying to DantesKite, supplies search strategies for earlier submissions. None supplies experimental readings or methods in this source unit.
- DantesKite [[./thread#^c31791796|DantesKite]] assumes household ventilation systems should have HEPA filters and proposes leaving water near a wall as a mold test. The comment provides neither system-specific compatibility information nor validation that this test identifies a consequential indoor mold problem.

## Conditions

- Individual sensitivity and season matter: fuzzfactor [[./thread#^c31800843|fuzzfactor]] says the balance between outdoor allergens and indoor accumulations can change substantially throughout the year. No location or outdoor pollutant measurements are supplied.
- Temperature can constrain ventilation. bckr [[./thread#^c31791096|bckr]] suggests using filtration at night when keeping windows open is too cold; this is a proposed comfort tradeoff, not a report of improved sleep or measured exposure.
- Noise may constrain DIY filtration. alister [[./thread#^c31796735|alister]] calls a typical box fan noisy even at its lowest setting, while h2odragon [[./thread#^c31791146|h2odragon]] suggests a speed controller for quieter operation. Neither quantifies noise or the filtration achieved at reduced speed.
- Humidity could complicate longer replacement intervals: h2odragon [[./thread#^c31791146|h2odragon]] warns that keeping larger filters longer also provides more time for material to grow in humid conditions. This is a proposed risk, not documented growth in the original poster's devices.
- Financial and housing circumstances limit the available choices. muzani [[./thread#^c31795285|muzani]] explicitly says poverty motivated their DIY attempt; moistly's [[./thread#^c31793745|moistly]] central-filter approach requires ductwork; and b20000 [[./thread#^c31791688|b20000]] suggests relocation without addressing its feasibility or cost. No actual prices or operating costs are given.

## Lived experiences

- The opening-post account answered by h2odragon [[./thread#^c31791146|h2odragon]] describes roughly a year using three purifiers, recurring stuffiness unless filters were replaced within about two months, and finding increased cleaning and ventilation less annoying. The symptoms and maintenance burden are self-reported; particle re-release is the author's explanation, not a measured finding.
- muzani [[./thread#^c31795285|muzani]] reports building a DIY HEPA filter because money was tight, finding it only somewhat effective, and experiencing substantially more relief after removing what they call 'moss.' They also mention 'antihistamine (Vitamin E) shots.' The treatment identity is unclear, and the account cannot separate the contributions of removal, filtration, and treatment.
- moistly [[./thread#^c31793745|moistly]] reports an always-on circulation blower with a deeply pleated filter estimated to contain over 40 square feet of material. They replace it annually while acknowledging that replacement should be more frequent. This establishes a reported configuration and routine, not an effective or safe replacement interval for other homes.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Whether stopping filtration would improve the original poster's situation remains unresolved. h2odragon [[./thread#^c31791146|h2odragon]] makes benefit conditional on symptoms without the devices, while fuzzfactor [[./thread#^c31800843|fuzzfactor]] emphasizes possible necessity and successful capture. There is no follow-up comparison of clean filters, aged filters, ventilation, and cleaning.
- Comparative efficacy and maintenance requirements remain uncertain. alister [[./thread#^c31796735|alister]] recalls strong DIY performance, muzani [[./thread#^c31795285|muzani]] reports limited benefit, and moistly [[./thread#^c31793745|moistly]] describes a much larger central installation. Different equipment and unspecified room conditions prevent a direct comparison or a general conclusion about purifier design.
- This source unit supplies no PM2.5, NO2, or CO2 readings, sensor models, calibration information, or measured removal rates. moistly's [[./thread#^c31793745|moistly]] filter-area estimate and alister's [[./thread#^c31796735|alister]] construction dimensions describe hardware rather than air quality. The discussion cannot establish pollutant-specific effectiveness or sensor reliability.
- No cognitive outcomes, measured sleep changes, or cooking-, traffic-, or wildfire-related exposure experiences are reported. h2odragon's [[./thread#^c31791146|h2odragon]] bedside placement and bckr's [[./thread#^c31791096|bckr]] nighttime operation are suggestions, not sleep findings. fuzzfactor's [[./thread#^c31800843|fuzzfactor]] outdoor-allergen caveat leaves the effect of opening windows conditional; it does not resolve those other outdoor-exposure scenarios.
