# [Scientist says cleaning indoor air could make us healthier and smarter](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=28082348)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants debate claims that cleaner indoor air can improve health and cognitive performance, asking how strong the evidence is and whether the article overstates findings from small or limited studies. The conversation distinguishes CO2, VOCs, particulates, and outdoor pollution, and considers when ventilation, filtration, or reducing indoor pollution sources is appropriate. Practical discussion covers opening windows, mechanical ventilation with heat recovery, air purifiers and DIY filters, range hoods, carpets, dust, humidity, radon, sensors, calibration, and measurement accuracy. The thread also explores whether cleaner indoor environments could affect allergy development, and broadens into disagreements about car pollution, tire and brake wear, car dependence, rural living, personal responsibility, and the limits of individual lifestyle changes.
Tags: indoor air quality, ventilation, air filtration, CO2 and cognition, health research, air quality sensors, transportation pollution, allergies

## Relevance

The comments discuss indoor PM, CO2 and VOC exposure; firsthand sensor readings and filtration results; ventilation and window-opening tradeoffs during wildfire smoke or poor outdoor air; and reported effects on allergies, comfort and cognition. They also challenge the strength and limits of the evidence, including small study samples, conflicting CO2 findings and sensor calibration concerns. ## Arguments

- The central practical distinction is between removing particles and replacing stale air. hollerith states that HEPA removes particulates, not VOCs or CO2 [[./thread#^c28083103|hollerith]]. gilgoomesh interprets the cited research as supporting greater outdoor-air exchange when outdoor air is suitable, rather than demonstrating that a recirculating purifier solves every problem [[./thread#^c28084295|gilgoomesh]]. These comments support matching an intervention to the pollutant actually measured.
- There is a substantive experimental argument for effects on cognitive performance. IntrepidWorm describes 24 volunteers spending six working days in controlled offices with altered ventilation, CO2 and VOC levels, followed by double-blind analytical and crisis-response tests [[./thread#^c28082817|IntrepidWorm]]. adamjb quotes reported cognitive-score increases of 61\% and 101\%, with p \< 0.0001 [[./thread#^c28082585|adamjb]]. These are reported differences on particular tasks, not evidence that filtration increases general intelligence by those percentages.
- Classroom research supplies another argument for intervention, although the supplied material contains commenters' descriptions rather than a complete assessment of the studies. barathr identifies the school-filter example as Porter Ranch following a gas leak and links a study reporting improved scores, while noting that ordinary purifiers would not meaningfully filter the leaking gas itself [[./thread#^c28082616|barathr]]. whalabi quotes an estimated 0.2-standard-deviation test-score increase per \$1,000 expenditure and a potential benefit for disadvantaged students attending polluted schools [[./thread#^c28085651|whalabi]].
- Participants invoke health effects extending beyond immediate discomfort. mensetmanusman supplies links associating air pollution with Alzheimer's disease, cancers, fertility problems, autism and mood disorders [[./thread#^c28082590|mensetmanusman]]. goodpoint similarly invokes research on CO2, VOCs, particles and health [[./thread#^c28086247|goodpoint]]. These are arguments based on reported associations; the supplied comments do not demonstrate that a particular household purifier prevents those diseases.
- Filtered mechanical ventilation is presented as a way to address particle ingress and indoor CO2 together. throw0101a recommends supplying filtered outdoor air to bedrooms and extracting from kitchens and bathrooms, citing ventilation standards and HRV/ERV arrangements [[./thread#^c28085542|throw0101a]]. room271 advocates continuous filtered ventilation with heat recovery, particularly during colder months, while acknowledging retrofit difficulties [[./thread#^c28085108|room271]]. This differs from simply recirculating indoor air through a filter.
- Source control receives substantial support. colechristensen identifies cooking, especially burning food, as a major particle source in personal measurements and argues for effective extraction [[./thread#^c28083051|colechristensen]]. MengerSponge recommends a range hood or stronger kitchen ventilation in response to measured cooking-related VOC increases [[./thread#^c28083147|MengerSponge]]. forgotmypw17 suggests reducing emissions from cleaning products and new furniture [[./thread#^c28082752|forgotmypw17]], while ZeroGravitas also identifies domestic combustion, including log fires, as a source [[./thread#^c28084709|ZeroGravitas]].
- Filter performance depends on the volume of air cleaned as well as single-pass capture. bsilvereagle recommends sizing equipment using CADR, air changes and room requirements [[./thread#^c28083620|bsilvereagle]]. blagie explains that lower capture efficiency can be offset by greater airflow [[./thread#^c28086763|blagie]]. colechristensen adds the qualification that ongoing particle generation and infiltration affect the eventual concentration, so repeated circulation alone does not guarantee identical results across systems [[./thread#^c28083224|colechristensen]].
- Traffic-related particulate pollution cannot be reduced to exhaust alone. kieranmaine quotes a UK government report attributing 60\% of primary road-transport PM2.5 and 73\% of road-transport PM10 to brake, tyre and road wear, corresponding to 7.4\% and 8.5\% of all UK primary emissions respectively [[./thread#^c28084988|kieranmaine]]. The quotation explicitly describes non-exhaust estimates as highly uncertain. These are source-specific mass fractions, not percentages of all pollution exposure or health damage.
- Several commenters distinguish avoiding combustion and industrial particles from avoiding all biological exposure. gpm argues that the small particles of concern principally come from sources such as vehicles and power generation, rather than microbes [[./thread#^c28082924|gpm]]. legulere cites research connecting pollution with allergies while distinguishing that exposure from playing with animals or soil [[./thread#^c28083645|legulere]]. Their argument supports clean indoor air alongside outdoor activity, rather than treating all forms of environmental exposure as interchangeable.

## Counterarguments

- The article's framing and commercial incentives attract criticism. cenophor objects to anecdotes, causal claims drawn from correlations and possible filtration-industry influence [[./thread#^c28082861|cenophor]]. oneplane questions whether the headline and biographical material adequately establish the underlying evidence [[./thread#^c28082656|oneplane]]. However, these comments do not establish fraud or demonstrate that the reported experiments were invalid; fighterpilot specifically replies that the cited study involved a double-blind intervention, not merely observational correlation [[./thread#^c28082995|fighterpilot]].
- Even commenters sympathetic to the experimental evidence identify important limitations. criticaltinker highlights the small participant count, possible effects carrying over between consecutive days, variability in uncontrolled factors and the need for real-office confirmation [[./thread#^c28083109|criticaltinker]]. fighterpilot argues that repeated testing adds information but agrees that larger samples and replication are needed [[./thread#^c28083159|fighterpilot]], subsequently acknowledging uncertainty about the number of tests [[./thread#^c28083481|fighterpilot]]. Repeated observations should not be presented as additional independent participants.
- The cognitive interpretation is explicitly disputed. learc83 invokes submarine studies and operational experience allegedly showing no cognitive effects at much higher CO2 concentrations [[./thread#^c28085869|learc83]]; maceurt makes a similar claim about military testing [[./thread#^c28151232|maceurt]]. These comments provide no detailed results sufficient to reconcile their claims with the office experiments. They nevertheless prevent representing the discussion as agreement about impairment at ordinary indoor concentrations.
- Calling a moderately elevated CO2 reading acutely toxic overstates what the discussion establishes. SomaticPirate reports 800 ppm in an office and describes the air as toxic [[./thread#^c28083222|SomaticPirate]]; Johnny555 directly distinguishes that CO2 reading from the danger of 800 ppm carbon monoxide [[./thread#^c28083549|Johnny555]]. breput subsequently accepts that 800 ppm might not impair performance while maintaining that higher readings correlate with personal symptoms [[./thread#^c28084123|breput]].
- Opening windows is conditional advice. divbzero reports sharp improvements from an outdoor breeze during ordinary conditions but bought filters and detectors for wildfire smoke [[./thread#^c28082951|divbzero]]. breput warns that smoke from upwind fires can make closed windows and recirculating filtration preferable [[./thread#^c28084344|breput]]. Neither account supports keeping windows permanently open or permanently closed regardless of outdoor conditions.
- Moving to the countryside is not a reliable substitute for assessing exposure. ajuc says rural winter air can be worse than urban air in Central and Eastern Europe because of household coal heating [[./thread#^c28085288|ajuc]]. oakfr identifies agricultural chemicals and secondary ozone as rural concerns [[./thread#^c28086338|oakfr]], and cableshaft notes transported smoke and ozone [[./thread#^c28086406|cableshaft]]. These are contextual objections to raxxorrax's proposed rural solution, not measurements establishing that all rural areas are worse [[./thread#^c28084098|raxxorrax]].
- Both claims that EVs solve particulate pollution and claims that they offer no local-air benefit are challenged. SideburnsOfDoom points out that exhaust filters cannot eliminate tyre and other non-exhaust emissions [[./thread#^c28085451|SideburnsOfDoom]]. Conversely, gambiting describes an older diesel taxi idling outside the home as a local pollution source that an EV would remove [[./thread#^c28085315|gambiting]]. Regenerative-braking experiences support a possible reduction in brake wear, but do not establish the net particulate balance for every vehicle.
- Early exposure does not guarantee freedom from allergies. beachy invokes studies associating farm childhoods with less asthma [[./thread#^c28083131|beachy]], but ch4s3 reports severe allergies and asthma despite growing up with farm animals and extensive outdoor exposure [[./thread#^c28083277|ch4s3]]. feanaro describes a spouse developing pet allergies at 15 after years of exposure [[./thread#^c28084282|feanaro]]. These anecdotes challenge categorical promises of protection without disproving a population-level association.
- Universal claims for cheap DIY purifiers encounter practical and evidentiary objections. jjeaff says a box fan plus filter can match or exceed expensive equipment [[./thread#^c28082651|jjeaff]], whereas Johnny555 reports weak airflow through a DIY filter and better airflow from a \$200 commercial purifier [[./thread#^c28083572|Johnny555]]. dynm also challenges ABCLAW's proposed twofold improvement based on visual inspection, saying personal experiments did not support that inference [[./thread#^c28082765|dynm]]. The disagreement concerns particular designs, not whether any DIY filtration can work.
- Ionization is a disputed feature rather than an unqualified improvement. eurasiantiger distinguishes ionizers from ozone generators and claims low-emission standards apply [[./thread#^c28083682|eurasiantiger]]. dragonwriter responds with an EPA citation asserting that ionizing cleaners can still generate ozone and disputing the claimed regulatory coverage [[./thread#^c28083859|dragonwriter]]. The supplied exchange contains no emissions measurements for a specific product, so neither its safety nor its ozone output is established.

## Assumptions

- A single air-quality number is not a complete exposure assessment. mensetmanusman emphasizes that particles with the same PM2.5 designation can have different compositions [[./thread#^c28083194|mensetmanusman]]. piyh distinguishes visible carpet debris from smaller airborne particles [[./thread#^c28082969|piyh]], and cenophor cautions that visibility does not determine harmfulness [[./thread#^c28082861|cenophor]]. Consequently, visible dust, smell, PM readings and gas concentrations cannot be treated as interchangeable evidence.
- Filter labels require interpretation. colechristensen warns about loose HEPA marketing and recommends looking for numerical capture specifications, but also claims MPR 2800 furnace filters outperform HEPA without supplying comparative test results in the comment [[./thread#^c28083085|colechristensen]]. hourislate describes MERV 12 and MERV 13 filters as HEPA [[./thread#^c28083384|hourislate]]. Those descriptions do not independently establish certification, particle-size performance or installed airflow.
- CO2 monitoring assumes that the device measures CO2 rather than estimates it from another signal. jfim warns that inexpensive eCO2 devices can be substantially wrong because they approximate concentration [[./thread#^c28083365|jfim]]. This distinction matters when interpreting an apparent ventilation problem: a displayed CO2-equivalent value is not automatically comparable with a direct CO2 reading.
- Automatic calibration assumes exposure to an appropriate baseline. Youden says some sensors assume regular fresh-air exposure and can therefore behave poorly in continuously occupied residential rooms; the suggested alternatives are disabling automatic calibration and periodically calibrating manually, or providing the expected fresh-air exposure [[./thread#^c28085493|Youden]]. Youden reports satisfactory SCD30 operation with automatic calibration disabled. These are model-dependent operating considerations, not independently verified instructions for every sensor.
- Apparent sensor variability may include electronics and sampling effects. jfim reports noisy SenseAir S8 output with a switching supply and discusses using steadier regulated power [[./thread#^c28083267|jfim]]. breput notes that the CO2 sensor shared a supply with a servo and LCD [[./thread#^c28083979|breput]]. bruce343434 suggests uneven mixing and averaging [[./thread#^c28085749|bruce343434]]. These are possible explanations for fluctuations; averaging a trace would not by itself establish absolute accuracy.
- A response test is weaker than calibration, and the reported units need care. porknubbins treats a match-induced jump as evidence that a PM sensor works, but later describes its baseline as 12 ppm PM2.5 [[./thread#^c28104379|porknubbins]]. eurasiantiger had explicitly questioned measurement accuracy [[./thread#^c28083635|eurasiantiger]]. That account demonstrates sensitivity to smoke without establishing a calibrated particle-mass concentration; its numbers should not be directly compared with CO2 ppm or Johnny555's reported AQI values [[./thread#^c28083225|Johnny555]].
- A perceived association between stuffiness and a CO2 display does not isolate CO2 as the cause. breput reports noticing symptoms around 800–1000 ppm while acknowledging calibration uncertainty [[./thread#^c28082909|breput]]. koheripbal describes feeling different above 1000 ppm but says it is difficult to quantify [[./thread#^c28089794|koheripbal]]. The latter's explanation that morning downstairs spikes occur because CO2 is heavier than air is an interpretation of the observations, not a demonstrated airflow mechanism.
- The hygiene debate combines different kinds of exposure and evidence. londons\_explore asks whether cleaning-product exposure could explain effects attributed to excessive cleanliness [[./thread#^c28083130|londons\_explore]]. occz discusses early food-allergen exposure as a possible basis for broader extrapolation [[./thread#^c28085533|occz]]. criticaltinker cites a model of future RSV and influenza outbreaks following pandemic interventions [[./thread#^c28083043|criticaltinker]]. None of those comments supplies a direct trial showing that household particle filtration causes or prevents allergic disease.

## Conditions

- The window-opening decision depends on local, changing outdoor conditions. cableshaft reports an air-quality index moving from over 100 one day to below 50 the next, recommends considering trends and notes gaps in rural monitoring coverage [[./thread#^c28086406|cableshaft]]. ihattendorf questions whether relocation remains a solution if air quality subsequently changes [[./thread#^c28089273|ihattendorf]]. A location's rural, urban or historically clean label does not establish its current suitability for ventilation.
- Occupancy and actual airflow matter more than whether a window is nominally open. inter\_netuser quotes Shanghai classroom findings with substantial air exchange yet CO2 above 1000 ppm in 45\% of classrooms [[./thread#^c28083110|inter\_netuser]]. fighterpilot clarifies that these are occupied indoor measurements, not evidence for the earlier claim about outdoor city concentrations, and narrows the window-opening advice to a lightly occupied room [[./thread#^c28083175|fighterpilot]]. breput reports situations requiring fans despite an open window [[./thread#^c28084326|breput]].
- Temperature and humidity impose practical limits. throw0101a recommends considering heat or energy recovery when outdoor temperatures exceed 30°C or fall below 10°C, and separate humidity control when comfortable temperatures coexist with high relative humidity [[./thread#^c28085720|throw0101a]]. These are the commenter's purchasing guidelines, not measured universal cutoffs. koheripbal reports summer heat and humidity conflicting with ventilation and server operation [[./thread#^c28089794|koheripbal]]; XorNot emphasizes Australian winter heat loss and street-facing windows [[./thread#^c28083138|XorNot]].
- Retrofitting ventilation depends on space, building layout and budget. room271 describes MVHR as expensive and potentially difficult to install in an older home, often alongside airtightness and insulation work [[./thread#^c28085108|room271]]. elric reports using a DIY Zehnder package with flexible ducts [[./thread#^c28085039|elric]]. radu\_floricica describes saving for an approximately €2,000 combined ventilation, conditioning and purification unit, rather than reporting an installed result [[./thread#^c28084640|radu\_floricica]]. These accounts do not establish a universally affordable retrofit.
- Kitchen extraction is constrained by rental equipment, space and capture performance. groby\_b says some over-range microwaves do exhaust outdoors effectively, but better models cost more [[./thread#^c28083116|groby\_b]], and describes insufficient kitchen space for a separate hood and microwave [[./thread#^c28083150|groby\_b]]. SomaticPirate is willing to pay higher rent for genuine extraction [[./thread#^c28083191|SomaticPirate]]. MengerSponge notes that inadequate flow, distance from the source or cross-currents could defeat an otherwise outdoor-vented hood [[./thread#^c28108908|MengerSponge]].
- Higher-rated filtration must suit the blower and installation. ihattendorf warns that added resistance can damage systems not designed for it [[./thread#^c28089356|ihattendorf]]. throw0101a recommends checking MERV 16 compatibility and using deeper filters for greater surface area [[./thread#^c28085720|throw0101a]]. pkulak highlights loading from hair and pollen without prefiltration [[./thread#^c28083986|pkulak]]. These qualifications limit the usefulness of simply buying the highest-rated replacement filter.
- Costs include replacement media, airflow, noise and availability. jjeaff proposes a \$20 fan plus a filter [[./thread#^c28082651|jjeaff]], while pkulak argues that approximately \$150 commercial units may offer better long-term value through prefiltration and longer filter life [[./thread#^c28083963|pkulak]]. bcrosby95 reports using DIY units after wildfires because commercial purifiers were sold out [[./thread#^c28082954|bcrosby95]]. These are 2021 prices and purchasing experiences, not current quotes or a verified total-cost comparison.
- Monitoring has its own affordability and reliability tradeoffs. jfim estimates that a DIY monitor with suitable electronics skills can cost under \$100 [[./thread#^c28083365|jfim]]. andreyf identifies approximately \$200 and \$150 CO2 monitors based on a linked comparison paper and their sensor components [[./thread#^c28112725|andreyf]]. The recommendations are not firsthand side-by-side calibration results in this export, and a lower-cost device's usefulness depends on what it actually measures.
- Reducing traffic exposure through lifestyle or relocation depends on jobs, transport and income. wccrawford describes rural life with town five miles away and public transport requiring advance booking [[./thread#^c28085408|wccrawford]]. tester34 reports managing without a car in rural Eastern Europe through rail, cycling and suitable work, while noting that many acquaintances need cars for employment and bear substantial costs [[./thread#^c28084876|tester34]]. throwaway984393 cautions that reduced driving does not automatically produce additional public transport without funding [[./thread#^c28083207|throwaway984393]].
- Outdoor exercise is not automatically the better exposure or comfort choice. tartoran qualifies the proposed benefit by recommending routes with little traffic and more nature [[./thread#^c28083730|tartoran]]. Johnny555 reports choosing an indoor spin bike to avoid road-collision risk [[./thread#^c28083835|Johnny555]]. r00fus chooses early, low-traffic routes [[./thread#^c28084224|r00fus]], but later challenges the applicability of aggregate motor-vehicle crash statistics to cycling [[./thread#^c28091625|r00fus]]. The exchange does not quantify the combined pollution, exercise and injury tradeoff.

## Lived experiences

- Johnny555 reports the clearest numerical particle-filtration result: with outdoor AQI near 100, running a furnace blower through MERV 11 reduced indoor AQI from about 70 to below 10 in approximately 20 minutes, then to low single digits after another 20 minutes [[./thread#^c28083225|Johnny555]]. For a rough accuracy check, the Davis Airlink read within five AQI points of four nearby PurpleAir sensors outdoors; Johnny555 explicitly notes the absence of a nearby calibrated official monitor [[./thread#^c28083723|Johnny555]].
- porknubbins lived roughly 100 metres from a busy road and reported PM2.5 readings mostly in single digits or low teens, rising into the hundreds when a match was extinguished beside the sensor and then falling quickly [[./thread#^c28083338|porknubbins]]. This is a local sensor account, not evidence that most homes have little particulate exposure. The later unit description and lack of reference calibration limit quantitative interpretation [[./thread#^c28104379|porknubbins]].
- crazygringo reports that a \$100 HEPA purifier made no observable PM10/PM2.5 difference on ordinary days in a residential Brooklyn side street, where readings were already low [[./thread#^c28091081|crazygringo]]. During smoke reaching New York from wildfires, the purifier reportedly restored indoor readings to their usual level, substantially below outdoors. No numerical concentrations or independent calibration results are provided.
- cmurf describes 32 consecutive days of Denver air-pollution warnings involving ozone and smoke, with outdoor running or cycling followed by hours of coughing and itchy eyes [[./thread#^c28083142|cmurf]]. The reported consequence was reduced outdoor exercise. This is firsthand symptom reporting during a pollution episode, without measurements separating the effects of ozone from smoke particles.
- koheripbal describes a five-person household where downstairs CO2 rose as bedroom doors opened in the morning and sometimes took a couple of hours of open doors and windows to approach an approximately 420 ppm outdoor baseline [[./thread#^c28089794|koheripbal]]. A sizeable HVAC system did not prevent the buildup. Feeling worse above 1000 ppm was subjective, and adding plants produced no noticeable effect.
- breput reports approximately 18 months of home-office CO2 data from an MHZ-19B project, persistent uncertainty about calibration, perceived discomfort above roughly 800–1000 ppm and a preferred 400–600 ppm range [[./thread#^c28082909|breput]]. Opening a window often helped, but some conditions required forced ventilation; the proposed roles of pressure, temperature and wind were not established experimentally [[./thread#^c28084326|breput]].
- dbish found Awair Element readings useful for deciding when to open office or living-room doors and windows, estimating agreement within about 100 ppm against a quoted 75 ppm manufacturer specification [[./thread#^c28082636|dbish]] [[./thread#^c28082934|dbish]]. Separately, dbsmith83 discovered basement CO2 buildup after buying a monitor; an earlier illness resolved, but its cause and relationship to the house remained uncertain [[./thread#^c28087884|dbsmith83]].
- wirrbel reports difficulty staying awake in crowded office meeting rooms, unlike remote meetings, and a sense that mechanically conditioned office air was insufficiently fresh [[./thread#^c28083649|wirrbel]]. No CO2 readings accompany the account, so the experience raises a ventilation hypothesis without demonstrating the cause of drowsiness.
- elric reports no longer needing to open windows after installing filtered mechanical ventilation, with less pollen, fewer mosquitoes and a continuous sense of fresh air [[./thread#^c28084941|elric]]. The DIY system supplies living and sleeping areas and extracts from kitchen, bathroom and utility spaces. Expected winter heating savings were explicitly still untested [[./thread#^c28085039|elric]].
- binkHN reports large VOC increases on an Awair monitor during cooking, including oven use without frying or added oil [[./thread#^c28083100|binkHN]] [[./thread#^c28087170|binkHN]]. Replies establish that the appliances used gas and the range hood exhausted outdoors [[./thread#^c28083534|binkHN]]. However, binkHN had not compared hood-on with hood-off operation [[./thread#^c28083276|binkHN]], so the account does not measure how much exposure the hood prevented.
- stephen\_g reports that frying skin-on salmon on an induction hob smoked up the house, whereas using an air fryer produced almost no noticeable smoke and required less supervision [[./thread#^c28083487|stephen\_g]]. empiricus, who owns gas and induction equipment, reports faster, more uniform heating with induction at practical cooking temperatures [[./thread#^c28084608|empiricus]]. These are cooking and comfort observations, not calibrated pollutant comparisons between appliance types.
- 7thaccount reports a substantial allergy improvement after upgrading household filtration and adding portable HEPA units, including months without allergy medication [[./thread#^c28082618|7thaccount]]. A follow-up describes a sub-\$100 Holmes tower, added filters, half-speed operation, prefilter cleaning and filter replacement around every six months [[./thread#^c28083200|7thaccount]]. The experience supports a personal benefit but does not isolate the contribution of each device or verify the advertised capture percentages.
- fabianhjr reports feeling much better after combining a bedroom HEPA filter, improved ventilation and daily robotic vacuuming and mopping [[./thread#^c28082646|fabianhjr]]. Back windows were used instead of street-facing ones [[./thread#^c28083076|fabianhjr]]. blagie also reports improved allergies during the pandemic but explicitly cannot distinguish the effects of home filtration, outdoor masking or changed exposure [[./thread#^c28086763|blagie]]. Both accounts involve multiple simultaneous changes.
- vpj reports no noticeable improvement after approximately a week of using a new purifier while sleeping, suggesting that baseline air quality might already have been good [[./thread#^c28082613|vpj]] [[./thread#^c28084596|vpj]]. There are no accompanying pollutant readings or sleep measurements. This null experience is an important counterpart to reports of substantial relief.
- Cleaning experiences conflict. jimmaswell reports a year in an unvacuumed carpeted shared house without a noticeable difference in well-being [[./thread#^c28087908|jimmaswell]], later limiting the argument to people without allergies or severe asthma [[./thread#^c28090780|jimmaswell]]. ericwood says vacuuming greatly reduces dust- and pollen-related misery and motivated a switch to wood flooring [[./thread#^c28089574|ericwood]]. LegitShady reports extracting substantial additional dust with a stronger corded vacuum after robotic cleaning [[./thread#^c28082856|LegitShady]].
- Allergy histories show substantial individual and geographic variation. 8note reports impaired smell and taste while growing up with a dog, improving years after its death [[./thread#^c28082836|8note]]. vmception developed pollen symptoms after travelling to another country [[./thread#^c28083023|vmception]]. melomal describes hay fever beginning around age 15 and disappearing after a later international move [[./thread#^c28086842|melomal]]. These accounts do not identify a single protective or harmful amount of childhood exposure.
- BatteryMountain reports four years using a combined dehumidifier and HEPA device after moving from an inland climate described as approximately 10\% humidity to a coastal setting at 40–60\% [[./thread#^c28084129|BatteryMountain]]. Reported improvements include drier towels and laundry, less condensation, neutral odour and no further need to wash mould from ceilings. The user still airs rooms periodically and clarifies that open windows undermine moisture control and filtration efficiency [[./thread#^c28084927|BatteryMountain]] [[./thread#^c28115257|BatteryMountain]].
- Purifier noise has opposite reported effects. mensetmanusman has used a purifier for children's white noise for roughly ten years, deliberately increasing speed at night [[./thread#^c28083227|mensetmanusman]]. hourislate describes comfortable sleep with multiple fans but also increased sensitivity to outdoor allergens, without establishing why [[./thread#^c28083384|hourislate]]. Leparamour reports six weeks of severe irritation and impaired concentration from a neighbour's purifier bass and vibration travelling through the building, even with noise-cancelling headphones [[./thread#^c28083980|Leparamour]].
- Vehicle wear accounts support competing mechanisms without measuring airborne pollution. gambiting reports brake pads still measuring like new after 10,000 miles in a heavy plug-in hybrid used largely with regenerative braking [[./thread#^c28085275|gambiting]]. varjag reports Leaf pads lasting six years and about 90,000 km [[./thread#^c28085031|varjag]]. Conversely, sokoloff reports front tyres approaching replacement at 21,000 miles on a Leaf, earlier than expected for comparable conventional cars [[./thread#^c28085843|sokoloff]].
- krkoch reports installing a fan to lower pressure beneath the house and seeing radon readings fall from approximately 120 to 20, written as bq without a volume denominator [[./thread#^c28084491|krkoch]]. This is a reported before-and-after result for source-entry control. It does not validate dwd's activated-carbon proposal [[./thread#^c28083050|dwd]] or andi999's suggestion that HEPA would be needed [[./thread#^c28083800|andi999]].
- icelancer reports deploying 30 fan-and-filter setups at work alongside enclosed UVC devices, vaccination incentives, distancing and other measures, with five externally acquired COVID cases and no reported workplace spread [[./thread#^c28082826|icelancer]]. This is an implementation anecdote with several co-interventions, not an estimate of filtration's independent effectiveness.
- r00fus reports running a whole-house fan when outdoor AQI is favourable, often early or late in the day, including at least 15 minutes during cold weather and sometimes overnight instead of air conditioning in summer [[./thread#^c28083485|r00fus]]. asah separately reports checking a box-fan filter's effectiveness with a particle meter during wildfire-related exposure, but provides no numerical results in the comment [[./thread#^c28087168|asah]]. These are practical routines whose success depends on local conditions and equipment.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The magnitude, threshold and mechanism of ordinary indoor CO2 effects remain unsettled in this unit. XorNot asserts impairment above 600 ppm [[./thread#^c28083037|XorNot]], breput reports subjective effects around 800–1000 ppm [[./thread#^c28082909|breput]], and learc83 invokes contrary submarine evidence [[./thread#^c28085869|learc83]]. The controlled-office study supplies a different kind of evidence, but criticaltinker's methodological qualifications remain [[./thread#^c28083109|criticaltinker]]. The comments do not establish a universal concentration at which cognition or comfort deteriorates.
- There is no direct NO2 measurement, NO2 sensor validation or NO2-specific intervention result in the supplied unit. maxerickson discusses NOx generally in modern vehicle exhaust [[./thread#^c28085447|maxerickson]]. modeless asserts higher asthma rates in homes with gas stoves [[./thread#^c28083122|modeless]], while nelox requests a source [[./thread#^c28083254|nelox]] and colechristensen replies from particle measurements and a hypothesis about food burning [[./thread#^c28083266|colechristensen]].
