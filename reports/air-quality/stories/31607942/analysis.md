# [Indoor air filtration could lead to increased airborne endotoxin levels (2020)](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=31607942)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion asks whether findings about endotoxins from air filtration should change how people use purifiers. Participants consider whether filter age, trapped bacteria, and particle size affect endotoxin release, and whether replacing or cleaning filters can help. Others weigh filtration benefits against the study’s uncertainties, call for replication, and share approaches to ventilation and indoor monitoring. A side discussion covers cooking emissions, cleaning practices, allergy symptoms, and how much air-quality readings can guide action.
Tags: air-filtration, HEPA, endotoxins, indoor-air-quality, filter-maintenance, ventilation, research-interpretation, air-quality-monitoring

## Relevance

Comments weigh the possible endotoxin tradeoff from filtration against reductions in PM2.5, with disagreement about whether the result has practical health significance and whether replacing filters is enough. Firsthand accounts describe cooking-related particle readings, gas-stove pollutants, outdoor smoke and freeway exposure, and the limits of consumer monitors; the evidence about endotoxins remains uncertain. ## Arguments

- Ventilation can outperform recirculating filtration when outdoor air is clean. After two years of monitoring, thecrims0nchin [[./thread#^c31610649|thecrims0nchin]] describes opening windows during cooking and cleaning as much faster than multiple HEPA units and a substantial carbon filter. This is a household comparison without supplied clearance times or airflow measurements.
- Removing combustion sources may improve air quality beyond reducing particles. QuercusMax [[./thread#^c31610834|QuercusMax]] attributes additional emissions to gas combustion and cites studies associating gas stoves with asthma and other respiratory problems. These are claims about external research, not firsthand health outcomes. danans [[./thread#^c31616663|danans]] reports that, after removing combustion appliances, outdoor pollution became the main remaining air-quality problem.
- Some commenters favor retaining filtration despite the endotoxin concern. 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c31609480|5e92cb50239222b]] weighs a large reported reduction in personal PM2.5 exposure against their reading that endotoxin levels approximately doubled. thecrims0nchin [[./thread#^c31610649|thecrims0nchin]] similarly considers particulate removal worthwhile even with older filters. These are individual risk judgments, not demonstrations of net clinical benefit.
- Cleaning methods can limit dust redistribution and reduce reliance on sprayed products. hinkley [[./thread#^c31610912|hinkley]] describes damp dusting and argues that vacuum brushes can loft material that never enters the vacuum. thecrims0nchin [[./thread#^c31611493|thecrims0nchin]] interprets the cleaning discussion as favoring simple wet cleaning over spray cleaners for air quality. Neither supplies comparative airborne-particle or VOC measurements.

## Counterarguments

- Electric cooking still produces particles. hgomersall [[./thread#^c31610747|hgomersall]] reports substantial PM10 during induction cooking, while danans [[./thread#^c31611479|danans]] reports PM2.5 increases from cooking food on induction when the extractor is forgotten. These accounts challenge any inference that removing gas eliminates cooking pollution; the PM10 observation should not be treated as a PM2.5 measurement.
- Replacing a filter does not clearly eliminate the reported endotoxin effect. outworlder [[./thread#^c31610590|outworlder]] explicitly retracts an initial replacement-only explanation after quoting increased alveolar deposition with both new and used filters. bricemo [[./thread#^c31610548|bricemo]], replying to a proposed reassuring reinterpretation of the title, says replacement removes large endotoxins but leaves small ones.
- A dirty filter does not necessarily become worse at capturing particles. thaeli [[./thread#^c31609458|thaeli]] says media filtration becomes more efficient as loading increases, while airflow falls or fan energy rises. This separates particle-capture efficiency, total air-cleaning performance, and possible biological emissions, which several other comments treat together.
- Disinfecting a filter is not established as a solution. sdze [[./thread#^c31610561|sdze]] reports spraying a car filter with disinfectant, but provides no measured result. QuercusMax [[./thread#^c31610792|QuercusMax]] argues that killing bacteria would not necessarily destroy their endotoxins, and outworlder [[./thread#^c31610604|outworlder]] questions whether droplets reach material embedded inside the filter.
- Washable filtration remains a proposal rather than a demonstrated remedy. m0llusk [[./thread#^c31610034|m0llusk]] suggests filters designed for regular cleaning; hollerith [[./thread#^c31610508|hollerith]] asks for an example capable of capturing the small particles of concern, and outworlder [[./thread#^c31610614|outworlder]] says HEPA filters generally cannot be cleaned.
- Visible dirt and household dust are not demonstrated health hazards merely by being present. avalys [[./thread#^c31611160|avalys]] challenges the absolute danger and offers a healthy, rarely cleaning relative as a secondhand counterexample, later clarifying that absolute risk is the question [[./thread#^c31611349|avalys]]. hinkley [[./thread#^c31612614|hinkley]] corrects the cited surface-cleaning evidence to food-borne pathogens and cross-contamination. Neither the relative's health nor those surface studies establishes the inhalation risk of measured PM2.5.

## Assumptions

- The window-versus-filter comparison assumes sufficiently clean incoming air and adequate air exchange. thecrims0nchin [[./thread#^c31610649|thecrims0nchin]] explicitly acknowledges the outdoor-air condition, but supplies no ventilation rate, purifier airflow, room volume, or comparative pollutant-decay curves. Their description of HEPA as slow therefore remains specific to an unspecified setup.
- The pro-purifier tradeoff assumes particulate reduction outweighs any added endotoxin hazard. The PM2.5 and endotoxin ratios discussed by 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c31609480|5e92cb50239222b]] concern different exposures; without baseline doses and health-response information, they cannot be numerically balanced into a net health effect.
- The tentative zero-sum argument assumes comparable consequences from inhaling bacteria versus inhaling endotoxins released after filtration. thecrims0nchin [[./thread#^c31611357|thecrims0nchin]] also acknowledges an important complication: material deposited on surfaces may be cleaned away, whereas material released from a circulating filter may become airborne. Equivalent toxin production and lung exposure are not established.
- The proposed mechanisms are competing explanations, not observations in this export. zcw100 [[./thread#^c31610782|zcw100]] emphasizes bacteria eventually dying anyway and possibly growing on neglected filters; enragedcacti [[./thread#^c31609676|enragedcacti]] interprets filtration as creating additional endotoxins; neuroma [[./thread#^c31609357|neuroma]] speculates about mechanically producing different microparticles. None supplies a direct test of their mechanism.
- Humidity and equipment design are assumed to matter, but their effects are unquantified. thecrims0nchin [[./thread#^c31610649|thecrims0nchin]] asserts a relationship between humidity, health, and particles. kkfx [[./thread#^c31610232|kkfx]] proposes that concentration, moisture, airflow, and device type affect colonization, while questioning what kind of purifier was studied. The discussion supplies no humidity target or tested comparison between device families.
- Biological plausibility is not equivalent to demonstrated household harm. sudden\_dystopia [[./thread#^c31610155|sudden\_dystopia]] quotes Wikipedia about LPS-associated cellular senescence and neurodegeneration. Applying that claim to ordinary purifier exposure requires exposure and dose evidence absent from the supplied discussion.

## Conditions

- Geography and nearby sources constrain window-opening advice. thecrims0nchin [[./thread#^c31610649|thecrims0nchin]] explicitly describes access to clean outdoor air as a privilege. By contrast, danans [[./thread#^c31616663|danans]] identifies outdoor pollen, smoke, and NOx from a nearby freeway as their biggest remaining problems. These accounts support opposite ventilation priorities under different outdoor conditions, without reporting a controlled window-opening comparison.
- Energy costs and comfort influence practical choices. thecrims0nchin [[./thread#^c31610649|thecrims0nchin]] accepts higher heating or cooling bills to ventilate, prefers large quiet HEPA units, and is considering filtered heat exchange without reporting an installation. thaeli [[./thread#^c31609458|thaeli]] adds that loaded filters impose an airflow or fan-energy penalty.
- Monitoring affordability affects access to evidence. thecrims0nchin [[./thread#^c31611941|thecrims0nchin]] obtained three monitors cheaply secondhand, whereas meco [[./thread#^c31614593|meco]] says poor reviews of affordable monitors have discouraged purchasing one. No prices, calibration costs, or validated comparison of budget devices are supplied.
- Cleaning choices also involve price, smell, and material compatibility. WastingMyTime89 [[./thread#^c31631986|WastingMyTime89]] reports switching from vinegar to inexpensive bulk citric acid because it stores compactly and lacks the smell. sul\_tasto [[./thread#^c31616942|sul\_tasto]] raises possible countertop damage from vinegar. These are practical and sensory considerations, not measured reductions in PM2.5, NO2, or CO2.
- The symptom discussion includes barriers to medical assessment. the\_only\_law [[./thread#^c31610659|the\_only\_law]] describes a previous consultation ending with an OTC recommendation, while YeahNO [[./thread#^c31613700|YeahNO]] says insurance copays discourage seeking care. This contextualizes difficulty investigating symptoms, but provides no evidence identifying an airborne cause.

## Lived experiences

- thecrims0nchin [[./thread#^c31610649|thecrims0nchin]] reports two years of household air monitoring. They identify their instruments as used uHoo, LaserEgg, and Airthings devices, acknowledge that none is especially accurate, and describe using trends to make guesses [[./thread#^c31611941|thecrims0nchin]]. The export contains neither raw readings nor calibration checks, so these accounts support perceived trends more strongly than precise exposure estimates.
- After switching from electric to gas, thecrims0nchin [[./thread#^c31610829|thecrims0nchin]] initially reports similar PM10 and PM2.5 but concerning CO2, NO2, and VOC behavior, hypothesizing that food explains the particles. After danans presents a study claim, thecrims0nchin [[./thread#^c31611615|thecrims0nchin]] accepts that gas produces more particles and food contributes little. That later concession is a change of interpretation, not a reported repeat measurement resolving the initial observation.
- danans [[./thread#^c31611479|danans]] describes an induction-equipped home designed for low baseline PM2.5, CO2, and VOCs. During cooking, PM2.5 is the only monitored quantity said to rise substantially, specifically when the extractor is forgotten. Cooking remains the leading indoor source only comparatively, after other combustion sources were removed [[./thread#^c31616663|danans]]; no absolute concentrations are given.
- 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c31609480|5e92cb50239222b]] reports that using a purifier lowers their yearly average PM2.5 exposure approximately 60–80-fold. This is the clearest numerical personal exposure claim, but the monitor, calculation method, location, baseline concentration, and relationship to symptoms are unspecified.
- the\_only\_law [[./thread#^c31610539|the\_only\_law]] reports severe tearing, nasal blockage, and repeated sneezing while wondering about a dirty filter. High seasonal pollen and COVID beginning around the same time are explicit competing explanations; no filter inspection or exposure measurement confirms the suspicion. Separately, specialist [[./thread#^c31610897|specialist]] reports improvement after sinus surgery, daily saline rinses, and antihistamine spray, rather than after an air-quality intervention.
- dylan604 [[./thread#^c31610721|dylan604]] reports that their household filters clog before the suggested 90-day interval despite having no carpet, in a home with a cat. This supports household-specific maintenance needs, but clogging is not evidence of endotoxin production or its onset.
- Wistar [[./thread#^c31610847|Wistar]] built box-fan purifiers with four MERV 13 furnace filters, which they describe as HEPA, and added 254 nm and purported 222 nm UVC lamps. They question whether air remains in the enclosure long enough for effective treatment. Their household had not contracted COVID, but they explicitly acknowledge luck; no pollutant, UV-dose, or infection-control effectiveness measurements are supplied.

## Unresolved disagreements

- How much does replacement mitigate endotoxins? ryanmarsh [[./thread#^c31609363|ryanmarsh]] quotes a passage favoring regular replacement and argues that the title obscures this mitigation. bricemo [[./thread#^c31610548|bricemo]] distinguishes large from small endotoxins, while outworlder [[./thread#^c31610682|outworlder]] quotes increased alveolar deposition with both new and used filters. Havoc [[./thread#^c31611106|Havoc]] questions why smaller material remains after removing the presumed source. The relationship between airborne concentrations, particle sizes, and lung deposition is not resolved by these excerpts.
- Whether the endotoxin finding warrants stopping purification remains unanswered quantitatively. tikkun [[./thread#^c31609105|tikkun]] asks that practical question; SnowHill9902 [[./thread#^c31609241|SnowHill9902]] calls it a choice between hazards. thaeli [[./thread#^c31609458|thaeli]] leaves open whether the effect matters in practice, and CamperBob2 [[./thread#^c31610797|CamperBob2]] calls for replication. kjax [[./thread#^c31609482|kjax]] also asks how long new filters last before meaningful emissions, without receiving a substantive answer in the supplied comments.
- The relative contributions of fuel and food remain disputed. thecrims0nchin [[./thread#^c31610829|thecrims0nchin]] initially observes similar particle behavior across fuels, while danans [[./thread#^c31611479|danans]] invokes research claiming substantially greater PM2.5 from gas and also personally observes food-generated PM2.5 on induction. Cooking methods, food, extraction, instruments, and comparable exposure periods are not supplied, so neither the difference's magnitude nor the later claim that food contributes only a tiny share is established here.
- Sensor selection and measurement reliability remain unresolved. meco [[./thread#^c31614593|meco]] asks which metrics matter and whether affordable monitors are trustworthy; iancmceachern [[./thread#^c31616395|iancmceachern]] offers brief product recommendations without validation. Combined with the acknowledged inaccuracies from thecrims0nchin [[./thread#^c31611941|thecrims0nchin]], the discussion provides no reliable device comparison or absolute PM2.5, NO2, or CO2 concentrations against which to assess exposure.
- Several proposed improvements lack demonstrated results. thecrims0nchin [[./thread#^c31610649|thecrims0nchin]] doubts thin carbon layers and finds substantial carbon filtration difficult to evaluate. nanomonkey [[./thread#^c31611022|nanomonkey]] proposes UV, water filtration, and high-temperature treatment, but reports no tests. Wistar [[./thread#^c31610847|Wistar]] supplies a DIY implementation while explicitly questioning treatment time. None establishes household endotoxin removal or a quantified health benefit from these additions.
- There are no firsthand cognitive-performance or sleep outcomes in this unit, and no reported CO2-linked symptom experiment. The cooking observations from thecrims0nchin [[./thread#^c31610829|thecrims0nchin]] do not connect gas readings to such effects. sudden\_dystopia [[./thread#^c31610155|sudden\_dystopia]] supplies a biological claim rather than cognition measurements, while the symptoms reported by the\_only\_law [[./thread#^c31610539|the\_only\_law]] have substantial competing explanations.
- Traffic and smoke exposure are identified but not characterized. danans [[./thread#^c31616663|danans]] names freeway NOx and outdoor smoke without concentrations, source verification, or a specific wildfire episode. Together with the clean-air limitation acknowledged by thecrims0nchin [[./thread#^c31610649|thecrims0nchin]], this leaves unresolved when opening windows would reduce total exposure versus importing pollution, and how well the described systems perform during wildfire smoke.
