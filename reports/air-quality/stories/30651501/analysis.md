## Arguments

- bookofjoe [[./thread#^c30651508|bookofjoe]] clarifies that the headline reports an association between historical redlining and present-day pollution disparities in U.S. cities. The supplied discussion contains no study results or methods establishing the magnitude or cause of that association.
- baron816 [[./thread#^c30652606|baron816]] proposes that polluters seek cheap land and probably nearby cheap labor, while acknowledging that racism also plays a role. This is a proposed economic explanation, not a firsthand observation or measurement.
- hedora [[./thread#^c30658253|hedora]] recalls a UC Berkeley paper whose geographically detailed, roughly year-averaged Bay Area PM2.5 levels appeared to align with socioeconomic patterns. This is a recollection of research, without concentrations, effect sizes, or an identified paper.

## Counterarguments

- Replying directly to baron816, rayiner [[./thread#^c30652934|rayiner]] argues that economically motivated siting can itself reflect structural racism: expressly discriminatory historical policies can create conditions that produce racial disparities even without present-day prejudiced intent. This challenges treating cheap land and racism as separate explanations; it does not dispute the proposed attraction of cheap land.

Neither comment supplies evidence testing those mechanisms or their relative contributions.
