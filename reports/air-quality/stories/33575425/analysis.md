# [Analyzing Carbon Dioxide levels while attending IETF-115 in London, UK](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=33575425)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion centers on interpreting CO₂ readings across flights, hotels, classrooms, offices, and homes. Participants debate sensor calibration and pressure effects, ventilation design, open windows, HVAC controls, and whether CO₂ is a useful proxy for stale air or infection risk. Other threads cover perceived effects on alertness, masks and COVID precautions, air-quality monitors, plants and CO₂ scrubbing, and the tradeoffs involved in improving indoor air.
Tags: CO₂ monitoring, ventilation, air quality, sensors, COVID-19, HVAC

## Relevance

The discussion covers indoor and travel CO2 readings, possible effects on alertness and cognition, sensor calibration and reliability, and ventilation choices including opening windows, HVAC, and filtration. It includes firsthand classroom, bedroom, office, and transit measurements, with some disagreement about symptoms and sensor accuracy. ## Arguments

- Fresh-air supply is the main practical explanation offered for differences between buildings. Drawing on monitoring work, ahaucnx [[./thread#^c33576769|ahaucnx]] distinguishes ventilation that brings outdoor air inside from internal recirculation, warning that turning on a hotel fan or adjusting air conditioning may not supply enough fresh air.
- Possible cognitive benefits motivate measuring and improving ventilation. Arbortheus [[./thread#^c33577626|Arbortheus]] expects better classroom attention, while iudqnolq [[./thread#^c33576438|iudqnolq]] wants to discover whether more fresh air improves programming performance. These are expectations and an intended personal investigation, rather than demonstrated improvements in school attainment or coding performance.
- Monitoring is more useful when it changes building operation. Reason077 [[./thread#^c33577473|Reason077]] proposes room CO2 sensors controlling fresh-air intake; Animats [[./thread#^c33580603|Animats]] says combined temperature, humidity and CO2 sensing already exists in larger buildings. pifm\_guy [[./thread#^c33579677|pifm\_guy]] argues that an unattended display is insufficient without ventilation control and an alert when acceptable conditions cannot be maintained.
- Opening windows is presented as a simple ventilation option where conditions permit. rsync [[./thread#^c33579712|rsync]] argues that occupants should have operable windows, and kragen [[./thread#^c33587161|kragen]] calculates that suitable window area and airflow could ventilate a crowded classroom. The calculation supports feasibility under its assumptions, rather than establishing that every open window provides sufficient exchange.
- Heat recovery offers a proposed compromise between fresh air and thermal comfort. Reason077 [[./thread#^c33577815|Reason077]] describes bringing in filtered outdoor air while transferring heat from exhaust air; hedgehog [[./thread#^c33580469|hedgehog]] argues that energy-recovery ventilation can substantially reduce ventilation heat loss. Neither supplies measured installation costs or energy savings in this discussion.
- The discussion contains an explicit argument for measuring more than CO2. jll29 [[./thread#^c33577186|jll29]] urges monitoring PM2.5, PM10, NO and NO2 in London and links reports concerning pollution and asthma. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c33578683|ahaucnx]] recommends beginning with PM, CO2 and TVOCs to understand household ventilation and pollution sources. These recommendations do not establish that one measurement represents the others.

## Counterarguments

- Feeling stuffy is not a dependable CO2 measurement. JohnBooty [[./thread#^c33577814|JohnBooty]] initially equates stuffiness with high CO2, but JohnBooty [[./thread#^c33578023|JohnBooty]] subsequently acknowledges temperature and other correlated cues. sireat [[./thread#^c33581444|sireat]] reports complaints of stuffiness at approximately 650 ppm after an office became warmer, while dataflow [[./thread#^c33576776|dataflow]] reports no noticeable difference between below 600 ppm and above 1200 ppm.
- Not every displayed CO2 value comes from a dedicated CO2 measurement. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c33578683|ahaucnx]] recommends NDIR or photoacoustic sensing and calls CO2 estimates derived from TVOC sensors very inaccurate. Their observation that inexpensive monitors often share sensor modules is not a demonstration that complete devices have equivalent accuracy.
- The Aranet discussion complicates blanket claims about calibration and altitude errors. phphphphp [[./thread#^c33576370|phphphphp]] quotes documentation specifying pressure compensation down to 750 hPa, distinguishing fresh-air manual calibration from automatic baseline calibration. This makes the additional 24\% flight correction quoted by MichaelZuo [[./thread#^c33577255|MichaelZuo]] uncertain without knowing the actual device configuration and processing. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c33576395|ahaucnx]] separately reports vibration sensitivity in some NDIR sensors, while describing the Aranet's Senseair Sunrise as more resistant.
- Good aircraft ventilation cannot simply be assumed throughout a journey. kortilla [[./thread#^c33576404|kortilla]] claims cabin air is replaced roughly every five minutes, but sampo [[./thread#^c33576632|sampo]] cites other people's ground-phase measurements of 2000–4000 ppm. MichaelZuo [[./thread#^c33577255|MichaelZuo]] reads the article's graphs as remaining above 1200 ppm in flight, whereas dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c33577571|dzhiurgis]] reports approximately 1000 ppm after air conditioning started. These accounts differ in aircraft, operating conditions, instruments and provenance.
- Subjectively fresher transport air may still have substantial particulate pollution. Litost [[./thread#^c33578832|Litost]] quotes a report saying that 2.5 hours on a bus produced one-third of the particulate exposure of one hour on the Tube. Reason077 [[./thread#^c33581627|Reason077]] nevertheless finds the modern Tube fresher than crowded buses and believes ventilation and brake-dust conditions have improved. Different pollutants, dates and sensory impressions prevent a single defensible ranking from these comments.
- Ordinary houseplants are challenged as a meaningful substitute for ventilation. sampo [[./thread#^c33577929|sampo]] and kragen [[./thread#^c33579810|kragen]] use rough respiration and plant-growth calculations to argue that substantial growing biomass would be needed per occupant. Nighttime behavior is also disputed: xenonite [[./thread#^c33577357|xenonite]] emphasizes respiration without photosynthesis, while jefftk [[./thread#^c33577416|jefftk]] points to plants that fix carbon at night. None reports a household installation that maintained low CO2 through plants alone.
- Ventilation cannot deliver a concentration below the air being supplied. In response to the desired 330 ppm household target, ad404b8a372f2b9 [[./thread#^c33576938|ad404b8a372f2b9]] explains why opening a window would not suffice, and MertsA [[./thread#^c33581750|MertsA]] distinguishes ordinary outdoor-air ventilation from actual CO2 removal. This limits what even an effective heat-recovery system can accomplish.
- Opening windows is therefore conditional, rather than an unconditional air-quality improvement. peteforde [[./thread#^c33577486|peteforde]] reports that opening a window helped high indoor CO2, but Reason077 [[./thread#^c33577399|Reason077]] describes road fumes and dust on London buses, and jll29 [[./thread#^c33577186|jll29]] raises outdoor particulate and nitrogen-oxide exposure. Lower CO2 could come with greater exposure to outdoor pollutants when those are elevated; that tradeoff is an inference from the concerns raised, not a paired window-open/window-closed measurement supplied here.

## Assumptions

- Using CO2 as an infection-exposure proxy assumes that accumulated exhaled breath is an important source and that the reading represents the air being inhaled. JohnBooty [[./thread#^c33635404|JohnBooty]] reports a CO2 spike associated with a gas oven, illustrating another source. djmips [[./thread#^c33576313|djmips]] argues that shouting changes viral dispersal, and bArray [[./thread#^c33579455|bArray]] emphasizes airflow paths and filtration. Consequently, a room CO2 reading alone does not establish an individual's infectious exposure.
- Automatic baseline calibration assumes that the instrument periodically encounters an appropriate fresh-air baseline. iudqnolq [[./thread#^c33576438|iudqnolq]] describes inexpensive devices treating a recent minimum as roughly 420 ppm. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c33576618|ahaucnx]] reports schoolrooms that remain above ambient overnight, causing this assumption to underestimate CO2. An empty room is not necessarily a valid calibration environment.
- Claims of learning to sense CO2 assume that symptoms can be separated from temperature, humidity, odors and expectations created by seeing a meter. afusalan [[./thread#^c33576746|afusalan]] initially recommends sleepiness as a cue, but afusalan [[./thread#^c33577130|afusalan]] later discloses never having measured CO2. OJFord [[./thread#^c33580857|OJFord]] asks whether another commenter has guessed the concentration before checking the display; no resulting blinded test is supplied.
- kragen's [[./thread#^c33587161|kragen]] classroom estimate assumes 30 occupants, approximately 1 kg of CO2 production per person per day, outdoor air around 420 ppm and a 1000 ppm target, yielding roughly 330 liters per second of ventilation. Its window-size examples assume suitable airflow through the opening; they are calculations, not measurements of a particular classroom's actual ventilation.
- The numerical thresholds discussed serve different purposes. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c33576769|ahaucnx]] uses below 1000 ppm as an achievable classroom ventilation target, whereas Litost [[./thread#^c33578832|Litost]] quotes occupational limits of 5000 ppm over eight hours and 15000 ppm over fifteen minutes. These comments do not establish either threshold as a universal boundary for comfort, cognition or infection risk.
- Product recommendations require attention to their provenance. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c33576446|ahaucnx]] identifies involvement in the AirGradient open-source hardware project, and ahaucnx [[./thread#^c33578683|ahaucnx]] explicitly says they do not own either an Air Quality Egg or an Aranet. Their practical experience is relevant, but it does not constitute an independent comparison of those products; p1esk's [[./thread#^c33579406|p1esk]] request for a specific competitive justification remains unanswered.

## Conditions

- Building age, layout and funding constrain school improvements. mistr0 [[./thread#^c33577604|mistr0]] describes a UK secondary-school classroom in a 1960s building, doors leading into other classrooms that cannot routinely be opened during lessons, and an expensive prospective AC retrofit. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c33578608|ahaucnx]] offers the UK SAMHE project's free school monitors, addressing measurement access without establishing funding for ventilation work.
- Weather and comfort limit window-based ventilation. dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c33577571|dzhiurgis]] finds opening windows impractical in very cold, hot or humid climates. Reason077 [[./thread#^c33577778|Reason077]] says London bus passengers commonly keep windows shut to avoid cold, and djmips [[./thread#^c33576313|djmips]] anticipates higher winter heating costs from increased fresh-air supply. These are contextual constraints, not evidence that occupants everywhere make the same tradeoff.
- Regional building practices differ in the accounts. ArchitectAnon [[./thread#^c33577601|ArchitectAnon]] says Scottish new homes require bedroom CO2 monitoring, but the guidance quoted by sampo [[./thread#^c33578019|sampo]] specifies the principal bedroom where infiltration is below 15 m³/hr/m² at 50 Pa. matsimitsu [[./thread#^c33579367|matsimitsu]] describes Dutch new homes with room monitors controlling mechanical ventilation, with room-specific control in more expensive homes. These are commenter reports and quoted guidance, not a verified survey of current national requirements.
- Equipment prices can make monitoring a meaningful purchase. In this 2022 discussion, peteforde [[./thread#^c33577486|peteforde]] gives an Awair Element price of \$299. jobigoud [[./thread#^c33583545|jobigoud]] quotes \$229 for a Senseair Sunrise sensor module, which should not be confused with the price of a complete Aranet monitor. Neither comment establishes the cheapest reliable option.
- Plant-based proposals face substantial space and daylight requirements. OJFord [[./thread#^c33581095|OJFord]] says their former office could not accommodate the suggested vegetation per person; kragen [[./thread#^c33581130|kragen]] adds several square meters of windows or skylights per occupant, and OJFord [[./thread#^c33581240|OJFord]] raises humidity concerns. These are proposed constraints, rather than operating results from a planted office.
- Subambient household CO2 removal is discussed as an expensive maintenance undertaking. Under a hypothetical ventilation scenario, kragen [[./thread#^c33586338|kragen]] estimates roughly \$150 monthly for lime, over 100 kg of replacement material and approximately 190 kg of waste per month. pifm\_guy [[./thread#^c33579775|pifm\_guy]] additionally raises consumable-volume and chemical-byproduct concerns. These are unverified calculations and cautions, not a priced, tested home system.

## Lived experiences

- The most concrete classroom account comes from mistr0 [[./thread#^c33577604|mistr0]]: 25–30 students in a 60-minute lesson produce reported readings of 2000–2500 ppm, rising to 3000–3500 during consecutive lessons. Separately, ahaucnx [[./thread#^c33576769|ahaucnx]] reports regularly seeing over 3500 ppm in crowded unventilated classrooms and below 1000 ppm throughout the day in well-ventilated classrooms. Neither supplies raw measurement records in the comments.
- sireat [[./thread#^c33581444|sireat]] reports two instruments—a maintenance meter and an Aranet4—both reading around 650 ppm in an office occupied by four people. Staff felt stuffy after air conditioning was switched off and temperature rose from 22°C to 26°C. The same commenter reports their bedroom regularly reaching 3000 ppm by morning when windows remain closed; no corresponding sleep outcome is given.
- peteforde [[./thread#^c33577486|peteforde]] bought an Awair after suspecting poor circulation, found high CO2 and observed improvement when opening a window. They subsequently moved to a home with an HRV system, plants and greener surroundings, reporting below 600 ppm with windows closed. Several housing conditions changed together, so this account cannot isolate the contribution of each.
- Other household readings illustrate different occupancy conditions without establishing typical household exposure. ad404b8a372f2b9 [[./thread#^c33577283|ad404b8a372f2b9]] reports 1600 ppm within an hour with one person and a cat in the room; rufus\_foreman [[./thread#^c33577361|rufus\_foreman]] reports about 1200 ppm at home. stuaxo [[./thread#^c33577075|stuaxo]] reports an Aranet4 reading of 497 ppm in a room unoccupied for several hours.
- dataflow [[./thread#^c33576776|dataflow]] reports no detectable sleepiness or other difference above 1200 ppm compared with below 600 ppm. dataflow [[./thread#^c33577805|dataflow]] says their ability to guess readings probably comes from knowing how long doors and windows have been closed, while their sense of stuffiness seems related to humidity, dust and odors.
- Several commenters describe substantially greater sensitivity. colechristensen [[./thread#^c33576923|colechristensen]] reports heavy-feeling air in the lungs above about 800 ppm. iofiiiiiiiii [[./thread#^c33586213|iofiiiiiiiii]] reports an abnormal feeling around 800–900 ppm and difficulty completing thoughts around 1000 ppm, including noticing central-ventilation failures before owning a meter. quantum\_magpie [[./thread#^c33577314|quantum\_magpie]] associates readings above 1000 ppm with sleepiness and sensations of swelling eyes and tingling teeth. These are personal symptom associations, not established diagnostic thresholds.
- Other symptom accounts lack measured concentrations. rwmj [[./thread#^c33576766|rwmj]] describes dizziness and sleepiness in one crowded, poorly ventilated KVM Forum lecture theatre. zahrc [[./thread#^c33576849|zahrc]] reports headaches and tiredness with rapid relief after opening a window. pistoriusp [[./thread#^c33580838|pistoriusp]] describes forehead warmth after moving from a warm country with open windows to a cold country, while dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c33577630|dzhiurgis]] reports overnight nasal stuffiness.
- Cooking appears in one relevant firsthand account: JohnBooty [[./thread#^c33635404|JohnBooty]] says they can notice when a downstairs gas oven is switched on and that a meter confirms a CO2 spike. They explicitly allow that smell or other correlated factors may explain the sensation. No CO2 magnitude, NO2 reading or particulate measurement accompanies the account.
- dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c33577571|dzhiurgis]] reports using a Qingping monitor on a plane: CO2 was initially poor, then hovered around 1000 ppm after air conditioning began around the middle of boarding. A train also remained around 1000 ppm. This is a specific travel account that differs from the article readings discussed by other commenters.
- Aircraft comfort accounts introduce pressure as another variable. mleonhard [[./thread#^c33576757|mleonhard]] says the only long flight after which they awoke refreshed was on a 787 and suspects altitude-related discomfort elsewhere. Baeocystin [[./thread#^c33576968|Baeocystin]] reports barometer readings equivalent to about 6000 feet on roughly six Southwest 737 flights, contradicting the accompanying claim that non-787 aircraft invariably maintain an 8000-foot equivalent.
- Reason077 [[./thread#^c33577399|Reason077]] describes cold-day London buses as poorly ventilated, with food smells, breath, road fumes and dust. Reason077 [[./thread#^c33581627|Reason077]] recalls black nasal discharge after Tube travel in the early 2000s but says the modern Tube feels substantially fresher. These are firsthand sensory and bodily observations, without contemporaneous pollutant measurements.
- p1mrx [[./thread#^c33579740|p1mrx]] reports installing a Raspberry Pi and CO2 sensor that activate a bathroom exhaust fan when readings are high, but gives no before-and-after concentrations or energy costs. Animats [[./thread#^c33580603|Animats]] describes a San Francisco meeting venue with opening windows, ceiling fans and HVAC that were not coordinated; the proposed automatic control sequence was an unrealized improvement.
- iudqnolq [[./thread#^c33576438|iudqnolq]] reports Aranet4 readings remaining plausible for months without recalibration and says the manufacturer's calibration claims influenced the purchase. They explicitly lack equipment to verify those claims, so this is evidence of satisfactory apparent stability rather than a reference-standard accuracy test.
- Exposure precautions also vary personally. nicoburns [[./thread#^c33576215|nicoburns]] describes wearing an N95 on public transport and avoiding densely crowded rooms when practical. Article author hardaker [[./thread#^c33597947|hardaker]] reports wearing an N95 almost continuously except while eating or drinking, and hardaker [[./thread#^c33597702|hardaker]] reports no COVID as of November 14. That limited follow-up cannot establish protection attributable to the mask, ventilation or measured CO2.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The causal cognitive effect of ordinary indoor CO2 concentrations remains unresolved within this evidence. iofiiiiiiiii [[./thread#^c33586213|iofiiiiiiiii]] reports noticeable impairment at concentrations that dataflow [[./thread#^c33576776|dataflow]] does not consciously feel. iudqnolq [[./thread#^c33576438|iudqnolq]] refers to evidence of brain fog but has not yet established a personal programming benefit. No supplied controlled study separates CO2 from heat, odors, other pollutants or expectation, and absence of a perceived symptom does not itself measure cognitive performance.
- Sleepiness and restorative sleep are not shown to be the same outcome. cobertos [[./thread#^c33576493|cobertos]] finds sleeping on planes unusually easy and speculates about raising CO2 as a sleep aid. avian [[./thread#^c33576551|avian]] worries about such a device, and lucb1e [[./thread#^c33577969|lucb1e]] explicitly admits uncertainty about safety. No commenter reports testing a validated sleep intervention. Reason077 [[./thread#^c33577661|Reason077]] also disputes mleonhard's altitude-sickness explanation, leaving aircraft sleep and comfort attribution unsettled.
- The reason for aircraft CO2 spikes is not established. akira2501 [[./thread#^c33576273|akira2501]] suggests disabled air-conditioning packs; sokoloff [[./thread#^c33576381|sokoloff]] qualifies that explanation to situations requiring maximum power. etrautmann [[./thread#^c33576380|etrautmann]] suggests combustion, but jeffbee [[./thread#^c33577247|jeffbee]] notes that bleed air is drawn before the combustion section, and mleonhard [[./thread#^c33576757|mleonhard]] identifies the 787's electrically supplied air. hardaker [[./thread#^c33597870|hardaker]] says a Boeing explanation was added later, but its contents are not supplied here. Operating changes and instrument effects therefore remain competing explanations.
- Reliable product selection and household-specific monitoring priorities remain open. p1esk [[./thread#^c33577346|p1esk]] asks about competing monitors and which gases matter in a 40-year-old Florida house; ahaucnx [[./thread#^c33578683|ahaucnx]] offers a starting measurement set while disclaiming ownership of the compared products. simonebrunozzi [[./thread#^c33580669|simonebrunozzi]] reports frustration with conflicting sensor reviews. There is no supplied controlled comparison resolving accuracy, calibration burden or the appropriate pollutant mix for that house.
- This source unit provides little direct evidence about PM2.5 or NO2 and none about wildfire smoke. jll29 [[./thread#^c33577186|jll29]] recommends monitoring those pollutants, Litost [[./thread#^c33578832|Litost]] supplies a secondhand particulate-exposure comparison, and JohnBooty [[./thread#^c33635404|JohnBooty]] reports a cooking-related CO2 spike. There are no firsthand numerical PM2.5 or NO2 readings, measured cooking-filtration results, or simultaneous indoor/outdoor tests establishing when opening windows worsens those exposures.
- The mask branch disputes the interpretation of evidence, rather than supplying an air-quality experiment. azakai [[./thread#^c33577756|azakai]] quotes a CDC observational association between mask or respirator use and lower odds of a positive COVID test, alongside laboratory filtration evidence. timr [[./thread#^c33578926|timr]] challenges survey bias, and timr [[./thread#^c33600414|timr]] insists that laboratory performance does not establish real-world illness prevention. OJFord [[./thread#^c33581223|OJFord]] objects that the original question concerned personal mask performance rather than mandates. VancouverMan's [[./thread#^c33588298|VancouverMan]] inference from continued transmission to ineffectiveness is challenged by HyperSane [[./thread#^c33599180|HyperSane]] on incomplete adherence. These conflicting comments do not constitute population-wide HN agreement or an independently reviewed clinical synthesis.
- Neither the practicality nor the health value of maintaining a home at 330 ppm is demonstrated. rufus\_foreman [[./thread#^c33579442|rufus\_foreman]] rejects a quoted commercial specification of 'as low as 400 ppm'; ThrowawayTestr [[./thread#^c33585370|ThrowawayTestr]] clarifies that air scrubbers exist even if they are not ordinary HVAC equipment. Plant alternatives also remain speculative: pfdietz [[./thread#^c33577827|pfdietz]] acknowledges that a specific rapid cornfield CO2-depletion claim was hearsay. No supplied account shows a residential system achieving the requested target or a health or cognitive advantage over ordinary outdoor-air concentrations.
