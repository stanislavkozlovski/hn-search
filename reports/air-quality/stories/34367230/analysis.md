# [The gas industry is paying Instagram influencers to gush over gas stoves (2020)](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=34367230)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion of gas-stove marketing and health concerns quickly expands into whether gas cooking should be restricted, what evidence links it to indoor pollution, and whether ventilation or changes to building codes can reduce exposure. Participants compare gas, resistive electric, and induction cooking, weighing responsiveness, heat control, cookware and cooking techniques, cleaning, cost, electrical capacity, and household preferences. They also debate whether the controversy is driven by climate policy, public health, industry lobbying, or partisan messaging, and suggest taxes, incentives, or gradual transitions away from gas.
Tags: gas stoves, induction cooking, indoor air quality, NO₂ and ventilation, cooking appliances, climate policy, regulation, industry marketing, energy transition, consumer choice

## Relevance

Comments address indoor exposure from gas cooking, including NOx and other combustion products, and debate the evidence, health implications, and the role of ventilation. They compare gas with induction and electric cooking, including practical performance and cost tradeoffs, and distinguish cooking-related particles from pollutants produced by burning gas. ## Arguments

- Gas combustion may add an indoor exposure distinct from cooking fumes. resoluteteeth [[./thread#^c34367569|resoluteteeth]] frames the concern around pollution inside homes, while stephen\_g [[./thread#^c34368045|stephen\_g]] identifies combustion-generated NOx as the additional hazard. This supports considering the burner and the heated food as separate sources; neither comment supplies measured concentrations.
- Ventilation remains relevant with electric cooking. bob1029 [[./thread#^c34367712|bob1029]] argues for extraction above every range because high-temperature cooking produces potentially harmful compounds. throwaway22032 [[./thread#^c34367565|throwaway22032]] separately describes frying smoke and boiling-related moisture, making the case for ventilation about both respiratory exposure and household comfort.
- Ensuring that extraction actually runs could matter alongside installing it. juancn [[./thread#^c34368635|juancn]] reports that commercial kitchens in their locality interlock gas valves with extraction systems. ceejayoz [[./thread#^c34367439|ceejayoz]] suggests ventilation requirements as a possible intervention. The thread supplies no effectiveness measurements for these arrangements.
- A risk need not be obvious to individual users to warrant investigation. In response to ufmace’s argument that millions of users have not noticed pollution [[./thread#^c34367832|ufmace]], cinntaile [[./thread#^c34367971|cinntaile]] links an article described as dating from 2003, and smileysteve [[./thread#^c34368084|smileysteve]] discusses the difficulty of establishing links to childhood asthma. These are references to research and epidemiological reasoning, not reported study results.

## Counterarguments

- The relative importance of burner emissions is disputed. bob1029 [[./thread#^c34367712|bob1029]] calls gas combustion’s contribution negligible compared with frying above roughly 400°F. WithinReason [[./thread#^c34368167|WithinReason]], replying to stephen\_g’s NOx objection, similarly suggests NO2 might be negligible beside food emissions. Neither supplies a comparison of pollutant concentrations, doses, or health effects.
- Ordinary extraction may not adequately control combustion gases. stephen\_g [[./thread#^c34368045|stephen\_g]] says studies they have seen find most domestic range hoods insufficient, except very high-end models; they reiterate concerns about removal speed in [[./thread#^c34368275|stephen\_g]]. This challenges treating the presence of a hood as proof of adequate protection, but the studies’ methods and results are not reproduced.
- The claim that functioning gas heating is inherently clean depends on the appliance’s exhaust arrangement. ccalloway [[./thread#^c34367446|ccalloway]] argues that respiratory risks imply a seriously defective heating system. stephen\_g [[./thread#^c34368275|stephen\_g]] counters by distinguishing unflued heaters, flued furnaces that discharge pollution outdoors, and cooking burners that release combustion products into the home.
- The magnitude of the health risk and the necessity of eliminating gas remain contested. rsynnott [[./thread#^c34367669|rsynnott]] acknowledges a possible health risk but characterizes evidence for a large effect as weak. ceejayoz [[./thread#^c34368233|ceejayoz]] argues that the discussion has not established that gas stoves cannot be made safe. These are assessments of the evidence and possible mitigation, not demonstrations of safety.
- Replacing gas can sacrifice functions that some cooks value. glanzwulf [[./thread#^c34369496|glanzwulf]] emphasizes charring tortillas and vegetables directly over flame; mikrl [[./thread#^c34369634|mikrl]] values cooking during outages and the experience of flame when sautéing. These preferences explain resistance to switching without resolving the exposure question.
- Induction’s usability is not uniformly praised. wink [[./thread#^c34367975|wink]], who has not owned one, relays friends’ complaints about touch controls and potential repair costs. jsdalton [[./thread#^c34367746|jsdalton]], despite preferring induction overall, values gas’s visible flame as feedback about heat output. These accounts qualify claims of universal convenience.

## Assumptions

- Gas leakage should not be conflated with measured PM2.5. bedbot [[./thread#^c34367784|bedbot]] proposes continuous particulate leakage while explicitly acknowledging not knowing the numbers. ectopod [[./thread#^c34367955|ectopod]] responds that particulate production requires combustion, including a possible standing pilot. The exchange supplies a disputed explanation, not evidence of continuous PM2.5 emissions from an unlit appliance.
- Comparisons depend on which electric technology people have used. eddyg [[./thread#^c34368775|eddyg]] explicitly distinguishes induction from other electric stoves in response to juancn’s preference for gas. mikrl [[./thread#^c34369634|mikrl]] identifies their unfavorable comparison as an old gas range versus a glass-top electric stove; this does not establish gas’s superiority to induction.
- Total fuel consumption does not establish indoor exposure. CalRobert [[./thread#^c34367387|CalRobert]] and zip1234 [[./thread#^c34367423|zip1234]] challenge the relevance of comparing stove fuel use with a car. Their parent commenter, throwaway22032 [[./thread#^c34367437|throwaway22032]], clarifies that the fuel comparison concerned carbon emissions, while separate sentences addressed indoor air. The ancestry matters: the clarification should not be omitted when describing the dispute.
- Treating an open window as beneficial assumes that the incoming air and resulting airflow improve the relevant indoor conditions. throwaway22032 [[./thread#^c34367333|throwaway22032]] reports opening a window and running extraction, but provides no outdoor pollution readings, airflow information, or before-and-after exposure measurements. The benefit is assumed in this account rather than demonstrated.
- Some distrust rests on presumed sponsorship or motives rather than examination of exposure evidence. fullshark [[./thread#^c34367355|fullshark]] identifies the funder of a linked study, and pclmulqdq [[./thread#^c34368007|pclmulqdq]] infers an agenda behind its respiratory-health framing. Neither comment supplies a methodological critique or measurements showing that the health claim is wrong.

## Conditions

- Purchase costs vary substantially by product and market. hospadar [[./thread#^c34367606|hospadar]] bought a cheap induction range used but still identifies price as a disadvantage. In these January 2023 comments, basch [[./thread#^c34369649|basch]] reports an IKEA cooktop below \$1,000 and a portable unit bought for about \$40, then priced at \$70. LargoLasskhyfv [[./thread#^c34372603|LargoLasskhyfv]] reports German portable-unit prices of €39.90 or €49.90 including tax. These are historical reports, not current quotations.
- Electrical infrastructure can add work and cost to switching. thedougd [[./thread#^c34367966|thedougd]] is waiting for a panel upgrade before considering induction. XorNot [[./thread#^c34367502|XorNot]] describes replacing gas with induction on a dedicated circuit in Australia. tallanvor [[./thread#^c34367856|tallanvor]] corrects another commenter’s characterization of US electricity, stating that electric ranges normally use 240 V.
- Housing tenure and perceived property value can constrain adoption. glanzwulf [[./thread#^c34369496|glanzwulf]] cannot replace the stove in their German rental. notyourav [[./thread#^c34371768|notyourav]] reports being warned that switching to induction would reduce their home’s value, without reporting an actual valuation loss. tomatotomato37 [[./thread#^c34369162|tomatotomato37]] worries landlords would choose cheap coil replacements; that is a forecast, not an observed outcome.
- Ventilation provision and moisture concerns vary by location. throwaway22032 [[./thread#^c34367565|throwaway22032]] reports that new UK kitchens require extraction regardless of fuel and speculates that moisture may matter less in low-humidity parts of California. ceejayoz [[./thread#^c34367898|ceejayoz]] reports differing US state requirements and no federal requirement. These are commenters’ descriptions, not independently checked building-code guidance.
- A gas cooking appliance does not identify the household’s heating system or even its fuel supply. astura [[./thread#^c34367831|astura]] reports oil heating and a stove supplied by an outdoor propane tank; fatnoah [[./thread#^c34369419|fatnoah]] describes similar arrangements throughout their neighborhood. dsr\_ [[./thread#^c34369422|dsr\_]] has a street gas connection sufficient for an oven but not heating. These cases limit generalizations based on a single category of gas-equipped homes.
- Replacement timing and cookware compatibility affect the practical cost of source substitution. fatnoah [[./thread#^c34369389|fatnoah]] intends to wait until a new stove is needed despite enthusiasm for induction. hospadar [[./thread#^c34367606|hospadar]] notes incompatible copper cookware, while MDWolinski [[./thread#^c34368297|MDWolinski]] proposes a transfer plate that heats cookware in a conventional electric fashion. The thread gives no total retrofit-cost comparison.

## Lived experiences

- WithinReason [[./thread#^c34368167|WithinReason]] reports lung irritation lasting days after preheating an electric oven to a high temperature when they forget the extraction hood. This is the clearest firsthand respiratory symptom account in the unit. It is a personal attribution without pollutant measurements, a diagnosis, or a controlled comparison.
- throwaway22032 [[./thread#^c34367333|throwaway22032]] routinely opens a window and runs an extractor while using a gas hob. They also judge combustion to contribute less to their ventilation needs than frying smoke or boiling moisture [[./thread#^c34367565|throwaway22032]]. This documents a practice and a subjective comparison, not measured exposure reduction.
- ceejayoz [[./thread#^c34367898|ceejayoz]] reports having no kitchen extractor, finding that particularly annoying when searing steaks, and planning to address it during renovation. The account illustrates a practical ventilation gap without identifying pollutant levels or a health outcome.
- hospadar [[./thread#^c34367606|hospadar]] reports that switching to induction reduced waste heat, overheating pot handles, and rapid warming of a small kitchen, while improving cooking speed and control. jbotdev [[./thread#^c34367635|jbotdev]] similarly reports faster heating, less retained burner heat, and easier cleaning. These are firsthand comfort and usability improvements; neither reports an air-quality measurement.
- thedougd [[./thread#^c34367966|thedougd]] finds their gas range controllable but frustrating to clean and maintain, including burners that light slowly. illegalsmile [[./thread#^c34368172|illegalsmile]] similarly describes difficult grate cleaning after initially choosing gas because induction was unfamiliar and expensive. Their interest in switching is grounded in maintenance experience rather than demonstrated respiratory improvement.
- cameronhowe [[./thread#^c34367846|cameronhowe]] contrasts working with gas in a kitchen against using induction at home, favoring induction’s cleaning, responsiveness, and pan detection. Conversely, mikrl [[./thread#^c34369634|mikrl]] strongly preferred a rented home’s gas range to their current glass-top electric stove. These conflicting accounts involve different equipment and settings.
- the\_third\_wave [[./thread#^c34369387|the\_third\_wave]] reports adopting induction in a gas-free Dutch development in 1996, then later cooking and heating with wood in Sweden, where they own forest and land. Their preference emphasizes control over fuel costs. They provide no indoor smoke measurements or respiratory outcomes, so this is not evidence that wood offered cleaner personal exposure.

## Unresolved disagreements

- This unit contains no reported PM2.5, NO2, or NOx concentration measurements, sensor models, calibration checks, or controlled before-and-after tests. bob1029’s relative-emissions claim [[./thread#^c34367712|bob1029]], stephen\_g’s descriptions of studies [[./thread#^c34368045|stephen\_g]], and bedbot’s particulate explanation [[./thread#^c34367784|bedbot]] therefore cannot establish numerical exposure differences or sensor reliability. The relative contributions of combustion and cooking remain unresolved.
- The size and causality of respiratory effects remain unsettled within the supplied evidence. smileysteve [[./thread#^c34368084|smileysteve]] raises the difficulty of linking childhood asthma to gas exposure, while rsynnott [[./thread#^c34367669|rsynnott]] doubts evidence for a large effect. WithinReason’s electric-oven irritation [[./thread#^c34368167|WithinReason]] establishes a reported experience, not comparative asthma risk or the health benefit of replacing a gas stove.
- The thread does not determine when opening windows helps enough, or when it worsens exposure. throwaway22032 [[./thread#^c34367333|throwaway22032]] supplies a ventilation practice, and stephen\_g [[./thread#^c34368275|stephen\_g]] discusses exhaust pollution outdoors, but neither provides outdoor readings. There are no firsthand traffic- or wildfire-smoke comparisons that would support a window-opening rule for those conditions.
- Filtration and alternative protective measures are largely untested suggestions here. juancn [[./thread#^c34368635|juancn]] proposes ventilation and air filters without specifying filter performance; bombcar [[./thread#^c34367751|bombcar]] suspects microwaves and pressure cookers would produce fewer particles; guilhas [[./thread#^c34367935|guilhas]] suggests masks without specifications. None provides measurements establishing effectiveness against particles or combustion gases.
- Whether the natural-gas discussion transfers directly to propane remains unanswered. fatnoah [[./thread#^c34369623|fatnoah]], a propane-stove owner, reports finding mostly natural-gas information during a short search and asks for propane-specific evidence. The comment’s fuel-composition and energy-density observations do not establish comparative indoor exposure.
- There are no reported cognitive tests, sleep changes, or indoor CO2 readings in this source unit. The relevant firsthand outcomes concern respiratory irritation, as described by WithinReason [[./thread#^c34368167|WithinReason]], and cooking comfort, as described by hospadar [[./thread#^c34367606|hospadar]]. Those accounts cannot answer the approved question’s cognition, sleep, or personal CO2-exposure components.
