## Description

Discussion focuses on the public health consequences of air pollution, particularly possible cognitive effects associated with lead exposure and the persistence of lead in aviation fuel. Participants also consider indoor air filters and plants, asking which plants absorb CO2 and whether laboratory findings apply in real homes.
Tags: air pollution, lead exposure, public health, indoor air, houseplants

## Relevance

The comments connect air pollution to possible cognitive harm and discuss indoor filtration, plant-based CO2 reduction, and uncertainty about whether plant findings apply in real homes. ## Arguments

- bjourne [[./thread#^c21564441|bjourne]] argues that airborne lead from traffic may have damaged children’s cognition and contributed to rising crime from the 1960s through the 1990s. They cite temporal correlation with Western leaded-gasoline phaseouts and argue that pollution-related cognitive harm deserves greater concern. This is a causal hypothesis and ethical argument, not firsthand measurement or demonstrated causation.
- nl [[./thread#^c21561253|nl]] recommends combining an indoor air filter with plants selected for CO2 absorption under indoor lighting. The recommendation comes from reading; no personal intervention, exposure measurements, or resulting health improvements are reported.
- mhishambr [[./thread#^c21562947|mhishambr]] calls for action on air pollution but supplies no specific intervention, evidence, or experience.

## Counterarguments

- nl [[./thread#^c21561253|nl]] qualifies the plant recommendation by saying recent research finds plants poor at removing general particle pollution. Within their proposal, CO2 uptake and particle removal are separate purposes; the comment does not establish plants as a replacement for filtration.
- Following supercanuck’s question about effective plants [[./thread#^c21562459|supercanuck]], steve1977 [[./thread#^c21562800|steve1977]] asks whether they work under real-life conditions rather than laboratory conditions. Their airtight-box example is explicitly speculative, not a verified description of the linked experiment.

## Assumptions

- bjourne’s proposed lead–crime explanation [[./thread#^c21564441|bjourne]] assumes the historical correlation reflects a meaningful causal contribution. The supplied comment offers no exposure estimates, causal analysis, or assessment of competing explanations.
- Applying nl’s reported plant result [[./thread#^c21562771|nl]] to household advice assumes experimental CO2 uptake translates into a useful reduction in an occupied room. Room size, plant quantity, ventilation, and occupants’ CO2 production would matter to that inference, but are not supplied; steve1977 [[./thread#^c21562800|steve1977]] directly questions this transfer.
- nl’s combined recommendation [[./thread#^c21561253|nl]] assumes the selected filter and plants adequately address their respective targets. No filter specifications, particle-removal measurements, or evidence connecting the proposed CO2 reduction to better cognition or health appear in the comment.

## Conditions

- Answering supercanuck [[./thread#^c21562459|supercanuck]], nl [[./thread#^c21562771|nl]] reports that the prayer plant performed best among plants tested in the linked paper, lowering CO2 by 7\% over seven hours at 300 lux. This is a secondhand experimental result, not a household reading. Starting and ending CO2 concentrations, experimental volume, controls, and measurement uncertainty are absent from the supplied text.
- In this November 2019 discussion, postpawl [[./thread#^c21564537|postpawl]] replies to the historical gasoline-phaseout argument by identifying leaded aviation gasoline as an ongoing US source. They attribute to the FAA a figure of more than 167,000 piston-engine aircraft relying on avgas. That aircraft count provides geographic and source context, but does not quantify emissions or anyone’s personal exposure.
- bjourne [[./thread#^c21564441|bjourne]] places the gasoline phaseouts in most of the Western world during the 1980s and 1990s. Their historical argument should therefore retain those geographic and temporal limits.
