# [World air pollution map](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=10675653)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion evaluates a global air-pollution map's data, coverage, and presentation. Participants ask for clearer legends, units, pollutant distinctions, and explanations of dots and color scales; compare the map with other services; question discrepancies between cities and measurement coverage; and discuss the use of monitoring stations and modeled estimates.
Tags: air pollution, data visualization, mapping, measurement, air-quality index, user experience

## Relevance

Comments discuss a map covering NO2 and PM2.5, its government-monitoring and modeled data sources, and limitations in its scale, legend, and local accuracy. A commuter also reports that the map’s comparison conflicted with their impression of street-level air, illustrating uncertainty in interpreting the data. ## Arguments

- adorable [[./thread#^c10683403|adorable]], identifying himself as Plume Labs founder David, proposes using pollution forecasts to time running, cycling, and activities with children. This is a practical exposure-management proposal; the comment supplies no before-and-after exposure measurements or demonstrated health benefit.
- sdm [[./thread#^c10682541|sdm]] argues that reporting unspecified particulate matter obscures the distinction between PM10 and PM2.5. dstyrb [[./thread#^c10683897|dstyrb]] similarly requests separate PM2.5 and NO2 views. Their criticism concerns whether an aggregate index provides enough information for personal decisions, including what advice such as “take care” versus “take it easy” means.
- cmsmith [[./thread#^c10682136|cmsmith]] and arrayjumper [[./thread#^c10682108|arrayjumper]] request data provenance, while ljegou [[./thread#^c10682383|ljegou]] reports overlapping circles and missing scales. wodenokoto [[./thread#^c10685080|wodenokoto]] also cannot tell whether cloud-like regions and city circles share a scale. These objections identify barriers to interpreting exposure information; they do not establish that the underlying sensors are inaccurate.
- dstyrb [[./thread#^c10683897|dstyrb]] prefers AQICN for Chinese forecasts because it provides more specific information and intra-city maps. stinos [[./thread#^c10681942|stinos]], farresito [[./thread#^c10682335|farresito]], and analytically [[./thread#^c10683530|analytically]] also recommend alternative displays. These comments support checking more detailed local information, but provide no independent comparison of measurement accuracy.

## Counterarguments

- The data-source questions receive a substantive answer from adorable [[./thread#^c10683403|adorable]]: he says the map combines measurements from 11,000 monitoring stations with air-quality models, covering NO2, O3, PM10, and PM2.5. 11thEarlOfMar [[./thread#^c10682148|11thEarlOfMar]] quotes an article claiming roughly half a million daily data points from 11,000 stations. These are the founder’s account and a quoted article, not independent validation of coverage, calibration, or accuracy.
- Some allegedly missing information was available through navigation: smokinjuan [[./thread#^c10682431|smokinjuan]] says clicking a dot reveals a legend, and pavement [[./thread#^c10682445|pavement]] finds city pages labeled with the Plume Air Quality Index. Likewise, techaddict009 [[./thread#^c10682152|techaddict009]] initially requests country-based city selection, then corrects himself after finding it below the map [[./thread#^c10682157|techaddict009]]. These corrections address discoverability without resolving the requests for clearer information on the main map.
- In reply to sdm’s complaint about a scale ending at 150 [[./thread#^c10682541|sdm]], adventured [[./thread#^c10683288|adventured]] points out that reported concentrations around 600 micrograms per cubic meter in China or India do not correspond directly to the site’s scale. An index value and a mass concentration cannot simply be compared numerically; the thread does not establish the conversion or what the display limit conceals.
- AQICN is not treated as an unquestioned reference: aselzer [[./thread#^c10682525|aselzer]] suspects that areas measuring PM2.5 appear more polluted than areas lacking those measurements. This challenges the alternative-map recommendations, although the proposed coverage bias is not demonstrated through station inventories or matched readings.

## Assumptions

- deepGem [[./thread#^c10682213|deepGem]] guesses that the data mostly comes from government-owned sensors. adorable [[./thread#^c10683403|adorable]] later describes station measurements and modeling but does not establish station ownership. Government provenance should therefore remain an assumption.
- pavement [[./thread#^c10682445|pavement]] explicitly expresses uncertainty about the index calculation, suggesting that it combines NO2, ozone, carbon monoxide, and particles, with particulate source or toxicity mattering. adorable [[./thread#^c10683403|adorable]] subsequently names NO2, O3, PM10, and PM2.5 but supplies no weighting formula. Neither carbon-monoxide inclusion nor toxicity-based weighting is established by the supplied discussion.
- jrcii [[./thread#^c10682211|jrcii]] tentatively interprets darker colors as greater pollution and dot size as population. These are interface guesses, not documented encodings. The continuing questions from wodenokoto [[./thread#^c10685080|wodenokoto]] show that circle size and the relationship between circles and surrounding regions remain unclear.
- aries1980 [[./thread#^c10680158|aries1980]] uses smell and Vienna’s reported clean-air reputation to question London’s displayed ranking; aselzer [[./thread#^c10682525|aselzer]] uses population density and traffic to question comparisons involving Cologne, Dortmund, and Vienna. These are plausibility checks, not equivalent measurements of a specified pollutant over matched locations and periods.
- adorable [[./thread#^c10683403|adorable]] describes colors relative to WHO annual and daily recommendations. dstyrb [[./thread#^c10683897|dstyrb]] asks whether this means breathing a year’s worth of particles in one day. That interpretation is a question arising from unclear explanation, not a reported dose calculation or an established health implication.

## Conditions

- The usefulness of the display depends on local pollution severity. sdm [[./thread#^c10682541|sdm]] says that, as someone in China, a scale ending at 150 is unhelpful. adventured [[./thread#^c10683288|adventured]] mentions approximately 600 micrograms per cubic meter readings in China or India, but provides no pollutant designation, location, date, or firsthand measurement for those figures.
- Geographic resolution matters for traffic exposure. aries1980 [[./thread#^c10680158|aries1980]] discusses a specific daily cycling route to Oxford Street, whereas the comparison offered is with Vienna’s average. dstyrb [[./thread#^c10683897|dstyrb]] values intra-city maps. A street-level experience and a city-wide summary need not describe the same exposure.
- Coverage also varies geographically. adorable [[./thread#^c10683403|adorable]] says uncovered areas use models; simeondd [[./thread#^c10681975|simeondd]] reports Bulgaria missing from the map. dstyrb [[./thread#^c10683897|dstyrb]] asks whether public US embassy data could improve African coverage, but the comment does not establish where suitable feeds exist.
- Access to the information can vary by location and language: mynegation [[./thread#^c10682354|mynegation]] reports French interfaces for Toronto and Ottawa and an English interface for Montreal, with no visible language switch. This is a usability limitation, not evidence about those cities’ pollution.

## Lived experiences

- aries1980 [[./thread#^c10680158|aries1980]] reports cycling daily from East London to Oxford Street and finding the air inconsistent with a displayed comparison favoring Oxford Street over Vienna’s average: “My nose tells the opposite.” This is firsthand sensory experience along a traffic route, without personal PM2.5 or NO2 readings, quantified exposure, or reported medical effects. The claims about the cities’ pollution rankings rely on linked articles that are not reproduced here.
- sdm [[./thread#^c10682541|sdm]] reports finding the scale and particulate labeling unsuitable while in China. dstyrb [[./thread#^c10683897|dstyrb]] describes choosing AQICN for Chinese pollution forecasts because of its specificity. These are firsthand experiences using pollution information, rather than measurements taken with personal instruments.
- Responding to jrcii’s proposed color interpretation [[./thread#^c10682211|jrcii]], lobo\_tuerto [[./thread#^c10682279|lobo\_tuerto]] reports that Mexico City’s initial map color disagrees with the pollution level shown after clicking through. This is a concrete observed display inconsistency, but the thread does not identify its cause or which representation is more accurate.
- exabrial [[./thread#^c10683252|exabrial]] describes persistent smog or fog “when you walk around” in a reply referring to India and China. The account gives no particular city, date, instrument reading, or health symptom, and does not clearly establish the extent of the commenter’s firsthand experience. It cannot substantiate uniform conditions across either country.

No matched pollutant readings, station locations, timestamps, or personal exposure measurements settle those concerns.

This is a speculative country-level assessment versus an unspecified city-map observation; neither supplies a numerical Sofia reading or a common observation period.
- The founder’s explanation does not fully resolve index interpretation or reliability. adorable [[./thread#^c10683403|adorable]] supplies broad pollutant and source information, but dstyrb [[./thread#^c10683897|dstyrb]] still wants numerical detail and clearer threshold explanations, and lobo\_tuerto [[./thread#^c10682279|lobo\_tuerto]] reports inconsistent displays. The supplied comments contain no sensor calibration tests, uncertainty estimates, or explanation of how modeled and measured values are combined.
- Health consequences are discussed only indirectly. stinos [[./thread#^c10681942|stinos]] recalls an unavailable map estimating life-expectancy reductions from combined pollution, without reproducing estimates or methods. aries1980 [[./thread#^c10680158|aries1980]] supplies an odor-based observation, not a measured health or comfort outcome. This unit supplies no firsthand evidence about cognition, sleep, or symptom changes and no study findings sufficient to evaluate those outcomes.
- This source unit does not establish whether opening windows, changing ventilation, using filtration, or changing cooking practices improves personal exposure, nor does it report wildfire-smoke interventions or their costs and tradeoffs. adorable [[./thread#^c10683403|adorable]] proposes timing outdoor activities, but reports no intervention results. mikeash [[./thread#^c10682625|mikeash]] distinguishes the map from CO2-emissions arguments; that reply provides no indoor CO2 measurements or evidence about ventilation.
