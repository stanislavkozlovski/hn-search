# [Ask HN: How to filter incoming air to a building](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=24212898)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion addresses how to keep wildfire smoke out of homes while maintaining ventilation. Replies favor recirculating indoor air through high-airflow HEPA filtration or DIY filter fans, while the asker distinguishes that from the challenge of filtering incoming air drawn in by exhaust systems and explores heat-recovery ventilators and intake filters.
Tags: wildfire smoke, indoor air quality, air filtration, ventilation, HVAC

## Relevance

The discussion directly addresses wildfire smoke, filtering indoor air, furnace filters, HEPA and DIY filtration, and the tradeoff between recirculation and bringing in fresh air. It also raises uncertainty about how intake products perform under smoke conditions and the need to manage ventilation despite a leaky home. ## Arguments

- Xcelerate [[./thread#^c24213102|Xcelerate]] recommends recirculating indoor air and adding a high-airflow HEPA purifier because smoke can enter through building leaks. Their emphasis is on the volume of air filtered. They also suggest activated charcoal for some smell-causing VOCs, while warning that thin charcoal filters may have short useful lives.
- Supporting Xcelerate, dredmorbius [[./thread#^c24213457|dredmorbius]] argues that repeated indoor filtration can outperform filtering incoming air once. This is a proposed advantage of multiple passes, not a measured comparison of intake and recirculation systems in the poster’s home.
- dredmorbius [[./thread#^c24213457|dredmorbius]] cites an external comparison reporting PM2.5 reductions after 50 minutes of 71\% with a box-fan filter and 89\% with a HEPA purifier. They present DIY filtration as a cheaper, somewhat less effective option. These are quoted third-party results, not firsthand measurements; the supplied comment lacks enough experimental detail to establish performance in other rooms or smoke conditions.

## Counterarguments

- Replying to the recirculation recommendation, jxramos [[./thread#^c24215010|jxramos]] explains that exhaust fans and an exhaust-only air conditioner pull outdoor air inside, creating a need for a controlled, filtered intake. In a separate reply to the box-fan suggestion, jxramos [[./thread#^c24216506|jxramos]] reiterates that they already have recirculating filters. Their objection concerns the remaining ventilation problem, rather than disputing that indoor filtration removes particles.
- Xcelerate [[./thread#^c24213102|Xcelerate]] doubts that most box fans can move enough air through a filter, although they describe a reportedly successful high-MERV filter and squirrel-cage fan assembly seen on Reddit. dredmorbius [[./thread#^c24213457|dredmorbius]] counters the broader skepticism with the linked PM2.5 comparison. Neither provides their own airflow measurements, and the examples involve different equipment.
- verdverm [[./thread#^c24213345|verdverm]] proposes two filters, a box fan, and painter’s tape. Replying directly, dredmorbius [[./thread#^c24213491|dredmorbius]] argues that one filter can be nearly as effective or better because of increased airflow, while costing less and conserving supplies. They recommend sealing the filter to the fan’s intake side. The thread provides no measurements comparing these particular arrangements.

## Assumptions

- Xcelerate [[./thread#^c24213102|Xcelerate]] assumes ordinary building leakage makes filtering all incoming smoke impractical. jxramos [[./thread#^c24215010|jxramos]] accepts that making a house completely airtight requires substantial work, but still seeks a controlled intake. Neither supplies leakage measurements or establishes how much infiltration a filtered intake would prevent.
- jxramos [[./thread#^c24215010|jxramos]] treats fresh-air exchange as necessary for humidity, oxygen, and stuffiness. These are their stated ventilation concerns; the comment contains no oxygen, humidity, or CO2 readings and does not establish which factor is causing discomfort.
- dredmorbius [[./thread#^c24213457|dredmorbius]] cites 5–6 air exchanges per hour for homes or bedrooms and a minimum of 0.35 per hour. Their discussion moves between outdoor-air exchange and repeated filtration of indoor air without clearly separating the two. The supplied material does not establish that these rates describe equivalent processes or requirements.

## Conditions

- The California wildfire setting makes outdoor-air quality central to the decision. jxramos [[./thread#^c24215010|jxramos]] describes exhaust equipment that draws air indoors, while jxramos [[./thread#^c24216506|jxramos]] questions whether intake products intended for ordinary pollen and dust can perform adequately in smoke. Advice for routine ventilation therefore has an unresolved limit under these conditions.
- The window-intake options collected by jxramos [[./thread#^c24214894|jxramos]] expose the practical tradeoff: admitting outdoor air could address the desired air exchange while also admitting wildfire pollution if filtration is inadequate. This is an implication of the proposals, not a reported window-opening experiment. No outdoor pollution threshold, favorable opening time, or measured open-versus-closed comparison is supplied.
- Cost and availability influence the proposed improvements. dredmorbius [[./thread#^c24213457|dredmorbius]] describes DIY filtration as costing a fraction of manufactured HEPA units, and dredmorbius [[./thread#^c24213491|dredmorbius]] favors one filter partly to reduce expense and preserve supplies. Xcelerate [[./thread#^c24213102|Xcelerate]] urges prompt purchasing because they expect purifiers to sell out; this is a forecast, not a documented shortage. No prices or operating costs are given.

## Lived experiences

- jxramos [[./thread#^c24215010|jxramos]] reports buying three Honeywell air filters the previous year and running them continuously. They also report outdoor air being pulled in by exhaust fans and an exhaust-only air conditioner. This is firsthand equipment-use experience, but it includes no measured particle reduction, pressure differential, or health, sleep, or cognitive outcome.
- jxramos [[./thread#^c24214894|jxramos]] reports calling an HVAC company and learning about a heat-recovery ventilator that exchanges heat and filters incoming air; the company reportedly stocks Fantech products, with filters not designed for smoke-filled intake air. They also collect grow-room fan/carbon-filter combinations, a DIY window filter, a passive carbon window product, and inline filter boxes. The call is firsthand information gathering; the product capabilities and everyday-dust performance are reported information, not their own smoke tests.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The discussion never resolves how to combine effective smoke removal with the filtered replacement air jxramos [[./thread#^c24215010|jxramos]] requests. Xcelerate [[./thread#^c24213102|Xcelerate]] prioritizes recirculation because of leakage, while jxramos [[./thread#^c24216506|jxramos]] emphasizes that recirculation already exists and intake remains unsolved. These positions address overlapping but different needs and need not be mutually exclusive.
- Actual wildfire performance remains unknown for the proposed ventilator, window products, carbon filters, and inline systems. jxramos [[./thread#^c24214894|jxramos]] explicitly distinguishes reported everyday-dust performance from unknown smoke performance, and jxramos [[./thread#^c24216506|jxramos]] identifies this as the largest remaining uncertainty. No firsthand intake-versus-recirculation trial settles the choice.
- Health effects are largely unexamined. Xcelerate [[./thread#^c24213102|Xcelerate]] explicitly does not know whether smoke odor itself is harmful or whether the concern is the particulates, although they prefer removing the smell. dredmorbius [[./thread#^c24213457|dredmorbius]] links a possible COVID-19 benefit without presenting supporting findings in the comment. Neither establishes a health outcome from the proposed interventions.
- For this source unit, there are no firsthand PM2.5 readings, NO2 or CO2 measurements, sensor reliability assessments, or reports connecting measurements to cognition or sleep. The quantitative particle result is externally cited by dredmorbius [[./thread#^c24213457|dredmorbius]]; jxramos [[./thread#^c24215010|jxramos]] supplies equipment experience and ventilation concerns. Cooking and traffic exposure are not examined, so this discussion cannot settle those parts of the broader question.
