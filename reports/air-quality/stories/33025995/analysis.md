# [Many indoor air quality sensor products are a scam](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=33025995)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

The discussion examines whether consumer indoor-air monitors provide trustworthy measurements, with particular concern about devices that estimate gases from proxy sensors, sensor placement and self-heating, calibration, and misleading absolute readings. Participants distinguish sensors that can still be useful for tracking changes from those suitable for precise measurements, and compare consumer, professional, and DIY options. The thread also explores independent product testing, reviewers’ incentives, unreliable online reviews, changing product designs, privacy concerns around connected devices, and the cost of building accurate monitors.
Tags: indoor air quality, sensors, measurement accuracy, consumer products, product testing, DIY, privacy

## Relevance

The discussion directly assesses indoor air sensors, including CO2, PM2.5, and VOC readings. Firsthand comparisons report both useful changes with ventilation or cooking and unreliable or inconsistent absolute readings; commenters also discuss sensor types, calibration, placement, self-heating, and the limits of low-cost devices. ## Arguments

- Relative readings can guide useful actions even without precise absolute concentrations. com2kid [[./thread#^c33026516|com2kid]] uses changes to decide when to open an office window or increase kitchen extraction; cosentiyes [[./thread#^c33028370|cosentiyes]] similarly values consistent changes over exact numbers. Their argument depends on reliable responsiveness, rather than establishing that every inexpensive monitor provides it.
- Direct CO2 sensing matters. Majromax [[./thread#^c33026854|Majromax]] corrects the suggestion that VOCs are usually inferred from CO2: inexpensive devices may instead estimate CO2 from VOC-related measurements, a correction clairity [[./thread#^c33027667|clairity]] accepts. briandrupieski [[./thread#^c33026550|briandrupieski]] recommends checking for NDIR sensing. mrsteveman1 [[./thread#^c33030569|mrsteveman1]] adds that photoacoustic SCD4x devices also measure actual CO2, so conventional NDIR is not the only legitimate approach.
- The practical accounts support choosing ventilation according to outdoor conditions. site-packages1 [[./thread#^c33026479|site-packages1]] reports falling CO2 after opening windows and exhausting air with a fan; palisade [[./thread#^c33030460|palisade]] instead closes windows during outdoor pollution and observes improved air-quality readings with a HEPA purifier. These are reports about different interventions and signals, not evidence that filtration substitutes for CO2 ventilation.
- Clean surroundings do not establish clean indoor air. z9znz [[./thread#^c33027790|z9znz]] identifies building materials, paint, veneers, adhesives and fabrics as potential indoor sources. Maursault [[./thread#^c33029296|Maursault]], replying in that thread, already considers their old rural home dusty and problematic despite perceiving the surrounding outdoor air as unusually clean.
- Finished-device design can matter as much as the sensor component. AirGradient developer ahaucnx [[./thread#^c33027349|ahaucnx]] describes heat from processors, particle sensors and CO2 sensors interfering with temperature measurement, and proposes lower power consumption, component separation and convection through the enclosure. advisedwang [[./thread#^c33026473|advisedwang]] stresses that specifications and repeated testing are necessary to establish whether an engineering solution actually works.
- Useful product evaluation requires reference measurements and disclosed limitations. monopoliessuck [[./thread#^c33026599|monopoliessuck]] calls for comparisons against a known-good control, while bsilvereagle [[./thread#^c33035814|bsilvereagle]] values identifiable, replaceable sensors with accessible datasheets. s0rce [[./thread#^c33026677|s0rce]] points to South Coast AQMD laboratory and field evaluations, but the supplied discussion contains the recommendation, not those evaluation results.

## Counterarguments

- The blanket claim that consumer CO2 and particle monitors are worthless is disputed by conflicting ownership reports. bsder [[./thread#^c33026626|bsder]] says their purchases never correlated with reality; \_yb2s [[./thread#^c33027221|\_yb2s]], replying directly, describes inexpensive devices whose CO2 responds as expected and whose outdoor PM2.5 readings closely match PurpleAir. Neither account establishes performance across the market.
- Responsiveness cannot simply be assumed because a device displays changing numbers. therealdrag0 [[./thread#^c33029282|therealdrag0]] reports substantial apparent under-response during wildfire smoke, while rgbrenner [[./thread#^c33029666|rgbrenner]] reports three adjacent Awair units giving widely different readings. These experiences limit the argument that approximate trends are always sufficient.
- Agreement between devices is not proof of accuracy. monopoliessuck [[./thread#^c33026599|monopoliessuck]] explicitly warns that two agreeing products can share the same error. autoexec [[./thread#^c33026436|autoexec]] recognizes this limitation in a proposed plan to buy monitors three at a time; that comment describes a shopping strategy, not a completed validation experiment.
- Some apparent inconsistency reflects measurement uncertainty rather than a defective instrument. FiatLuxDave [[./thread#^c33030534|FiatLuxDave]] explains that radon readings such as 2.4 and 1.9 pCi/L can agree within typical uncertainty, despite displays showing tenths. They attribute substantial uncertainty to low radioactive-event counts and emphasize that numerical resolution is not accuracy.
- The suggestion to dispense with monitoring and simply open a window, made by 2OEH8eoCRo0 [[./thread#^c33027301|2OEH8eoCRo0]], encounters concrete limits. mdp2021 [[./thread#^c33032214|mdp2021]] raises indoor cooling and heating costs during an energy crisis; palisade [[./thread#^c33030460|palisade]] describes closing windows when outdoor pollution rises. Opening windows is therefore conditional on both exposure and comfort.
- Commenters disagree about calling inaccurate products scams. zokier [[./thread#^c33026296|zokier]] reserves the term for malicious intent; IggleSniggle [[./thread#^c33026594|IggleSniggle]] considers it reasonable when a product fails its advertised function despite easily available checks. NoPicklez [[./thread#^c33029925|NoPicklez]] distinguishes imperfect measurement from non-functionality and asks whether acceptable error and disclosure standards should determine the boundary.

## Assumptions

- CO2 is sometimes treated as a ventilation indicator rather than a complete air-quality assessment. dawnerd [[./thread#^c33027775|dawnerd]] explicitly infers a need to ventilate from elevated CO2 despite lacking particle and other measurements. That inference does not establish concentrations of PM2.5, NO2 or specific VOCs, particularly given the proxy-measurement problem described by Majromax [[./thread#^c33026854|Majromax]].
- Outdoor calibration assumes an appropriate reference concentration. s0rce [[./thread#^c33026699|s0rce]] suggests treating outdoor air as roughly 400 ppm, whereas sbierwagen [[./thread#^c33027147|sbierwagen]] describes approximately 420 ppm and problems with an automatic baseline of 390 ppm. These are 2022 comments; neither supplies a contemporaneous reference-gas measurement for the particular calibration location.
- Expected behavior and agreement with another consumer device are weaker evidence than independent calibration. \_yb2s [[./thread#^c33027221|\_yb2s]] compares readings with expected ventilation behavior and PurpleAir, while e63f67dd-065b [[./thread#^c33026430|e63f67dd-065b]] compares a device with a nearby airport weather station. Such comparisons can reveal concerns, but the comments do not establish identical sampling conditions or reference uncertainty.
- Health thresholds are asserted more confidently than they are demonstrated here. sbierwagen [[./thread#^c33026571|sbierwagen]] claims noticeable physiological effects begin above 1000 ppm, and gsich [[./thread#^c33029604|gsich]] suggests negative effects around that concentration. Neither provides study results in the supplied text; these statements should not be treated as an established safety boundary or a demonstrated cognitive threshold.
- Perceived cleanliness, odors and visible dust are not pollutant-specific measurements. Maursault [[./thread#^c33027366|Maursault]] judges outdoor air favorably from experience and indoor air poorly from visible particles; everyone [[./thread#^c33026938|everyone]] asks whether an open-window room amid Irish dairy fields has good air. Neither account determines PM2.5 concentration, particle composition or gaseous exposure.
- Evidence comes from different positions and should retain those distinctions. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c33027349|ahaucnx]] reports development experience as an AirGradient supplier; S201 [[./thread#^c33027244|S201]] cites a PurpleAir correction paper while promoting their own alternative; runjake [[./thread#^c33026908|runjake]] relays favorable opinions from three scientists. These are respectively manufacturer testimony, a cited research claim with a disclosed project interest, and secondhand endorsement—not interchangeable independent validation.

## Conditions

- Rural versus urban location is insufficient to predict exposure. In response to everyone's description of rural Ireland [[./thread#^c33026938|everyone]], megous [[./thread#^c33027704|megous]] says nearby heating sources can make village air unpleasant in colder months, while remotely heated urban areas can fare better away from main roads. This is a conditional argument, not a measured rural–urban comparison.
- Outdoor particle measurements may depend on weather and aerosol type. matheweis [[./thread#^c33044237|matheweis]], living with frequent fog, says research led them to consider a heated sampling inlet to prevent fog-related errors; they do not report a completed build or validation. S201 [[./thread#^c33027244|S201]] separately says PurpleAir needs a correction factor during smoke events to better match government instruments.
- The price estimates are historical and illustrative. shadowpho [[./thread#^c33028108|shadowpho]] estimates approximately \$50 each for CO2 and particle components and a finished multisensor product above \$350. sbierwagen [[./thread#^c33026571|sbierwagen]] reports a roughly \$60 DIY CO2 sensor, while ThrowawayR2 [[./thread#^c33029860|ThrowawayR2]] identifies a commercial HVAC option below \$500 that is not intended for standalone use. Integration, enclosure design and testing complicate price comparisons.
- Housing affordability constrains available improvements. Maursault [[./thread#^c33029296|Maursault]] remains in a dusty, deteriorating 1940s home because rent is cheap, runs a bedroom box fan with a HEPA filter continuously and spends daytime outdoors. They hope filtration helps but supply no measured reduction or health outcome.
- Radon monitoring involves cost, duration and representativeness tradeoffs. FiatLuxDave [[./thread#^c33030534|FiatLuxDave]] contrasts a roughly \$25 charcoal kit with much more sensitive equipment costing over \$10,000, describes measurements often requiring 48 hours or longer, and suggests hiring better equipment through a specialist. They also recall a household with shower-associated radon from well water, illustrating how a short snapshot can miss intermittent exposure.
- Data access and privacy affect practical usability. EVa5I7bHFq9mnYK [[./thread#^c33028098|EVa5I7bHFq9mnYK]] disconnected a networked CO2 detector because occupancy patterns could be inferred from its data; no breach is reported. sliken [[./thread#^c33027409|sliken]] values local data export but is unsure about that capability on the View Plus. robga [[./thread#^c33026933|robga]] uses four AirGradients with ESPHome and Home Assistant, while izacus [[./thread#^c33027631|izacus]] considers an API worth a relatively high purchase price.
- Recommendations depend on product version and availability. ThrowawayR2 [[./thread#^c33026848|ThrowawayR2]] warns that manufacturers can change internals without maintaining the tested design. AirGradient developer ahaucnx [[./thread#^c33028205|ahaucnx]] acknowledges temperature inaccuracies in an older compact kit and claims a newer design addresses them; newman314 [[./thread#^c33027155|newman314]] encountered unavailable stock. A favorable report therefore cannot automatically validate every revision or similarly branded model.

## Lived experiences

- rufus\_foreman [[./thread#^c33028833|rufus\_foreman]] reports approximate home-office CO2 readings of 430 after windows and fans have been running, 700 after a short closure, 1200 after a long closure, and 4000 with windows closed while someone cooks downstairs on an unvented gas stove. The comment provides a useful within-home pattern but does not identify the monitor or establish its calibration.
- After six months with several Airthings View Plus units, site-packages1 [[./thread#^c33026479|site-packages1]] reports PM2.5 rising during fires, CO2 rising with closed windows and falling after ventilation, and VOC readings tracking an old air conditioner. sliken [[./thread#^c33027409|sliken]] similarly reports cooking and forest-fire responses, a substantial bedroom CO2 spike around 3 a.m., and monitoring around radon mitigation without providing its numerical outcome.
- izacus [[./thread#^c33027631|izacus]] says Airthings readings matched smells and stale-room sensations and helped substantially improve sleep, but does not identify the decisive intervention or pollutant. mrspuratic [[./thread#^c33034674|mrspuratic]] reports becoming cranky while working around a displayed 1200 ppm CO2, versus a low near 400 ppm; the same device shows cooking-related PM/VOC changes and beverage-related VOC changes. These are subjective associations, not cognitive testing.
- genewitch [[./thread#^c33040687|genewitch]] bought two unnamed monitors to investigate whether CO2 contributed to a spouse's insomnia. They report outdoor readings around 500 ppm, a response to dry ice, and strong cooking-related AQI changes, while explicitly declining to trust the devices for VOC or formaldehyde problems. No finding about the insomnia is reported.
- palisade [[./thread#^c33030460|palisade]] reports an IQAir AirVisual Pro responding to ovens, visiting groups, factory fires and California forest fires, and air-quality readings improving when a Honeywell HPA300 HEPA purifier runs. They close doors and windows when outdoor air worsens and find its Germ setting quiet enough. The account contains no numerical particle reduction; the broader claim that smoke reaches Maine is not presented as their own measurement there.
- therealdrag0 [[./thread#^c33029282|therealdrag0]] describes an \$80 monitor that usually stayed low and poorly tracked online AQI. During visible wildfire smoke reported as 150 AQI, it remained below 50 about 80\% of the time and below 100 otherwise. They consider it untrustworthy, although the comparison was not described as a colocated reference-instrument test.
- cbhl [[./thread#^c33027396|cbhl]] bought a Wirecutter-recommended Kaiterra Laser Egg+ Chemical and suspected it estimated CO2 from TVOC because its CO2 readings disagreed with a same-brand Laser Egg+ CO2. They say Wirecutter subsequently changed its recommendation to a different sensor type. This is an ownership comparison and suspected mechanism, not a documented teardown or calibration.
- Several users describe clear responses to everyday emissions. SrslyJosh [[./thread#^c33027039|SrslyJosh]] reports Awair VOC responses to food, hair products and newly unpacked items; tclancy [[./thread#^c33028269|tclancy]] reports an unusually severe indicated deterioration during indoor painting. odysseus [[./thread#^c33026741|odysseus]] describes Winix purifiers increasing fan speed after flatulence. These observations establish perceived responsiveness, not contaminant identity, toxicity or removal efficiency.
- jeffreygoesto [[./thread#^c33026173|jeffreygoesto]] built a bedside Pi system whose relative signal helps identify dirty nappies without unnecessarily interrupting a child settling to sleep, later identifying the sensor as a Bosch BME680 [[./thread#^c33031616|jeffreygoesto]]. Separately, s0rce [[./thread#^c33026715|s0rce]] reports using sensors in a laboratory to detect solvent-vapor changes while finding absolute quantification difficult.
- Side-by-side reliability experiences are mixed. rgbrenner [[./thread#^c33029666|rgbrenner]] reports three adjacent Awairs disagreeing; newman314 [[./thread#^c33027155|newman314]] returned two Vitalight CO2 monitors after inconsistent readings persisted through recalibration; wil421 [[./thread#^c33026358|wil421]] describes Microcenter sensors requiring smoothing and discarded readings. By contrast, williamscales [[./thread#^c33030840|williamscales]] owns two Aranets and recalls receiving calibration information, but supplies no comparative results.
- Syonyk [[./thread#^c33027587|Syonyk]] reports dismantling a TemTop P1000, generally consistent readings across two TemTop devices, and CO2 and VOC signals changing independently. EVa5I7bHFq9mnYK [[./thread#^c33045301|EVa5I7bHFq9mnYK]] compared a Dienmern DM72B's temperature, humidity and CO2 against other devices, but explicitly did not calibrate PM2.5, formaldehyde or TVOC. Their favorable judgment of those channels rests on plausible responses, including smoke detection.
- Airthings ownership reports include unresolved faults alongside favorable comparisons. RL\_Quine [[./thread#^c33026696|RL\_Quine]] reports an apparently faulty unit and a prompt engineering request for more data; InitialLastName [[./thread#^c33030084|InitialLastName]] objects that this is not a replacement. f1shy [[./thread#^c33026675|f1shy]] suspects pressure error and describes dismissive support. queuebert [[./thread#^c33027871|queuebert]] reports agreement between their Airthings radon readings and a mail-in test, without reporting values or uncertainty.
- sbierwagen [[./thread#^c33027147|sbierwagen]] reports an SCD30 automatic-calibration routine setting a 390 ppm floor at seemingly inappropriate times. Their workaround was to disable it, expose the sensor outdoors for an hour and apply a software offset. They also describe self-heating and removing a decorative cover to improve ventilation. This is a firsthand workaround, not validation against certified reference gas.
- AirGradient developer ahaucnx [[./thread#^c33027349|ahaucnx]] says accurate temperature measurement took eight hardware iterations. In competitor testing, they report a device reading near ambient despite substantial internal heat; applying a strong fan then produced a reading more than 2°C below ambient, which they interpret as evidence of a compensating software offset [[./thread#^c33027462|ahaucnx]].
- Dust sources and filtration benefits remain unquantified in two older homes. Maursault [[./thread#^c33029296|Maursault]] suspects deteriorating wall materials and hopes a continuously running bedroom filter helps. Sunspark [[./thread#^c33031648|Sunspark]] reports persistent lint-like dust despite an outdoor exhaust duct and furnace filtration believed to be MERV 11. Maursault [[./thread#^c33036142|Maursault]] distinguishes bedding-associated fluff from darker accumulated dust and offers bedding and detergent explanations as hypotheses.
- Other exposure accounts lack pollutant-specific reference measurements. Mountain\_Skies [[./thread#^c33029353|Mountain\_Skies]] reports an inexpensive monitor rising in a garage containing cars, a lawnmower and stored fuel, then falling outdoors. dawnerd [[./thread#^c33027775|dawnerd]] reports surprisingly high CO2 on flights with an Aranet4, but gives no concentrations or comparison with aircraft ventilation specifications.
- Temperature experiences illustrate both apparent agreement and placement problems. londons\_explore [[./thread#^c33027793|londons\_explore]] found about five brands agreeing within 0.1°C after a night in a sealed box. e63f67dd-065b [[./thread#^c33026430|e63f67dd-065b]] found discouraging temperature and humidity differences against a nearby airport, despite an outdoor CO2 reading of 410. LorenPechtel [[./thread#^c33028853|LorenPechtel]] reports up to roughly 2° of lag-related error in an outdoor thermometer used to decide when opening windows would be comfortable.

## Unresolved disagreements

- This source unit does not establish NO2 concentrations or quantified traffic exposure. rufus\_foreman's gas-stove account reports CO2 [[./thread#^c33028833|rufus\_foreman]]; megous offers a near-road caveat without measurements [[./thread#^c33027704|megous]]; Mountain\_Skies reports unspecified garage-related readings [[./thread#^c33029353|Mountain\_Skies]]. These observations cannot supply missing NO2 measurements or separate traffic exhaust from other sources.
- The health and cognition questions remain largely unanswered. izacus reports improved sleep [[./thread#^c33027631|izacus]], mrspuratic reports irritability around a displayed CO2 level [[./thread#^c33034674|mrspuratic]], and genewitch reports an investigation into insomnia without an outcome [[./thread#^c33040687|genewitch]]. The supplied accounts do not isolate CO2 from co-varying exposures or establish clinical benefits, cognitive impairment, or a reliable effect threshold.
- The best balance between ventilation and keeping polluted outdoor air out is not quantified. site-packages1 reports lower CO2 with open windows and an exhaust fan [[./thread#^c33026479|site-packages1]], while palisade reports improved air-quality readings with windows closed and filtration during outdoor pollution [[./thread#^c33030460|palisade]]. Neither provides simultaneous measurements sufficient to determine an optimal compromise, and mdp2021 adds an unpriced heating penalty [[./thread#^c33032214|mdp2021]].
- Reliable product rankings remain unsettled. \_yb2s reports success with inexpensive devices [[./thread#^c33027221|\_yb2s]], therealdrag0 reports apparent wildfire under-reading [[./thread#^c33029282|therealdrag0]], and cbhl reports a recommendation that apparently used estimated CO2 [[./thread#^c33027396|cbhl]]. The discussion lacks a common reference instrument, shared protocol and model-revision controls that would reconcile these accounts.
- Software temperature compensation remains a design disagreement. ahaucnx views the fan-induced below-ambient result as evidence of offset-related risk [[./thread#^c33027462|ahaucnx]]. In reply, ivoc defends partially using an offset after design validation and says a strong fan blowing into their sensor is outside supported conditions [[./thread#^c33032880|ivoc]]. The exchange does not establish which approach performs best across ordinary installations.
- Indoor plants as CO2 control are disputed without measurements. genewitch proposes plants as CO2 scrubbers [[./thread#^c33040593|genewitch]]; kolinko calls practical indoor scrubbing ineffective and claims an apartment would need a very large planted area [[./thread#^c33173482|kolinko]]. Neither supplies measured removal rates, lighting conditions or a household trial, so the thread does not validate plants as a substitute for ventilation.
- VOC responsiveness remains distinct from knowing health-relevant exposure. SrslyJosh observes responses to many unrelated household sources [[./thread#^c33027039|SrslyJosh]], while genewitch explicitly distrusts their monitor's VOC and formaldehyde readings [[./thread#^c33040687|genewitch]]. Even the direction of the nappy detector's plotted change receives different speculative explanations from qqqwerty [[./thread#^c33026369|qqqwerty]] and notduncansmith [[./thread#^c33026535|notduncansmith]]. No supplied calibration resolves compound identity, concentration or associated risk.
