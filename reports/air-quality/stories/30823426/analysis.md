# [New type of ultraviolet light makes indoor air as safe as outdoors](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=30823426)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion of far-UVC lighting as a possible way to reduce airborne pathogens indoors. Participants examine how novel the approach is, its claimed efficacy and safety for skin and eyes, possible effects on materials and chemistry, ozone concerns, alternatives such as ventilation and HVAC disinfection, costs, and potential applications in homes and institutions.
Tags: far-uvc, indoor-air-quality, infection-control, ultraviolet-safety, ventilation, air-disinfection

## Relevance

The thread weighs an air-treatment technology against ventilation and filtration, with discussion of PM2.5 and other pollutants, possible chemical by-products, and safety uncertainties. Most comments focus on infection control and far-UVC safety rather than the requested pollutants. ## Arguments

- a1371 [[./thread#^c30825422|a1371]] distinguishes protection against airborne viruses from broader air safety, explicitly including PM2.5 and other airborne compounds. fennecs [[./thread#^c30825891|fennecs]] similarly prioritizes mould, microplastics, microfibres and car pollution over making ordinary buildings sterile. Their argument supports ventilation and particle control alongside any disinfection treatment; neither supplies pollutant measurements.
- \_Microft [[./thread#^c30823685|\_Microft]] quotes the article's claimed 184 equivalent air changes per hour from far-UVC, compared with a claimed practical range of 5–20 for other approaches. avnigo [[./thread#^c30828116|avnigo]] quotes more than 98\% microbial inactivation within five minutes. These are reported research results supporting microbial air treatment, rather than measurements made by commenters in their homes.
- novok [[./thread#^c30827577|novok]] proposes UV as an additional HVAC treatment stage, keeping occupants away from irradiation and potentially avoiding the extra airflow resistance of filter material. The suggested advantages include easier retrofits and less replacement work, but the comment contains no measured electricity use, maintenance history or comparison with particle filtration.
- someguydave [[./thread#^c30826438|someguydave]] argues that illuminating an occupied room can treat airborne microbes faster than waiting for air to circulate through ducts. rocqua [[./thread#^c30829748|rocqua]] adds that room irradiation could disinfect exposed contaminated surfaces. These proposed advantages concern microbial control; the comments do not establish reductions in particulate or gaseous pollution.
- Spooky23 [[./thread#^c30829606|Spooky23]] proposes opening windows as an inexpensive ventilation improvement, while explicitly acknowledging that this does not solve particulate exposure when outdoor particles are a problem. The claimed '90\% of the benefit at 5\% of the cost' is an unsupported estimate, not a reported experiment or cost analysis.
- bonestamp2 [[./thread#^c30827724|bonestamp2]] proposes operating room lamps only when movement sensors indicate no occupants; avnigo [[./thread#^c30828116|avnigo]] suggests intermittent operation according to occupancy and ventilation; ggm [[./thread#^c30827488|ggm]] suggests nightly treatment with a mobile unit. These are proposed ways to limit irradiation or equipment requirements, without measurements demonstrating that their schedules provide adequate protection.

## Counterarguments

- angry\_octet [[./thread#^c30825993|angry\_octet]] [[./thread#^c30826101|angry\_octet]] argues that UV treatment can introduce chemical exposure risks through reactions involving existing pollutants and cleaning agents, including sodium hypochlorite. sandworm101 [[./thread#^c30826633|sandworm101]] also worries about plastics and medicines degrading. These are concerns about unintended effects, supported partly by linked literature, without household measurements of resulting compounds.
- Responding to ozone concerns, hausen [[./thread#^c30824730|hausen]] reports that another far-UV excimer-lamp study measured less than 0.005 ppm ozone, compared with a stated FDA medical-device maximum of 0.05 ppm. This challenges the suggestion that substantial ozone production is inevitable, but it is a report of one study's measurements, with no measurement protocol or residential exposure data supplied in the discussion.
- andrewflnr [[./thread#^c30824437|andrewflnr]] argues that wording such as 'may be safe' does not justify immediate widespread deployment. exmadscientist [[./thread#^c30852660|exmadscientist]] makes a narrower methodological objection to linked skin-safety research: measuring familiar DNA lesions might miss damage products associated with a different wavelength. The commenter explicitly distinguishes insufficient evidence from proving that the researchers' conclusions are false.
- thedougd [[./thread#^c30830600|thedougd]] challenges the assumption that installing a UV bulb in an HVAC system reliably disinfects passing air: exposure time may be insufficient, and plastic or rubber components may deteriorate. The commenter says some installations are better at keeping evaporator coils clean. solarmist [[./thread#^c30859432|solarmist]] asks for supporting reading, which is not supplied in this export.
- mrfusion [[./thread#^c30824654|mrfusion]] invokes the hygiene hypothesis against indiscriminate sanitization, while samvher [[./thread#^c30824751|samvher]] worries about disrupting microbes on skin and food. These are proposed biological tradeoffs, not reports of immune problems or microbiome changes caused by the discussed lamps.
- manmal [[./thread#^c30827367|manmal]] favors investigating far-UVC in operating rooms and hospitals with resistant bacteria, but rocqua [[./thread#^c30829869|rocqua]] points out that a safety mechanism relying on an outer dead-skin layer might not protect exposed surgical tissues. In reply, manmal [[./thread#^c30864914|manmal]] suggests strong irradiation before surgery, changing the proposed exposure setting rather than resolving safety during surgery.

## Assumptions

- The comparison presented by \_Microft [[./thread#^c30823685|\_Microft]] uses equivalent air changes as a microbial-inactivation benchmark. It does not demonstrate 184 actual outdoor-air exchanges or corresponding removal of PM2.5, NO2 or CO2. The commenter's estimated room wind speed is an illustration of hypothetical physical air replacement, not an airflow measurement.
- oneepic [[./thread#^c30824447|oneepic]] notes that the discussed room experiment used airborne Staphylococcus rather than COVID-19. mattbaker [[./thread#^c30824529|mattbaker]] replies with the article's rationale that this organism is slightly less sensitive to far-UVC than coronaviruses. Applying that experiment to coronavirus protection therefore depends on the stated surrogate-organism argument; it is not a reported occupied-room COVID transmission trial.
- The proposed human-safety mechanism depends on absorption in protective surface layers. After initially questioning eye safety, rocqua [[./thread#^c30829363|rocqua]] [[./thread#^c30829785|rocqua]] supplies a published explanation involving absorption in the ocular tear layer. zuminator [[./thread#^c30827305|zuminator]] explicitly retracts an earlier analogy with ordinary sunlight after bradknowles [[./thread#^c30827036|bradknowles]] explains atmospheric blocking of UVC. exmadscientist [[./thread#^c30828623|exmadscientist]] also challenges the explanation that shorter wavelengths have less energy.
- Comparisons assume that a purchased device produces the relevant wavelength and dose. alliao [[./thread#^c30824081|alliao]] says 222 nm products are uncommon and warns about conventional UV lamps and fake purple LEDs. tofof [[./thread#^c30824431|tofof]] quotes a source describing commercial 222 nm lamps as excimer devices. General reports of owning HVAC UV bulbs therefore cannot establish experience with the particular far-UVC system discussed.
- mjevans [[./thread#^c30825978|mjevans]] explicitly labels the attribution of poor indoor environments to LEED priorities an 'armchair opinion.' The discussion does not establish that certification or energy efficiency caused the reported ventilation problems. Likewise, nitrogen's [[./thread#^c30826739|nitrogen]] example of spending 5\% more energy for 5\% more enjoyment and productivity is hypothetical, not evidence of a measured cognitive benefit.

## Conditions

- Outdoor air quality determines whether window opening is attractive. Spooky23 [[./thread#^c30829606|Spooky23]] acknowledges the particulate limitation, while radu\_floricica [[./thread#^c30827092|radu\_floricica]], writing from Bucharest, strongly rejects the premise that outdoor air is necessarily safe. The Bucharest comment supplies no concentrations or source attribution. Together they limit lvs's [[./thread#^c30824644|lvs]] assertion that ventilation has 'no real downsides'; they do not quantify when outdoor pollution outweighs indoor dilution benefits.
- In this March 2022 discussion, tofof [[./thread#^c30824431|tofof]] reports finding a 5 W lamp priced around \$1,000 and a 20 W lamp around \$1,700. These are quoted product prices, not complete installation costs or measured treatment performance. londons\_explore [[./thread#^c30828902|londons\_explore]] asks how much electrical power effective room treatment requires, but the supplied replies do not resolve that operating-cost question.
- nitrogen [[./thread#^c30826739|nitrogen]] identifies upfront construction cost as an obstacle to better air, sound and thermal comfort, and argues that quality and efficiency can coexist. Wowfunhappy [[./thread#^c30826549|Wowfunhappy]] favors substantial air-quality gains with only marginal additional energy use, expressing a different tolerance for operating tradeoffs. prox [[./thread#^c30827656|prox]] reports local business opposition to stronger ventilation standards, without identifying the location or providing cost data.
- mattlondon [[./thread#^c30829256|mattlondon]] suggests the London Underground as a possible application because passengers can spend 5–20 minutes in an enclosed carriage with allegedly no fresh-air circulation. This identifies a setting where passengers have limited control over ventilation, but the comment provides no measured ventilation rate, particulate concentration or infection reduction.

## Lived experiences

- nitrogen [[./thread#^c30826694|nitrogen]] describes living in a high-rise where lint and smells from laundry, cooking, cannabis and bathrooms entered the unit, alongside inadequate sound isolation. Moving to a detached house outside the city improved reported quality of life, especially quietness. This is a firsthand account of contamination and comfort problems, without pollutant readings or evidence separating air-quality benefits from reduced noise.
- azinman2 [[./thread#^c30827579|azinman2]] reports the opposite high-rise experience in San Francisco: many neighbors, but no noticeable neighbor noise or smells. This directly limits a generalization from nitrogen's account to all dense housing. Neither account establishes chemical air quality from the presence or absence of detectable odors.
- hn\_throwaway\_99 [[./thread#^c30824666|hn\_throwaway\_99]], bradknowles [[./thread#^c30827040|bradknowles]] and azinman2 [[./thread#^c30827586|azinman2]] report having UV treatment installed in home HVAC systems. bradknowles calls the bulbs expensive, whereas azinman2 says the installation was not terribly expensive. None reports before-and-after pollutant concentrations, infection rates, symptoms, sleep or cognitive performance, and their equipment is not sufficiently specified for a controlled comparison.
- closetnerd [[./thread#^c30824342|closetnerd]] owns an air filter and cites noise and replacement requirements as reasons for preferring the proposed UV approach. This is firsthand evidence of filtration's practical burdens, but no experience with the proposed replacement or demonstration that it would address the same pollutants.
- at\_a\_remove [[./thread#^c30826050|at\_a\_remove]] reports a serious photosensitivity disorder and finds the prospect of this lighting frightening. a1371 [[./thread#^c30825422|a1371]] also anticipates discomfort with ten hours of daily UV exposure. These establish individual concerns and circumstances, rather than observed injuries from far-UVC installations.
- manmal [[./thread#^c30864914|manmal]] reports indirectly losing a grandfather to complications following infection during hip surgery, motivating interest in hospital disinfection. The account does not identify an airborne transmission route or demonstrate that UV would have prevented the infection.
- thedougd [[./thread#^c30830638|thedougd]] reports damaging rubber while using ozone to remove odor from soccer equipment. This is firsthand evidence of material damage during ozone treatment, but it does not establish the ozone concentration or material effects produced by the far-UVC lamps under discussion.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The approved pollutant and health questions remain largely unmeasured in this source unit. a1371 [[./thread#^c30825422|a1371]] and fennecs [[./thread#^c30825891|fennecs]] mention PM2.5 and other pollutants, but no supplied comment reports PM2.5, NO2 or CO2 sensor readings or calibration checks. Cooking appears as an odor source in nitrogen's account [[./thread#^c30826694|nitrogen]], and car pollution as a concern in fennecs's comment. There are no wildfire-smoke measurements or measured effects on sleep or cognition.
- The significance of UV-generated chemistry remains disputed. hausen [[./thread#^c30824730|hausen]] cites low ozone in one experiment; bawolff [[./thread#^c30828081|bawolff]] questions whether a tenfold margin below the cited limit is reassuring. solarmist [[./thread#^c30824615|solarmist]] repeats a claim that only shorter vacuum-UV wavelengths generate ozone, while angry\_octet [[./thread#^c30826098|angry\_octet]] raises broader air-cleaning chemistry. The discussion does not reconcile these claims or establish concentrations under different room conditions.
- Long-term and individual safety remain unsettled. solarmist [[./thread#^c30824490|solarmist]] quotes compliance with an eight-hour skin-exposure threshold, and BurningFrog [[./thread#^c30827184|BurningFrog]] interprets cautious wording as potentially routine scientific qualification. andrewflnr [[./thread#^c30824437|andrewflnr]] remains unconvinced. GekkePrutser [[./thread#^c30834679|GekkePrutser]] questions reliance on the eye's wet surface, and exmadscientist [[./thread#^c30852660|exmadscientist]] questions the completeness of damage endpoints. These exchanges provide neither proof of universal safety nor demonstrated injury from the studied installation.
- The best combination of ventilation, filtration and UV is unresolved. \_Microft [[./thread#^c30823685|\_Microft]] favors UV on the reported microbial-equivalent exchange rate, while angry\_octet [[./thread#^c30826101|angry\_octet]] favors air exchange because it avoids additional photochemistry. Spooky23 [[./thread#^c30829606|Spooky23]] favors inexpensive window opening with a particulate caveat. No supplied comparison measures overall exposure, health outcomes, costs and comfort across these alternatives in the same setting.
- Possible microbial ecological effects remain speculative. Meerax [[./thread#^c30825815|Meerax]] and tailrecursion [[./thread#^c30827826|tailrecursion]] ask whether surviving microbes could select for UV resistance; solarmist [[./thread#^c30826077|solarmist]] argues that resistance to destructive physical chemistry would be difficult. No resistance experiment is supplied, and the reported surviving fraction does not establish inherited resistance. samvher's [[./thread#^c30824751|samvher]] concern about beneficial microbiomes likewise lacks observed outcomes here.
- Consumer-product reliability is unresolved and should not be confused with air-sensor reliability. supernova87a [[./thread#^c30828289|supernova87a]] worries about unwanted wavelengths and manufacturing quality, while tofof [[./thread#^c30824431|tofof]] describes excimer technology rather than the LEDs assumed in part of that exchange. When ceejayoz [[./thread#^c30832124|ceejayoz]] presumes Amazon listings are conventional UVC, ahahahahah [[./thread#^c30835306|ahahahahah]] points out that the parent explicitly said far-UVC. No commenter supplies spectral testing that verifies or disproves those listings' claims.
