# [AirGradient: DIY Air Quality Monitoring with Open-Source Hardware & Firmware](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=27124671)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants discuss building and using open-source air-quality monitors, including sensor choice, CO2 calibration, particulate readings, and the limits of estimated CO2 sensors. They compare DIY projects and community monitoring networks, explore indoor and outdoor use, and ask about accuracy, weatherproofing, data sharing, platform support, and the project’s business model.
Tags: open source hardware, air quality monitors, CO2 sensors, particulate matter, calibration, citizen science

## Relevance

Covers air-quality monitoring for PM2.5 and CO2, sensor calibration and reliability, and the tradeoff between sealing a home against particles and ventilating to control CO2. Firsthand accounts describe wildfire exposure and using filtered intake air during fire season. ## Arguments

- Particle exclusion and ventilation can pull in opposite directions. AirGradient founder ahaucnx reports classroom observations in which sealing against PM2.5 increased CO2, and advocates filtered positive-pressure ventilation to address both [[./thread#^c27126640|ahaucnx]]. acidburnNSA describes using a filtered box fan in a window during fire season [[./thread#^c27126802|acidburnNSA]]. These accounts support considering indoor CO2 and outdoor particles together when deciding whether and how to admit outside air.
- Filtration has an encouraging firsthand smoke-event account: doggodaddo78 reports very high fire-period particle readings alongside indoor AQI around 5 while operating multiple filters [[./thread#^c27125433|doggodaddo78]]. This supports the usefulness of that household's particle-control setup, while providing no corresponding evidence about CO2, gaseous pollutants, or measured health improvement.
- For measuring CO2 itself, dheera favors NDIR sensors because they use CO2's infrared absorption rather than infer its concentration from other gases [[./thread#^c27125722|dheera]]. ahaucnx likewise recommends NDIR over TVOC-derived eCO2 [[./thread#^c27125785|ahaucnx]]. This is a distinction between measurement methods, not proof that every NDIR device is accurate. antoniuschan99 also identifies photoacoustic CO2 products, without supplying comparative measurements [[./thread#^c27126212|antoniuschan99]].
- jfim argues that approximate accuracy can suffice for household ventilation decisions: below 500 ppm means things look good, above 700 suggests considering opening windows, and above 1000 calls for opening them in their proposed scheme [[./thread#^c27130165|jfim]]. These are the commenter's practical heuristics, not demonstrated medical thresholds; their usefulness also depends on what pollutants opening the window would admit.
- Monitoring can make exposure management actionable. ahaucnx describes school alerts, including notifications to PE teachers, and reports a California daycare deploying 12 DIY monitors [[./thread#^c27126024|ahaucnx]]. 2bluesc describes recording measurements through Home Assistant and Grafana [[./thread#^c27125444|2bluesc]], while jfim records CSV data at 1 Hz for later analysis [[./thread#^c27126170|jfim]]. These establish implemented monitoring workflows, without demonstrating that logging alone improves health.

## Counterarguments

- Replying to concerns about bedroom CO2, clairity argues that CO2 is not a significant health concern except at extremely high, suffocating concentrations, invoking the conditions under which life evolved [[./thread#^c27132159|clairity]]. This challenges pugio's concern about possible headaches and cognitive effects [[./thread#^c27126592|pugio]]. Neither comment supplies exposure-and-outcome measurements or health-study results that resolve the disagreement.
- The rejection of eCO2 is qualified by jfim, who considers a humidity-compensated SGP30 adequate for a rough window-opening signal, while acknowledging potentially large errors when VOCs are abundant [[./thread#^c27127028|jfim]]. In another branch, ahaucnx reports sunscreen odor producing a very large apparent CO2 increase in a room described as having low CO2 [[./thread#^c27125803|ahaucnx]]. Adequacy therefore remains application- and environment-dependent rather than established for all household use.
- ahaucnx's claim that hundreds of deployed Senseair S8 sensors had experienced no failures [[./thread#^c27125785|ahaucnx]] is challenged by nmaggioni's report of unexplained spikes and apparent underflow or wraparound, persisting after repeated recalibration [[./thread#^c27128936|nmaggioni]]. Deployment survival and trustworthy readings are different reliability claims; the exchange does not establish the cause or prevalence of the anomaly.
- ahaucnx believes the PMS5003 offers more stable readings and longer life than the PMSA003 [[./thread#^c27125843|ahaucnx]]. In direct reply, 2bluesc says they found no convincing comparative data and reports nine months of continuous PMSA003 operation without problems [[./thread#^c27138665|2bluesc]]. Neither account constitutes a controlled comparison of accuracy or service life.
- ashtonkem reports abandoning a proposed outdoor monitor after their wife questioned its value where air quality was considered consistently excellent [[./thread#^c27130126|ashtonkem]]. This is a contextual objection to buying another instrument, not a measured finding about their indoor air or a demonstration that monitoring is unnecessary elsewhere.
- MattRix characterizes household, office, and school monitoring as a relatively new trend [[./thread#^c27125366|MattRix]]. bumby counters that CO2-based demand-controlled ventilation has existed for decades and attributes the apparent novelty to improving affordability [[./thread#^c27185987|bumby]]. The disagreement concerns accessibility and adoption, rather than evidence that indoor air quality has only recently become relevant.

## Assumptions

- Automatic CO2 calibration depends on exposure assumptions. jfim says the algorithm assumes occasional fresh air at 400 ppm, adjusts its baseline over days, and may be disableable [[./thread#^c27126706|jfim]]. ahaucnx says baseline calibration can take one to two weeks to engage, with manual calibration also available [[./thread#^c27125785|ahaucnx]]. agurk questions the fixed 400 ppm reference because outdoor background levels are already higher [[./thread#^c27127919|agurk]]. A device that never encounters the assumed baseline presents another unresolved applicability condition.
- An eCO2 estimate assumes a usable relationship between CO2 and the gases actually sensed. NortySpock explains this proxy dependence and warns that unusual gas ratios undermine it [[./thread#^c27125167|NortySpock]]. parsecs adds that the SGP30 uses separate ethanol and hydrogen signals, deriving eCO2 from hydrogen [[./thread#^c27125245|parsecs]]. jfim's favorable assessment additionally assumes humidity compensation and acknowledges a hard floor of 400 ppm eCO2 [[./thread#^c27127028|jfim]]. These details limit what apparent agreement near outdoor levels can establish.
- Agreement with another consumer monitor is an informal cross-check, not demonstrated absolute calibration. 2bluesc compares a device with nearby PurpleAir readings [[./thread#^c27138665|2bluesc]], and jfim explicitly lacks calibrated reference instruments for their CO2 comparison [[./thread#^c27127028|jfim]]. DoingIsLearning asks about comparison against known calibrated equipment, but the supplied exchange contains no answer establishing that validation [[./thread#^c27127755|DoingIsLearning]].

## Conditions

- The strongest motivation comes from particular exposure settings. ahaucnx describes seasonal wildfire PM2.5 in Northern Thailand and the need to protect schoolchildren [[./thread#^c27126024|ahaucnx]]; pugio describes living through Australian bushfires [[./thread#^c27126592|pugio]]; doggodaddo78 reports fire-period measurements in Paradise, California [[./thread#^c27125433|doggodaddo78]]. These settings explain the emphasis on smoke exclusion and filtered ventilation without establishing typical conditions for other places.
- Humidity and outdoor installation complicate sensor selection. sberder, who discloses operating an environmental-data business, reports past monitor problems in humid Thailand and Singapore and asks about temperature and humidity effects on Plantower readings [[./thread#^c27128150|sberder]]. saltcured asks how an outdoor DIY device would handle sunlight, wind, dust, rain, condensation, weatherproofing, and battery-powered communications [[./thread#^c27136657|saltcured]]. The supplied comments do not quantify these environmental errors or validate a complete outdoor design.
- The quoted entry costs are historical 2021 reports for different product scopes: throwaway32231 suggests a €15 SDS011 particulate sensor with USB interface [[./thread#^c27128678|throwaway32231]]; bodo11 points to an approximately €50 plug-and-play Sensor.Community option [[./thread#^c27127606|bodo11]]; smnv reports buying an approximately \$100 Qingping monitor covering CO2, TVOC, particles, temperature, and humidity [[./thread#^c27129014|smnv]]. These prices are neither current quotes nor like-for-like comparisons of accuracy or capability.
- DIY affordability also depends on tools, skills, and shipping. ahaucnx offers free PCBs with postage paid by the recipient [[./thread#^c27125763|ahaucnx]] and says shipping from Thailand normally takes about three weeks [[./thread#^c27126027|ahaucnx]]. sundvor, in Australia, would need to learn soldering and anticipates shipping difficulty [[./thread#^c27128748|sundvor]]. A free PCB therefore does not mean a free, immediately usable monitor.
- Power and packaging create a tradeoff between sensing methods. jfim reports that an S8 can draw enough current to sag a shared USB-powered 5 V rail, while the SGP30 is smaller, cheaper, and less power-hungry [[./thread#^c27127028|jfim]]. Those advantages must be weighed against using an indirect CO2 estimate; the thread does not connect the reported power issue to any particular other commenter's sensor malfunction.
- Usable data access can require substantial integration work. smnv reports regional restrictions, unsuccessful API approaches, and a custom local MQTT/Grafana workaround for the Qingping device [[./thread#^c27129014|smnv]]. By contrast, ahaucnx says AirGradient's open version can operate offline or send data to another platform [[./thread#^c27125877|ahaucnx]], and acidburnNSA runs local monitoring without a cloud dependency [[./thread#^c27126802|acidburnNSA]]. smnv's favorable sensor-quality assessment and reports of fan failures after roughly a year are secondhand, unlike their firsthand integration experience.
- Moving a pollution-producing activity outdoors is constrained by weather and comfort. sizzle recommends soldering outside when possible [[./thread#^c27127735|sizzle]], but TedDoesntTalk replies that this is workable at that time of year and not in winter [[./thread#^c27129302|TedDoesntTalk]]. This limits the practicality of the suggested source-avoidance measure; the thread supplies no measured comparison with indoor extraction.

## Lived experiences

- pugio describes enduring Australian bushfires, recommending PurpleAir for grassroots monitoring, and wanting to investigate a stuffy apartment bedroom and baby's room overnight [[./thread#^c27126592|pugio]]. The account establishes lived smoke exposure and perceived stuffiness. Elevated nighttime CO2, headaches attributable to it, cognitive impairment, and changes in sleep quality remain concerns rather than measured findings.
- doggodaddo78 reports using a Plantower PMS5003 with an Arduino during the 2020 fire they identify as the Bear Creek fire in Paradise, California [[./thread#^c27125433|doggodaddo78]]. They describe PM2.5 and PM10 readings pushing 900+, and indoor AQI around 5 with two 3M Filtrete 2900 filters, a continuously running fan, and a BlueAir 680. The 900+ values have no explicit units and are presented amid mixed PM/AQI terminology, so a concentration reduction or filtration efficiency cannot be calculated reliably.
- acidburnNSA reports instrument-triggered outdoor air-quality alerts associated with a neighbor's cigar, leaf blowing, and wood-stove use, and indoor alerts associated with dry shampoo and burned pancakes [[./thread#^c27126802|acidburnNSA]]. During fire season, balancing CO2 against PM2.5 was difficult; after several days, their preferred solution was a box fan with a filter blowing outdoor air inward through a window. No concentrations, before-and-after series, or health outcomes accompany that intervention.
- With windows open, jfim reports two S8 CO2 readings of 478 and 455 ppm and corresponding SGP30 eCO2 readings of 400 and 405 ppm [[./thread#^c27127028|jfim]]. They explicitly say that without calibrated instruments they cannot determine which readings are correct. This is a firsthand device comparison near background levels, without a closed-window control or evidence of performance at high CO2.
- ahaucnx reports triggering a TVOC sensor with sunscreen odor in a room described as having very low CO2, causing its apparent CO2 reading to rise dramatically [[./thread#^c27125803|ahaucnx]]. This is a concrete account of proxy interference, although no reference concentration, numerical error, or experimental protocol is supplied.
- dheera reports a PMSA003I returning PM2.5 zero until actual values were around 100ish, without stating units or how the reference values were established [[./thread#^c27125710|dheera]]. In a different build, 2bluesc reports a PMSA003 operating continuously for nine months without issues and outdoor readings within roughly one or two percent of nearby PurpleAir sensors [[./thread#^c27138665|2bluesc]]. These conflicting experiences involve differently identified variants and setups, limiting any model-wide conclusion.
- ahaucnx reports soldering driving measured PM2.5 into what they call very unhealthy territory and recommends a carbon-filter smoke absorber [[./thread#^c27126045|ahaucnx]]. In the resulting recommendation exchange, jfim says a recently purchased Aven 17701, described as a fan with activated carbon, seems to do the job [[./thread#^c27126220|jfim]]. Neither reports numerical particle reductions, gas-removal performance, or a measured change in symptoms.
- sundvor describes their son's former school being next to a major Melbourne thoroughfare for one year and expresses concern about possible harm [[./thread#^c27128778|sundvor]]. They also report owning three home purifiers and wanting a combined monitor. This is firsthand context about traffic proximity and protective behavior, without pollutant measurements, verified injury, or evidence of purifier effectiveness in that home.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Whether ordinary household CO2 concentrations meaningfully affect headaches, cognition, or sleep is unresolved in this unit. pugio raises the concern [[./thread#^c27126592|pugio]], while clairity dismisses significant effects short of extreme exposure [[./thread#^c27132159|clairity]]. No supplied comment reports a controlled health comparison, quantified overnight exposure linked to symptoms, or measured sleep improvement. rauhallinen's cited papers concern sensing and spectroscopy technology, rather than providing those health-outcome findings [[./thread#^c27127008|rauhallinen]].
- Absolute accuracy, calibration procedures, humidity sensitivity, and long-term comparative reliability remain unsettled. DoingIsLearning's request for calibrated-reference comparisons is unanswered [[./thread#^c27127755|DoingIsLearning]]; sberder's environmental-performance questions lack quantified answers [[./thread#^c27128150|sberder]]; and nmaggioni's S8 anomalies have no diagnosis or documented resolution [[./thread#^c27128936|nmaggioni]]. The anecdotes support investigating these issues rather than selecting a universally reliable sensor.
- The performance envelope of filtered outdoor-air intake is not established. ahaucnx claims positive-pressure systems can maintain near-zero PM2.5 and low CO2 on heavily polluted days, but the supporting measurements are in linked material not reproduced here [[./thread#^c27126640|ahaucnx]]. acidburnNSA supplies a practical favorable account without simultaneous numerical PM2.5/CO2 results [[./thread#^c27126802|acidburnNSA]]. Airflow, leakage, filter loading, operating cost, and comfort tradeoffs therefore cannot be compared from this export.
- Air conditioning's contribution to ventilation is specifically unanswered. metb asks whether the room underlying the positive-pressure discussion was air conditioned and whether the air conditioner supplied air exchange sufficient to reduce CO2 [[./thread#^c27128501|metb]]. No equipment type, outdoor-air provision, or comparison is supplied, so the thread cannot attribute CO2 reduction to air conditioning.
- NO2 exposure and cooking-specific mitigation are substantial gaps. sundvor's traffic-adjacent school account contains no NO2 readings [[./thread#^c27128778|sundvor]], and acidburnNSA's burned-pancake alerts identify neither NO2 nor the cooking fuel [[./thread#^c27126802|acidburnNSA]]. This unit cannot compare gas versus electric cooking, evaluate kitchen exhaust, quantify traffic-related NO2, or establish whether the reported particle-filtering arrangements control those gaseous exposures.
