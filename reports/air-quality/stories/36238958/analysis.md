# [US CDC sets first federal target for ventilation of indoor air to lower risk](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=36238958)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants discuss federal ventilation targets intended to reduce indoor transmission risks, focusing on air changes per hour, equivalent clean-air delivery, filtration, UV treatment, and fresh-air exchange. The thread weighs infectious-disease benefits against energy use, building codes, installation costs, and the difficulty of retrofitting schools and other existing spaces. Participants compare ventilation with filtration, discuss heat-recovery systems and indoor pollutants such as CO₂ and cooking fumes, and share experiences with home HVAC upgrades. Broader debate concerns the timing of public-health guidance, the evidence behind ventilation, and the authority of agencies to set standards.
Tags: ventilation, indoor air, filtration, infectious disease, building standards, public health

## Relevance

The discussion compares outdoor-air ventilation with filtration and UV, including the distinction between reducing infectious particles and addressing CO2 or cooking fumes. It includes firsthand accounts of ventilation and filtration at home, comfort and energy tradeoffs, and uncertainty about benefits and implementation costs. ## Arguments

- Clean indoor air is presented as preventive public-health infrastructure. lucubratory [[./thread#^c36239700|lucubratory]] and isp [[./thread#^c36241013|isp]] compare it with clean water; isp [[./thread#^c36249844|isp]] clarifies that the concern is airborne infection in occupied enclosed spaces, including places without conspicuous smoke or other pollution. This is an argument for reducing exposure even when occupants do not notice dirty air.
- Auracle [[./thread#^c36245164|Auracle]] argues that school filtration and air exchangers could prevent illness and preserve productivity across pandemics. Responding to a question about children’s lower direct risk, Auracle [[./thread#^c36248246|Auracle]] emphasizes crowded classrooms and transmission to parents and grandparents. The claimed exceptional value for money is an opinion, without a cost-effectiveness calculation in the supplied discussion.
- The ventilation target allows several ways to reduce infectious particles. jefftk [[./thread#^c36241815|jefftk]] quotes guidance explaining that 5 ACH includes effective air changes from filtration or germicidal UV, which need not introduce outdoor air. When anon946 [[./thread#^c36241544|anon946]] proposes a standard based on viral removal or inactivation, wiml [[./thread#^c36251674|wiml]] explains that eACH already serves that purpose.
- Particle removal and broader indoor air quality have different requirements. bgentry [[./thread#^c36242320|bgentry]] argues for both filtration and fresh-air intake because particulate cleaning does not resolve CO2 buildup, gas-appliance fumes, formaldehyde, or other off-gassing. some\_random [[./thread#^c36243106|some\_random]] similarly considers HEPA filtration an improvement within a sealed room while questioning the remaining stale-air problem.
- enragedcacti [[./thread#^c36241713|enragedcacti]] relays a recap claiming that classroom window opening produced up to a 14.1-fold reduction in COVID transmission, one HEPA filter matched two partly open winter windows with a 2.5-fold reduction, and two HEPA filters achieved a fourfold reduction. These are claims about linked studies, not firsthand measurements; their methods, baselines, and applicability cannot be checked from this export.
- devonkim [[./thread#^c36241036|devonkim]] reports that a local school district using classroom air-cleaning equipment had substantially fewer COVID-related absences than nearby districts without it. The commenter explicitly expresses limited confidence and supplies no absence counts or adjustment for differences between districts, making this a suggestive local report rather than demonstrated causation.
- Practical proposals include portable commercial filters and homemade Corsi–Rosenthal boxes, suggested by dv\_dt [[./thread#^c36243833|dv\_dt]] in response to a hayfever account. gniv [[./thread#^c36242553|gniv]] recommends an always-on purifier plus opening windows a couple of times daily as inexpensive and effective. Neither comment supplies measured results for those proposals.
- Heat recovery is offered as a way to combine outdoor-air ventilation with lower conditioning costs. jefftk [[./thread#^c36241829|jefftk]] recommends a heat exchanger, while Aspos [[./thread#^c36243136|Aspos]] quotes heat-exchange efficiencies ranging from approximately 50\% to 70–80\%. Those efficiency figures are referenced general claims, separate from Aspos’s subjective household experience.

## Counterarguments

- Claims that airflow alone would have prevented all disruption are challenged. exabrial [[./thread#^c36240209|exabrial]] calls fans everything that was needed and says sufficiently ventilated businesses should never have been affected; hackernewds [[./thread#^c36240317|hackernewds]] asks for supporting evidence. devonkim [[./thread#^c36258996|devonkim]] subsequently clarifies that the school equipment offered as an example was more sophisticated than extra fans and filters. PM\_me\_your\_math [[./thread#^c36263656|PM\_me\_your\_math]] also rejects a quoted claim that earlier implementation would have prevented the pandemic; this discussion does not establish that counterfactual.
- School investment has competing risk framings. jjtheblunt [[./thread#^c36248077|jjtheblunt]] asks whether older people and those with comorbidities faced much greater danger than children. Auracle [[./thread#^c36248246|Auracle]] replies that school transmission can expose vulnerable household members, and jjtheblunt [[./thread#^c36249227|jjtheblunt]] accepts that point. Lower direct risk to children therefore does not settle the school-filtration argument within this exchange.
- taeric [[./thread#^c36245360|taeric]] questions whether some indoor-air claims rely on softer outcomes than the clean-water analogy suggests, and taeric [[./thread#^c36249728|taeric]] considers stove dangers and ventilation-as-a-miracle claims overstated while supporting better ventilation codes. isp [[./thread#^c36249844|isp]] then identifies a difference in subject: their argument concerned infectious aerosols, whereas taeric had been thinking about pollution and household appliances.
- gwbas1c [[./thread#^c36241693|gwbas1c]] sees five air changes per hour as conflicting with energy-saving building codes, and EVa5I7bHFq9mnYK [[./thread#^c36271252|EVa5I7bHFq9mnYK]] calls that rate infeasible when incoming air requires heating or cooling. jefftk [[./thread#^c36241829|jefftk]] qualifies the objection: the target includes filtration or UV, and outdoor-air exchange can use heat recovery. The thermal objection is strongest when the target is interpreted as five complete outdoor-air replacements.
- mortify [[./thread#^c36248709|mortify]] speculates that reducing microbial exposure could impair immune development or increase allergies and asthma, invoking the hygiene hypothesis. PM\_me\_your\_math [[./thread#^c36263907|PM\_me\_your\_math]] similarly questions clean-room-like expectations and notes that outdoor air also contains pathogens. lucubratory [[./thread#^c36249629|lucubratory]] challenges this reasoning through a sarcastic contaminated-water analogy, while isp [[./thread#^c36264529|isp]] disputes the evolutionary premise. These comments provide no direct evidence resolving the proposed long-term tradeoff.
- The target’s novelty is disputed. Maxburn [[./thread#^c36240626|Maxburn]] says MERV-13 filtration and at least four air changes per hour were already recommended commercially, and throw0101c [[./thread#^c36241543|throw0101c]] asks whether ASHRAE requirements already covered the issue. These objections concern existing recommendations and codes; they do not demonstrate that occupied buildings actually achieve those conditions.

## Assumptions

- lkbm [[./thread#^c36242420|lkbm]] gives approximately 421 ppm as the atmospheric CO2 concentration at the time of the 2023 discussion, claims slight cognitive effects at roughly twice that concentration, and says health is largely unaffected until approximately five times it. These are unsupported assertions within the supplied text, not household readings, verified causal findings, or universal safety thresholds.
- George83728 [[./thread#^c36243512|George83728]] prefers outdoor air through an insect screen and assumes people can acclimatize to temperature and allergies, potentially after years of discomfort. No evidence is supplied that such acclimatization would resolve allergic symptoms or make everyone healthier; the commenter separately acknowledges the smoke-related need for filtration.
- a\_subsystem [[./thread#^c36246909|a\_subsystem]] relays an AC company’s warning that UV treatment may make filters dirty faster because of dead organisms. This is secondhand maintenance advice, not a reported measurement of filter loading, microbial removal, or replacement frequency.
- aaron695 [[./thread#^c36240188|aaron695]] welcomes the guidance’s discussion of temperature and humidity, and mortify [[./thread#^c36248709|mortify]] assumes humidity adjustment can reduce inhaled pathogens. Neither supplies humidity measurements, an operating range, or comparative outcomes, so this unit cannot establish a practical humidity prescription.

## Conditions

- School costs require preserving a substantial correction. devonkim [[./thread#^c36241036|devonkim]] initially wrote about filters costing approximately \$100,000 each, but devonkim [[./thread#^c36258996|devonkim]] later clarified that \$100,000 was a figure seen for the whole district and cited approximately \$20,000 list price for a stationary unit. Separately, enragedcacti [[./thread#^c36248740|enragedcacti]] cited a Denver project costing under \$800 installed and \$361 annually in electricity per classroom. These concern different equipment or projects and are not interchangeable estimates. mrguyorama [[./thread#^c36249175|mrguyorama]] maintained that even the smaller expense could face school-board resistance.
- Window opening depends on the incoming air. bgentry [[./thread#^c36242320|bgentry]] identifies indoor gases that motivate fresh-air intake, and gniv [[./thread#^c36242553|gniv]] proposes periodic opening. However, credit\_guy [[./thread#^c36240187|credit\_guy]] situates the discussion during smoke in the Northeastern US, and George83728 [[./thread#^c36243512|George83728]] recognizes filtration’s importance during that episode. Together, these comments support making window advice conditional on outdoor conditions; they contain no measurements comparing indoor buildup with smoke admitted through windows.
- Allergens and thermal comfort can constrain outdoor-air strategies. HPsquared [[./thread#^c36243450|HPsquared]] reports worse hayfever at home than in an air-conditioned office, while George83728 [[./thread#^c36243512|George83728]] explicitly identifies allergies and temperature as obstacles to open windows. Neither account establishes the outdoor pollen concentration, which building feature caused the difference, or a universally workable window schedule.
- Fresh-air needs depend on occupancy, while ACH also depends on room volume. Abekkus [[./thread#^c36243850|Abekkus]] proposes approximately 15 CFM of outdoor air per person for CO2 and says this is fourteen times less than the 5-eACH rate for their own small, crowded house. That ratio is household-specific. In a separate historical classroom example, enragedcacti [[./thread#^c36241975|enragedcacti]] calculates only 2.7 ACH from 450 CFM for 1,000 square feet with ten-foot ceilings, assuming perfect distribution.
- Requirements and actual operation vary by jurisdiction and building. sokoloff [[./thread#^c36242324|sokoloff]] says ASHRAE adoption is local and reports finding no incorporation of ASHRAE 62 in Massachusetts or their city. 0\_\_\_\_\_0 [[./thread#^c36241473|0\_\_\_\_\_0]] cites bathroom requirements allowing either a window or mechanical ventilation, but HPsquared [[./thread#^c36243481|HPsquared]] observes that windows often remain closed. A permitted ventilation opening therefore does not establish that useful ventilation occurs.
- The unusually intensive household installation described by jmacd [[./thread#^c36242613|jmacd]] accompanied a new house with extra air handlers. jmacd [[./thread#^c36246040|jmacd]] attributes comfort at high throughput to extensive ducting and numerous low-velocity outlets. The account supports a particular design approach, but supplies no installation cost or evidence that an existing home could achieve the same airflow and comfort with a simple filter upgrade.

## Lived experiences

- jmacd [[./thread#^c36242613|jmacd]] describes a comfortable new home with sound-dampened airflow, filtration described as nearly HEPA, and activated carbon added for odors. jmacd [[./thread#^c36246040|jmacd]] reports five filtered outdoor-air exchanges per hour plus roughly ten internal filtered circulations, with many outlets avoiding strong localized drafts. These are reported system rates and comfort impressions; no pollutant readings, airflow-verification method, or measured odor reduction is provided.
- In that household of two adults and three children, jmacd [[./thread#^c36242613|jmacd]] reports fewer apparent viral transmissions and less simultaneous illness, although colds still circulate. Alertness also seems better, but the commenter explicitly acknowledges other lifestyle changes. jmacd [[./thread#^c36246062|jmacd]] specifies switching from hard cardio to long walks and spending less evening time sitting and snacking, leaving the air system’s contribution uncertain.
- a\_subsystem [[./thread#^c36244343|a\_subsystem]] reports adding UV to an AC system in a house under two years old and subsequently moving from family illness every couple of months to rarely being sick. The household included two adults and more than one child. The commenter explicitly labels this anecdotal; no exposure measurements, comparison household, or laboratory-confirmed infection counts are supplied.
- HPsquared [[./thread#^c36243450|HPsquared]] reports substantially better hayfever symptoms at an air-conditioned workplace than at home and wants a similar improvement at home. This is firsthand symptom variation across environments, without measurements identifying whether filtration, pollen entry, humidity, or another difference explains it.
- Aspos [[./thread#^c36243136|Aspos]] reports greater productivity and better sleep after installing several energy-recovery ventilators at home, explicitly calling the account subjective and anecdotal. There are no accompanying CO2 readings, sleep measurements, or objective cognitive tests.
- taeric [[./thread#^c36249728|taeric]] reports feeling essentially the same in a previous house without forced air and a current house with it, which contributes to skepticism about dramatic personal benefits. taeric [[./thread#^c36250343|taeric]] also describes an episode in which friends’ COVID illness did not spread to their own family despite no confidence in exceptional hygiene. Neither observation establishes comparable air quality or isolates ventilation’s effect.
- lostlogin [[./thread#^c36247187|lostlogin]], replying within the household UV discussion, reports being sick only once since the pandemic began, with that illness being COVID. The commenter credits staying home when sick and mask wearing. This is a separate household’s account showing another explanation for improved wellness during the same period, rather than a direct test of the UV installation.

## Unresolved disagreements

- This unit supplies no firsthand indoor PM2.5, NO2, or CO2 concentration series and no sensor model, calibration, accuracy comparison, or reliability test. jmacd [[./thread#^c36246040|jmacd]] reports exchange rates, bgentry [[./thread#^c36242320|bgentry]] lists pollutant concerns, and lkbm [[./thread#^c36242420|lkbm]] asserts CO2 effect levels. None establishes actual household pollutant exposure or validates a sensor.
- Cooking, traffic, and wildfire exposure remain thinly documented. bgentry [[./thread#^c36242320|bgentry]] flags gas-appliance fumes, while taeric [[./thread#^c36249728|taeric]] thinks household-stove dangers are often overstated; neither supplies cooking measurements, a gas-versus-electric comparison, or an extraction-hood test. credit\_guy [[./thread#^c36240187|credit\_guy]] mentions the Northeastern smoke episode without personal exposure readings. There is no substantive traffic-exposure account in this unit, and these omissions cannot establish that any exposure is harmless.
- The magnitude and transferability of infection reduction remain uncertain. enragedcacti [[./thread#^c36241713|enragedcacti]] supplies large study-summary effects without study methods, and devonkim [[./thread#^c36241036|devonkim]] supplies a district comparison without numerical outcomes or controls. The export cannot establish which results apply to a particular room, filter arrangement, occupancy, or outdoor-air rate.
- The infection-benefit disagreement narrows during the exchange. isp [[./thread#^c36258203|isp]] expects a dramatic reduction rather than disease disappearance. taeric [[./thread#^c36259338|taeric]] explains that earlier claims about children undermined trust; isp [[./thread#^c36264449|isp]] agrees those claims were problematic, and taeric [[./thread#^c36264500|taeric]] accepts the distinction between evidence-based and unsupported predictions while allowing that later evidence may have been missed. They share support for improved ventilation, but the supplied exchange does not settle the expected benefit.
- Improved alertness, productivity, sleep, and comfort cannot be assigned to a specific pollutant or mechanism. jmacd [[./thread#^c36242613|jmacd]] acknowledges lifestyle confounding, and Aspos [[./thread#^c36243136|Aspos]] labels the sleep and productivity improvements subjective. Neither demonstrates whether lower CO2, fewer particles, reduced infection, thermal comfort, or unrelated changes produced the reported improvement.
- No supplied account determines when or how long to open windows to balance indoor gases against outdoor smoke, allergens, and temperature. gniv [[./thread#^c36242553|gniv]] proposes opening them a couple of times daily, George83728 [[./thread#^c36243512|George83728]] prefers outdoor air but acknowledges smoke and comfort constraints, and HPsquared [[./thread#^c36243450|HPsquared]] reports environment-dependent allergy symptoms. Their accounts leave the practical exposure tradeoff unquantified.
- Long-term immune effects remain disputed without direct evidence. mortify [[./thread#^c36248709|mortify]] raises possible increases in asthma, allergies, or susceptibility after reduced microbial exposure; lucubratory [[./thread#^c36249629|lucubratory]] and isp [[./thread#^c36264529|isp]] challenge the reasoning. The discussion does not distinguish measured effects of reducing respiratory pathogens from effects of changing other environmental microbial exposures.
