# [25\% of Cars Cause 90\% of Automobile Air Pollution](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=9504996)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants scrutinize the claim that a minority of cars produces most automobile pollution, asking what “badly tuned” means, how the roadside measurements were made, and whether vehicle age, maintenance, driving behavior, or engine problems explain the difference. Discussion covers emissions inspections, roadside detection, exemptions, enforcement, and the political feasibility and fairness of targeting high-polluting vehicles. Broader comparisons consider ships, trucks, aircraft, small engines, and the local effects of pollution, while another thread asks whether replacing or retrofitting older cars saves emissions once manufacturing impacts are included.
Tags: vehicle emissions, automobile pollution, emissions testing, vehicle maintenance, transport policy, air pollution sources, shipping emissions, older vehicles, manufacturing impacts, regulation

## Relevance

The discussion covers vehicle-related particulate and ultrafine-particle exposure, roadside measurements, and a firsthand account of managing exposure while driving with asthma. It also debates emissions monitoring and how well visible exhaust or spot testing identifies high emitters. ## Arguments

- A small group of high emitters could offer substantial opportunities for reducing traffic pollution. VeejayRampay [[./thread#^c9505302|VeejayRampay]] interprets the headline this way; jchrisa [[./thread#^c9505329|jchrisa]] proposes targeting the worst vehicles instead of checking every vehicle, and teach [[./thread#^c9505600|teach]] suggests roadside emissions detectors paired with license-plate cameras. These are proposed interventions, without demonstrated detection accuracy or exposure reductions in this export.
- Traffic proximity matters for exposure. arcseco [[./thread#^c9507010|arcseco]] quotes a University of Toronto release saying concentrations decline with distance more slowly than anticipated. The quoted measurements are approximately 20,000 ultrafine particles/cm³ in summer and 30,000 in winter, with winter increases attributed to stagnation and reduced evaporation. These are secondhand research figures, not arcseco's personal measurements, and they describe particle number concentration rather than PM2.5 mass.
- The health concerns extend beyond unpleasant exhaust smells, but the supporting claims have different evidentiary status. arcseco [[./thread#^c9507010|arcseco]] quotes a researcher describing ultrafine particles penetrating deeply into lungs and traveling through the body. SCAQTony [[./thread#^c9506926|SCAQTony]] quotes secondary material associating shipping-related sulfur pollution with respiratory problems and heart-attack risk. DanBC [[./thread#^c9506203|DanBC]] attributes illness and deaths to diesel-promoting policies. None supplies an individual exposure–outcome measurement or a pollutant-specific clinical analysis within the comments.
- Source controls are presented as a reason vehicle exhaust has improved. mapt [[./thread#^c9508308|mapt]] credits cleaner engines, catalytic converters, inspections, lower-sulfur diesel and particulate filters, and argues that low sulfur content enables effective exhaust treatment. This supports maintaining emissions equipment and addressing additional combustion sources, although the stronger assertion that automobile pollution is largely solved is the commenter's assessment, not a result established here.
- Port electrification is presented as an implemented improvement. Responding to losangelino [[./thread#^c9505676|losangelino]], who was unsure whether shore-power efforts had progressed, mturmon [[./thread#^c9505893|mturmon]] says the Port of Los Angeles implemented Alternative Maritime Power and that cruise ships were using it despite resistance from cargo shippers. This corrects the suggestion that nothing happened, but supplies no measured reduction in nearby residents' exposure.
- Repair, engine replacement and retirement incentives offer different routes to reducing exhaust. Retric [[./thread#^c9506258|Retric]] argues repairs can improve fuel economy while making cities more livable; rpcope1 [[./thread#^c9506153|rpcope1]] advocates fitting newer engines into older cars; peter303 [[./thread#^c9506028|peter303]] recalls California programs purchasing and scrapping old vehicles through pollution credits. Their relative costs and actual pollutant reductions are not quantified in this discussion.

## Counterarguments

- The claim that 16 ships equal all cars in pollution conflates pollutants. genericuser [[./thread#^c9505412|genericuser]] emphasizes the conditional wording 'can produce'; vonmoltke [[./thread#^c9505449|vonmoltke]] identifies sulfur compounds as the specific comparison; TheCoelacanth [[./thread#^c9506338|TheCoelacanth]] says cars emit relatively little sulfur and more of other pollutants. seanflyon [[./thread#^c9507198|seanflyon]] clarifies that this correction does not deny sulfur's harmfulness. A sulfur comparison cannot establish equivalent total pollution or personal exposure.
- Distance from people limits the usefulness of aggregate emissions comparisons, but ships cannot simply be dismissed as remote. pjc50 [[./thread#^c9505592|pjc50]] argues ships are usually outside city centers; j\_baker [[./thread#^c9506196|j\_baker]] responds from beside Oakland's port, describing both ships and associated diesel trucks. losangelino [[./thread#^c9505676|losangelino]] points to ships idling near Long Beach. qanael [[./thread#^c9506757|qanael]] separately raises efficiency per unit of cargo, which addresses transport efficiency rather than neighborhood exposure.
- A large percentage of emissions from a small fleet fraction does not establish a large absolute burden. sgustard [[./thread#^c9506218|sgustard]] argues that increasingly concentrated emissions could accompany falling total pollution. nitrogen [[./thread#^c9506468|nitrogen]] imagines a future where rare combustion vehicles dominate a very small remaining total. This qualifies danmaz74's [[./thread#^c9506382|danmaz74]] argument that a hypothetical 5\% producing 90\% should necessarily be removed.
- The roadside observations do not establish that the vehicles were 'badly tuned' or that tune-ups would fix them. sp332 [[./thread#^c9505285|sp332]] says the researchers measured passing exhaust without inspecting or experimentally repairing vehicles. caseysoftware [[./thread#^c9505317|caseysoftware]] identifies vehicle age as another possible explanation while explicitly acknowledging a lack of supporting data. The causal diagnosis in the headline discussion therefore exceeds what these descriptions of the measurements establish.
- Claims that new-car regulations provide little benefit face an internal rebuttal. exabrial [[./thread#^c9506981|exabrial]] contrasts apparently clean new cars with a visibly smoking old vehicle while criticizing regulation. jcromartie [[./thread#^c9507041|jcromartie]] replies that the clean new cars could themselves be evidence that regulation works. Neither observation quantifies emissions, but the anecdote does not by itself support regulatory ineffectiveness.
- Inspection programs can leave substantial gaps, although exemption arguments differ. michael\_h [[./thread#^c9506349|michael\_h]] criticizes California exemptions and describes having driven an exempt 1974 car daily. sliverstorm [[./thread#^c9507374|sliverstorm]] argues older vehicles generally represent few vehicles and relatively little mileage. organsnyder [[./thread#^c9505876|organsnyder]] instead emphasizes defective emissions systems on 10–20-year-old vehicles and proposes enforcing each vehicle's original standards. These positions disagree about which vehicles matter most and how broad testing should be.
- Targeted enforcement and compulsory repairs can impose unequal burdens. atom-morgan [[./thread#^c9505387|atom-morgan]] expects spot checks of visibly poor vehicles to concentrate in low-income areas; ohitsdom [[./thread#^c9506219|ohitsdom]] proposes repair or replacement subsidies. mgarfias [[./thread#^c9505861|mgarfias]] challenges the assumption that repair is necessarily economical, describing difficult diagnosis even with tools and expertise.

## Assumptions

- Generalizing the headline requires assumptions about both the denominator and sample representativeness. smeyer [[./thread#^c9505743|smeyer]] quotes a description of 100,000 cars passing probes on one major Toronto road. Dylan16807 [[./thread#^c9505290|Dylan16807]] distinguishes automobile pollution from emissions by all vehicles, and dang [[./thread#^c9506290|dang]] confirms the headline was narrowed accordingly. This does not establish the same distribution across cities, countries, all transport modes or total ambient pollution.
- The proposed explanations for high emissions are mechanisms to investigate, not diagnoses established for the sampled vehicles. crxgames [[./thread#^c9505310|crxgames]] discusses air–fuel ratios, akallio9000 [[./thread#^c9505531|akallio9000]] suggests sensor faults and misfires, and jobu [[./thread#^c9505872|jobu]] cites diesel carbon buildup. ams6110 [[./thread#^c9506066|ams6110]] cautions that modern engine computers adjust operation automatically, making routine 'tune-up' language misleading. sliverstorm [[./thread#^c9505360|sliverstorm]] also distinguishes oil burning from excess fuel consumption.
- Smell, visible smoke, vehicle age and fuel economy are being used as informal indicators without validation against the relevant pollutants. organsnyder [[./thread#^c9505275|organsnyder]] infers their old car belonged among the worst emitters from its exhaust smell; michael\_h [[./thread#^c9506349|michael\_h]] offers high fuel economy and careful maintenance as context for an old car. alwaysdoit [[./thread#^c9505979|alwaysdoit]] explicitly asks whether visible or smellable exhaust reliably identifies the worst polluters, and receives no substantive answer in the supplied export.
- Inspection results must represent ordinary driving for them to establish routine exposure reductions. danudey [[./thread#^c9505526|danudey]] claims temporary preparation could let normally failing vehicles pass British Columbia's AirCare test and reports that failure rates fell from 14\% to 8\%. Those figures alone cannot distinguish lasting fleet improvement from changes in test behavior or other causes. sukilot [[./thread#^c9505645|sukilot]] interprets the decline as evidence of a successful but unfinished program.
- Buying a replacement car reduces the old vehicle's contribution only if its subsequent use is accounted for. 7952 [[./thread#^c9508230|7952]] points out that a serviceable car may simply be sold onward, while possibly displacing an even worse vehicle. koolkat [[./thread#^c9505398|koolkat]] asks about manufacturing pollution versus continued use, and sukilot [[./thread#^c9505703|sukilot]] suggests factory emissions may be easier to control. No comparative exposure analysis is supplied.
- Additional links do not independently validate the claims within this source unit. collyw [[./thread#^c9505363|collyw]] challenges the original shipping comparison's news source; SCAQTony [[./thread#^c9505538|SCAQTony]] supplies alternative organizations and publications. Those references broaden the claimed sourcing, but their underlying methods and findings are not included here. janvidar [[./thread#^c9505543|janvidar]] also stresses that different meanings of 'air pollution' must be separated.

## Conditions

- Inspection coverage depends on jurisdiction and vehicle age. In these 2015 accounts, collyw [[./thread#^c9505345|collyw]] describes annual checks for older cars owned in the UK and Spain; bryanlarsen [[./thread#^c9505404|bryanlarsen]] describes Ontario testing every two years after seven years; danmaz74 [[./thread#^c9506396|danmaz74]] reports Italy testing after four years and then every two years. These are historical commenter reports, not verified current requirements.
- Within-country variation is substantial. joshwd [[./thread#^c9505418|joshwd]] says southwest Virginia does not require emissions tests, and theandrewbailey [[./thread#^c9505426|theandrewbailey]] describes county differences in Pennsylvania. chiph [[./thread#^c9505481|chiph]] describes testing in populous Texas counties and distinguishes onboard computer checks from tailpipe probes. cowsandmilk [[./thread#^c9506453|cowsandmilk]] invokes large population-density differences to explain selective coverage; that rationale does not itself establish negligible exposure everywhere rural.
- Where someone lives relative to pollution sources and prevailing winds may affect both exposure and housing cost. Drawing on growing up near Detroit, danans [[./thread#^c9506373|danans]] associates locations north and west of people and factories with better air and more expensive property. organsnyder [[./thread#^c9506443|organsnyder]] counters that Michigan has poor air quality partly because it is downwind of Chicago. Neither supplies household measurements or a quantified housing-price relationship.
- Repair affordability can determine whether a polluting vehicle remains in service. organsnyder [[./thread#^c9505275|organsnyder]] says replacing an ineffective catalytic converter would have cost more than the car was worth. mgarfias [[./thread#^c9505861|mgarfias]] cites professional labor at roughly \$75–100/hour and potentially lengthy diagnosis. bryanlarsen [[./thread#^c9505404|bryanlarsen]] describes an Ontario waiver after \$450 of repair spending even if the problem persists, illustrating the tension between affordability and actual emissions reduction.
- Retirement incentives must compete with owners' valuation and replacement needs. malyk [[./thread#^c9507031|malyk]] reports California buyback payments of \$1,500 for low-income owners and \$1,000 for others. ScottBurson [[./thread#^c9506877|ScottBurson]] says offers for a 1993 Toyota MR2 were below its value to them, while being unsure whether the letters offered \$500 or \$1,000. The thread does not establish participation rates or the exposure benefit per subsidy dollar.
- Retrofitting depends on certification requirements as well as mechanical feasibility. c\_i\_v [[./thread#^c9506419|c\_i\_v]] describes California referee approval, inconsistent judgments and reports of difficult requirements such as transferring the donor vehicle's fuel tank. The commenter favors more comprehensive operational emissions testing as an alternative. These reported barriers qualify rpcope1's [[./thread#^c9506153|rpcope1]] proposal to preserve older cars with newer engines.
- Shipping exposure depends on fuel rules and geography. WilliamSt [[./thread#^c9506016|WilliamSt]] claims ships switch to cheaper sulfur-rich fuel after leaving the EU; Gravityloss [[./thread#^c9521219|Gravityloss]] points to sulfur regulations taking effect in 2015 in participating areas. These comments support distinguishing regional controls from worldwide practice, but do not demonstrate that the regulations eliminated health harms.
- Climate and local vehicle culture affect which older vehicles remain in use. bryanlarsen [[./thread#^c9505404|bryanlarsen]] and astrodust [[./thread#^c9506607|astrodust]] associate Ontario road salt with faster vehicle retirement, while serf [[./thread#^c9509130|serf]] describes conspicuous classic-car exhaust on Southern California summer weekends. HeyLaughingBoy [[./thread#^c9506752|HeyLaughingBoy]] cautions that salt does not selectively destroy polluting vehicles, so corrosion is not a targeted emissions intervention.

## Lived experiences

- ksab [[./thread#^c9506850|ksab]], identifying themself as lead author of the ultrafine-particle mapping paper, provides the strongest firsthand measurement account. At a Toronto roadside facility operating since 2006, they routinely observe CO2 peaks as vehicles pass, with some passages also producing NOx, black-carbon or ultrafine-particle peaks. They explicitly say that associating measurements with an individual vehicle can be extremely difficult even with road video. No numerical peak values, calibration results or indoor readings are supplied in this comment.
- organsnyder [[./thread#^c9505331|organsnyder]] reports asthma and particular sensitivity to vehicle exhaust in Michigan. They anticipate visibly suspect vehicles, switch the car to recirculation, change lanes or alter routes; these options work less well when walking or cycling. This is a firsthand avoidance strategy, without measured cabin concentrations or quantified symptom improvement. In a separate ownership account, organsnyder [[./thread#^c9505275|organsnyder]] reports an ineffective catalytic converter, a persistent warning light and repair costs exceeding the car's value.
- Accounts of noticeable exhaust differ by setting. maxerickson [[./thread#^c9505830|maxerickson]] says older carbureted trucks are more noticeable than smoking modern vehicles where they live in Michigan. ams6110 [[./thread#^c9505900|ams6110]] reports strong smells behind restored classic cars compared with little detectable odor from modern cars. serf [[./thread#^c9509130|serf]] describes fuel, oil and sometimes coolant odors from enthusiast vehicles in Southern California. These establish sensory experiences, not pollutant concentrations or comparative health risks.
- j\_baker [[./thread#^c9506196|j\_baker]] lives beside Oakland's port and reports pollution from ships and the diesel trucks serving them. They say recent state and federal regulations have made the problem less severe. This is a resident's account of perceived improvement, without before-and-after air measurements or medical outcomes.
- danans [[./thread#^c9508574|danans]] remembers visiting Los Angeles from Michigan during the 1980s and being able to 'taste' smog for the first time, while acknowledging subsequent improvement in Los Angeles. The experience explains their relative judgment of Michigan air, but is historical and sensory rather than a measurement of present conditions.
- mgarfias [[./thread#^c9505861|mgarfias]] reports spending approximately 20–30 hours diagnosing and repairing a persistent misfire despite owning capable diagnostic equipment and following service procedures. rpcope1 [[./thread#^c9506153|rpcope1]] reports installing newer engines in older Volvos and other vehicles. These are firsthand accounts of repair difficulty and retrofit feasibility; neither reports measured emissions or nearby air quality before and after.
- ScottBurson [[./thread#^c9506877|ScottBurson]] reports receiving multiple offers to retire a 1993 Toyota MR2 and declining because the vehicle was worth more than the offered amount. This illustrates a practical limit to voluntary scrappage, rather than evidence that this particular car was a high emitter.
- madaxe\_again [[./thread#^c9505607|madaxe\_again]] reports seeing trucks producing heavy black exhaust during travel in Russia and other former Soviet states. kaeluka [[./thread#^c9505648|kaeluka]] and smeyer [[./thread#^c9505743|smeyer]] respond that the study measured Toronto traffic. madaxe\_again [[./thread#^c9506935|madaxe\_again]] then clarifies that the broader claim is an extrapolation about poorer regions. The travel observations do not establish a global emissions distribution.
- collyw [[./thread#^c9505995|collyw]] describes cycling for most trips in a polluted city and using a car for seasonal weekend kayaking, with the car having passed emissions tests. In the ancestry of a discussion about test workarounds, this is not an admission of having used one. It supplies travel and ownership context, without comparative cycling-versus-driving exposure measurements.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The approved question's principal indoor comparisons remain unanswered by this unit. ksab [[./thread#^c9506850|ksab]] reports roadside CO2 and NOx peaks, and arcseco [[./thread#^c9507010|arcseco]] quotes ultrafine-particle counts; neither supplies PM2.5 mass concentrations, NO2-specific concentrations or indoor CO2 readings. organsnyder [[./thread#^c9505331|organsnyder]] describes recirculation without measurements. There are no supplied experiments comparing open versus closed windows, household ventilation or filtration, cooking emissions, or wildfire-smoke exposure, and no measured cognition or sleep effects.
- Measurement accuracy and vehicle attribution are separate unresolved issues. ksab [[./thread#^c9506850|ksab]] explicitly identifies difficulty assigning a plume to a particular vehicle, but does not report calibration, uncertainty or detection accuracy. josefresco [[./thread#^c9505298|josefresco]] questions how roadside observations establish effects of maintenance and driving style. c\_i\_v [[./thread#^c9506419|c\_i\_v]] separately questions how well limited operating-point inspections represent emissions across driving conditions. No comparative validation resolves these issues.
- The link from roadside particle counts to personal health burden is incomplete. arcseco [[./thread#^c9507010|arcseco]] supplies quoted concentrations and a proposed biological mechanism, while organsnyder [[./thread#^c9505331|organsnyder]] supplies an asthma-related sensitivity account. Neither establishes individual dose, a safe distance from traffic, a PM2.5- or NO2-specific effect, or the magnitude of benefit from a particular avoidance action. Odor complaints likewise do not quantify the harmful components.
- Michigan's air is characterized differently depending on location and comparator. danans [[./thread#^c9506373|danans]] considers it generally good when upwind of sources; organsnyder [[./thread#^c9506443|organsnyder]] disputes that using linked regional air-quality information; danans [[./thread#^c9508574|danans]] answers with comparisons to worse California conditions and a childhood experience. The exchange does not reconcile statewide indicators, neighborhood variation, historical conditions and personal susceptibility.
- The best inspection strategy remains contested: jchrisa [[./thread#^c9505329|jchrisa]] favors targeting gross emitters, atom-morgan [[./thread#^c9505387|atom-morgan]] raises unequal enforcement, and organsnyder [[./thread#^c9505876|organsnyder]] favors enforcing vehicles' original standards. Evidence of program effectiveness is also incomplete: danudey [[./thread#^c9505526|danudey]] says British Columbia's AirCare was scrapped, while Kluny [[./thread#^c9505608|Kluny]] still describes Vancouver testing. The supplied discussion does not reconcile that program-status discrepancy or compare resulting exposure reductions.
- The relative priority of cars, trucks, ships and smaller engines cannot be ranked from the supplied comparisons. phkahler [[./thread#^c9505357|phkahler]] questions whether heavy trucks were included; shaunrussell [[./thread#^c9505451|shaunrussell]] requests comparable studies of other transport modes; csours [[./thread#^c9506708|csours]] adds lawn equipment. Combined with the sulfur-specific correction from vonmoltke [[./thread#^c9505449|vonmoltke]], these comments leave a need for comparisons using the same pollutant, location, activity level and population exposure.
- Repairing, retrofitting, replacing or tolerating remaining high emitters involves unresolved tradeoffs. Retric [[./thread#^c9506258|Retric]] emphasizes repair benefits, mgarfias [[./thread#^c9505861|mgarfias]] emphasizes diagnostic costs, and 7952 [[./thread#^c9508230|7952]] questions whether replacement actually removes the old source. danmaz74 [[./thread#^c9506382|danmaz74]] prioritizes eliminating a concentrated residual share, whereas nitrogen [[./thread#^c9506468|nitrogen]] emphasizes its possible small absolute size. No common cost, emissions and exposure accounting settles these positions.
