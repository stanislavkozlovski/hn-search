# [San Francisco and Portland Ranked Most Polluted Major Cities in the World (Live)](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=24436499)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion disputes a live ranking that placed San Francisco and Portland among the world’s most polluted cities during severe wildfire smoke. Participants debate whether smoke counts as pollution and whether the title implies human-caused sources, track rapidly changing rankings, and compare the emergency conditions with cities that experience poor air quality routinely.
Tags: wildfire smoke, air quality index, pollution definitions, San Francisco, Portland

## Relevance

The thread concerns live PM2.5 air-quality readings during wildfire smoke, including changing city rankings and a reported PurpleAir reading. Comments distinguish wildfire smoke from other pollution sources and compare the exceptional episode with typical conditions, while offering little information about personal health effects or indoor mitigation. ## Arguments

- Wildfire smoke belongs in an assessment of current particulate exposure: 0x2a describes the ranking as current PM2.5 AQI [[./thread#^c24437028|0x2a]] and cites WHO and Wikipedia as including smoke within air pollution [[./thread#^c24437769|0x2a]]. These are the commenter's references; the supplied discussion does not reproduce their evidence.
- bamboozled argues that bad air remains pollution regardless of whether its source is wildfire or factories [[./thread#^c24436872|bamboozled]]. In a separate reply about shifting rankings, bamboozled considers having been worst very recently sufficient to defend the headline [[./thread#^c24436897|bamboozled]]. Neither argument supplies additional measurements.

## Counterarguments

- PieSquared objects that the headline suggests manufactured pollutants when the immediate problem is wildfire smoke [[./thread#^c24436824|PieSquared]]. pstrateman [[./thread#^c24437263|pstrateman]] and happytoexplain [[./thread#^c24439159|happytoexplain]] likewise appeal to the colloquial meaning of pollution as human-caused. Their objection concerns interpretation of the headline, rather than evidence that the smoky air is clean.
- A separate objection concerns timing: 30minAdayHN says San Francisco is no longer worst while explicitly acknowledging its bad pollution [[./thread#^c24436884|30minAdayHN]], and calls rapidly switching positions clickbait [[./thread#^c24436876|30minAdayHN]]. wheelerwj similarly reports Portland ahead and disputes treating the AQI ranking as a pollution ranking [[./thread#^c24436870|wheelerwj]].
- netsharc argues that a nearby major forest fire makes the extreme reading unsurprising, comparing the headline to advertising a fireplace's exceptional temperature [[./thread#^c24436847|netsharc]]. This challenges the representativeness and news value of the snapshot; it provides no evidence about exposure safety.

## Assumptions

- For source unit 24436499, part 0, the stated metric is current PM2.5 AQI, according to 0x2a [[./thread#^c24437028|0x2a]]. Interpreting this as a ranking of every pollutant, long-term exposure, or personal dose would exceed the supplied evidence. The reported AQI numbers should not be treated as PM2.5 mass concentrations.
- Several replies use claims about human responsibility for the fires to challenge the natural-versus-human pollution distinction: dutch3000 says the fire was human-caused [[./thread#^c24436960|dutch3000]], bamboozled invokes human influence on fire conditions [[./thread#^c24437612|bamboozled]], and hnlurker invokes a gender-reveal ignition [[./thread#^c24441661|hnlurker]]. These comments provide no supporting attribution evidence or measurements connecting particular causes to the reported city readings.

## Conditions

- The discussion concerns a wildfire episode affecting San Francisco and Portland, with Simulacra describing broader air-quality disruption across California [[./thread#^c24437268|Simulacra]]. netsharc explicitly situates the extreme readings near a major forest fire [[./thread#^c24436847|netsharc]]. These comments support an event-specific geographic context, not a permanent ranking of these cities.
- nine\_zeros claims that ordinary Jakarta air resembles San Francisco air during this historic wildfire episode, while San Francisco usually has very low pollution [[./thread#^c24436849|nine\_zeros]]. bozoUser extends the comparison to ordinary conditions in places such as Delhi versus the Bay Area and surrounding states during the fires [[./thread#^c24436912|bozoUser]]. These are unquantified comparisons; neither comment supplies matched measurements, exposure durations, or an explicit personal account of living in those cities.
- Replying to bozoUser's comparison of living conditions, dutch3000 argues that western wildfire emergencies are becoming normalized and that destruction of homes and businesses persists beyond the smoke episode [[./thread#^c24437001|dutch3000]]. This adds recurrence and material-loss qualifications to the analogy. It supplies no financial estimates and does not establish comparative health effects from the air itself.

## Lived experiences

- Urgo reports inspecting the live ranking approximately 38 minutes after submission and seeing Portland at 220 and San Francisco at 181 [[./thread#^c24436803|Urgo]]. wheelerwj also reports finding Portland ahead when opening the link [[./thread#^c24436870|wheelerwj]]. These are firsthand observations of displayed readings, not measurements taken with commenters' own instruments or reports of physical symptoms.
- In reply to Urgo, 0x2a says readings had fluctuated throughout the day and reports PurpleAir showing San Francisco above 300 US PM2.5 AQI, linking a map and screenshot [[./thread#^c24436989|0x2a]]. The comment was posted at 22:00 UTC, after Urgo's 21:36 UTC report. It establishes what the commenter reported seeing, without establishing calibration, representativeness, or personal exposure.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The definition of pollution remains contested. 0x2a invokes references that include smoke [[./thread#^c24437769|0x2a]], while pstrateman maintains that including smoke need not mean including wildfires and claims most pollution-related smoke comes from crop burning [[./thread#^c24443852|pstrateman]]. The latter prevalence claim is unsupported within the export; the exchange does not resolve the distinction.
- The headline's usefulness remains disputed: 30minAdayHN objects to emphasizing unstable positions [[./thread#^c24436876|30minAdayHN]], while bamboozled accepts a very recent worst ranking [[./thread#^c24436897|bamboozled]]. 0x2a reports updating the title to include Portland [[./thread#^c24437091|0x2a]], but the export records no agreement about how a live ranking should be framed.
- The difference between San Francisco's reported 181 and PurpleAir's reported value above 300 cannot establish sensor unreliability or agreement. Urgo [[./thread#^c24436803|Urgo]] and 0x2a [[./thread#^c24436989|0x2a]] describe different observations at different times; the comments do not align sensor locations, averaging periods, corrections, or aggregation methods, and provide no validation data.
- Evidence gap, rather than an expressed disagreement: the numerical accounts from Urgo [[./thread#^c24436803|Urgo]] and 0x2a [[./thread#^c24436989|0x2a]] concern outdoor AQI displays. This unit provides no firsthand health, cognition, sleep, or comfort outcomes; no NO2, CO2, or indoor measurements; and no reported trials of filtration, ventilation, cooking controls, or traffic-exposure reduction. It therefore cannot establish practical mitigation tradeoffs or when opening windows would improve indoor air versus admitting wildfire smoke.
