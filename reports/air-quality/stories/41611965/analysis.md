# [Infineon's CO2 Sensor Monitors Indoor Air Quality](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41611965)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants examine a new compact photoacoustic CO2 sensor, comparing its claimed accuracy and operating range with optical NDIR and other sensor designs. Discussion covers outdoor performance, temperature and noise sensitivity, calibration drift, measurement power and self-heating, component sourcing, and potential uses in indoor monitors, wearables, and distributed pollution networks.
Tags: CO2 sensors, photoacoustic sensing, NDIR, sensor accuracy, calibration, air quality monitoring

## Relevance

Examines CO2 sensor accuracy and reliability, including comparisons of photoacoustic and optical NDIR devices, calibration drift, environmental sensitivity, and measurement uncertainty. Comments also describe outdoor CO2 variation and how it can limit the benefit of ventilation. ## Arguments

- Direct CO2 sensing is distinguished from estimated CO2. vardump [[./thread#^c41612623|vardump]] warns that some devices infer CO2 from humidity, temperature and VOCs; shadowpho [[./thread#^c41612725|shadowpho]] describes alcohol-based estimates that assume human breathing and says they give poor results. These are arguments for checking what a device actually measures, rather than treating every air-quality reading as a CO2 measurement.
- Outdoor CO2 monitoring can inform ventilation decisions. cyberax [[./thread#^c41614854|cyberax]] argues that outdoor readings reveal when ventilation cannot deliver the desired reduction, while crazygringo [[./thread#^c41619223|crazygringo]] describes substantially different outdoor baselines across weather conditions. Their discussion supports comparing indoor and outdoor conditions instead of automatically treating outside air as 400 ppm.
- Photoacoustic sensing has a theoretical sensitivity advantage. Drawing on spectroscopy experience, OldGuyInTheClub [[./thread#^c41612909|OldGuyInTheClub]] explains that absorption produces a signal against an ideally zero background, avoiding a small difference between two large optical measurements. OldGuyInTheClub [[./thread#^c41618988|OldGuyInTheClub]] describes modulated light producing heating and pressure waves detected by a microphone; this explains the measurement mechanism, not a demonstrated health benefit.
- For the highway-adjacent dust account, seper8 [[./thread#^c41615989|seper8]] recommends a HEPA purifier and mentions IKEA purifiers and separate sensors. e44858 [[./thread#^c41618376|e44858]] recommends the Sensirion SEN54, describing temperature, humidity and VOC measurements plus a fan for faster response. These are proposed responses and product recommendations; neither comment reports measured particle reduction after installation.
- Several commenters propose more geographically detailed exposure monitoring. FL33TW00D [[./thread#^c41612878|FL33TW00D]] considers a continuous CO2/PM2.5 wearable; triwats [[./thread#^c41613143|triwats]] suggests bicycle-mounted devices and shared pollution maps. rapjr9 [[./thread#^c41616839|rapjr9]] points to BikeNet as an example of mapping town CO2, but supplies no measurements or evaluation results from that project.

## Counterarguments

- The announcement does not establish that inexpensive real CO2 sensing has only just become possible. Answering vardump, tzs [[./thread#^c41612798|tzs]] reports that the earlier PASCO2V01 already existed and had better nominal accuracy: ±(30 ppm + 3\%) over 400–5,000 ppm, versus ±(50 ppm + 5\%) over 400–3,000 ppm for the new device. The identified integration improvement is reducing the emitter supply from 12 V to 5 V; both still require 3.3 V for digital components.
- The accuracy terms are not competing alternatives. zootboy [[./thread#^c41614343|zootboy]] interprets the specification as whichever is worse, ±50 ppm or ±5\%; metaphor [[./thread#^c41614697|metaphor]] corrects this to an additive specification, giving ±70 ppm at 400 ppm and ±170 ppm at 3,000 ppm. metaphor also distinguishes the characterized accuracy range from the 0–32,000 ppm operating range and cites reference uncertainty of ±2\% and a sampling-rate caveat above one measurement per minute. These are reported datasheet interpretations, not independent measurements supplied here.
- Outdoor failures do not by themselves establish failure in the advertised indoor application. moogly [[./thread#^c41616815|moogly]] notes the announcement's indoor scope and criticizes AirGradient's marketing presence. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c41616880|ahaucnx]] replies that environmental failure conditions remain relevant to understanding sensor technology. The underlying test account itself says photoacoustic sensors generally perform well under normal indoor conditions [[./thread#^c41613827|ahaucnx]].
- An ideally zero-background technique is not immune to practical interference. OldGuyInTheClub [[./thread#^c41618927|OldGuyInTheClub]] identifies temperature, pressure, humidity, illuminated contaminants and noise at the modulation frequency as possible complications. They describe on/off-resonance measurements and synchronous detection as mitigations, while explicitly not knowing whether the compact devices provide the former capability.
- A plausible response does not establish authenticity or calibrated accuracy. ale42 [[./thread#^c41612716|ale42]] questions the provenance of inexpensive Sensirion parts on AliExpress, and throwup238 [[./thread#^c41614174|throwup238]] speculates about substituted hardware or emulation without identifying a tested counterfeit. In reply, tobi1449 [[./thread#^c41624207|tobi1449]] says their device responds as expected when windows open but acknowledges that comparison with a verified sensor would be needed.
- The proposed wearable network faces representativeness objections. crazygringo [[./thread#^c41612919|crazygringo]] worries that a wrist sensor would disproportionately sample recently exhaled breath. akira2501 [[./thread#^c41618665|akira2501]] goes further, asserting that air-quality measurement requires fixed devices, but provides no comparative test establishing that categorical restriction. The conflicting desk observations from christina97 [[./thread#^c41613213|christina97]] and stevenhuang [[./thread#^c41614240|stevenhuang]] leave placement and airflow as unresolved factors.

## Assumptions

- Automatic baseline correction assumes regular access to air with a known CO2 concentration. ckocagil [[./thread#^c41613969|ckocagil]] describes approximately weekly fresh-air exposure and assumes outdoor concentration is roughly uniform; wkat4242 [[./thread#^c41614057|wkat4242]] similarly describes a 400 ppm baseline. Animats [[./thread#^c41613032|Animats]] warns that drift correction based on minimum readings can misbehave in environments such as greenhouses, where photosynthesis may lower CO2 below ambient. These descriptions should not be treated as a verified account of every sensor's algorithm.
- Reference-instrument calibration is partly assumed rather than documented. avianlyric [[./thread#^c41615413|avianlyric]] proposes laboratory calibration using controlled gases and assumes reference instruments retain calibration for a known interval. That reply explains a possible approach; it does not establish the actual calibration procedure used in ahaucnx's comparisons.
- The explanation for photoacoustic outdoor underperformance remains a hypothesis. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c41613827|ahaucnx]] reports observing the performance problem but says they believe internal algorithms were developed around indoor temperature and concentration ranges. The observation and the proposed cause have different evidentiary status.
- The indoor values used for personal action are not established health thresholds. crazygringo [[./thread#^c41619223|crazygringo]] opens a window at about 800 ppm in winter to improve concentration and accepts around 1,000 ppm on some summer days. The comment does not demonstrate that either value marks a cognitive impairment threshold, or report a measured improvement after ventilation.
- Battery-life predictions depend on the actual sensing hardware and sampling schedule. sbierwagen [[./thread#^c41615269|sbierwagen]] predicts daily charging for an NDIR wearable because of light-source power consumption. FL33TW00D [[./thread#^c41616218|FL33TW00D]] proposes a particular STCC4/BMV080 sensor combination, but provides no implemented device or battery test. The daily-charge prediction is therefore not a measured property of that proposed wearable.
- Additional capabilities remain design ideas. londons\_explore [[./thread#^c41616510|londons\_explore]] proposes modifying firmware to measure moisture and VOCs through absorption spectra and describes the microcontroller and optical hardware [[./thread#^c41616651|londons\_explore]]; no successful multi-gas measurements are supplied. Likewise, dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c41612790|dzhiurgis]] proposes laptop integration using an existing fan, without demonstrating that the resulting placement and airflow would produce representative readings.

## Conditions

- The ventilation account is geographically and meteorologically specific. crazygringo [[./thread#^c41619223|crazygringo]] describes an unnamed city with windy winter days and hot, stagnant summer inversions. iforgotpassword [[./thread#^c41620398|iforgotpassword]] had expected higher winter CO2 because of heating and reduced greenery, but supplies no measurements. Also, outdoor readings of 700–800 ppm alongside indoor readings around 1,000 ppm do not demonstrate that ventilation has zero potential benefit; they limit how far outside air could lower the concentration, qualifying cyberax's description of ventilation as futile [[./thread#^c41614854|cyberax]].
- Reported component prices in this 2024 discussion span several budgets: londons\_explore [[./thread#^c41616510|londons\_explore]] cites approximately \$10 MH-Z19 sensors, rainburg [[./thread#^c41612709|rainburg]] quotes about \$22 for a Senseair S88 or \$13 each above 100 units, and skykooler [[./thread#^c41614297|skykooler]] reports \$26 for the Infineon part at Mouser. nimish [[./thread#^c41612670|nimish]] describes roughly \$25–40 components in small quantities. These are component prices, not demonstrated total costs for calibrated, enclosed monitors.
- Particle-monitor recommendations do not clearly satisfy the stated budget. clumsysmurf [[./thread#^c41612752|clumsysmurf]] asks for PM2.5/PM10 sensing below \$100 each; trog [[./thread#^c41612808|trog]] subsequently acknowledges that the suggested CO2.click products cost substantially more. At the other end, simonebrunozzi [[./thread#^c41630593|simonebrunozzi]] asks for the best indoor CO2 sensor with a budget up to \$1,000, without receiving an answer in this export.
- Long-term maintenance can matter more than initial component price. Animats [[./thread#^c41613032|Animats]] contrasts a stated ten-year device life with older devices claiming fifteen-plus years and imagines a hotel maintaining a thousand sensors. They also describe calibration with known gases; nimish [[./thread#^c41612670|nimish]] notes that a CO2 gas setup is expensive. Answering a sourcing question, Animats [[./thread#^c41614473|Animats]] reports that the advertised Murata devices avoiding this recalibration requirement were unavailable through the major distributors they checked.

## Lived experiences

- ahaucnx [[./thread#^c41613827|ahaucnx]] reports extensive indoor and outdoor comparisons of photoacoustic and optical NDIR sensors against scientific reference instruments. They found photoacoustic devices satisfactory under ordinary indoor conditions, substantially worse outdoors, and susceptible to low-frequency interference, potentially from ventilation systems or refrigerators. They consequently prefer optical sensors for their monitors. ahaucnx identifies an AirGradient company affiliation elsewhere [[./thread#^c41614111|ahaucnx]]; the underlying curves and detailed protocol are linked but not reproduced in the supplied text.
- ahaucnx [[./thread#^c41614156|ahaucnx]] reports rural outdoor CO2 reaching 700–800 ppm and says reference instruments verified those concentrations. This is a firsthand report of reference-checked elevated outdoor readings. The separate attribution to plants is debated in the replies and is not established merely by verifying the concentration.
- crazygringo [[./thread#^c41619223|crazygringo]] reports outdoor readings in the low 400s on windy winter days and 700–800 ppm during hot, windless summer inversions. They crack a window when indoor CO2 reaches about 800 ppm in winter and accept approximately 1,000 ppm indoors on certain summer days. Vehicle emissions and possibly power plants are their suggested sources, not independently identified causes; no before/after concentration or cognitive test is supplied.
- mschuster91 [[./thread#^c41615636|mschuster91]], replying to an explanation of nighttime plant respiration, reports a cannabis grow tent reaching roughly 800 ppm by the end of its artificial night under an 18-hour-light/six-hour-dark schedule. This supports a nighttime increase in that particular enclosed growing environment, without isolating which organisms or processes produced it.
- clumsysmurf [[./thread#^c41612752|clumsysmurf]] reports repeatedly finding black dust on a desk in a building about 500 feet from a busy highway. This is an observation of deposited material, not a PM2.5 or PM10 concentration measurement, particle-size analysis, confirmed traffic-source attribution, or reported health outcome.
- The desk accounts conflict. christina97 [[./thread#^c41613213|christina97]] says their desk-mounted CO2 monitor shows no detectable difference when they are present versus absent. Replying directly, stevenhuang [[./thread#^c41614240|stevenhuang]] says an SCD41 shows a large spike within a minute of sitting down and suggests good airflow might explain the other experience. Neither account controls placement, ventilation, calibration or sampling settings.
- sbierwagen [[./thread#^c41615330|sbierwagen]] reports building an SCD30 meter five years earlier and finding unexpectedly high power consumption and self-heating when polling more often than every ten minutes unless substantial airflow passes over the sensor. The account does not quantify the resulting CO2 error or establish the same behavior for other sensor models.
- Several inexpensive-sensor endorsements lack reference validation. londons\_explore [[./thread#^c41616510|londons\_explore]] says MH-Z19 sensors appear to work well but explicitly cannot assess their accuracy. IgorPartola [[./thread#^c41612639|IgorPartola]] says previously used approximately \$25 sensors worked well, and shadowpho [[./thread#^c41612725|shadowpho]] reports favorable experience with SCD30/31/40 devices. These are usability impressions, not comparable accuracy measurements.
- There are working integration examples: babl-yc [[./thread#^c41612927|babl-yc]] built an open-source monitor using an SCD40; nimish [[./thread#^c41612670|nimish]] made a breakout with a boost converter for an older 12 V Infineon sensor; radicality [[./thread#^c41613408|radicality]], replying in the SCD41 discussion, reports a sensor obtained from M5Stack with readings submitted every five seconds through ESPHome/Home Assistant. Successful integration and frequent reporting do not themselves establish accuracy.
- For particles, christina97 [[./thread#^c41613251|christina97]] reports using a Plantower PMS5003 with ESPHome and calls it pretty good, without giving readings or comparison results. trog [[./thread#^c41612808|trog]] likes an existing CO2.click CO2 monitor but explicitly has not purchased its PM monitor. carstenhag [[./thread#^c41613310|carstenhag]] describes a community pollution-monitoring device left on a shared-flat balcony in Heilbronn, Germany, but reports no measured concentrations or exposure improvements.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Calibration in persistently elevated air remains unanswered. Rebelgecko [[./thread#^c41623184|Rebelgecko]] asks how to prevent a sensor whose minimum is 700 ppm from resetting that minimum to 400 ppm, and whether disabling automatic correction leads to drift. ahaucnx's earlier response [[./thread#^c41614770|ahaucnx]] distinguishes comparison with reference data from calibration, but does not resolve this operational question. hyperknot [[./thread#^c41614904|hyperknot]] describes twenty minutes outdoors as a usual calibration practice; the reported outdoor variability leaves its accuracy uncertain.
- The rural nighttime CO2 source is disputed. darby\_nine [[./thread#^c41615109|darby\_nine]] suggests decomposition and says the linked material does not identify living plants; avianlyric [[./thread#^c41615343|avianlyric]] argues for plant respiration and infers attribution from nighttime peaks. Projectiboga [[./thread#^c41620263|Projectiboga]] instead attributes CO2 to microorganisms and claims evening oxygen release. nroets [[./thread#^c41618542|nroets]] and stavros [[./thread#^c41620657|stavros]] emphasize plants' net CO2 uptake, while tedivm [[./thread#^c41619244|tedivm]] explains that net uptake can coexist with periods of net release. The thread supplies no source-separating experiment.
- The extent of sensor-specific failure is unclear. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c41613827|ahaucnx]] describes testing sensor technologies and updating specifications for the new Infineon device, without explicitly identifying that new model as a tested unit. The supplied text does not quantify outdoor errors or indoor acoustic interference. When dr\_kiszonka [[./thread#^c41617707|dr\_kiszonka]] asks about humming, OldGuyInTheClub [[./thread#^c41618988|OldGuyInTheClub]] explains internally generated pressure waves; no account establishes audible annoyance or a comfort impact.
- A best-product ranking is not established. simonebrunozzi's request for the best indoor sensor up to \$1,000 remains unanswered [[./thread#^c41630593|simonebrunozzi]]. In the Aranet4 branch, thadk [[./thread#^c41613602|thadk]] says they believe it contains a Senseair Sunrise optical sensor, while elliottkember [[./thread#^c41613166|elliottkember]] characterizes the announced alternative as cheaper and more accurate without providing a comparison. These comments do not resolve accuracy, calibration stability, battery life and price together.
- Mobile measurement remains an open design question. triwats [[./thread#^c41613143|triwats]] explicitly asks whether a bicycle-mounted device would only be useful while stationary. crazygringo's concern about nearby exhaled air [[./thread#^c41612919|crazygringo]] and akira2501's fixed-device requirement [[./thread#^c41618665|akira2501]] are not settled by a moving-versus-stationary test. The discussion does not determine when such devices represent surrounding ambient air versus localized personal exposure.
- Ozone is raised without supporting measurements. specto [[./thread#^c41618261|specto]] calls it a missing pollutant, claims only governments measure it outdoors and says levels are increasing. TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c41620180|TeMPOraL]] asks whether increases might be beneficial at another altitude, and RF\_Savage [[./thread#^c41620467|RF\_Savage]] replies that ozone is harmful to breathe. The export provides no evidence verifying the monitoring exclusivity, trend or anyone's ozone exposure.
- Health and intervention outcomes remain largely unexamined in this source unit. crazygringo [[./thread#^c41619223|crazygringo]] describes concentration as a reason for opening a window, without documenting cognitive improvement; clumsysmurf [[./thread#^c41612752|clumsysmurf]] reports dust rather than illness; seper8 [[./thread#^c41615989|seper8]] recommends filtration without reporting its effects. There are no supplied health-study results, sleep outcomes or controlled comfort comparisons. There are also no numeric PM2.5/PM10 readings, NO2 measurements, cooking or wildfire accounts, or paired measurements showing windows increasing particle or NO2 exposure. Consequently, this part cannot establish the broader health effects or filtration-versus-ventilation tradeoffs requested by the approved question.
