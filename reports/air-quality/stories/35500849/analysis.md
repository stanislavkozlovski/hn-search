# [Car exhaust filters fail to clean up ‘ultrafine’ air pollution](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=35500849)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion considers research that diesel particulate filters may have limited effects on ultrafine emissions, alongside questions about how exhaust particles compare with tire, brake, and road-wear pollution. Participants debate which sources matter most for air quality and health, how particle size and composition affect exposure, and whether the headline distracts from other emissions. The thread explores regulation and measurement, vehicle weight and electric-vehicle tradeoffs, and potential interventions such as improved filters, cleaner fuels, and reducing car dependence. A long side discussion contrasts propane or LPG vehicles with electric vehicles, including emissions, efficiency, infrastructure, safety, and conversion costs.
Tags: ultrafine particles, diesel filters, vehicle pollution, tire and road wear, emissions regulation, alternative fuels

## Relevance

The discussion concerns ultrafine and other particulate pollution from traffic, including firsthand reports of deposits on balconies and skin, roadside cycling exposure, and uncertainty about particle sources and measurement. Comments compare exhaust controls with tire and brake wear and discuss masks and filters. ## Arguments

- Exhaust controls can substantially reduce one pollution measure while leaving another elevated. schiffern [[./thread#^c35505142|schiffern]] quotes study results reporting an 81\% decline in black carbon between 2014 and 2021, versus a 26\% decline in ultrafine particle counts below 100 nm, with little change in liquid particles below 30 nm. These are reported research findings, not the commenter's own measurements.
- Non-exhaust pollution deserves attention alongside exhaust. moffkalast [[./thread#^c35501745|moffkalast]] argues that tyre emissions persist with electrification and cites a 2000-fold comparison with exhaust. sandworm101 [[./thread#^c35502053|sandworm101]] proposes capturing tyre particles through airflow and filters and changing chemical additives. Neither supplies a tested mitigation design; the meaning of the numerical comparison is contested in replies.
- Exposure sources extend beyond vehicles. amluto [[./thread#^c35503407|amluto]] summarizes a Bay Area inventory as 5\% vehicle exhaust, 20\% road dust plus tyre and brake emissions, 8\% commercial cooking, and 12\% residential wood combustion, explicitly declining to vouch for data quality. They argue that cooking emissions concentrated around restaurants may create substantial pedestrian exposure even when their regional contribution appears modest.
- Some commenters favor targeting remaining conspicuous sources after earlier controls have reduced exhaust. coredog64 [[./thread#^c35503830|coredog64]] argues for addressing diminishing returns; amluto [[./thread#^c35505479|amluto]] identifies gardening equipment, some trucks, and motorcycles; WirelessGigabit [[./thread#^c35508004|WirelessGigabit]] adds leaf-blower exhaust and resuspended dust. These are prioritization arguments without comparative exposure-reduction measurements.
- Practical suggestions combine exposure avoidance and filtration. throwaway290 [[./thread#^c35501632|throwaway290]] describes checking local AQI, closing windows, running an indoor air filter, and avoiding cycling on bad days. hedgehog [[./thread#^c35503943|hedgehog]] recommends an N95 for strenuous roadside cycling and dusty yard work. The thread supplies no measured before-and-after effectiveness for these measures.
- Improving exhaust filtration is proposed as an intervention that could happen before widespread vehicle replacement. xvilka [[./thread#^c35501252|xvilka]] argues for better filters; this is a proposal rather than evidence that a particular replacement filter removes the residual ultrafine emissions discussed in the study.
- Cleaner propulsion or fuel is another proposed route to reduced local exposure. Gordonjcp [[./thread#^c35501724|Gordonjcp]] claims propane produces less CO, NOx, SOx, and particulate pollution than petrol, while iamerroragent [[./thread#^c35508409|iamerroragent]] argues that electric vehicles substantially improve city air. Neither provides comparative personal-exposure measurements, and the non-exhaust discussion limits any inference that electrification eliminates all traffic pollution.

## Counterarguments

- Total particle mass does not settle the airborne exposure comparison. hokkos [[./thread#^c35503452|hokkos]] challenges the relevance of the 2000-fold tyre figure; ip26 [[./thread#^c35504146|ip26]] notes that total emissions plus a statement that ultrafines exist does not establish their relative quantities. jeffbee [[./thread#^c35503909|jeffbee]] counters that tyre and road particles can remain airborne like similarly sized exhaust particles. The replies dispute the comparison rather than demonstrate that either source is harmless.
- A small current exhaust contribution may indicate successful regulation. fooblaster [[./thread#^c35503645|fooblaster]] challenges amluto's inference that regulators focused on the wrong sources, arguing that controls may explain why exhaust now contributes relatively little. This is compatible with subsequently targeting other sources, as coredog64 [[./thread#^c35503830|coredog64]] proposes.
- Lower ambient concentration does not necessarily mean lower inhaled dose during exercise. Responding to \_dain\_'s claim that car interiors are more polluted [[./thread#^c35501319|\_dain\_]], BaculumMeumEst [[./thread#^c35501433|BaculumMeumEst]] quotes research reporting black-carbon concentrations of 6.3 μg/m³ in motorized transport versus 3.4 μg/m³ while walking or cycling, but a reversed relationship after accounting for inhalation. The quotation also reports higher PM2.5 doses among cyclists in another study. These are distinct reported findings, not personal readings or a calculation of cycling's net health effects.
- Turning recirculation off does not establish that incoming cabin air is unfiltered. sokoloff [[./thread#^c35501720|sokoloff]] directly challenges that assumption in BaculumMeumEst's interpretation [[./thread#^c35501433|BaculumMeumEst]], pointing to the cabin filter's intended function. No vehicle-specific filtration efficiency, maintenance condition, or operating comparison is supplied.
- Wood heating cannot be evaluated as though every appliance were an open fireplace. generalizations [[./thread#^c35503736|generalizations]] argues that wood can be cheaper and resilient during power failures; grecy [[./thread#^c35504002|grecy]] describes effective modern stoves in extreme cold, countering flakeoil's picture of inefficient fireplaces requiring frequent attention [[./thread#^c35503930|flakeoil]]. amluto [[./thread#^c35505444|amluto]] subsequently distinguishes well-designed stoves from fireplaces and accepts the former in principle.
- Overall city health does not isolate the effect of polluted air. medler [[./thread#^c35503355|medler]] counters the claim that Manhattan is necessarily unhealthy by asserting that residents are comparatively healthy. schiffern [[./thread#^c35504757|schiffern]] identifies wealth as a confounder, and davidmurdoch [[./thread#^c35512309|davidmurdoch]] suggests walking and diet. None provides an analysis separating these factors from air exposure.
- Filtering smaller particles is not presented as a universally monotonic difficulty. nuccy [[./thread#^c35501647|nuccy]] claims increasingly fine exhaust filtration imposes increasing energy costs, whereas LaputanMachine [[./thread#^c35501852|LaputanMachine]] describes a HEPA filtration minimum around 0.3 μm and diffusion-based capture below 0.1 μm. schiffern [[./thread#^c35505142|schiffern]] suggests diesel filters use different mechanisms. The HEPA explanation therefore cannot establish the performance or energy requirements of a specific exhaust filter.

## Assumptions

- Vehicle mass and material consumption are repeatedly used as proxies for pollution without a demonstrated conversion to inhaled dose. Neil44 [[./thread#^c35501791|Neil44]] reasons from tyre lifetime, fooblaster [[./thread#^c35503571|fooblaster]] compares fuel consumption with tyre replacement, and chongli [[./thread#^c35502036|chongli]] applies fourth-power vehicle-mass scaling to tyre and road wear. None supplies a calculation connecting those quantities to airborne PM2.5 or ultrafine exposure; ZeroGravitas [[./thread#^c35505652|ZeroGravitas]] also disputes the premise that equivalent EVs must be heavier.
- A roadside pattern supports a local-source hypothesis without identifying particle composition or health impact. wouldbecouldbe [[./thread#^c35503639|wouldbecouldbe]] notes that only the road-facing balconies accumulate black material, but neither that observation nor the original report establishes its PM2.5 content. Their accompanying claim of roughly five years lower city life expectancy [[./thread#^c35501789|wouldbecouldbe]] has no supporting analysis in the supplied discussion.
- Immediate symptoms and sensory impressions cannot establish every long-term hazard. xiphias2 [[./thread#^c35503783|xiphias2]] reports asthma symptoms near combustion sources and no comparable reaction to EV tyres, which distinguishes their experience without demonstrating an absence of tyre-related risk. Similarly, \_dain\_'s assertion connecting new-car smell with cancer [[./thread#^c35501319|\_dain\_]] is not supported by exposure or health evidence here.
- Claims that modern wood stoves have negligible emissions remain assumptions. amluto [[./thread#^c35505444|amluto]] explicitly says they lack actual data for that belief. grecy [[./thread#^c35504002|grecy]] claims substantial particulate reductions from repeated combustion, but provides no emissions measurements establishing either their size or negligible residual exposure.
- The claim that accumulated indoor smoke exposure exceeded a particular walk depends on unknown circumstances. throwaway290 [[./thread#^c35501632|throwaway290]] infers that maxbond probably had not closed windows or used filtration. maxbond's account [[./thread#^c35501534|maxbond]] does not establish those household conditions, indoor concentrations, or relative doses.
- Cleaner exhaust is assumed to imply indoor safety in part of the propane discussion. Gordonjcp [[./thread#^c35510082|Gordonjcp]] makes categorical claims about absent CO and hydrocarbons and harmless forklift exhaust, but supplies no emission tests or indoor exposure measurements. The discussion does not substantiate that safety conclusion or establish NO2 or CO2 concentrations in an occupied space.

## Conditions

- Source contributions depend on location and traffic mix. amluto's percentages concern the Bay Area [[./thread#^c35503407|amluto]], while 1970-01-01 [[./thread#^c35502904|1970-01-01]] links research summaries emphasizing diesel trucks and buses in near-road pollution. These statements do not establish a universal exhaust-versus-tyre proportion or the mixture at a particular residence.
- Cold-region road conditions can change the non-exhaust problem. marginalia\_nu [[./thread#^c35501872|marginalia\_nu]] describes studded tyres abrading road surfaces, seasonal restrictions, and research they interpret as especially relevant to colder regions. This qualification limits extrapolation from studded-tyre findings to ordinary tyres everywhere.
- Heating alternatives depend on fuel prices, power reliability, and climate. Why\_O\_My [[./thread#^c35504098|Why\_O\_My]] associates increased Dutch wood burning with expensive natural gas; generalizations [[./thread#^c35503736|generalizations]] emphasizes unreliable electricity; grecy [[./thread#^c35504002|grecy]] describes living where −40 is common. hansvm [[./thread#^c35504306|hansvm]] offers a low-wage scenario in which collected wood and limited daily labor make a stove economical within a winter. These are local accounts and asserted economics, not universal cost comparisons.
- Ventilation can carry a thermal cost as well as an exposure implication. amluto [[./thread#^c35505444|amluto]] argues that fireplaces expel substantial heated indoor air and draw in cold makeup air, reducing useful heating. That appliance-specific account does not supply measurements of indoor pollutant removal or show that more incoming outdoor air is always beneficial.
- The economics of a proposed cleaner-fuel conversion vary geographically. Gordonjcp [[./thread#^c35504941|Gordonjcp]] quotes about £1000 to convert an existing petrol car and claims cheaper fuel. iamerroragent [[./thread#^c35508409|iamerroragent]] questions savings in the United States because of propane pricing, lower energy density, conversion expense, and warranty concerns. Neither supplies a complete cost or exposure-benefit calculation.
- Fuel availability and operating restrictions constrain conversion proposals. toast0 [[./thread#^c35504640|toast0]] emphasizes filling infrastructure, fleet advantages, and tank requirements; WirelessGigabit [[./thread#^c35508152|WirelessGigabit]] reports Belgian underground-parking restrictions for LPG vehicles. However, toast0's specific California inspection experience concerns CNG [[./thread#^c35507785|toast0]], and Gordonjcp [[./thread#^c35510047|Gordonjcp]] distinguishes CNG's storage pressure from LPG's. Those experiences should not be treated as interchangeable.

## Lived experiences

- xiphias2 [[./thread#^c35503783|xiphias2]], describing significant asthma, reports becoming very sick around nearby combustion vehicles, cigarettes, and burning furniture, but not noticing sickness from electric-car tyres. This is a firsthand account of symptoms and perceived triggers, without pollutant-specific measurements.
- wouldbecouldbe [[./thread#^c35501789|wouldbecouldbe]] reports white balcony surfaces becoming black within 1.5–2 weeks beside a busy urban road where vehicles travel around 30 km/h. They later add that balconies on the opposite side do not show the same accumulation and that local industry is elsewhere [[./thread#^c35503639|wouldbecouldbe]]. This is a repeated deposition observation, not a measured airborne concentration.
- user3939382 [[./thread#^c35502072|user3939382]] describes black smudges on a damp towel after wiping their face following a Manhattan commute involving train travel, later emphasizing approximately 15 minutes of walking [[./thread#^c35504532|user3939382]]. LeanderK [[./thread#^c35503650|LeanderK]] does not recall comparable experiences in subways used mostly in Munich. Neither account identifies the dust or measures its respirable fraction.
- nannal [[./thread#^c35501455|nannal]] reports that their air-quality sensor reveals good and bad days without another apparent explanation. They are considering a portable version that would prompt masking. No numerical readings, sensor model, calibration information, or completed portable-monitor trial is reported.
- throwaway290 [[./thread#^c35501375|throwaway290]] reports sometimes coughing on high-AQI days even without much physical exertion and consulting pollution information before extended walking or cycling. They also describe closing windows and running an indoor filter when outdoor conditions are bad [[./thread#^c35501632|throwaway290]], without reporting measured indoor improvements.
- maxbond [[./thread#^c35501534|maxbond]] describes a nighttime walk through haze, later seeing approximately 1 mm ash particles and suspecting a nearby prescribed burn. They report shortness of breath and difficulty concentrating over the following days and regret not wearing a mask they carried. The visible ash is not a PM2.5 measurement, and neither the source nor medical causation was confirmed.
- Why\_O\_My [[./thread#^c35504098|Why\_O\_My]] reports noticing increased wood-burning odors in the Netherlands. moffkalast [[./thread#^c35511236|moffkalast]] describes winter evenings in their unnamed city as severely smoky from materials burned for heating. amluto [[./thread#^c35503407|amluto]] also describes smoked-turkey vendors as unpleasant to be around. These accounts document perceived exposure and discomfort without quantified concentrations.
- Operational experiences with propane conflict but do not measure its air-quality benefits. gambiting [[./thread#^c35502120|gambiting]] reports owning an LPG car that worked in harsh winters after starting briefly on petrol; doubled112 [[./thread#^c35501863|doubled112]] instead recounts an acquaintance's freezing-valve problems. Gordonjcp [[./thread#^c35516736|Gordonjcp]] reports normal coolant temperature while towing with an LPG-converted Range Rover. The last observation addresses one vehicle's operation, not its pollutant output.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The relative inhalation hazard of tyres, road dust, brakes, and exhaust remains unresolved. alex\_duf [[./thread#^c35502695|alex\_duf]] explicitly expresses uncertainty between competing claims. ip26 [[./thread#^c35504146|ip26]] requests an appropriate ultrafine comparison, while jeffbee [[./thread#^c35504282|jeffbee]] asserts that the research is sound. 6510 [[./thread#^c35503789|6510]] supplies links to reporting and an emissions-testing company, but no quoted protocol that resolves the thread's disagreements about mass, particle number, suspension, composition, or health impact.
- The black material observed on balconies and faces is not identified. inconceivable [[./thread#^c35501895|inconceivable]] attributes the balcony deposit to tyres and brakes; 1970-01-01 [[./thread#^c35502049|1970-01-01]] proposes a broader urban mixture; sandworm101 [[./thread#^c35502015|sandworm101]] raises a possible mold source. dghughes [[./thread#^c35502838|dghughes]] suggests burning a sample, but provides no validation of that proposed identification method. For the commute account, grepfru\_it [[./thread#^c35503417|grepfru\_it]] notes that NYC subway trains do not use rubber tyres, without establishing the actual source of the observed dirt.
- The effectiveness of proposed engineering fixes is unquantified. yyyk [[./thread#^c35504169|yyyk]] challenges the unspecified airflow and tyre-filtration proposal. For exhaust, schiffern [[./thread#^c35505142|schiffern]] reports persistent liquid ultrafines, while dingaling [[./thread#^c35506887|dingaling]] claims filter regeneration merely relocates captured pollution. The supplied discussion does not measure regeneration exposure or demonstrate which redesigned filter or capture system would reduce the remaining problem.
- Sensor reliability and the value of portable monitoring remain unsettled. nannal [[./thread#^c35501455|nannal]] favors local sensor-triggered masking, whereas throwaway290 [[./thread#^c35501632|throwaway290]] considers regional AQI sufficient for their routine and says accurate monitors tend to be large and expensive. No calibration, reference comparison, detection limits, pollutant coverage, or numerical personal PM2.5 readings are supplied.
- The window tradeoff is only partially addressed. throwaway290 [[./thread#^c35501632|throwaway290]] recommends closing windows when outdoor AQI is poor, implying concern about admitting polluted air. No commenter reports a comparison showing when opening windows improves indoor conditions, how filtration changes that choice, or what happens when outdoor pollution coincides with indoor cooking or accumulated CO2. There is no measured ventilation-versus-filtration outcome here.
- NO2, indoor CO2, and household cooking remain major evidence gaps in this unit. amluto [[./thread#^c35503407|amluto]] discusses commercial-cooking inventory estimates and low-NOx water heaters, while Gordonjcp [[./thread#^c35501724|Gordonjcp]] and [[./thread#^c35510082|Gordonjcp]] discusses exhaust gases. These comments provide no NO2-specific exposure readings, occupied-room CO2 measurements, or household cooking experiments; they cannot establish gas-specific effects on cognition or comfort.
- Net health comparisons remain contested rather than calculated. BaculumMeumEst [[./thread#^c35501433|BaculumMeumEst]] highlights inhaled dose during cycling, but supplies no balancing of pollution harm against exercise benefits. In the Manhattan branch, mhb [[./thread#^c35505951|mhb]] asks for alternatives with better costs and benefits, while mort96 [[./thread#^c35505621|mort96]] argues that harms must first be examined and later proposes walking, cycling, and public transport improvements [[./thread#^c35506179|mort96]]. No supplied analysis resolves those tradeoffs.
- Cognition and sleep claims have narrow support. maxbond [[./thread#^c35501534|maxbond]] reports difficulty concentrating after suspected prescribed-burn exposure, without cognitive testing or a quantified dose. throwaway290 [[./thread#^c35501632|throwaway290]] mentions exposure during sleep, not observed sleep impairment or improvement. The smoke account is not a confirmed wildfire exposure, so this unit provides no direct wildfire comparison or measured sleep benefit from filtration or ventilation.
