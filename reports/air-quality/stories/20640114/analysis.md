# [How to Measure Indoor Air Quality with a Raspberry Pi, RuuviTags and PMS7003](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=20640114)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants discuss a DIY indoor air-quality monitor using a Raspberry Pi, particulate sensor, and environmental tags, including what measurements can support practical decisions. A major thread concerns sensor accuracy, calibration, humidity effects, sensor specifications, and the risk that inexpensive commercial devices report misleading CO₂ or particle readings. Participants compare DIY systems with ready-made monitors and discuss data access, privacy, network integration, and air purifiers. The choice of Java for the project prompts debate about performance, memory use, deployment, and developer familiarity, while other comments cover outdoor sensor limitations and monitoring noise.
Tags: air-quality monitoring, Raspberry Pi, DIY sensors, sensor accuracy, CO₂, open data

## Relevance

The thread focuses on measuring indoor PM and CO2, sensor accuracy, calibration, and limits of low-cost devices, including humidity effects on outdoor readings. Comments discuss using readings to judge purifier performance and when to ventilate, while distinguishing real CO2 sensors from estimated eCO2. ## Arguments

- Monitoring could make air-quality decisions actionable: joshefin [[./thread#^c20641289|joshefin]] proposes converting particulate readings into an air-quality index and sending alerts to prompt purification, vacuuming, or opening windows. This answers softgrow's [[./thread#^c20641222|softgrow]] question about practical value, but neither supplies a measured before-and-after result for those interventions.
- Sensor identity matters more than a broad air-quality label. throwaway9d0291 [[./thread#^c20642939|throwaway9d0291]] recommends checking component datasheets and choosing an NDIR sensor for actual CO2 measurement. Their subsequent recommendations distinguish several researched CO2 sensors from a cheaper, less accurate but still directly measuring Senseair S8 Residential [[./thread#^c20651988|throwaway9d0291]]. These are component recommendations, not comparative tests supplied in the discussion.
- Low-cost particulate sensors may still support approximate decisions. joshefin [[./thread#^c20641352|joshefin]] argues that the PMS7003 is unsuitable for exact concentrations but adequate for broad pollution categories. That is a claim about practical usefulness despite limited precision, rather than a claim of research-grade accuracy.
- The relevant pollutant depends on the setting. joshefin [[./thread#^c20643855|joshefin]] argues that particulate information may be more useful locally than the CO2 monitoring discussed for Netatmo. Their description of RuuviTag covers temperature, humidity, and pressure [[./thread#^c20643109|joshefin]]; those measurements should not be mistaken for particulate or gas measurements. leggomylibro [[./thread#^c20640772|leggomylibro]] additionally cautions that some broadly responsive gas sensors produce an aggregate response rather than distinguish individual gases.
- Purifiers are proposed as the intervention that monitoring alone cannot provide. sumoboy [[./thread#^c20641596|sumoboy]] argues for room or whole-house purification, while magoon [[./thread#^c20643455|magoon]] recommends a bedroom purifier with automatic sensor-driven operation and a quiet low setting. These comments establish proposed uses and owner preferences, but not measured exposure reductions or health benefits.
- Accessible historical data is an important practical requirement for some participants. electriclove [[./thread#^c20644987|electriclove]] wants accurate readings available through a queryable interface without a subscription, and throwaway9d0291 [[./thread#^c20651952|throwaway9d0291]] explains that even an identified CO2 sensor is insufficient for their purposes without computer-readable output. This favors inspectable, usable monitoring systems without establishing their measurement accuracy.

## Counterarguments

- The Xiaomi recommendation receives a specific measurement challenge. throwaway9d0291 [[./thread#^c20642871|throwaway9d0291]] says the advertised combined Sensirion TVOC/CO2 sensor appears to estimate CO2 from hydrogen rather than measure it directly, and cites concerns about Xiaomi particulate sensing. They explicitly express uncertainty about this particular model; the cited 15\% specification is not an observed error from pmg's devices [[./thread#^c20641966|pmg]].
- Poor performance for one output does not necessarily establish poor performance for every output. magicalhippo [[./thread#^c20647463|magicalhippo]] describes an external consumer-versus-professional comparison as showing reasonably good TVOC performance but poor CO2 performance from a MOX-based device. This is their reading of a linked study, and the interpretation of its calibration remains disputed in the replies.
- Sheltering an indoor sensor from rain and sun may not make it a reliable outdoor instrument. joshefin [[./thread#^c20640819|joshefin]] says high humidity substantially affects low-cost PM measurements and describes attempted heating or correction methods as mostly unsuccessful. solstice [[./thread#^c20640607|solstice]], who owns a LaserEgg, offers humidity-related particle clumping as a hypothesis; tyingq [[./thread#^c20640683|tyingq]] speculates about temperature tolerance and corrosion. These explanations have different evidentiary status and are not supplied experimental results.
- A universal need for purification is challenged by location-specific observations. rofo1 [[./thread#^c20642185|rofo1]] reports sufficiently low particulate readings in Vienna to conclude that purification was unnecessary there. MartinMond [[./thread#^c20642298|MartinMond]] directly disputes that conclusion for winter or inversion conditions, but supplies no competing measurements. The exchange supports sensitivity to sampling conditions, not a settled verdict about Vienna.
- Some participants reject the premise that inexpensive measurements are useful at all. 693471 [[./thread#^c20648057|693471]] argues that bad data is worse than no data and distrusts cheap sensors. That broad skepticism conflicts with joshefin's [[./thread#^c20641352|joshefin]] narrower claim that inexpensive sensors can support approximate categories; neither comment supplies an independent validation dataset resolving the difference.
- Usability concerns extend beyond accuracy. r3bl [[./thread#^c20643138|r3bl]] reports instability, network activity, and difficulty obtaining offline operation from other Xiaomi products, while explicitly saying they have not used the air purifier. joshefin [[./thread#^c20643310|joshefin]] agrees with the broader software and privacy concerns. These experiences qualify confidence in the ecosystem, but are not direct tests of the air-quality monitor.

## Assumptions

- pmg's [[./thread#^c20641966|pmg]] practice of seeking fresh air when the CO2 display exceeds 1,200 assumes that the display represents CO2 adequately and that this is a useful intervention threshold. The comment does not establish a medical, cognitive, or sleep threshold, and throwaway9d0291 [[./thread#^c20642871|throwaway9d0291]] questions whether the device directly measures CO2.
- Opening windows assumes that outdoor air offers an improvement for the pollutant of concern. bubblewrap [[./thread#^c20644307|bubblewrap]] suggests windows as an alternative to purification, and joshefin [[./thread#^c20641289|joshefin]] includes them among possible responses to a pollution alert. Neither supplies simultaneous indoor/outdoor readings or demonstrates that opening windows improved exposure.
- rofo1's [[./thread#^c20642185|rofo1]] conclusion that low measured PM10 makes purification unnecessary assumes that this observation adequately represents the relevant exposure. The reported PM10 result is not a PM2.5 measurement and does not establish CO2, NO2, TVOC, or year-round conditions; MartinMond [[./thread#^c20642298|MartinMond]] specifically challenges its seasonal representativeness.
- Price and automation are sometimes treated as proxies for effectiveness. sumoboy [[./thread#^c20649338|sumoboy]] assumes more expensive filtration is needed for particles or chemicals that matter, while magoon [[./thread#^c20643455|magoon]] praises a purifier's sensor and automatic mode. Neither establishes, through measurements supplied here, that purchase price or automatic operation predicts the resulting exposure.

## Conditions

- Geographic and seasonal conditions limit transferability. MartinMond [[./thread#^c20642298|MartinMond]] identifies Vienna winters and inversions as exceptions to rofo1's favorable experience. throwaway8941 [[./thread#^c20642189|throwaway8941]] reports unusually high winter outdoor CO2 in an unnamed location, especially after approximately 19:00; the missing location, instrument, and sampling details prevent applying that claim elsewhere.
- The August 2019 discussion places directly measuring CO2 components within a modest DIY budget. throwaway9d0291 [[./thread#^c20651988|throwaway9d0291]] quotes approximately \$20 from China for an S8 Residential, and later prefers that option to the MH-Z19B when obtainable, while suggesting an MH-Z19B with calibration disabled as a fallback based on a linked discussion [[./thread#^c20654212|throwaway9d0291]]. leggomylibro [[./thread#^c20644517|leggomylibro]] recalls an approximately \$10 MH-Z19B price and flags warm-up and power demands, making wall-powered use more practical than brief intermittent operation.
- Xiaomi monitor prices vary substantially with purchasing route. pmg [[./thread#^c20641966|pmg]] quotes under \$50 in mainland China and approximately \$80 through an importer [[./thread#^c20642105|pmg]]; bentpins [[./thread#^c20642893|bentpins]] reports a \$64 AliExpress listing, while throwaway9d0291 [[./thread#^c20652055|throwaway9d0291]] describes a \$52 purchase plus \$8 forwarding. These are contemporaneous quoted prices, not current offers or proof of equivalent versions.
- A more expensive or English-language listing does not necessarily provide equivalent capabilities. auslander [[./thread#^c20643128|auslander]] proposes a \$150 AliExpress alternative, but throwaway9d0291 [[./thread#^c20644444|throwaway9d0291]] replies that it identifies only its CO2 sensor and lacks a machine-readable interface. Their later reply confirms that the shared S8 designation does not resolve the missing information about other sensors or data access [[./thread#^c20651952|throwaway9d0291]].
- Purifier affordability is disputed. electriclove [[./thread#^c20645115|electriclove]] recommends a \$100 Winix C535 from Costco, while sumoboy [[./thread#^c20649338|sumoboy]] dismisses expectations of effective filtration from a \$100 unit and favors better filters or whole-HVAC equipment. magoon [[./thread#^c20643455|magoon]] instead emphasizes the Coway's low total ownership cost. No supplied cost breakdown or comparative exposure measurements settle these competing value judgments.
- Low component prices can conceal assembly time and infrastructure costs. aftbit [[./thread#^c20641859|aftbit]] reports building an ESP8266/BME280 temperature, humidity, and pressure station for under \$10 in parts but spending a weekend on it. AceJohnny2 [[./thread#^c20641204|AceJohnny2]] explicitly prefers a \$10–\$30 finished device, while xnyan [[./thread#^c20641562|xnyan]] quotes inexpensive Zigbee sensors plus a roughly \$50 branded hub. These examples concern environmental telemetry, not the complete cost of accurate PM2.5 or CO2 monitoring.

## Lived experiences

- rofo1 [[./thread#^c20642185|rofo1]] reports building a similar monitor and testing multiple locations, including a Vienna reading of PM10 at or below 10 µg/m³. This is a firsthand numerical particulate report, but the sensor, sampling duration, season, and indoor/outdoor placement are unspecified.
- joshefin [[./thread#^c20640819|joshefin]] reports that their PMS7003 readings have been comparable to official government measurements available for their neighborhood. This is firsthand qualitative agreement, without paired values, error statistics, or evidence of co-located sampling; it does not establish performance under all outdoor conditions.
- pmg [[./thread#^c20641966|pmg]] reports placing Xiaomi monitors beside a laser cutter for particles, in a paint booth for TVOC, and in an office conference room to guide fresh-air decisions. These are actual deployment experiences, but the comment supplies no exposure time series, reference comparison, or measured improvement after ventilation.
- Replying to pmg's ventilation threshold, throwaway8941 [[./thread#^c20642189|throwaway8941]] reports winter outdoor CO2 of at least 1,500 ppm and more than 2,000 ppm after approximately 19:00. This is a numerical claim about their local environment, without disclosed measurement methods or corroboration. It cannot establish that those conditions are typical, or that outdoor air was worse than their indoor air.
- \_def [[./thread#^c20643219|\_def]] reports often becoming tired in a small flat and wonders whether CO2 could explain it. The fatigue is firsthand; elevated CO2, a causal connection, and improvement through ventilation are untested hypotheses in this account.
- magoon [[./thread#^c20643455|magoon]] reports owning a Coway AP-1512HH in each bedroom and describes its low setting as very quiet. tareqak [[./thread#^c20643638|tareqak]] also owns one, purchased following a review recommendation. Neither reports quantified pollutant reductions, better sleep, improved cognition, or symptom changes.
- Havoc [[./thread#^c20643670|Havoc]] reports switching off a Raspberry Pi air-sensor setup because its small fan's buzzing interfered with peace and quiet. joshefin [[./thread#^c20643946|joshefin]] replies that the PMS7003 fan is very quiet. Havoc does not identify the sensor model, so these are differing comfort reports, not necessarily contradictory assessments of identical hardware.
- throwaway9d0291 [[./thread#^c20642939|throwaway9d0291]] reports buying a Taobao device with disclosed sensors and a TCP interface returning JSON for their home monitoring system. Chinese documentation and English measurement labels were manageable with translation. This establishes an implemented data-access arrangement, not an independently measured accuracy result.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The TVOC study's interpretation remains explicitly contested. throwaway9d0291 [[./thread#^c20653199|throwaway9d0291]] argues that favorable Foobot performance followed regression-based field calibration against research equipment, limiting its relevance to ordinary buyers. magicalhippo [[./thread#^c20657558|magicalhippo]] responds that the calibration dataset itself already looks reasonably good except for one outlier. The supplied exchange does not settle uncalibrated performance or generalizability.
- Calibration and humidity correction lack a demonstrated procedure. tehlike [[./thread#^c20641513|tehlike]], replying within the PM-sensor discussion, claims that opening windows provides needed weekly calibration, but supplies no protocol or validation. igor47 [[./thread#^c20641041|igor47]] recommends a BME280 for humidity compensation, whereas joshefin [[./thread#^c20640819|joshefin]] describes correction attempts as mostly unsuccessful. None demonstrates a dependable correction or establishes that outdoor air is a valid calibration baseline.
- The practical accuracy of the proposed setup remains incompletely answered. heyoni [[./thread#^c20641095|heyoni]] asks whether it can measure purifier effectiveness, and tareqak [[./thread#^c20643659|tareqak]] asks for comparison with Netatmo. wil421 [[./thread#^c20643688|wil421]] suggests identifying the underlying sensor, while close04 [[./thread#^c20643824|close04]] proposes a future sensor comparison without presenting results. Asked for a better outdoor instrument, joshefin [[./thread#^c20641390|joshefin]] cannot recommend one; their below-70\%-humidity suggestion is not accompanied by validation data.
- Ventilation and filtration benefits are proposed rather than quantified. joshefin [[./thread#^c20641289|joshefin]] lists opening windows, vacuuming, and purification as possible responses, while sumoboy [[./thread#^c20649338|sumoboy]] argues for more capable filtration. The discussion supplies no controlled comparison of these interventions and no demonstration that particulate purification addresses the reported CO2 concerns. Outdoor winter claims from throwaway8941 [[./thread#^c20642189|throwaway8941]] and MartinMond [[./thread#^c20642298|MartinMond]] make the assumption that windows always help uncertain, but do not show a measured window-opening exposure increase.
- Some purchase-critical details remain unanswered or contradictory. pmg [[./thread#^c20642105|pmg]] says the Xiaomi device supports English, while tobtoh [[./thread#^c20642202|tobtoh]] points out that the linked listing says Chinese-only. swebs [[./thread#^c20643135|swebs]] asks whether internet access is necessary, and dotancohen [[./thread#^c20642959|dotancohen]] asks whether the mobile app is mandatory. The supplied replies do not establish those requirements for the actual air-quality monitor.
- The approved health and exposure question extends well beyond this source unit's evidence. \_def's [[./thread#^c20643219|\_def]] fatigue hypothesis is not a measured CO2-health relationship; throwaway9d0291's [[./thread#^c20642939|throwaway9d0291]] toast example comes from an external report about sensor failures, not a firsthand cooking experiment. The numerical particulate account from rofo1 [[./thread#^c20642185|rofo1]] concerns PM10, not PM2.5. No supplied account quantifies PM2.5 exposure, measures NO2, assesses cognition or sleep, or describes traffic or wildfire-smoke exposure. Those topics remain unanswered here.
