# [Wildfire Smoke in Northeastern US Visible on GOES East Images](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=36231941)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion reacts to wildfire smoke spreading across the northeastern United States, with reports of hazy skies, strong odors, high pollution readings, and respiratory irritation. Participants share practical precautions, including limiting outdoor activity, using masks and indoor filtration, and keeping windows closed when appropriate; they also compare homemade box-fan filters with commercial purifiers and discuss sensors, maps, forecasts, and satellite imagery. Broader debate examines where the fires began and how smoke traveled, the roles of weather, climate change, forest management, prescribed burns, and development near wildlands, as well as the risks and constraints of different interventions. The thread also considers the uneven attention the event receives across regions and the public response to climate risks.
Tags: wildfire smoke, air quality, public health, air filtration, protective masks, air quality monitoring, weather, forest management, prescribed burns, climate change, public policy

## Arguments

- Reducing outdoor smoke entry while filtering indoor air is a recurring practical proposal. COGlory [[./thread#^c36232879|COGlory]] recommends closing doors and windows, preferring interior filtration, and avoiding exhaust that draws replacement air indoors. In that reply tree, bagels [[./thread#^c36235986|bagels]] recommends a filtered box fan; the discussion therefore supports fan-powered filtration despite the opening comment's broad warning against running fans.
- Filtration has support from reported before-and-after readings, beyond recommendations alone. channel\_t [[./thread#^c36239002|channel\_t]] reports a substantial indoor AQI reduction with a furnace filter taped to a box fan, while jxramos [[./thread#^c36261258|jxramos]] reports lower indoor meter readings after running HEPA filtration. These observations support usefulness in those homes without establishing equivalent performance across buildings or devices.
- Local measurements can make exposure management more concrete. Replying to whether an unfiltered home is better than outdoors, jxramos [[./thread#^c36234967|jxramos]] recommends measuring the actual environments and reports a measurable improvement from a car cabin filter. jxramos [[./thread#^c36234957|jxramos]] also describes using PurpleAir to find cleaner places for outdoor time, making monitoring useful for decisions beyond simply observing a bad regional number.
- Opening a window can provide relief when outdoor air is filtered on entry. s0rce [[./thread#^c36236499|s0rce]] reports drawing air through a MERV 13 filter mounted in a window during hot, smoky weather without air conditioning, cooling the home while monitors indicated removal of most small particles. This supports a conditional alternative to keeping a hot home completely closed.
- Several commenters argue for reducing exertion during smoke episodes. bagels [[./thread#^c36235986|bagels]] recommends skipping outdoor exercise; danenania [[./thread#^c36234833|danenania]] particularly warns against running and cycling, citing learning the hard way; seadan83 [[./thread#^c36247879|seadan83]] extends the concern to indoor exercise without effective filtration. These are experience-based precautions, not demonstrated universal exercise thresholds.
- Respiratory protection is proposed for unavoidable outdoor exposure. JohnTHaller [[./thread#^c36233983|JohnTHaller]] recommends an N95 or better while reporting NYC AQI above 400, and fragmede [[./thread#^c36236138|fragmede]] points out that people may already have N95s from the pandemic. The comments establish availability and perceived usefulness, rather than measured protection factors during this event.
- Indoor sources still matter when outdoor smoke is the main problem. jxramos [[./thread#^c36261258|jxramos]] reports increasing readings during smoky cooking, and grogenaut [[./thread#^c36237797|grogenaut]] says their sensors chiefly reinforced avoiding indoor cooking and opening windows. Neither account compares cooking fuels, measures NO2, or tests the net effect of operating a kitchen exhaust hood.

## Counterarguments

- Being indoors with windows closed does not guarantee low exposure. lbotos [[./thread#^c36232300|lbotos]] could still smell and feel smoke inside a closed NYC apartment, vinaypai [[./thread#^c36232475|vinaypai]] saw haze down an indoor hallway, and jrockway [[./thread#^c36232163|jrockway]] reported coughing and only limited improvement despite filters running almost at full power. These accounts qualify blanket reassurance about sheltering indoors.
- Sealing a home and adding humidity can create competing comfort problems. In direct reply to COGlory's humidifier advice [[./thread#^c36232879|COGlory]], coffeebeqn [[./thread#^c36235249|coffeebeqn]] reports that keeping their home sealed for more than a week made it excessively humid. s0rce [[./thread#^c36236499|s0rce]] found incoming air practically necessary on hot days without AC. Neither closed windows nor extra humidity worked as an unconditional recommendation.
- A cheap fan-and-filter assembly did not work satisfactorily for everyone. nerdponx [[./thread#^c36237750|nerdponx]] reports inadequate apparent airflow despite researching the filter choice. rampant\_ai [[./thread#^c36238395|rampant\_ai]] attributes this to resistance and recommends greater filter area, while reportgunner [[./thread#^c36238541|reportgunner]] blames pushing rather than pulling air. Those replies offer possible explanations, not measurements establishing why that particular attempt failed.
- Monitoring's practical value is disputed. grogenaut [[./thread#^c36237797|grogenaut]] says the devices mostly confirmed obvious precautions, sometimes disagreed with public readings, and encouraged fretting; they preferred spending on filters or a respirator. This contrasts with jxramos [[./thread#^c36234957|jxramos]], who used mapped readings to find outdoor respite, and eunoia [[./thread#^c36234195|eunoia]], who found owning a PurpleAir and using its map valuable.
- The claim that existing N95s make the problem solved is challenged within the discussion. cameldrv [[./thread#^c36235994|cameldrv]] presents that simple solution, whereas seadan83 [[./thread#^c36247920|seadan83]] says an N95 helps but advocates N99 protection, and tjohns [[./thread#^c36232417|tjohns]] reports discovering a meaningful difference between an N95 and an N100 respirator during extreme smoke. None supplies a controlled comparison supporting a universal adequacy boundary.
- Comparing wildfire smoke to a familiar campfire does not settle its risk. rcme [[./thread#^c36235975|rcme]] asks whether the acute hazard is actually greater, and jstanley [[./thread#^c36239230|jstanley]] explains why an accepted familiar risk would be a useful benchmark. In response, kortex [[./thread#^c36236939|kortex]] identifies as asthmatic and argues campfire exposure is itself harmful, while brianpan [[./thread#^c36238206|brianpan]] emphasizes that moving away from a campfire differs from being surrounded by regional smoke. The replies challenge reassurance without quantifying the comparison.
- A low displayed AQI and a noticeable smoke smell can coexist in these reports. maxerickson [[./thread#^c36235534|maxerickson]] recalls smelling smoke around AQI 30. diebeforei485 [[./thread#^c36238446|diebeforei485]] proposes that an averaged rather than current reading explains it, but the actual display, averaging method, and local conditions are not established in the exchange.

## Assumptions

- This source unit, story 36231941 part 0, principally supports analysis of wildfire smoke and particle monitoring. It supplies no indoor NO2 or CO2 measurements, cognition tests, or controlled sleep-quality comparisons. jxramos's gas-station observation concerns a VOC mode [[./thread#^c36261258|jxramos]], and EMCymatics's overnight account concerns a changed voice after sleeping with windows open [[./thread#^c36232667|EMCymatics]]; neither establishes effects attributable to NO2 or CO2.
- Reported numbers must retain their original meaning. channel\_t explicitly describes AQI [[./thread#^c36239002|channel\_t]], whereas jxramos gives indoor readings of 10 or 12 falling to 2 or 3 without stating units [[./thread#^c36261258|jxramos]]. Those latter values cannot safely be relabeled as either AQI or PM2.5 concentration. Different locations, averaging periods, and sensor corrections also limit comparisons between commenters' numbers.
- Sensor-reliability evidence has different strengths. S201 [[./thread#^c36234242|S201]] reports that an EPA comparison found PurpleAir's sensors fairly close to expensive instruments after a correction factor, which they incorporated into a DIY clone; this is a claim about a linked study, not calibration data reproduced here. eunoia [[./thread#^c36234195|eunoia]] offers an owner's endorsement, slaw [[./thread#^c36236927|slaw]] describes buying a Smartmi after a favorable YouTube review, and kit maintainer/seller ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36234769|ahaucnx]] claims enhanced accuracy from dual sensors. These are not interchangeable forms of validation.
- Responsiveness does not establish absolute accuracy or pollutant specificity. jxramos [[./thread#^c36261258|jxramos]] explicitly acknowledges difficulty verifying absolute accuracy, then describes readings responding plausibly to smoke, cooking, filtration, and a gas station. Those correlations support practical usefulness but do not identify every pollutant, validate the VOC measurement, or demonstrate NO2 removal by a car filter.
- Reported symptoms and recovery periods are individual accounts with limited causal resolution. crazygringo [[./thread#^c36236520|crazygringo]] describes coughing and watering eyes shortly after going outdoors, while seadan83 [[./thread#^c36247879|seadan83]] attributes recovery lasting days and once weeks to smoke exposure. The comments provide no clinical diagnoses, personal exposure measurements linked to those symptoms, or controls that establish a general dose-response relationship.
- Symptom relief is distinct from demonstrated exposure reduction. COGlory [[./thread#^c36232879|COGlory]] reports relief from steam and carrot juice, explicitly acknowledging no scientific support for the carrot-juice idea. 2devnull [[./thread#^c36242666|2devnull]] claims benefit from NAC, vitamin C, and anti-inflammatory medication, and grogenaut [[./thread#^c36237797|grogenaut]] suggests antihistamines. No supplied evidence establishes that these measures remove pollutants or prevent smoke-related harm.
- Descriptions such as livable or substantially improved do not establish complete protection. tjohns [[./thread#^c36232417|tjohns]] reports making one room livable under extreme conditions without a final reading; channel\_t [[./thread#^c36239002|channel\_t]] reports bringing AQI below 100, not eliminating particles. Likewise, grogenaut's suggestion that offices usually have good filters [[./thread#^c36237797|grogenaut]] is an assumption about buildings, not a measured comparison.

## Conditions

- Exposure varied sharply across the Northeast during the June 2023 episode. JohnTHaller [[./thread#^c36233983|JohnTHaller]] reported NYC AQI above 400, xwowsersx [[./thread#^c36234928|xwowsersx]] reported 250 in Connecticut, and rhodysurf [[./thread#^c36236493|rhodysurf]] reported a Rhode Island maximum around 100. gnulinux [[./thread#^c36234964|gnulinux]] said Boston looked and felt normal; xwowsersx [[./thread#^c36235074|xwowsersx]] attributed that contrast to the plume passing through western Connecticut and south of Boston. Regional proximity did not imply equal conditions.
- Distance from the fires was an unreliable standalone guide to exposure. Responding to why NYC appeared worse than Canadian locations, alephxyz [[./thread#^c36235117|alephxyz]] cites a cold front and rain, while cmrdporcupine [[./thread#^c36237403|cmrdporcupine]] describes winds carrying Northern Quebec smoke south and reports unexpectedly mild conditions after returning to southern Ontario. These explanations are consistent with uneven transport, but the thread does not independently verify the meteorology.
- Smoke's geographic reach should not be confused with its concentration at a person's breathing location. dredmorbius [[./thread#^c36236229|dredmorbius]] links a visualization of fire activity and surface PM2.5 approaching NYC, while opdahl [[./thread#^c36238280|opdahl]] reports a news account of particles reaching Norway without supplying a local concentration. politelemon's unanswered question about apparently clear nighttime satellite imagery [[./thread#^c36238389|politelemon]] also shows uncertainty about interpreting images as exposure information.
- Duration and recurrence matter alongside a peak reading. In the Seattle discussion, lotsofpulp [[./thread#^c36233112|lotsofpulp]] did not recall smoke in 2022, but Analemma\_ [[./thread#^c36234091|Analemma\_]] recalls weeks of sporadic AQI around 80, contrasting with continuous AQI around 200 for a week or two in 2020. dboreham [[./thread#^c36234302|dboreham]] describes recurring Montana smoke from August through September and sometimes October. These remain place-specific recollections rather than a uniform western-US exposure history.
- Heat, humidity, and access to cooling constrain window decisions. s0rce's filtered window intake [[./thread#^c36236499|s0rce]] addressed hot weather without AC; coffeebeqn's excessive humidity [[./thread#^c36235249|coffeebeqn]] followed more than a week of sealing the house; mhink [[./thread#^c36234425|mhink]] recalls smoke making the 2021 heat dome worse. A strategy that reduces particle entry can still leave substantial comfort problems.
- Affordability and availability shape preparedness. bagels [[./thread#^c36235986|bagels]] presents a box-fan filter as inexpensive, but channel\_t [[./thread#^c36239029|channel\_t]] warns that furnace filters disappear from store shelves during AQI emergencies. danenania [[./thread#^c36234833|danenania]] similarly describes purifier shortages and says their household bought one per room partly because they had a young child. Red\_Leaves\_Flyy [[./thread#^c36237399|Red\_Leaves\_Flyy]] bought discounted N95s in advance. These accounts provide no comparable total-cost figures.
- Access to cleaner places changes the available options. jxramos [[./thread#^c36234957|jxramos]] could travel toward the coast or other areas showing cleaner readings, while mtalantikite [[./thread#^c36234476|mtalantikite]] recalls little escape from Delhi's pollution inside an Airbnb. grogenaut [[./thread#^c36237797|grogenaut]] proposes working from an office, but supplies no measurements proving that an office would be cleaner for another reader.
- Reported extremes have important geographic and evidentiary limits. tjohns [[./thread#^c36232417|tjohns]] was directly downwind of the CZU fire at a Bay Area home; jakeinspace [[./thread#^c36237126|jakeinspace]] reported 480 around their parents' Philadelphia location; lamontcg [[./thread#^c36235515|lamontcg]] cites Portland exceeding 500 in 2020 but explicitly cannot recover the roughly 800 readings they remember. These are not matched citywide measurements.

## Lived experiences

- tjohns [[./thread#^c36232417|tjohns]] describes a 2020 Bay Area exposure around AQI 800 directly downwind of the CZU Lightning Complex. They sealed doors and windows with plastic and placed three purifiers, each rated for 1,200 square feet, in one bedroom to make that room livable. In another reply, tjohns [[./thread#^c36234687|tjohns]] says their logs show a peak of 766. The account conveys an unusually severe local situation, not a validated regional maximum.
- channel\_t [[./thread#^c36239002|channel\_t]] reports that a simple filter taped to a box fan worked during Portland's 2020 fires: their indoor sensor went from roughly AQI 250 or higher to below 100 fairly quickly, and the filters rapidly turned black. No exact elapsed time, filter specification, or simultaneous outdoor comparison is provided.
- tzs [[./thread#^c36232346|tzs]] reports that an older HEPA purifier generally kept their Puget Sound living room below roughly AQI 10 during smoke episodes, recalling outdoor values as high as about 250. They explicitly hesitate to endorse the currently sold version because of possible brand/manufacturer changes; this is an account of their longstanding device.
- jxramos [[./thread#^c36261258|jxramos]] reports indoor readings around 10–12 after smoke infiltration falling to 2–3 after approximately 20 minutes of HEPA operation, with units unspecified. They also observed increases with smoky cooking and a large increase in the device's VOC mode at a gas station. Separately, jxramos [[./thread#^c36234967|jxramos]] reports a measurable improvement from a Bosch HEPA car cabin filter, without figures or a comparison protocol.
- s0rce [[./thread#^c36236499|s0rce]] describes installing a 20-by-20-by-4-inch MERV 13 HVAC filter in a window and drawing outdoor air through it with a box fan. They report cooling the home and verifying removal of most small particles using air-quality monitors, but provide no concentrations, airflow measurements, or gas readings.
- grogenaut [[./thread#^c36237797|grogenaut]] had multiple particulate sensors and Arduinos during Seattle smoke episodes. They saw little indoor AQI change when AC was switched on or off or after briefly going outside. They also drove to a location showing very high PurpleAir readings and found relatively low readings on two devices. This is a firsthand discrepancy report, not proof that either measurement system was correctly calibrated.
- crazygringo [[./thread#^c36236520|crazygringo]] reports coughing within minutes of briefly going outside in NYC, followed by watering eyes, unlike their own experiences around campfires or barbecues. crazygringo [[./thread#^c36236551|crazygringo]] also describes conditions becoming subjectively much worse as displayed AQI rose from 200 to 400 within an hour; that reaction does not establish a proportional relationship between AQI and health damage.
- seadan83 [[./thread#^c36247879|seadan83]] reports taking days, and once weeks, to recover from smoke exposure. danenania [[./thread#^c36234833|danenania]] describes learning the hard way to avoid vigorous exercise in smoky conditions and discusses coughing, itchy eyes, and fatigue. jes [[./thread#^c36234361|jes]] calls the recurring Washington State period headache season. These accounts describe symptoms and disruption without clinical assessment or quantified personal doses.
- EMCymatics [[./thread#^c36232667|EMCymatics]] left windows open while sleeping and woke with what they describe as a voice two octaves lower. The account directly connects an overnight window-opening episode with perceived throat or voice effects, but reports neither pollutant measurements nor sleep duration, sleep quality, or next-day cognition.
- mtalantikite [[./thread#^c36234476|mtalantikite]] reports a darkened Brooklyn living room, orange light outside, and local AQI readings of 330–350. They compare this with a Delhi winter when readings sometimes exceeded 500 and their Airbnb offered little respite, leading them to sleep with a keffiyeh around their face. Their broader impression that residents were accustomed to pollution should not be generalized into evidence about residents' health or acceptance.
- Indoor infiltration appeared in several forms: lbotos [[./thread#^c36232300|lbotos]] smelled and felt smoke with apartment windows closed; vinaypai [[./thread#^c36232475|vinaypai]] saw visible hallway haze; and jrockway [[./thread#^c36232163|jrockway]] coughed despite intensive filtration and abandoned an evening walk. bluefishinit [[./thread#^c36232127|bluefishinit]] reports a caller ending a conversation because of burning eyes and breathing difficulty, later clarifying that the caller was indoors [[./thread#^c36232582|bluefishinit]]. That last account concerns another person's symptoms, not the commenter's own exposure.
- coffeebeqn [[./thread#^c36235249|coffeebeqn]] experienced excessive humidity after sealing their home for more than a week. COGlory [[./thread#^c36232879|COGlory]] reports that steam usually relieved their eyes and sinuses and that carrot juice helped a sore throat. These describe comfort effects and a practical drawback of sealing, without demonstrating changes in particle exposure.
- jxramos [[./thread#^c36234957|jxramos]] describes becoming restless after days indoors and using air-quality maps to find cleaner outdoor destinations. alexose [[./thread#^c36232299|alexose]] says seeing another region encounter smoke brought back feelings from 2020. These accounts document psychological and everyday-life burdens, rather than measured impairment of cognition.
- Sensory accounts show the breadth of disruption without supplying equivalent particle measurements. ChrisMarshallNY [[./thread#^c36232620|ChrisMarshallNY]] describes Long Island as sepia-toned and smelling like a smokehouse; duxup [[./thread#^c36232579|duxup]] recalls Minnesota smoke obscuring the end of streets and resembling a nearby house fire; mig39 [[./thread#^c36232524|mig39]] describes recurring nearby smoke making Fort McMurray surreal. IAmGraydon [[./thread#^c36234807|IAmGraydon]] distinguishes Virginia's milder conditions during this event from the acrid smoke of recurring local peat fires.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The magnitude of acute risk remains insufficiently contextualized. rcme [[./thread#^c36235975|rcme]] and [[./thread#^c36236245|rcme]] asks for a wildfire-versus-campfire comparison, but replies supply symptom anecdotes, susceptibility considerations, and differences in duration and ability to escape rather than quantified comparable doses. ssnistfajen [[./thread#^c36237780|ssnistfajen]] emphasizes respiratory conditions; nickjj [[./thread#^c36239399|nickjj]] speculates about hazardous materials burning in wildfires without evidence of this plume's composition. The exchange does not settle risk for a given person or duration.
- DIY filter geometry remains contested. gabereiser [[./thread#^c36240015|gabereiser]] describes a four-filter box with the fan blowing into it, whereas rampant\_ai [[./thread#^c36238395|rampant\_ai]] and reportgunner [[./thread#^c36238541|reportgunner]] advocate pulling through filters. rampant\_ai also recommends greater filter area; themaninthedark [[./thread#^c36240671|themaninthedark]] discusses pressure buildup, and Tade0 [[./thread#^c36239559|Tade0]] guesses that pulling improves the seal. No controlled comparison in the supplied material resolves fan direction, pressure, leakage, or the failed build.
- The discussion cannot establish a clean ranking of NYC versus historical San Francisco smoke. kaesar14 [[./thread#^c36234499|kaesar14]] claims SF had not exceeded 249, while whimsicalism [[./thread#^c36243639|whimsicalism]] recalls documented PurpleAir readings above 300 and \_jab [[./thread#^c36237140|\_jab]] cites regional agency records around 200. whimsicalism [[./thread#^c36243569|whimsicalism]] explicitly questions comparability. Separately, \_jab [[./thread#^c36237098|\_jab]] challenges a current SF sensor reading of 320 posted by jxramos [[./thread#^c36235000|jxramos]] because nearby sensors were below 42. Location, averaging, correction, and outliers remain unresolved.
- Which consumer meter to trust remains only partly answered. neogodless [[./thread#^c36235728|neogodless]] asks for a reliable selection criterion; S201 [[./thread#^c36234242|S201]] provides a study-based argument for corrected readings from PurpleAir-type sensors, while jxramos [[./thread#^c36261258|jxramos]] relies on plausible responses despite uncertain absolute accuracy and grogenaut [[./thread#^c36237797|grogenaut]] reports a substantial field discrepancy. The thread supplies neither a reproducible product comparison nor a diagnostic procedure for suspect readings.
- Mask adequacy is unsettled at specific exposure levels. JohnTHaller [[./thread#^c36233983|JohnTHaller]] recommends N95 or better, seadan83 [[./thread#^c36247920|seadan83]] asserts that smoke calls for N99, and tjohns [[./thread#^c36232417|tjohns]] favors an N100 respirator based on an extreme personal episode. The accounts provide no fitted performance measurements, gas-exposure measurements, or dose comparisons that justify treating any rating as complete protection.
- The window-opening tradeoff is only partly resolved. s0rce [[./thread#^c36236499|s0rce]] supplies a favorable filtered-intake example for heat relief; EMCymatics [[./thread#^c36232667|EMCymatics]] supplies an adverse overnight open-window account; coffeebeqn [[./thread#^c36235249|coffeebeqn]] reports humidity problems from prolonged closure. No supplied account compares unfiltered ventilation during cleaner outdoor periods, tracks CO2 accumulation while sealed, measures NO2, or establishes a ventilation schedule balancing all these concerns.
- Prescribed burning introduces a smoke tradeoff that the discussion does not quantify. bariumbitmap [[./thread#^c36235319|bariumbitmap]] identifies smoke inhalation as a public-health obstacle while citing research favoring expanded prescribed burning in some regions. quickthrower2 [[./thread#^c36237998|quickthrower2]], recalling Australia's 2019 bushfires, finds the smell of controlled burns reassuring. putnambr [[./thread#^c36250563|putnambr]] explicitly labels Colorado complaints about smoke restrictions as hearsay, and gdubs [[./thread#^c36241857|gdubs]] describes an agricultural burn igniting a tree despite rain and humidity. No personal exposure comparison establishes the net benefit or safest implementation.
- A limited SO2 side debate directly compares proposed pollutant exposure with wildfire-smoke health harms, but offers no usable personal-risk estimate. alexose [[./thread#^c36232810|alexose]] asks whether added sulfur pollution could be preferable to smoke; theandrewbailey [[./thread#^c36232597|theandrewbailey]] argues deliberate SO2 exposure would be worse; perihelions [[./thread#^c36232872|perihelions]] and ceejayoz [[./thread#^c36232707|ceejayoz]] respond that proposed stratospheric placement and quantities change that comparison. These remain claims about prospective interventions, without measured inhaled doses or a demonstrated health balance.
