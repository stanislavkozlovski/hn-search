# [Corsi–Rosenthal Box](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=31438825)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion shares experience with Corsi–Rosenthal and other DIY air cleaners, comparing their effectiveness, noise, size, cost, and filter lifespan with commercial purifiers. Participants ask how filter ratings and fan placement affect airflow and filtration, and discuss visible dust capture and sensor measurements. Side threads compare filtration with UV germicidal approaches, including concerns that UV may alter pollutants or produce ozone, and note the design’s prior use during wildfire seasons.
Tags: Corsi–Rosenthal box, DIY air filtration, MERV filters, indoor air quality, UV disinfection

## Relevance

The discussion includes firsthand use of box-fan filter builds for wildfire smoke, dust, and an asthmatic pet, along with visible filter loading and particle readings. Replies compare filtration effectiveness, airflow, noise, space, and filter construction. ## Arguments

- qbasic\_forever [[./thread#^c31455820|qbasic\_forever]] argues that Corsi–Rosenthal paper filters remove pollutants effectively within a few air changes, while questioning filterless, ion, and electrostatic cleaners. These are performance claims without accompanying measurements; the comment does not establish effectiveness against particular gases.
- alliao [[./thread#^c31455807|alliao]] argues that low cost and high recirculating airflow can make the DIY design competitive with expensive purifiers, despite less pristine output than IQAir. The claimed market-wide superiority is an opinion without comparative airflow, filtration, or price data.
- legulere [[./thread#^c31455823|legulere]] invokes air pollution's mortality and disease burden as a reason to take filtration seriously, linking Our World in Data. The comment provides no estimate of health benefits attributable to their purifier.
- SideburnsOfDoom [[./thread#^c31456369|SideburnsOfDoom]] proposes upper-room ultraviolet germicidal irradiation as another approach, potentially requiring fewer moving parts and less electricity. No installation experience or energy measurements accompany the proposal.
- Mister\_Snuggles [[./thread#^c31457399|Mister\_Snuggles]] and errantmind [[./thread#^c31458812|errantmind]] argue that pulling air through filters before it reaches the fan keeps the fan cleaner and extends its life. Mister\_Snuggles makes this environment-dependent; neither supplies longevity measurements.

## Counterarguments

- rich\_sasha [[./thread#^c31455727|rich\_sasha]] questions whether consumer purifiers make a noticeable difference and suspects a marketing fad, while explicitly acknowledging no experience comparing heavily polluted air before and after purification. This is untested skepticism, answered by legulere's observations [[./thread#^c31455823|legulere]] and qbasic\_forever's filter-based explanation [[./thread#^c31455820|qbasic\_forever]].
- Physical comfort and convenience complicate the low-cost argument: akeck [[./thread#^c31457801|akeck]] considers the boxes impractically bulky for some rooms, while Mister\_Snuggles [[./thread#^c31457437|Mister\_Snuggles]] finds their fan sufficiently loud to restrict its operation with a motion sensor.
- Responding to the UV proposal, inetknght [[./thread#^c31456788|inetknght]] distinguishes killing germs from removing particles. legulere [[./thread#^c31456816|legulere]] additionally warns that UV can chemically alter pollutants with undesirable effects, citing a paper whose findings are not reproduced in the supplied text. These objections challenge treating UV and particle filtration as interchangeable.

## Assumptions

- cagenut's health inference [[./thread#^c31457667|cagenut]] depends on some fraction of the collected dust otherwise reaching their lungs. Their conditional suggestion that even one tenth would make the device worthwhile is not a measured inhaled dose or health outcome; filter discoloration does not identify particle sizes.
- legulere [[./thread#^c31455823|legulere]] treats the sensor's responses to bed-making, frying, and window opening as evidence that it measures real changes. Those observations support responsiveness, but do not independently establish calibration, absolute accuracy, or the meaning of a reading of 0 or 1.
- Having built a unit, bad416f1f5a2 [[./thread#^c31457413|bad416f1f5a2]] hypothesizes that filters intended for relatively slow airflow could fail under a nearby fan blowing directly at them. In reply, Mister\_Snuggles [[./thread#^c31458059|Mister\_Snuggles]] argues that furnace fans move more air and that reinforcement varies, contrasting metal-supported MERV 13 and unsupported MERV 2 examples. Their discussion identifies filtration, airflow resistance, and reinforcement as relevant variables without experimentally establishing the proposed failure mechanism.

## Lived experiences

- cagenut [[./thread#^c31457667|cagenut]] built a four-filter unit during the January Omicron wave and reports replacing its filters after a stated six months because they had changed from white material with blue flecks to thick, dusty gray material. This is firsthand evidence of visible accumulation, with no measured particle concentration, infection outcome, or reported human symptom improvement.
- legulere [[./thread#^c31455823|legulere]] reports that their purifier's particulate sensor falls to 0 or 1 while it runs, rises somewhat when making the bed, falls when opening windows under conditions they describe as bad air, and rises sharply during frying. The numerical scale, units, and particle-size range are unspecified. The comparison of frying readings to China or India is an informal characterization, not a documented geographic comparison.
- Mister\_Snuggles [[./thread#^c31457437|Mister\_Snuggles]] reports years of using a furnace filter attached to a box fan for cat-litter dust. Following a veterinarian's recommendation, they observed a significant, immediate improvement for an asthmatic cat. The fan's noise led them to switch it on only after the cat used the litter box. This is an animal-health anecdote without quantified dust or respiratory measurements.
- akeck [[./thread#^c31457801|akeck]] reports building similar devices for years, using one in the basement and smaller Fellowes and Rabbit Air units upstairs. Their experience supports a practical distinction between rooms that can accommodate a large DIY box and rooms where compactness matters.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The dispute over noticeable benefits remains only partly addressed: rich\_sasha [[./thread#^c31455727|rich\_sasha]] asks whether purification makes a perceptible difference, while legulere [[./thread#^c31455823|legulere]] supplies sensor and filter observations. These observations do not establish a perceptible human health, cognition, or sleep benefit. The explicit symptomatic improvement concerns Mister\_Snuggles's cat [[./thread#^c31457437|Mister\_Snuggles]].
- Sensor reliability remains unresolved. legulere [[./thread#^c31455823|legulere]] supplies no model, units, calibration procedure, independent reference measurement, or explicit PM2.5 designation. brainbag's follow-up asking whether the sensor was added to a DIY setup [[./thread#^c31456799|brainbag]] receives no supplied answer, so the observations cannot confidently be attributed to a Corsi–Rosenthal build.
- Window opening helped in legulere's reported circumstances [[./thread#^c31455823|legulere]], whereas ihm [[./thread#^c31455706|ihm]] describes outdoor wildfire smoke motivating filtration. The comments provide no simultaneous indoor/outdoor measurements or window-opening experiment during smoke exposure. Whether opening windows would worsen exposure in that setting remains untested, and there are no NO2, CO2, or traffic-exposure measurements to establish broader ventilation tradeoffs.
- The UV disagreement is unresolved: replying to legulere's chemistry warning [[./thread#^c31456816|legulere]], alliao [[./thread#^c31462101|alliao]] interprets the concern as ozone production, quotes a claim that wavelength and intensity selection may mitigate unintended chemistry, and endorses correctly implemented 222 nm far UV-C. No supplied measurements resolve byproduct exposure, practical safety, or comparative performance; inetknght's distinction between disinfection and particle removal [[./thread#^c31456788|inetknght]] still limits the comparison.
- Comparative purifier performance cannot be quantified from the supplied evidence. tpowell [[./thread#^c31455879|tpowell]] links testing by a namesake, and hjkl0 [[./thread#^c31455745|hjkl0]] links a Matthias Wandel comparison involving a homemade cleaner, but neither reproduces results. Consequently, those comments do not substantiate alliao's broad superiority claim [[./thread#^c31455807|alliao]] or establish comparative clean-air delivery, noise, energy use, or operating cost.
- felurx's question about whether pulling rather than pushing improves airflow or filtration [[./thread#^c31457126|felurx]] receives proposed explanations involving fan cleanliness and filter construction, including Mister\_Snuggles's responses [[./thread#^c31457399|Mister\_Snuggles]] [[./thread#^c31458059|Mister\_Snuggles]].
