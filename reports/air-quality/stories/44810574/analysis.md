# [Wired Called Our AirGradient Monitor 'Not Recommended' over a Broken Display](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44810574)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion centers on a review that rated an AirGradient indoor monitor poorly after its small display degraded, and whether the verdict fairly reflects the product or overweights one failure and the reviewer’s preferences. Participants assess the device’s display, indicator lights, dashboard, sensors, repairability, ease of use, price, and fit for different customers. The exchange also raises broader questions about product-review methods, sample sizes, deadlines, reviewer expertise, affiliate incentives, and how manufacturers should respond to criticism, defects, and warranty issues.
Tags: AirGradient, air-quality monitors, product reviews, display usability, hardware reliability, repairability, review methodology, consumer choice, journalism, product design

## Relevance

The discussion concerns indoor air monitors, including their displays and usability, but also their measurements and sensor features. Users describe overnight CO2 trends and changes associated with gas cooking, while the thread questions monitor testing methods and the value of specific metrics. ## Arguments

- Personal monitoring can make household air-quality patterns actionable. captainreynolds [[./thread#^c44811418|captainreynolds]] values stored time-series data, while GeekFortyTwo [[./thread#^c44812524|GeekFortyTwo]], replying to that account, describes cooking-related indications motivating a planned stove replacement. These support monitoring as a way to identify patterns and prompt action; they do not establish that an intervention improved health.
- CO2 measurement is presented as useful for understanding indoor air quality and ventilation. floppyd [[./thread#^c44811127|floppyd]] quotes the manufacturer's argument that omitting it is a significant limitation. This is a rationale for including a sensor, without accompanying ventilation measurements, CO2 concentrations, or evidence of cognitive effects.
- Accessible feedback can encourage engagement with measurements. newsclues [[./thread#^c44811372|newsclues]] describes LEDs, a small screen, a dashboard, and Home Assistant integration; altf4-0 [[./thread#^c44811493|altf4-0]] reports that the LEDs can display a chosen parameter. These owners find alternatives to a large numerical display useful, although that does not establish measurement accuracy.
- Openness and repairability can support continued monitoring. GeekFortyTwo [[./thread#^c44812524|GeekFortyTwo]] specifically values being able to keep using the device if its vendor disappears, alongside Home Assistant integration. AirGradient representative ahaucnx [[./thread#^c44810974|ahaucnx]] says repairability was designed into the product. These are continuity and maintenance benefits, rather than evidence that its sensors outperform competitors.
- Meaningful monitor comparisons should assess measurement performance and sustained operation. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c44837056|ahaucnx]] calls for consistent testing, and geerlingguy [[./thread#^c44812899|geerlingguy]] argues that multiple sensors, variable quality control, firmware, and years of intended use make evaluation substantial work. Neither supplies comparative accuracy results in this discussion.

## Counterarguments

- CO2 sensing need not determine every purchasing decision. jjulius [[./thread#^c44813132|jjulius]] suggests that a customer may specifically prioritize indoor PM2.5, while ratelimitsteve [[./thread#^c44812651|ratelimitsteve]] argues that fewer features can be acceptable at an appropriate price and reliability level. This challenges a universal product requirement, without establishing that PM2.5 measurements answer ventilation questions.
- Technical capability can be outweighed by difficulty accessing the information. instagib [[./thread#^c44821201|instagib]] wants an across-room display without visiting a webpage or app, and nothercastle [[./thread#^c44817204|nothercastle]] finds both the screen and initial CO2 setup frustrating. Their objections concern practical usability; they do not demonstrate inaccurate pollutant readings.
- A missing feature and a malfunctioning advertised feature are different purchasing experiences. sjsdaiuasgdia [[./thread#^c44811284|sjsdaiuasgdia]] and bryant [[./thread#^c44811442|bryant]] defend recommending a simpler device that works as intended. Conversely, mrheosuper [[./thread#^c44812464|mrheosuper]] argues that a screen should matter only to buyers who want one, and shkkmo [[./thread#^c44813778|shkkmo]] questions the consistency of accepting LED-only feedback on another indoor monitor.
- A warranty does not erase the inconvenience of failure. edent [[./thread#^c44811158|edent]] emphasizes repair effort and confidence, while ratelimitsteve [[./thread#^c44812651|ratelimitsteve]], after correcting the failure timeline, still considers failure within months grounds for a negative recommendation. Their argument supports reporting the actual ownership burden, rather than inferring sensor accuracy from repairability.
- Claims of a uniquely suitable product meet competing ownership accounts. In direct reply to nati0n's claim that AirGradient is the only real contender [[./thread#^c44810860|nati0n]], redbluered [[./thread#^c44815294|redbluered]] reports owning both AirGradient and Apollo Air and finding both nearly ideal depending on context. These are preferences, not controlled comparisons.

## Assumptions

- The evidence in this source unit is principally household anecdotes, product experiences, and arguments about reviews. rjkingan [[./thread#^c44811252|rjkingan]] praises AirGradient's commitment to accurate data, and Workaccount2 [[./thread#^c44812735|Workaccount2]] calls it excellent on a 'science check', but neither presents accuracy measurements. Such endorsements cannot establish technical superiority or population-wide agreement.
- LED colors require interpretation. altf4-0 [[./thread#^c44811493|altf4-0]] says the displayed parameter is configurable, and captainreynolds [[./thread#^c44811418|captainreynolds]] describes indicators for air status, connectivity, and power. Without the configuration, numerical thresholds, and underlying readings, a green-to-red change cannot independently identify a pollutant concentration or health-risk threshold.
- The spider-plant explanation assumes that the before-and-after conditions were comparable. captainreynolds [[./thread#^c44811418|captainreynolds]] attributes improved CO2 indications to adding about six plants, but provides no numerical readings, controlled comparison, or account of concurrent ventilation, occupancy, lighting, or sensor-setting changes. The reported association does not establish plant effectiveness.
- Display reliability and measurement reliability must be distinguished. shkkmo [[./thread#^c44813593|shkkmo]] quotes the review as saying the display deteriorated after months, correcting an ancestor's claim that the unit arrived broken. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c44812944|ahaucnx]] confirms delayed display failure and offered replacements. This does not establish failure of the PM2.5 or CO2 sensors; the manufacturer's assertion that it was not a quality-control failure also lacks a supplied technical diagnosis.

## Conditions

- Purchase price changes the display tradeoff. Workaccount2 [[./thread#^c44812735|Workaccount2]] says AirGradient was \$100 cheaper than the winning product, and jeroenhd [[./thread#^c44811698|jeroenhd]] questions whether the competing larger color screen justifies that difference. These are commenters' price comparisons, not a verified current price or total-cost analysis.
- Availability can prevent an otherwise preferred purchase. strogonoff [[./thread#^c44813859|strogonoff]] reports that AirGradient did not ship to their area when last checked, whereas IQAir served more countries. Their location is unspecified, so this establishes a distribution constraint without identifying any geographic pollution conditions.
- Affordability and maintenance effort affect acceptable reliability. kadoban [[./thread#^c44820809|kadoban]] argues that eliminating every electronic failure at a reasonable price is unrealistic; edent [[./thread#^c44811158|edent]] stresses that warranty service still consumes time and requires confidence. toomuchtodo [[./thread#^c44817165|toomuchtodo]] proposes buying spares for critical hardware needs, which requires additional upfront spending rather than demonstrating a need for redundant household air monitors.

## Lived experiences

- captainreynolds [[./thread#^c44811418|captainreynolds]] reports AirGradient indications changing from one green LED during the day to three or four red LEDs overnight with two people and multiple pets in the room. They also report consistently green indications after adding about six spider plants and interpret this as controlled CO2. This is firsthand monitoring reported through colors and a description of stored time-series data; no ppm values, graphs, or measured sleep outcomes are supplied.
- Replying to captainreynolds, GeekFortyTwo [[./thread#^c44812524|GeekFortyTwo]] reports that gas-stove use worsens indicated household air quality for an hour or two. Their spouse's preference for green LEDs encouraged attention to the readings, and the stove is now on the replacement list. The comment identifies neither the affected sensor nor pollutant concentrations, and reports a planned replacement rather than a completed intervention.
- holografix [[./thread#^c44811268|holografix]] reports years of reliable AirGradient data delivery to Prometheus and plotting air quality across several unspecified climate events and the introduction of a Samsung bedroom air filter. The comment supplies no readings, direction or size of change, or health outcome. It therefore documents sustained monitoring, without quantifying filtration effectiveness or identifying wildfire exposure.
- mind-blight [[./thread#^c44811124|mind-blight]] says they are lying awake because of an unspecified air-quality issue. Separately, they report a newly purchased home's radon tests being below remediation levels but showing spikes and variability. No concentrations, applicable threshold, or evidence connecting radon to wakefulness are given.
- nothercastle [[./thread#^c44817204|nothercastle]] reports difficulty initially zeroing the CO2 sensor and would hesitate to recommend the device to someone uninterested in technical adjustment. This is direct evidence of a setup burden, without information about calibration procedure, initial error, or subsequent accuracy.
- strogonoff [[./thread#^c44813859|strogonoff]] reports that their IQAir Pro monitor remained too bright for bedroom use, had a narrow readable viewing angle, and became unusable when its charging connection failed after approximately three months. redbluered [[./thread#^c44815339|redbluered]] separately prefers e-ink because they dislike blinking bedroom lights. These are comfort and hardware experiences, not controlled evidence of sleep effects.
- jesol [[./thread#^c44814334|jesol]] reports three AirGradient units working for years; dgllghr [[./thread#^c44810929|dgllghr]] likewise reports years of satisfactory operation, and lokar [[./thread#^c44813333|lokar]] reports positive experience with both pro and outdoor models. These accounts counter an assumption of universal early failure but do not estimate failure rates or demonstrate sustained calibration.
- edent [[./thread#^c44811158|edent]] reports owning a cheap Awair monitor whose USB-C charging and inconspicuous display meet their preferences, while explicitly saying they do not know whether it is accurate. This illustrates the difference between satisfaction with a monitor and verification of its measurements.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Comparative PM2.5 and CO2 accuracy remains unanswered. strogonoff [[./thread#^c44813859|strogonoff]] explicitly asks whether AirGradient measures nearly as well as IQAir and says they could not find SenseAir in a linked evaluation table. No supplied reply provides reference-instrument comparisons, calibration results, quantified drift, or numerical household PM2.5/CO2 readings. No NO2 measurement is reported.
- The health implications of the reported changes remain uncertain. captainreynolds [[./thread#^c44811418|captainreynolds]] describes overnight indicators, GeekFortyTwo [[./thread#^c44812524|GeekFortyTwo]] describes cooking-related deterioration, and mind-blight [[./thread#^c44811124|mind-blight]] attributes wakefulness to an unspecified air issue. This part contains no study findings, cognitive tests, systematic sleep measurements, or evidence assigning a health effect to a measured PM2.5, NO2, or CO2 exposure.
- The requested window-opening tradeoff cannot be resolved from this part. holografix [[./thread#^c44811268|holografix]] mentions unspecified climate events and a filter, while GeekFortyTwo [[./thread#^c44812524|GeekFortyTwo]] reports indoor cooking effects. Neither provides paired indoor/outdoor readings or a ventilation experiment. There are no identified traffic or wildfire-smoke episodes, nor evidence establishing when opening windows would improve or worsen personal exposure.
- Whether the reviewed display failure was exceptional or indicative of broader reliability problems remains disputed. AirGradient representative ahaucnx [[./thread#^c44813559|ahaucnx]] reports knowing of only a single-digit number of failed displays among tens of thousands of indoor monitors sold, while mrgoldenbrown [[./thread#^c44816383|mrgoldenbrown]] warns about reviewing one unit per model. These statements lack the follow-up and reporting information needed to determine a failure rate; ratelimitsteve [[./thread#^c44815179|ratelimitsteve]] nevertheless argues that reviewers must report what happened to their sample.
- The adequacy of the review's handling of failure remains contested. ratelimitsteve [[./thread#^c44824600|ratelimitsteve]] wants both the failure and customer-service experience reported, whereas dcow [[./thread#^c44846699|dcow]] argues that stopping before assessing a replacement made the experience incomplete. This disagreement concerns how to interpret reliability evidence, rather than whether the initial malfunction should be concealed.
- There is no agreed weighting of objective measurement testing against individual usability preferences. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c44837056|ahaucnx]] objects to a 'best' ranking without systematic assessment; jeroenhd [[./thread#^c44811746|jeroenhd]] defends the reviewer's preferences and reported five years using AirVisual Pros. radlad [[./thread#^c44812202|radlad]] suggests that familiarity with another product may itself affect adaptation. None of these positions settles sensor performance.
- Coverage of additional indoor pollutants is unresolved. mind-blight [[./thread#^c44811124|mind-blight]] asks about future radon detection, and liminal [[./thread#^c44816507|liminal]] asks about hydrogen sulfide measurement; neither receives an answer in the supplied part. These requests do not establish that the discussed monitors measure those pollutants or that either pollutant explains the reported comfort problems.
