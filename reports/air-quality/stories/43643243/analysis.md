# [America Is Backsliding Toward Its Most Polluted Era](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43643243)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion responds to concerns that US air-pollution protections are being weakened, with debate over pollution trends, the effects of regulation, health benefits, and how to interpret national data amid wildfires and other factors. Much of the conversation explores political identity and environmental priorities: whether voters can support outdoor recreation while backing policies seen as harmful to conservation, and how party loyalty, economic interests, local costs, and competing priorities shape those choices. Participants also discuss environmental regulation’s effects on small businesses, fishing and hunting, manufacturing, energy, and rural communities, alongside arguments about pollution in China, trade, and market-based policy.
Tags: air pollution, environmental regulation, politics, conservation, public health, energy policy

## Arguments

- Local pollution reduction can benefit people regardless of other countries’ emissions. mathieuh [[./thread#^c43643464|mathieuh]] illustrates this with living beside a coal plant and walking along polluted roads. ffsm8 [[./thread#^c43644989|ffsm8]] similarly advocates reducing urban combustion-engine traffic for immediate air-quality and noise benefits. These are arguments about local exposure and comfort, not firsthand measurements or demonstrated intervention outcomes.
- JKCalhoun [[./thread#^c43643507|JKCalhoun]] quotes projected benefits of preventing up to 4,500 premature deaths and 800,000 asthma cases, with up to \$46 billion in health benefits in a particular year. This supplies a health-and-economic argument for pollution controls, but the figures are an article excerpt’s projections; the supplied material does not identify their modeling assumptions or establish observed benefits.
- supplied\_demand [[./thread#^c43644182|supplied\_demand]] challenges claims of unchanged air quality using reported unhealthy-PM2.5-day counts: 724 in 2017 versus 822 in 2023, a 13.5\% increase, with 2023 described as the highest since 2012. supplied\_demand [[./thread#^c43646205|supplied\_demand]] also reports a decline from 1,195 in 2008 to 702 in 2016, approximately 41\%, and a 9\% increase from 2017 to 2018. These are interpretations of linked aggregate data, not personal monitoring results.
- triceratops [[./thread#^c43644377|triceratops]] argues that diesel-boat restrictions concern other people’s lungs and proposes financial assistance with upgrades. In a subsequent reply, triceratops [[./thread#^c43645170|triceratops]] identifies particulate pollution while boats are docked as the relevant exposure concern. This offers a source-control rationale, although no dockside concentrations, particle-size distribution, or health outcomes are supplied.

## Counterarguments

- rayiner [[./thread#^c43643736|rayiner]] argues that earlier regulatory rollbacks did not produce a corresponding six-year increase in air pollution. rayiner [[./thread#^c43643629|rayiner]] describes a broadly flattened long-term trend alongside substantial fluctuations: a reported 5.5\% increase during 2016–2018, a decrease exceeding 40\% the following year, and a subsequent 76\% increase from the earlier low. These figures challenge a simple deterioration narrative, but do not independently establish the effects of any regulation.
- wcarss [[./thread#^c43647819|wcarss]] objects that nationally summarized unhealthy fine-particulate days represent only one pollution metric and cannot be treated as a direct output of federal policy. The comment identifies wildfires, hurricanes, humidity, wind, local conditions, and state or municipal policies as possible influences. This criticism limits both the deterioration argument and claims that unchanged aggregate numbers demonstrate harmless rollbacks.
- s1artibartfast [[./thread#^c43644657|s1artibartfast]] doubts that exhaust discharged into the water by an ocean fishing boat meaningfully harms people’s lungs and questions whether restricting a few dozen vessels is a high-value intervention. This is explicitly the commenter’s understanding of the situation, without exposure measurements; it does not answer triceratops’ different concern about dockside particulates [[./thread#^c43645170|triceratops]].
- s1artibartfast [[./thread#^c43644482|s1artibartfast]] proposes replacing diesel engines at retirement or the next upgrade instead of requiring immediate replacement. dymk [[./thread#^c43645121|dymk]] counters that diesel engines last a long time, making retirement-based replacement potentially too slow, and invokes the history of diesel-emissions requirements. Neither provides a quantified comparison of exposure avoided, implementation time, and cost.

## Assumptions

- The numerical comparisons assume that the reported unhealthy-PM2.5-day figures are comparable across years. Their aggregation, geographic coverage, thresholds, and underlying concentrations are not reproduced here. As wcarss [[./thread#^c43647819|wcarss]] emphasizes, this metric cannot represent every pollutant or location; percentages quoted by rayiner [[./thread#^c43643629|rayiner]] should not be interpreted as equivalent percentage changes in each person’s inhaled exposure.
- Pollutant identification remains imprecise in the marine discussion. s1artibartfast [[./thread#^c43644657|s1artibartfast]] understands the issue to concern NOx and climate, while triceratops [[./thread#^c43645170|triceratops]] identifies dockside particulate pollution. Neither supplies NO2 measurements, and the particulate concern is not specifically quantified as PM2.5. These comments provide no evidence about indoor CO2 concentrations or effects.
- The health claims require evidence beyond what is supplied. JKCalhoun’s quoted prevention and monetary estimates [[./thread#^c43643507|JKCalhoun]] lack the underlying model, exposure assumptions, and valuation method. Separately, 93po [[./thread#^c43645463|93po]] attributes more than a million annual worldwide deaths to carbon-emitting power generation without providing a pollutant breakdown or supporting study in the comment. That claim cannot establish that CO2 itself causes those deaths.

## Conditions

- The marine setting matters. chneu [[./thread#^c43645127|chneu]] defends restrictions by discussing freshwater diesel use, but s1artibartfast [[./thread#^c43647190|s1artibartfast]] explicitly corrects the context to saltwater regulations with allegedly little lead-in time. The freshwater argument therefore does not establish the benefits of the particular rule being disputed. Dockside exposure and offshore exhaust also concern different locations, as triceratops [[./thread#^c43645170|triceratops]] indicates.
- Outdoor pollution varies with geography and episodic events. wcarss [[./thread#^c43647819|wcarss]] asks whether Chicago, Seattle, and Houston experienced similar conditions and identifies weather and fires as potential confounders. rayiner [[./thread#^c43653555|rayiner]] later suggests Canadian forest fires probably explain the 2023 increase, explicitly declining to attribute it to the president. This is a proposed explanation, not a measured wildfire-source contribution presented in the thread.
- Upfront affordability and the distribution of costs shape feasible improvements. s1artibartfast [[./thread#^c43646531|s1artibartfast]] argues that small operators can bear concentrated losses for broadly distributed benefits. triceratops [[./thread#^c43644377|triceratops]] favors assistance with upgrade costs. The discussion supplies no verified financing options, subsidy availability, or comparison between compliance costs and avoided local health damage.
- Health-benefit totals have disputed financial interpretations. dgfitz [[./thread#^c43643562|dgfitz]] divides \$46 billion by the combined death-and-asthma count and questions the resulting roughly \$60,000 per outcome; pintxo [[./thread#^c43643998|pintxo]] argues that \$1,000–\$2,000 annually can be modest for continuing asthma treatment. The supplied excerpt does not establish that its benefits are solely treatment savings. goda90 [[./thread#^c43643628|goda90]] suggests reduced healthcare-provider profit, while dymk [[./thread#^c43644135|dymk]] suggests reduced insurer losses; these are alternative incentive interpretations, not demonstrated financial results.

## Lived experiences

- s1artibartfast [[./thread#^c43643976|s1artibartfast]] reports having used a California fishing charter that subsequently closed because it could not fund a stated 300k engine retrofit, with employees losing their jobs. This is a firsthand connection to the practical consequences of an emissions rule, although the business finances and regulatory details are not independently documented. It is not an account of measured exposure or personal respiratory symptoms.
- s1artibartfast [[./thread#^c43648447|s1artibartfast]] describes seeing small fishing boats excluded from California harbors while container ships continue operating, and reports an impression that small businesses lose environmental disputes while large businesses receive exemptions. This conveys perceived unequal burdens. It supplies neither comparative ship emissions nor evidence that the two vessel classes face equivalent rules.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The timing and explanation of pollution fluctuations remain contested. 9283409232 [[./thread#^c43643856|9283409232]] attributes the reported decrease to COVID restrictions reducing driving and flights. rayiner [[./thread#^c43645889|rayiner]] replies that the decrease being discussed occurred in 2019 and that pollution increased in 2020; rayiner [[./thread#^c43645919|rayiner]] also places the later spike in 2023, after reopening in 2021. If that dating is correct, lockdowns do not explain the claimed 2019 decrease. The underlying series is not supplied, and wcarss [[./thread#^c43647819|wcarss]] disputes the broader causal reasoning on both sides.
- The marine exchange never establishes the actual respiratory benefit of the disputed restrictions or the best implementation schedule. s1artibartfast [[./thread#^c43644657|s1artibartfast]] questions meaningful harm, whereas triceratops [[./thread#^c43645170|triceratops]] points to dockside particulates. Their respective claims lack concentration or dose evidence. triceratops eventually acknowledges the fairness objections [[./thread#^c43648729|triceratops]], but that concession does not resolve exposure magnitude, the comparison with container ships, or whether subsidies or gradual replacement would achieve sufficient improvements.
- The asthma-cost dispute remains unsupported clinically and economically. dgfitz [[./thread#^c43656793|dgfitz]] calls asthma medically solved and attributes remaining costs to rent-seeking; pintxo [[./thread#^c43643998|pintxo]] emphasizes continuing chronic-illness treatment. Neither establishes the disease course or cost structure underlying JKCalhoun’s quoted projections [[./thread#^c43643507|JKCalhoun]]. The discussion also leaves the monetary estimate’s time horizon and treatment of premature mortality unspecified.
- This source unit leaves most household and personal-outcome questions unaddressed. Its relevant evidence consists of aggregate outdoor-air claims, such as supplied\_demand’s PM2.5 statistics [[./thread#^c43644182|supplied\_demand]], and an emissions-compliance anecdote from s1artibartfast [[./thread#^c43643976|s1artibartfast]]. It contains no firsthand pollutant readings, sensor-reliability comparisons, or reported pollution-related changes in cognition, sleep, or indoor comfort. Cooking, ventilation, filtration, and opening windows are not evaluated. Consequently, this part cannot establish when opening windows improves exposure or brings more outdoor pollution inside.
