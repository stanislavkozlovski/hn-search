# [Wildfire smoke is loaded with microbes](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=25477190)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion considers health risks from microbes carried in wildfire smoke, including occupational respiratory protection and fungal infections, and whether media coverage exaggerates risks from ordinary microbial exposure. Participants debate immune defenses and the distinction between routine exposure and unusual loads disturbed by fires. Other threads cover air quality monitors and HEPA filtration for wildfire-damaged homes, forest fire management, campground wood gathering, and the balance between safety rules and personal freedom.
Tags: wildfire smoke, microbes, respiratory protection, air quality monitoring, forest management

## Relevance

The thread discusses health risks from wildfire smoke and airborne microbes, including a firefighter’s firsthand report that a respirator improved recovery. A person whose home was damaged by wildfire asks about indoor and outdoor monitoring, with replies recommending sensors and filtration; commenters disagree about the magnitude of microbial risk. ## Arguments

- Wildfire exposure may include infectious hazards alongside smoke chemicals. jacobolus [[./thread#^c25477890|jacobolus]] quotes an article describing fire-driven winds aerosolizing soil fungi, including Coccidioides, which can cause valley fever, and Cryptococcus. The excerpt mentions fever, shortness of breath, pneumonia and meningitis, and says the CDC considers firefighters at risk. These are reproduced article claims, not firsthand diagnoses or independently supplied incidence data.
- wolco2 [[./thread#^c25477857|wolco2]] challenges the idea that microbial exposure necessarily strengthens immunity, arguing that infections can be deadly and disrupt microbial communities. wolco2 [[./thread#^c25479210|wolco2]] develops a bacterial-load and bacteriophage explanation, citing Davies et al. (2016). The decomposing-bodies example is an analogy; neither comment supplies wildfire exposure measurements or demonstrates the proposed mechanism in smoke-exposed people.
- Monitoring is proposed as a way to understand an uncertain postfire environment. pcmaffey [[./thread#^c25477858|pcmaffey]] wants to know what is present indoors and outdoors after a partially destructive wildfire. Responding to that situation, syntaxing [[./thread#^c25479421|syntaxing]] suggests either building a monitor or buying PurpleAir. This establishes a practical information need and suggested tools, without demonstrating that those tools can answer the entire safety question.

## Counterarguments

- nickthemagicman [[./thread#^c25477784|nickthemagicman]] accepts that smoke's toxic chemicals are problematic but portrays concern about microbes as exaggerated and initially suggests exposure builds immunity. nickthemagicman [[./thread#^c25478632|nickthemagicman]] argues that microbes are ubiquitous and adverse reactions are statistically unlikely except among susceptible people. These are opinions about risk, with no exposure-specific probabilities or supporting measurements.
- Replying to the homeowner's monitoring question, tyu2 [[./thread#^c25477909|tyu2]] questions whether additional measurement matters when HEPA filtration is, in their view, the only solution. They also say expensive purifiers often contain particle counters and characterize PM2.5 as harmful regardless. This favors spending on mitigation over separate monitoring, but the comment does not establish that filtration alone addresses every postfire hazard.
- Responding directly to that filtration advice, pcmaffey [[./thread#^c25478235|pcmaffey]] says the household already owns several air filters and still does not know whether being there is advisable. This limits the proposed solution: owning filters has not resolved their habitability uncertainty. It does not establish that the filters failed to remove particles.

## Assumptions

- Treating a low particle reading as evidence of overall safety would require evidence absent here. syntaxing [[./thread#^c25479421|syntaxing]] reports PM2.5 near zero, while pcmaffey [[./thread#^c25477858|pcmaffey]] asks more broadly what remains in the air around a damaged home and charred forest. No supplied measurements connect that particle result to microbial viability, chemical exposure or safe occupancy.
- The monitor recommendations do not validate sensor accuracy. syntaxing [[./thread#^c25479421|syntaxing]] says they use the same sensor as PurpleAir and believes PurpleAir uses two for redundancy; the model, calibration and accuracy are unspecified. cbsks [[./thread#^c25478293|cbsks]] merely reports hearing good things about PurpleAir. Redundancy and reputation are therefore suggestions, not demonstrated reliability in these conditions.
- The opposing microbial-risk arguments depend on unmeasured exposure and susceptibility. nickthemagicman [[./thread#^c25478632|nickthemagicman]] extrapolates from everyday microbial contact to very low risk, while wolco2 [[./thread#^c25479210|wolco2]] extrapolates from altered microbial loads and communities to possible harm. Neither establishes the organisms, viable dose or susceptibility relevant to the homeowner or firefighters discussed.

## Lived experiences

- davidbanham [[./thread#^c25481515|davidbanham]] reports starting to wear a reusable P3 respirator on the fireground about a year before commenting and experiencing a massive improvement in recovery over the days following a fire. This is firsthand, subjective evidence of perceived benefit; no symptoms, exposure measurements or controlled comparisons identify which smoke component or other factor explains the improvement.
- syntaxing [[./thread#^c25479421|syntaxing]] reports that Blueair was the only purifier they had used that brought indoor PM2.5 near zero during California wildfire smoke. This is a firsthand reported measurement outcome, but no numerical starting concentration, units, time series, calibration or outdoor comparison is provided. The comparison concerns their own devices, not all available purifiers.
- pcmaffey [[./thread#^c25477858|pcmaffey]] reports a home partially burned three months earlier, damaged areas sealed off and concern about air from the surrounding charred forest. pcmaffey [[./thread#^c25478235|pcmaffey]] adds that several filters are already in use. Their location, pollutant concentrations and health outcomes are unspecified; the experience concerns unresolved postfire exposure rather than a documented active-smoke measurement.
- labseven [[./thread#^c25478313|labseven]] reports using the PurpleAir and AirNow.gov maps. This is firsthand use of monitoring resources, without a comparison against local instruments or evidence that either map represents the homeowner's immediate indoor or outdoor conditions.
