# [Air pollution linked to “huge” reduction in intelligence](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=18219391)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants examine a study linking air pollution exposure in China with lower verbal and math test performance, questioning the headline, causal interpretation, confounders, and the use of education years as a measure of cognitive effects. They debate how IQ and intelligence should be measured and whether observed effects could be temporary or cumulative, and compare pollution exposure in China with conditions in the United States. Other threads consider air purifiers, exposure mitigation, electric transport, cycling and transit, power generation, and the role of research and regulation.
Tags: air pollution, cognition, research methods, education, China, transportation, energy policy

## Relevance

The comments examine evidence linking particulate air pollution with cognitive performance, including study methods, exposure levels, and limits on applying findings across locations. They also discuss PM2.5 versus PM10, filtration, and personal exposure in transit, while preserving uncertainty about causation and effect size. ## Arguments

- 19h [[./thread#^c18220088|19h]] quotes a study reporting that long-term pollution exposure impairs verbal and mathematical test performance, with verbal effects becoming more pronounced with age, especially among men and less educated people. titzer [[./thread#^c18220096|titzer]] quotes its estimate that reducing China's annual mean PM10 to 50 μg/m³ would move median performance to the 63rd percentile for verbal tests and the 58th for mathematics. These are reported study estimates, not firsthand observations of cognitive improvement.
- dcx [[./thread#^c18221504|dcx]] recommends HEPA filtration for coarse and fine particles, citing nominal removal of 99.97\% of qualifying particles from air passing through the filter. This supports a particle-control proposal; the comment supplies neither a measured whole-room reduction nor evidence that a purifier prevents the cognitive effects discussed.
- amluto [[./thread#^c18221588|amluto]] argues that recirculating filtration should be judged by clean air delivery rate: a 90\%-efficient filter with 50\% more airflow can outperform a more efficient filter moving less air. They recommend considering MERV 13 or better HVAC filtration and running the fan, while checking system compatibility with an HVAC contractor. They distinguish this from an outside-air intake, where capture efficiency has greater importance.
- meesterdude [[./thread#^c18222236|meesterdude]] recommends investigating monitor accuracy and suggests that a box fan with a filter may be useful. elektor [[./thread#^c18221273|elektor]] proposes using a handheld monitor to locate neighborhood pollution hotspots. Both support investigating actual exposure, but neither reports completed measurements or validates a device.
- Reason077 [[./thread#^c18220085|Reason077]] advocates electrifying urban transport, while Gibbon1 [[./thread#^c18222252|Gibbon1]] emphasizes reducing exhaust near homes and workplaces. flybrand [[./thread#^c18221145|flybrand]], identifying themselves as someone working in filtration, argues that stationary sources are easier to filter than vehicles because vehicles have limited space and are widely distributed. These arguments concern where pollutants are released and how readily emissions can be controlled.
- jesstaa [[./thread#^c18220333|jesstaa]] proposes walking and cycling in dense cities, and twelvechairs [[./thread#^c18220486|twelvechairs]] adds potential health benefits while acknowledging that these options do not suit everyone everywhere. Responding specifically to counteracting pollution's effects, QasimK [[./thread#^c18223328|QasimK]] suggests exercise, preferably in a park rather than beside a main road, but explicitly presents this as a guess.

## Counterarguments

- peacetreefrog [[./thread#^c18220254|peacetreefrog]] describes a possible confounder: urban and rural educational opportunities could differ, making pollution correlate with test performance even after controlling for years of education. flybrand [[./thread#^c18221164|flybrand]] adds income, education, and healthcare access as correlated factors. These are methodological objections, not demonstrations that the study omitted adequate controls; peacetreefrog expressly leaves that possibility open.
- asaph [[./thread#^c18220011|asaph]] quotes the study author's description of pollution as costing a year of education. Bartweiss [[./thread#^c18222942|Bartweiss]] objects that schooling, development, and older adults' ability to remember errands or make decisions are different outcomes. pavelrub [[./thread#^c18227201|pavelrub]], replying to speculation about accelerated forgetting, stresses that the study did not claim people literally forget a year's education.
- Symmetry [[./thread#^c18220227|Symmetry]] challenges an unconditional air-quality case for electric vehicles by citing reported Chinese studies finding two to five times as much particulate matter and smog-forming chemicals from electricity-powered vehicles as gasoline cars. village-idiot [[./thread#^c18220323|village-idiot]] argues that comparisons depend on the pollutant, fuel, and emissions controls, distinguishing coal generation, modern gasoline vehicles, and older diesel engines. These are secondary claims about emissions, not measurements of commuters' inhaled doses.
- stellar678 [[./thread#^c18221008|stellar678]] cites a literature review suggesting electric cars produce more than 90\% as much particulate pollution as combustion cars, attributing much of the remaining pollution to tires, brakes, and road wear. This challenges the idea that removing exhaust eliminates traffic-related particles, although the estimate and its interpretation are disputed in replies.
- Reason077 [[./thread#^c18221165|Reason077]] challenges flybrand's statement that all pollution is particulate [[./thread#^c18221125|flybrand]], emphasizing NOx and carbon monoxide and identifying diesel-related NOx as a European urban concern. The thread's particle-filter recommendations therefore do not, by themselves, demonstrate protection against these gaseous pollutants.
- Alternatives to driving are not established as uniformly cleaner for the traveler. astrostl [[./thread#^c18230408|astrostl]] relays an acquaintance's account of UK bicycle messengers developing lung disease and raises greater inhalation during exercise; this is explicitly secondhand anecdote, not mortality evidence. eneveu [[./thread#^c18223894|eneveu]] reports learning that Paris rail braking and grinding generate substantial particles, allegedly exceeding street traffic exposure, but supplies no personal measurements.

## Assumptions

- This analysis concerns story 18219391, part 0, from October 2018. Its numerical air-concentration evidence comes from quoted research and secondary data summaries, principally titzer [[./thread#^c18220096|titzer]] and Bartweiss [[./thread#^c18231784|Bartweiss]]. No supplied commenter reports numerical personal PM2.5, NO2, or CO2 sensor readings.
- Pollutant attribution depends on an unresolved assumption. Bartweiss [[./thread#^c18231784|Bartweiss]] describes the study's Air Pollution Index as the maximum of SO2, NO2, and PM10 subindices, assumes PM10 usually dominated, and notes that PM2.5 was not measured separately. Correlated fine particles or gases could therefore contribute to an association presented in terms of PM10.
- Equal particle mass is implicitly an inadequate basis for equating health effects across sources. Bartweiss [[./thread#^c18231784|Bartweiss]] argues that PM2.5 is more likely to enter the bloodstream and contain biologically active material than larger dust, pollen, or ash. This is the commenter's mechanistic interpretation, not a reported chemical analysis of participants' exposure or proof that larger particles are harmless.
- Bartweiss's conclusion that the Chinese cognition result is essentially irrelevant to the United States [[./thread#^c18231784|Bartweiss]] requires assumptions about the exposure-response relationship at lower concentrations. The supplied discussion provides no U.S. cognitive measurements establishing negligible effects. Being below a historical regulatory benchmark does not itself establish that inference.
- The benefit of relocating combustion depends on actual emissions controls, dispersion, and who remains exposed. rayiner [[./thread#^c18220465|rayiner]] argues that much particulate matter settles before reaching distant cities, while flybrand [[./thread#^c18221145|flybrand]] explains why stationary filtration is easier. Neither provides size-specific dispersion measurements or evidence that every relevant plant actually uses effective controls.
- Visible plumes do not establish pollutant identity in this discussion. dest [[./thread#^c18220261|dest]] retreats from certainty that the article depicts a nuclear plant after MichaelApproved [[./thread#^c18220355|MichaelApproved]] notes that coal plants also use cooling towers. StavrosK [[./thread#^c18220557|StavrosK]] adds that some designs combine vapor and other gases. The image dispute supplies no exposure measurement.
- Health-risk figures require attention to exposure pathway. wonderwonder [[./thread#^c18220966|wonderwonder]] describes windblown coal ash, groundwater contamination, and a reported cancer risk as high as one in fifty, while acknowledging the advocacy source. The comment does not isolate inhalation from other pathways, so that number cannot be treated here as a measured PM2.5-specific risk.

## Conditions

- Geographic and historical context matters for the cognition estimates. Bartweiss [[./thread#^c18231784|Bartweiss]] reports much higher Chinese PM10 concentrations than U.S. monitoring sites, including only four U.S. sites above an annual 50 μg/m³ in 2017. They also explain that the study's 50 μg/m³ comparison was a former EPA annual standard withdrawn in 2006. These are historical comparisons, not current standards or measurements of a reader's neighborhood.
- For PM2.5, Bartweiss [[./thread#^c18231784|Bartweiss]] reports thirteen U.S. cities exceeding an annual 12 μg/m³ benchmark and none exceeding 17 μg/m³, alongside a Wuhan study reporting concentrations above 75 μg/m³ 75\% of the time and above 150 μg/m³ 25\% of the time. Annual averages and frequencies of high readings are different summaries; they should not be treated as directly equivalent exposure measurements.
- Wildfire smoke appears only in Bartweiss's secondary geographic discussion [[./thread#^c18231784|Bartweiss]]: southwestern U.S. locations with high PM10 are described as affected by dust, ash, and wildfires, while some industrial Rust Belt locations have higher PM2.5-to-PM10 ratios. This supports attention to local sources and particle composition, but supplies no firsthand wildfire episode, indoor infiltration measurement, or mitigation result.
- Weather and setting qualify traffic-particle arguments. stellar678 [[./thread#^c18221008|stellar678]] notes that review estimates vary with rainy versus dry climates and snow tires. Reason077 [[./thread#^c18221112|Reason077]] argues that tire particles are often washed away but can be resuspended in dry tunnels. These disputed explanations make a single universal electric-versus-combustion PM estimate difficult to apply to an individual route.
- Equipment cost is explicit but performance comparisons are not. meesterdude [[./thread#^c18222236|meesterdude]] identifies an approximately \$400 AirVisual monitor, mentions cheaper alternatives with uncertain accuracy, and links a box-fan filter demonstration. apta [[./thread#^c18225789|apta]] suggests DIY filtration at a fraction of commercial cost. Neither supplies their own cost-versus-clean-air-delivery comparison.
- Moving emissions outside dense cities changes their distribution rather than guaranteeing elimination of exposure. Bartweiss [[./thread#^c18221396|Bartweiss]] argues that electricity generation and regional conditions affect electrification's benefits and warns about burdens on rural populations. WhompingWindows [[./thread#^c18222007|WhompingWindows]] similarly makes comparisons conditional on plant type, coal type, efficiency, and the pollutants being counted.
- A home purifier covers only part of a person's exposure. amluto [[./thread#^c18221588|amluto]] raises time away from home as a limitation, while eneveu [[./thread#^c18223894|eneveu]] considers a purifier worthwhile because they spend roughly half their time at home. Their exchange supports considering time spent in each environment rather than assuming a cleaner home resolves commuting exposure.

## Lived experiences

- eneveu [[./thread#^c18223894|eneveu]] reports owning a Philips AC4072/11 purifier for three years and spending approximately half their time at home. They think it helped their allergies but explicitly allow that the improvement might be placebo. They report no particle readings, controlled comparison, cognitive change, or sleep outcome.
- apta [[./thread#^c18225789|apta]] says they wear a respirator on the subway despite occasional looks from other people. In a follow-up, apta [[./thread#^c18235014|apta]] clarifies that they commute to NYC and that it substantially reduces subway smell. This is a firsthand comfort observation, without measurements establishing changes in PM2.5, NO2, or health risk.
- Reason077 [[./thread#^c18221697|Reason077]], speaking as a Londoner, reports that diesel buses smell unpleasant, including modern hybrids. This is a firsthand observation of odor and discomfort; it does not quantify particulate or NO2 exposure.
- rcMgD2BwE72F [[./thread#^c18220668|rcMgD2BwE72F]] describes daily Paris cycling close to vehicles and diesel exhaust, and later reports commuting by bicycle at least twice daily while finding it more distressing than public transport [[./thread#^c18238229|rcMgD2BwE72F]]. justinator [[./thread#^c18221127|justinator]] offers a more positive account of Paris cycling and rarely using the Métro. These are differing experiences of commuting comfort and safety, without comparable air-quality measurements.
- flybrand [[./thread#^c18221145|flybrand]] identifies professional experience in filtration and describes the practical difficulty of fitting pollution controls into distributed vehicles compared with stationary facilities. This is relevant practitioner testimony about implementation constraints, not a reported field trial or personal exposure reduction.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Causality and transferability remain incompletely established within the supplied discussion. peacetreefrog [[./thread#^c18220254|peacetreefrog]] requests stronger accounting for confounding, whereas Bartweiss [[./thread#^c18231784|Bartweiss]] praises the study's controls while arguing against broad U.S. applicability. trobertson [[./thread#^c18234809|trobertson]] explicitly accepts that their earlier broad interpretation was mistaken after reading Bartweiss's comparison; that exchange should not be represented as continuing disagreement between those two commenters.
- The educational-year analogy remains contested. ip26 [[./thread#^c18222587|ip26]] finds school-grade comparisons intuitive, and r00fus [[./thread#^c18221698|r00fus]] prefers them to IQ points. Bartweiss [[./thread#^c18222997|Bartweiss]] argues that this fails to explain decline in older adults. aero142 [[./thread#^c18222584|aero142]] asks about equivalence to IQ points, but jcranmer [[./thread#^c18225333|jcranmer]] offers a speculative conversion while also warning that the scales cannot be compared that way. No validated conversion or clear everyday-functional interpretation emerges.
- The size of electric vehicles' non-exhaust PM advantage remains disputed. Against stellar678's greater-than-90\% estimate [[./thread#^c18221008|stellar678]], Reason077 [[./thread#^c18221112|Reason077]] argues that much tire debris is relatively large and that regenerative braking greatly reduces brake wear. aembleton [[./thread#^c18221181|aembleton]] asks how far tire and road particles travel and whether rain removes them. The thread supplies no agreed measurement separating total generated debris from inhaled fine particles.
- The significance of subway particles remains uncertain. eneveu [[./thread#^c18223894|eneveu]] expresses concern about reported high concentrations, while apta [[./thread#^c18225789|apta]] recalls an article finding no health problems among long-term subway commuters. Both research accounts are secondhand here. apta's reported odor improvement [[./thread#^c18235014|apta]] does not resolve the apparent conflict about long-term health effects.
- Sensor reliability is discussed without validation data. meesterdude [[./thread#^c18222236|meesterdude]] warns that manufacturers' accuracy claims vary and links criticism of Foobot; elektor [[./thread#^c18221273|elektor]] has only preordered a handheld device. No calibration, reference-instrument comparison, or repeated personal readings are supplied. Separately, sneak [[./thread#^c18220265|sneak]] alleges possible suppression of accurate pollution information in a search product, but that allegation establishes no error in a particular sensor or study dataset.
- This part cannot resolve when opening windows helps or worsens exposure. amluto [[./thread#^c18221588|amluto]] discusses filtration of recirculated and incoming air, but supplies no window-opening experiment or indoor/outdoor comparison. flybrand [[./thread#^c18221164|flybrand]] mentions indoor fires generally, without a cooking account. CO2 appears in emissions comparisons such as village-idiot's [[./thread#^c18220323|village-idiot]], not indoor concentration measurements. No supplied account establishes ventilation effects on CO2, cognition, sleep, or cooking-related exposure.
- Whether personal interventions prevent or reverse the reported health damage remains unanswered. aaron695 [[./thread#^c18225647|aaron695]] recommends filters and monitoring, invokes cancer prevention, and asserts that existing damage has no known repair, without supporting outcome evidence in the comment. QasimK [[./thread#^c18223328|QasimK]] labels exercise a guess, while eneveu [[./thread#^c18223894|eneveu]] qualifies their allergy improvement as possibly placebo. These accounts do not establish cognitive recovery, cancer prevention, or improved sleep.
