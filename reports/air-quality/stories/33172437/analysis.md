# [Pittsburgh Airport installs an algae air purifier](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=33172437)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion questions whether an algae installation at an airport can meaningfully clean air or remove CO2, with participants estimating its capacity and considering energy use, maintenance, and the role of the device as a public display. The thread broadens into the effectiveness of plants and carbon capture, airport and aviation impacts, and competing views on trains, energy use, and population growth.
Tags: algae, CO2 removal, air purification, carbon capture, aviation, transportation

## Relevance

Evaluates an algae device as an indoor CO2 reducer, including skeptical estimates of its effectiveness and discussion of whether elevated CO2 affects cognition or sleep. Firsthand comments report office readings and limited perceived benefit, while ventilation is suggested as a practical alternative. ## Arguments

- Ventilation has the clearest practical support in this source. smt88 [[./thread#^c33173822|smt88]] recommends opening windows for indoor air pollution, and WalterBright [[./thread#^c33173657|WalterBright]] reports that cracking a window resolved elevated office CO2. That supports a CO2 intervention in one office; it does not establish an improvement across all pollutants or outdoor conditions.
- martopix [[./thread#^c33174647|martopix]] proposes an air-to-air heat exchanger to replace CO2-rich indoor air while recovering heat from the outgoing air, potentially reducing ventilation’s heating or cooling penalty. This is a recommendation without installation measurements, operating costs, or a comparison against the algae device.
- Answering maxrev17’s question about harm [[./thread#^c33176271|maxrev17]], ipqk [[./thread#^c33180414|ipqk]] asserts that elevated CO2 causes cognitive decline and worse sleep through reduced oxygen reaching the brain. These are health and mechanism claims; the comment supplies no supporting study results, exposure thresholds, or personal cognitive or sleep measurements.
- Some participants value greenery and algae devices for comfort independently of purification. bawolff [[./thread#^c33174290|bawolff]] imagines calming airport greenery, while jonahbenton [[./thread#^c33176592|jonahbenton]] reports enjoying an algae device’s appearance and bubbling sound. These benefits do not establish improved air quality.

## Counterarguments

- ajmurmann [[./thread#^c33173230|ajmurmann]] revises initial enthusiasm after comparing the home device’s reported equivalence to twenty indoor plants with a paper describing roughly 17\% CO2 removal in one cubic metre under optimal lighting. They doubt meaningful effects in a dwelling or airport. This is a study-based extrapolation, not a measurement of either device.
- kolinko [[./thread#^c33173470|kolinko]] estimates that the airport’s 500 litres of algae remove about 0.25 kg of CO2 daily, compared with about 1 kg exhaled daily per person. If applicable, those figures suggest very limited capacity relative to airport occupancy. Neither the installation’s actual uptake nor its effect on terminal CO2 is measured in the supplied comments.
- tkanarsky [[./thread#^c33173328|tkanarsky]] estimates from a photograph that the bubble columns process only a few litres of air per minute, compared with hundreds or thousands of cubic feet per minute for large purifiers. Their throughput objection is plausible reasoning from appearance, without measured airflow or pollutant removal. The suggested possibility of ion or ozone synergy is also unsupported speculation.
- valenceelectron [[./thread#^c33175368|valenceelectron]] quotes a study summary saying ordinary ventilation’s VOC removal would require 10–1000 plants per square metre to match. This challenges practical houseplant purification in typical buildings, but concerns VOCs rather than demonstrated CO2, PM2.5, or NO2 removal. In reply, nix23 [[./thread#^c33175630|nix23]] clarifies that their proposed application concerns closed space habitats.
- nix23 [[./thread#^c33174806|nix23]] claims CO2 and oxygen levels are normally not problematic, conflicting with ipqk’s health concerns [[./thread#^c33180414|ipqk]]. The denial provides no concentration-specific evidence; the reported room readings elsewhere in the discussion do not themselves settle whether those concentrations caused harm.
- Replying to an owner who noticed no difference, blitzar [[./thread#^c33174571|blitzar]] challenges claims about mental clarity, brain fog, and headaches as difficult to quantify. This identifies an evidence problem without establishing that the outcomes are inherently unmeasurable. ryanmerket’s unmeasured subjective experience [[./thread#^c33173814|ryanmerket]] also cannot establish zero physiological or air-quality effect.
- kwhitefoot [[./thread#^c33175881|kwhitefoot]] challenges bawolff’s proposed noise benefit from airport greenery [[./thread#^c33174290|bawolff]], arguing that substantial hedges would be needed and citing Gardermoen, Arlanda, and Copenhagen as quiet airports. This disagreement concerns acoustic comfort and building design, with no accompanying air-quality measurements.

## Assumptions

- The airport calculation requires occupancy over time. domoritz [[./thread#^c33173876|domoritz]] and scns [[./thread#^c33174809|scns]] challenge kolinko’s wording about a passenger merely passing by; stavros [[./thread#^c33174625|stavros]] reframes the daily removal as one person’s emissions over a quarter-day. majou [[./thread#^c33175143|majou]] identifies average occupancy as the relevant comparison, while nerdponx [[./thread#^c33175622|nerdponx]] notes that daily rates can be converted into other time units.
- Scaling plant experiments to occupied rooms assumes comparable organisms, illumination, air contact, and exposure duration. ajmurmann’s reported 17\% reduction [[./thread#^c33173230|ajmurmann]] lacks a time interval in the supplied comment, preventing a removal-rate calculation from that percentage alone. JohnJamesRambo’s quoted oxygen-use/tree comparison [[./thread#^c33173588|JohnJamesRambo]] likewise does not measure an algae scrubber’s performance.
- Continuous biological removal cannot simply be assumed from continuous lighting. pooper [[./thread#^c33173989|pooper]] asks whether plants can operate around the clock; georgyo [[./thread#^c33174560|georgyo]] says plants need day/night rest, while derrasterpunkt [[./thread#^c33174561|derrasterpunkt]] says the answer depends on C3 versus C4 photosynthesis. No experiment resolves those assertions or establishes their relevance to the device’s algae.
- jonahbenton’s expectation of possible hundredfold improvement over 10–20 years [[./thread#^c33176592|jonahbenton]] is a forecast about future engineering. TheSpiceIsLife [[./thread#^c33176870|TheSpiceIsLife]] quotes approximately 3–6\% actual and 11\% theoretical solar-energy conversion efficiency, but those figures do not establish that room-scale CO2 removal can improve hundredfold.
- The ventilation mechanism remains partly unspecified. ipqk [[./thread#^c33180378|ipqk]] associates bedroom buildup with an airtight building and the A/C being off, while icelancer [[./thread#^c33173825|icelancer]] says office HVAC provides little help. Neither describes outdoor-air intake or air-change rates. A bedroom reading falling after its door opens establishes a local change without showing how much CO2 leaves the building.

## Conditions

- Building layout constrains available interventions: WalterBright [[./thread#^c33173657|WalterBright]] can crack an office window, whereas icelancer [[./thread#^c33173825|icelancer]] occupies an interior office whose route to outside air involves another door roughly 100 feet away. ipqk [[./thread#^c33180378|ipqk]] describes a new LEED-platinum condo and buildup during fall or spring when the A/C is off. These settings should not be treated as interchangeable.
- Energy costs complicate the comparison. martopix [[./thread#^c33174647|martopix]] proposes heat recovery to limit the thermal cost of ventilation. aitch nyu’s commenter name is aitch nyu only if inferred; the supplied commenter a itchnyu is not used here. Instead, aitch nyu is omitted from this item, and JohnJamesRambo [[./thread#^c33173588|JohnJamesRambo]] reports 15,000 watts of plant lighting without preventing elevated CO2 during occupancy. The thread provides no comparable operating-cost figures for practical ventilation and algae purification.
- thesh4d0w [[./thread#^c33180170|thesh4d0w]] says the home device requires a monthly algae-replacement subscription. bmitc [[./thread#^c33183108|bmitc]] worries about renewal expense and plastic packaging, while seeing potential value in composting the remains. These are reported purchasing considerations; the export supplies no subscription price or lifecycle cost comparison.
- nix23 [[./thread#^c33175630|nix23]] anticipates circulation, aeration, filtration, and bacterial control as requirements for an algae system, warning that water could otherwise foul. These imply maintenance and operating burdens, but are hypothetical engineering concerns rather than a report of this product fouling or measured upkeep costs.

## Lived experiences

- WalterBright [[./thread#^c33173657|WalterBright]] bought a CO2 meter, found office readings sometimes above 1,000, and says cracking a window solved the problem. No sensor model, post-intervention concentration, or associated health change is reported.
- icelancer [[./thread#^c33173825|icelancer]] reports that two people sharing a small closed office regularly reach 1,000 ppm within two hours. With no exterior window and little apparent help from HVAC, they periodically open the office door and an exterior door about 100 feet away. They do not quantify the resulting reduction.
- ipqk [[./thread#^c33180378|ipqk]] reports bedroom CO2 increasing from approximately 500 ppm to 1,300 ppm or higher when the A/C is off in fall or spring, followed by a sharp decrease after opening the bedroom door on waking. They link a chart, but its contents are not included in the supplied text. No measured sleep outcome accompanies the readings.
- JohnJamesRambo [[./thread#^c33173588|JohnJamesRambo]] describes elevated CO2 when two people tended a house full of cannabis plants photosynthesizing under 15,000 watts of lighting. This motivates skepticism about biological scrubbing, but the account gives no numerical CO2 concentrations, ventilation details, or direct algae comparison.
- ryanmerket [[./thread#^c33173814|ryanmerket]] uses the home algae device in an office and does not notice a difference. They say they should obtain a CO2 reader, making this a subjective experience without a measured before-and-after result.
- jonahbenton [[./thread#^c33176592|jonahbenton]] reports several months of ownership and enjoys the device as a living lava lamp, with pleasant bubbling and visibly changing algae concentration. They estimate needing about 100 devices to materially change CO2 in a normal room, but provide no measured removal rate or room concentrations.
- aitchnyu [[./thread#^c33173393|aitchnyu]] reports using powerful lighting and injected CO2 to obtain visible oxygen bubbles in an aquarium, and asks for power-consumption figures per room occupancy. This is firsthand cultivation experience, including deliberately adding CO2; it is not a test of removing CO2 from occupied-room air.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The health dispute remains unresolved: ipqk [[./thread#^c33180414|ipqk]] alleges cognitive and sleep effects, while nix23 [[./thread#^c33174806|nix23]] generally dismisses problematic CO2 or oxygen levels. Neither supplies evidence establishing effects at the reported room concentrations, and the reduced-brain-oxygen explanation is unsupported within this source.
- Sensor reliability cannot be assessed from these accounts. WalterBright [[./thread#^c33173657|WalterBright]], icelancer [[./thread#^c33173825|icelancer]], and ipqk [[./thread#^c33180378|ipqk]] provide readings or trends without sensor models, calibration procedures, placement details, or independent checks. Their observations remain useful firsthand reports, with unknown measurement accuracy.
- Opening windows lowered reported CO2 for WalterBright [[./thread#^c33173657|WalterBright]], but smt88’s broader recommendation [[./thread#^c33173822|smt88]] is not tested against polluted outdoor air. This source supplies no PM2.5 or NO2 measurements, cooking-emission comparisons, traffic-exposure accounts, or wildfire-smoke experiences. It therefore cannot determine when opening a window would worsen overall exposure or how particle filtration would compare.
- The outdoor-tree claim is disputed without resolution. smt88 [[./thread#^c33173822|smt88]] says planting trees can increase outdoor air pollution; WalterBright [[./thread#^c33174188|WalterBright]] says this occurs only when existing vegetation is removed; msandford [[./thread#^c33175094|msandford]] cannot find support for smt88’s claim in the linked article. The supplied comments identify neither a specific pollutant nor measured personal exposure.
- Whether either algae installation materially improves occupied-space air remains unanswered. kolinko [[./thread#^c33173470|kolinko]] offers a removal estimate, while owners ryanmerket [[./thread#^c33173814|ryanmerket]] and jonahbenton [[./thread#^c33176592|jonahbenton]] supply no measured CO2 reduction. Their subjective accounts are compatible: noticing no air-related difference and enjoying an attractive, bubbling object can both occur.
