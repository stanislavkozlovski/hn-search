# [Are Gas Stoves Causing Asthma?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=36333878)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants debate whether gas stoves can contribute to asthma, distinguishing evidence that they may worsen existing symptoms from less conclusive claims about causing new cases. The thread discusses cooking emissions, range hoods and ventilation, housing conditions, and whether appliance rules or individual choices are appropriate. Participants also compare gas with induction and resistive electric cooking, weighing performance and cost against health, climate, renter, and public-policy concerns.
Tags: gas stoves, asthma, indoor air quality, ventilation, cooking emissions, induction cooking, public policy

## Relevance

Discussion directly concerns gas cooking emissions and asthma, whether gas worsens existing asthma or may contribute to new cases, and the role and effectiveness of kitchen exhaust. It includes practical concerns about noisy range hoods, inadequate ventilation in homes, and induction as an alternative, alongside disagreement about the strength of evidence. ## Arguments

- throwaway22032 [[./thread#^c36334770|throwaway22032]] argues that frying produces unwanted particles on gas, induction, and electric stoves, making extraction necessary regardless of fuel. lathiat [[./thread#^c36334953|lathiat]] likewise recommends using a rangehood for all cooking. These are general cooking-pollution claims, not measurements of PM2.5 or evidence that different fuels produce identical exposures.
- kungito [[./thread#^c36334756|kungito]] and mint2 [[./thread#^c36335464|mint2]] frame the concern as potentially creating new asthma cases, rather than merely aggravating existing asthma. eternityforest [[./thread#^c36336401|eternityforest]] emphasizes childhood exposure that children cannot choose or control. These comments advance a prevention argument but supply no individual medical histories or causal study results.
- lathiat [[./thread#^c36334953|lathiat]] recalls research suggesting roughly 12\% of asthma might be attributable to gas cooking, potentially falling to about 3\% with better ventilation. The commenter explicitly would need to retrieve the numbers again; these are approximate remembered estimates with no population or study design supplied, not a measured intervention or an individual's asthma probability.
- bbatha [[./thread#^c36341185|bbatha]] argues that induction outperforms gas and resistive cooking on most dimensions, making replacement practically attractive apart from tasks requiring an open flame. This supports an alternative to indoor gas cooking, but the comment supplies neither performance benchmarks nor exposure measurements.
- bbatha [[./thread#^c36349240|bbatha]] argues that household exposure affects children and renters who may not choose their appliances, and that health costs extend to insurance and public health systems. faeriechangling [[./thread#^c36336129|faeriechangling]] adds that stoves persist through successive occupants, conditionally arguing that stronger restrictions could reduce childhood asthma if the health claims are true. Arn\_Thor [[./thread#^c36335189|Arn\_Thor]] similarly invokes shared health costs; none quantifies those costs here.

## Counterarguments

- malfist [[./thread#^c36334441|malfist]] distinguishes uncertain asthma causation from what they characterize as certain aggravation of existing asthma. lockhouse [[./thread#^c36339792|lockhouse]] calls the causal evidence very weak and proposes requiring proper ventilation instead of banning gas appliances. These positions challenge confident prevention claims, but neither supplies supporting studies in this export.
- housemusicfan [[./thread#^c36334639|housemusicfan]] defends gas for wok cooking, while kelipso [[./thread#^c36335938|kelipso]] distinguishes widespread enthusiasm for gas from the narrower group of wok users. bbatha [[./thread#^c36341138|bbatha]] questions whether that preference generally corresponds to better performance. The thread therefore contains both cooking preferences and performance assertions, which should not be treated as equivalent evidence.
- lockhouse [[./thread#^c36339792|lockhouse]] claims gas offers faster heating, rapid temperature changes, and relatively clean combustion compared with other fossil fuels. bbatha [[./thread#^c36341185|bbatha]] directly disputes the speed advantage, asserting that resistive cooking heats faster and induction performs better overall. No comparative tests resolve this exchange, and relative cleanliness among fuels does not establish an indoor exposure level.
- basch [[./thread#^c36336437|basch]], replying to the recommendation for extraction regardless of fuel, reports that the article favors activated-charcoal filtration over a hood. basch expresses skepticism. This is a secondhand description of an unavailable article, not a demonstrated filtration advantage.
- AngryData [[./thread#^c36335866|AngryData]] disputes the shared-cost argument by claiming research shows smoking and obesity can reduce lifetime medical expenditure through earlier death. Arn\_Thor [[./thread#^c36336791|Arn\_Thor]] responds that childhood asthma does not follow that mechanism. No asthma-specific financial evidence is supplied, so the analogy does not settle the costs of cooking-related respiratory illness.

## Assumptions

- housemusicfan [[./thread#^c36334639|housemusicfan]] assumes the concern chiefly involves an already susceptible minority, using an unsupported 0.01\% figure. bbatha [[./thread#^c36335158|bbatha]] instead bets that people with or susceptible to asthma substantially outnumber people obtaining a wok-related advantage from gas. Neither provides population counts, and their comparison concerns different groups from gas enthusiasts generally.
- Treating ventilation as sufficient protection assumes suitable equipment, adequate installed performance, and actual use. callalex [[./thread#^c36334437|callalex]] asks how many stoves have properly sized, functioning hoods; lathiat [[./thread#^c36334953|lathiat]] describes installation and noise problems that could undermine those assumptions. The supplied replies do not establish the prevalence of effective ventilation.
- perryizgr8 [[./thread#^c36335634|perryizgr8]] assumes superior appliances should win through voluntary adoption, then argues renters could drive replacement through apartment selection [[./thread#^c36336203|perryizgr8]]. object-a [[./thread#^c36336262|object-a]] counters that few people choose an apartment primarily for its stove. perryizgr8 [[./thread#^c36336682|perryizgr8]] interprets that low priority as evidence the issue is unimportant, but the exchange does not establish that housing preferences reveal exposure risk.
- cafard [[./thread#^c36334940|cafard]] suggests gas stoves were more common in earlier generations while asthma was less common—or merely less discussed—and homes were leakier. This acknowledges both ventilation and recognition as possible confounders; it is not a controlled historical comparison demonstrating safety or harm.
- Arn\_Thor [[./thread#^c36349509|Arn\_Thor]] argues that much gas-cooking energy enters room air instead of the vessel, explicitly acknowledging a lack of figures. This is an unmeasured efficiency claim relevant to possible indoor heat and exposure tradeoffs, not a documented comfort improvement or pollutant reduction.

## Conditions

- In the Australian installations described by lathiat [[./thread#^c36334953|lathiat]], increasingly required roof ducting introduces resistance that the commenter believes common fans handle poorly. Short wall outlets or unducted configurations reportedly sound less objectionable. These are configuration-dependent noise observations; the comment does not establish that the quieter arrangements provide equivalent pollutant removal.
- lathiat [[./thread#^c36334953|lathiat]] identifies affordability and product availability as barriers to quiet ventilation. They quote bathroom-fan retail prices of \$110–130 for a commonly installed Clipsal model versus about \$85 for a quieter Fanco alternative, while reporting that quieter rangehoods appear concentrated among expensive brands. The bathroom-fan comparison does not establish the cost of a suitable kitchen hood.
- lockhouse [[./thread#^c36339792|lockhouse]] says gas is cheaper to operate in many localities, but supplies no tariffs, consumption figures, or replacement costs. Consequently, this part cannot establish a universal financial advantage for either gas or induction.
- Specialized cooking changes the comparison: bbatha [[./thread#^c36341185|bbatha]] acknowledges open-flame tasks such as pepper charring and wok hei, proposing a \$10 torch or propane grill as substitutes. Arn\_Thor [[./thread#^c36349486|Arn\_Thor]] speculates that bowl-shaped induction wok equipment could provide comparable heat, while acknowledging little testing and limited suitability for ordinary home kitchens. No exposure comparison evaluates these alternatives.
- giraffe\_lady [[./thread#^c36335732|giraffe\_lady]] claims that two-thirds of NYC residents rent and that landlords select appliances according to their own financial considerations. bbatha [[./thread#^c36349240|bbatha]] also highlights renters' limited control and frequently inadequate apartment ventilation. These comments identify potentially important housing constraints, but the rental proportion and ventilation prevalence are not verified here.

## Lived experiences

- lathiat [[./thread#^c36334953|lathiat]] describes substantial rangehood noise in their newly built Australian home and similar reports from other households. They report 65–70 dB even at the lowest setting and a noticeable sense of relief when switching the fan off. This is firsthand evidence of acoustic discomfort supplemented by informal reports; the numerical sound levels come without a documented measurement protocol.
- Recalling Australia in the 1990s, lathiat [[./thread#^c36334953|lathiat]] describes gas stoves beneath former wood-stove chimneys without active extraction and new homes with ceiling-height exhaust fans. They say directly overhead rangehoods became standard locally in the 2000s. This is a geographically and historically bounded account, not a housing survey or evidence of measured changes in childhood asthma.
- lathiat [[./thread#^c36334953|lathiat]] reports buying a flow hood and using the iPhone NIOSH app to investigate installed airflow, duct configuration, and noise. They also contacted Choice about testing against duct resistance, receiving only an acknowledgment. No airflow dataset, instrument validation, or before-and-after pollutant results are supplied.
- twelvedogs [[./thread#^c36335645|twelvedogs]] says their gas stovetop is malfunctioning and they are switching to induction because they regard it as faster and easier to clean. This is a reported replacement decision, not a completed comparison showing improved air quality, health, or comfort.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Whether gas cooking causes new asthma, how large any effect is, and how much ventilation changes it remain unresolved within this part. kungito [[./thread#^c36334756|kungito]] asserts the new-case interpretation, malfist [[./thread#^c36334441|malfist]] calls causation inconclusive, and lathiat [[./thread#^c36334953|lathiat]] recalls attributable-fraction estimates without retrieving their source. antiquark [[./thread#^c36335026|antiquark]] asks for studies comparing people raised with electric versus gas stoves; none is provided in the supplied replies.
- The relative effectiveness of activated charcoal and extraction remains undetermined. basch [[./thread#^c36336437|basch]] supplies only a skeptical article paraphrase, while callalex [[./thread#^c36334437|callalex]] raises hood sizing and functionality. There are no filter specifications, pollutant-specific removal results, or installed capture measurements establishing when either approach adequately controls exposure.
- The practical ventilation problem remains partly behavioral: lathiat [[./thread#^c36334953|lathiat]] argues that loud equipment discourages use and proposes remote fans, better pressure handling, and more representative testing. The thread does not quantify actual usage, establish which design changes solve the problem, or demonstrate that an adequately used hood eliminates any exposure difference between fuels.
- Preference and involuntary household exposure remain in tension. chillbill [[./thread#^c36346463|chillbill]] emphasizes letting people choose, and lockhouse [[./thread#^c36335668|lockhouse]] expresses a hypothetical willingness to accept higher insurance costs and shorter life rather than restrictions. bbatha [[./thread#^c36349240|bbatha]] emphasizes children and renters who cannot make that choice. Those value judgments do not themselves resolve the exposure or health evidence.
- For story 36333878, part 0, there are no PM2.5 concentrations, NO2 readings, indoor CO2 measurements, or air-quality sensor accuracy checks. throwaway22032 [[./thread#^c36334770|throwaway22032]] discusses particles without identifying their size, and lathiat [[./thread#^c36334953|lathiat]] discusses sound and airflow instruments rather than pollutant monitoring. These accounts cannot establish pollutant levels or the reliability of consumer air-quality sensors.
- This part supplies no firsthand changes in asthma symptoms, cognition, or sleep, and no window-opening experiment or traffic/wildfire exposure comparison. cafard [[./thread#^c36334940|cafard]] offers a hypothesis about leakier homes, while lathiat [[./thread#^c36334953|lathiat]] describes extraction and acoustic comfort. Neither establishes when outdoor air would improve indoor conditions versus introduce additional pollution, so that part of the approved question remains unanswered here.
