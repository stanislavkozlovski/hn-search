# [Installing air filters in classrooms has surprisingly large educational benefits (2020)](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43529257)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants debate whether a study linking classroom air filters to improved test scores supports the large effects highlighted in the story. They scrutinize the study design, statistical uncertainty, possible confounders, filter use, and whether other research supports benefits to cognition or attendance. The thread also covers practical air-quality measures in schools and homes, including ventilation, filtration, filter types, noise, maintenance, and the limits of opening windows in different climates.
Tags: classroom air quality, air filtration, education research, study methods, ventilation, public health, schools

## Relevance

Comments discuss classroom air filtration, possible health and cognitive effects, filter types and limits, and ventilation tradeoffs. Firsthand accounts describe allergy relief and fewer illnesses, while participants dispute whether the headline study supports large educational effects and whether filtration or other factors explain the results. Comments also note that outdoor pollution can make opening windows unhelpful. ## Arguments

- evil-olive [[./thread#^c43530415|evil-olive]] summarizes research associating pollution with worse short-term performance among chess players, umpires, office workers and factory workers, alongside long-term cognitive decline in older adults and educational benefits from reducing school-bus diesel emissions. TheOtherHobbes [[./thread#^c43533499|TheOtherHobbes]] also links supporting studies. These are commenters’ descriptions of research, not firsthand measurements or demonstrated replications of the particular classroom intervention.
- Cleaner air could improve education indirectly through fewer infections and absences. fifteenforty [[./thread#^c43529754|fifteenforty]] proposes this mechanism, while PaulKeeble [[./thread#^c43529961|PaulKeeble]] describes UK classroom comparisons that reportedly found clinically significant illness reductions despite pupils mixing during breaks. tehjoker [[./thread#^c43531438|tehjoker]] additionally predicts cognitive benefits from preventing SARS-CoV-2 infections; that is an expectation rather than a result established in this discussion.
- Mechanical filtration is presented as a practical intervention for particles, including PM2.5, and airborne infection. PaulKeeble [[./thread#^c43529933|PaulKeeble]] argues that MERV 13 Corsi–Rosenthal boxes can clear rooms faster than more restrictive HEPA configurations because they move more air. fifteenforty [[./thread#^c43529916|fifteenforty]] similarly emphasizes the tradeoff between capture efficiency per pass and total airflow. Neither supplies a firsthand comparison or establishes that every MERV 13 installation outperforms every HEPA purifier.
- Gas and odor removal require a different assessment from particle removal. amluto [[./thread#^c43531145|amluto]] recommends substantial activated carbon, sometimes specially treated media, rather than thin carbon layers. Aurornis [[./thread#^c43529895|Aurornis]] likewise recommends substantial carbon capacity for VOC removal. These are equipment recommendations; the thread contains no accompanying before-and-after VOC measurements.
- Ventilation is presented as the practical response to accumulated indoor CO2. PaulKeeble [[./thread#^c43529933|PaulKeeble]] distinguishes bringing in outside air for CO2 from filtering particles or VOCs. amluto [[./thread#^c43530351|amluto]] also treats CO2 as a useful ventilation indicator under appropriate conditions. This supports matching an intervention to its target pollutant rather than assuming a room purifier addresses everything.
- Commenters propose additional ways to reduce exposure when room air cannot readily be improved. PaulKeeble [[./thread#^c43529933|PaulKeeble]] recommends particulate respirators such as N95/FFP2-class devices in polluted or shared environments. permo-w [[./thread#^c43530146|permo-w]] raises UVC for infection control while acknowledging downsides. These proposals address particular hazards and do not establish removal of every airborne contaminant.
- yoshuaw [[./thread#^c43530138|yoshuaw]] argues that reducing airborne disease offers both health and financial benefits, citing a study said to attribute approximately 85\% of Greek sick days in 2023–2024 to COVID and influenza. The comment uses that figure to motivate cleaner indoor air; it does not demonstrate that filtration could prevent 85\% of sick days.

## Counterarguments

- The headline’s large educational effect receives specific statistical criticism. rayiner [[./thread#^c43529773|rayiner]] quotes an analysis alleging dependence on one data point and an unjustified linear trend; rnjailamba [[./thread#^c43531351|rnjailamba]] quotes Gelman rejecting the claimed evidential strength. stdbrouw [[./thread#^c43532636|stdbrouw]] says the reported uncertainty is compatible with a population effect of zero. These objections concern the estimate’s reliability and magnitude, rather than proving that filtration has no benefit.
- The methodological criticism is itself contested. stdbrouw [[./thread#^c43532636|stdbrouw]] says the discontinuity design already accounts for variation between classes, teachers, schools and years, challenging Aurornis’s description of an analysis that effectively assumes only filtration changed [[./thread#^c43529859|Aurornis]]. ted\_dunning [[./thread#^c43530434|ted\_dunning]] also points to demographic controls. The discussion therefore does not support describing the study as wholly unadjusted, although adjustment alone does not resolve its uncertainty.
- Aurornis [[./thread#^c43531074|Aurornis]] questions whether limited exposure during one school year could produce such dramatic gains and argues that other pollution studies examine different exposures. fc417fc802 [[./thread#^c43533312|fc417fc802]] correctly identifies a limitation in that rebuttal: several studies summarized in the parent comment concern short-term effects. Evidence for short-term pollution effects still does not automatically establish the classroom study’s particular effect size.
- conformist [[./thread#^c43530609|conformist]] and eru [[./thread#^c43530786|eru]] argue that publication and publicity preferentially select unusually large positive estimates, making smaller effects on replication more plausible. Conversely, mmooss [[./thread#^c43530320|mmooss]] urges extracting useful knowledge without treating every limitation as grounds for dismissal. The supplied discussion does not quantify the selection bias for this study.
- Improved test performance need not mean greater accumulated learning. fasthands9 [[./thread#^c43530679|fasthands9]] distinguishes better conditions during testing from improved underlying ability, using air conditioning during a running test as an analogy. d--b [[./thread#^c43530901|d--b]] suggests greater rest or a concentrating effect from the purifier’s hum as possibilities, explicitly without establishing either explanation.
- Claims about unused filters require the reply’s timing correction. Aurornis [[./thread#^c43531165|Aurornis]] raises nonuse as a weakness, but jancsika [[./thread#^c43531732|jancsika]] says some teachers stopped using filters after the short observation window, making long-run results difficult to interpret. Aurornis separately reports complaints that filters made air “too dry” and disputes actual moisture removal [[./thread#^c43529895|Aurornis]]. Nonuse after the initial window should not be silently treated as nonuse throughout it.
- The daycare’s reported UV intervention cannot establish the suitability of unspecified UV lights. LorenPechtel [[./thread#^c43541289|LorenPechtel]] questions which fixtures were installed, distinguishes upper-room disinfection from directly exposed lamps, and raises exposure concerns. The actual equipment and installation remain unidentified.
- The proposed explanation that ordinary indoor CO2 materially crowds out oxygen is challenged by frognumber [[./thread#^c43533821|frognumber]] and LorenPechtel [[./thread#^c43541054|LorenPechtel]]. vlovich123’s subsequent discussion of blood chemistry, neurological mechanisms and concentration thresholds is explicitly quoted from Claude [[./thread#^c43535030|vlovich123]]. It is not a measurement or independently substantiated study result within this source.

## Assumptions

- The gas leak explains why filters were purchased, not necessarily what exposure they reduced. maxerickson [[./thread#^c43530444|maxerickson]] says the leak had been fixed and the gas was gone; LorenPechtel [[./thread#^c43540499|LorenPechtel]] suggests reduced pathogen transmission as an alternative to ordinary pollution removal. fc417fc802 [[./thread#^c43533312|fc417fc802]] interprets the reported absence of unusual VOCs as removing a potential confounder. Treating the intervention as demonstrated methane removal would misread this context.
- The proposed school-wealth explanation has an important correction. AngryData [[./thread#^c43531116|AngryData]] and xattt [[./thread#^c43529891|xattt]] suggest that schools choosing to fund filtration might also spend more on education. Aurornis [[./thread#^c43531165|Aurornis]] replies that the filters were provided free, and permo-w [[./thread#^c43530067|permo-w]] identifies the gas company as the payer following parental intervention. That weakens this particular purchasing-selection explanation without establishing that all geographic or school-level differences were eliminated.
- Interpreting CO2 as a ventilation proxy assumes that gases accumulate and are removed in roughly comparable ways. amluto [[./thread#^c43530351|amluto]] explicitly limits that interpretation to spaces exchanging air with outdoors without other gas treatment, contrasting them with carbon-filtered classrooms and submarines. A benefit from fresh air therefore does not, by itself, identify CO2 as the causal agent.
- Claims about childhood immunity contain unresolved assumptions. sheepscreek [[./thread#^c43529835|sheepscreek]] assumes younger children have weaker immunity and proposes an explicitly arbitrary grade boundary. Balgair [[./thread#^c43530242|Balgair]] instead emphasizes limited previous exposure, while permo-w [[./thread#^c43535125|permo-w]] distinguishes potentially beneficial microbial exposure from exposure to dangerous pathogens. Nothing supplied establishes xwolfi’s concern that avoiding infections through cleaner air would weaken adult health [[./thread#^c43530071|xwolfi]].
- Real-world benefits assume that equipment remains operating and adequately maintained. PaulKeeble [[./thread#^c43529961|PaulKeeble]] reports adherence problems in classroom studies and attributes many to misunderstanding. MemexXx [[./thread#^c43532683|MemexXx]] asks what happens when filters are not replaced. The latter is an unanswered maintenance concern, not an observed deterioration in performance.

## Conditions

- Geographic generalization is disputed. Aurornis [[./thread#^c43529859|Aurornis]] quotes a study allocating filters to schools within five miles of a gas leak. hollerith [[./thread#^c43529951|hollerith]] emphasizes the Los Angeles setting and predicts less benefit where outdoor air is clean; sebastiennight [[./thread#^c43530811|sebastiennight]] asks how many families actually live in such places. No location-by-location exposure measurements are supplied to settle that prediction.
- Opening windows has pollutant-specific consequences. ajb [[./thread#^c43532272|ajb]] contrasts dilution of indoor contaminants with introducing outdoor diesel particles, tire particles and pollen. wahnfrieden [[./thread#^c43531086|wahnfrieden]] emphasizes nearby traffic and brake/tire dust, while eru [[./thread#^c43531000|eru]] notes outdoor dust. Thus fresh-air ventilation may improve CO2 or stuffiness while worsening particle or allergen exposure; this thread supplies no paired indoor/outdoor measurements for choosing particular times.
- Cold weather creates energy and comfort costs. Cthulhu\_ [[./thread#^c43533009|Cthulhu\_]] recommends opening windows for at least ten minutes daily, but LorenPechtel [[./thread#^c43541073|LorenPechtel]] objects that this can be expensive and exceed what existing HVAC capacity accommodates. willlma [[./thread#^c43550770|willlma]] describes a CO2–energy–comfort tradeoff. These replies challenge the universality of the schedule rather than demonstrating that winter ventilation is impossible.
- Evaporative cooling depends on local conditions. SoftTalker [[./thread#^c43530061|SoftTalker]] says swamp coolers need dry air, and bluGill [[./thread#^c43530498|bluGill]] infers a potential desert-dust problem. IneffablePigeon [[./thread#^c43532415|IneffablePigeon]] instead expects open windows to prevent humidity problems in most climates. Mistletoe’s request for the homeowner’s actual relative humidity [[./thread#^c43529997|Mistletoe]] receives no numerical answer, leaving that installation’s suitability unverified.
- The equipment prices are examples rather than a controlled cost comparison. tangjurine [[./thread#^c43530858|tangjurine]] reports buying a \$150 room HEPA purifier; aetherspawn [[./thread#^c43530780|aetherspawn]] questions whether a \$700 purifier matches the study’s equipment. Aurornis [[./thread#^c43529895|Aurornis]] recommends more expensive gas-filtration units containing substantial carbon, suggesting 10–20 pounds or more. No comparable airflow, operating-cost or replacement-cost figures accompany these prices.
- Funding can enable adoption without establishing economic payback. Balgair [[./thread#^c43530271|Balgair]] says a grant financed daycare filters and UV equipment. dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c43538629|dzhiurgis]] proposes parental contributions resembling a ten-year bond and asserts that systems would pay for themselves. The latter is a financing suggestion without a supporting cost or savings calculation.
- Sick-day economics may depend on local reporting practices. Responding to yoshuaw’s Greek disease figures [[./thread#^c43530138|yoshuaw]], socksy [[./thread#^c43543604|socksy]] suggests that taking leave for COVID may be more culturally accepted than taking leave for a cold. If so, recorded sick-day shares would not directly describe all infections or transfer unchanged to other countries.

## Lived experiences

- frognumber [[./thread#^c43533821|frognumber]] reports bedroom CO2 around 500 ppm with windows open and 800 ppm closed, alongside a perceived performance difference when fresh air is withheld. Their earlier comment gives a broader local range of approximately 400–1,000 ppm [[./thread#^c43530772|frognumber]]. They also report no obvious discomfort from having dry ice in a room, but give no concentration for that exposure. These are reported readings and sensations, without sensor identification, calibration or controlled attribution.
- jwpapi [[./thread#^c43529769|jwpapi]] describes Airthings monitoring combined with Pushover reminders to open a window as probably their largest productivity improvement in ten years. In a later reply, they explain that becoming absorbed in work leads them to forget ventilation and then feel less focused across the day [[./thread#^c43638056|jwpapi]]. No alert threshold, concentration history or objective productivity measure is supplied.
- jedc [[./thread#^c43530599|jedc]], identifying as a former submariner, reports that a fellow crew member developed disabling migraines when CO2 became too high and says operating another scrubber was the fix. This is firsthand observation of another person, with no concentration or controlled comparison. It directly challenges amluto’s initial suggestion that submarine experience shows no impairment.
- 7thaccount [[./thread#^c43530203|7thaccount]] reports a substantial quality-of-life improvement from multiple home filters in a high-pollen area and recalls allergy symptoms interfering with school. They also describe sensitivity to smells, including a running dishwasher. Their suggestion that a baking-soda filter removes some VOCs is explicitly tentative; no particle or gas measurements establish what caused or relieved the symptoms.
- pizzly [[./thread#^c43530815|pizzly]] recalls a persistent runny nose in particular air-conditioned school buildings, with relief outside or in other buildings, and says it distracted from learning. Their estimate that roughly 5\% of classmates were similarly affected came from hearing sniffing. Neither the contaminant nor the effectiveness of a proposed better AC filter was measured.
- culopatin [[./thread#^c43540798|culopatin]] recalls classroom tables smelling of paint thinner until spring and wonders about cognitive consequences. In reply, LorenPechtel [[./thread#^c43540953|LorenPechtel]] reports being unable to think properly in one college building and says this drove them out of college. They never identified the offending exposure; a separate house later found to contain black mold provides an analogy, not proof that the college problem was mold.
- MPSFounder [[./thread#^c43530314|MPSFounder]] reports painted-over asbestos in an office ceiling and worries that deteriorating paint could permit exposure [[./thread#^c43542865|MPSFounder]]. LorenPechtel [[./thread#^c43540874|LorenPechtel]] counters that leaving intact asbestos encapsulated can avoid exposure caused by disturbing it. No airborne-fiber measurement or actual deterioration is reported, and MPSFounder acknowledges uncertainty about broader causal claims.
- sheepscreek [[./thread#^c43529835|sheepscreek]] reports that their first grader misses considerable class time. This supports the personal importance of illness-related absence, but the comment provides no filtration comparison or evidence that air quality caused those absences.
- Balgair [[./thread#^c43530271|Balgair]] reports markedly fewer and less severe daycare illnesses after grant-funded equipment described as “N-95 air filters” and UV lights, while also crediting RSV vaccination. They subsequently bought equipment for home. HPsquared [[./thread#^c43532697|HPsquared]] proposes regression toward normal infection levels after reopening; Balgair concedes that rigorous attribution is impossible retrospectively, while reporting favorable impressions from remaining pre-COVID staff [[./thread#^c43534830|Balgair]].
- Mask experiences conflict with the claim that frequent infections are unavoidable. xwolfi [[./thread#^c43530071|xwolfi]] reports years without illness during mask mandates, followed by heavy illness after masks ended, but also describes staying home and substantial stress during the earlier period. fifteenforty [[./thread#^c43530405|fifteenforty]] reports about three infections in five years while continuing N95 use; tehjoker [[./thread#^c43531461|tehjoker]] reports one cold, testing negative for COVID, over five years of indoor N95 use. These uncontrolled accounts do not isolate masking from other exposure differences.
- theoreticalmal [[./thread#^c43529975|theoreticalmal]] uses swamp coolers and keeps windows open continuously for four months each year, preferring the comfort and morning birdsong to an enclosed air-conditioned home. They report neither humidity nor pollutant measurements, so this is a comfort preference and operating experience rather than proof of cleaner air.
- Winter airing produced different comfort experiences. nottorp [[./thread#^c43535113|nottorp]] recalls briefly opening school windows every day even at −20°C. dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c43538652|dzhiurgis]] remembers children protesting strongly about cold, whereas eru [[./thread#^c43541533|eru]] recalls only mild complaints near the window and a teacher insisting that the room smelled of sweaty teenagers. These accounts show different tolerances and classroom practices, not a universal outcome.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The magnitude and cause of the educational benefit remain unsettled. Analemma\_ [[./thread#^c43530172|Analemma\_]] reads the paper as an encouraging case study warranting follow-up, while craigdalton [[./thread#^c43533157|craigdalton]] considers the evidence too confounded to justify expectations of a huge effect. Aurornis [[./thread#^c43529859|Aurornis]] explicitly supports indoor filtration while rejecting confidence in the dramatic claim. Disagreement over this estimate should not be rewritten as opposition to cleaner air.
- The direct contribution of CO2, versus other accumulated gases or correlated conditions, remains unresolved. amluto [[./thread#^c43530736|amluto]] supplies a submarine-study link while acknowledging limited expertise; jedc [[./thread#^c43530599|jedc]] supplies a contrary operational anecdote. frognumber’s claim that problems arise around 5,000 ppm over hours [[./thread#^c43533821|frognumber]] differs from the 1,000/2,500 ppm cognitive-effect claims in vlovich123’s Claude quotation [[./thread#^c43535030|vlovich123]]. The source does not provide enough experimental detail to reconcile dose, duration, individual sensitivity or co-pollutants.
- Sensor reliability is raised but not tested. frognumber [[./thread#^c43530772|frognumber]] warns that cheap CO2 readings may be estimates derived from VOC sensing, yet does not identify how their own readings were obtained. jwpapi [[./thread#^c43529769|jwpapi]] names Airthings but supplies no model, calibration or reference comparison. The discussion therefore cannot establish the accuracy of the reported concentrations or distinguish a direct CO2 measurement from every possible proxy reading.
- Purifier noise could help or hinder concentration. amai [[./thread#^c43533545|amai]] finds the possibility of distracting fan noise important; brody\_hamer [[./thread#^c43533891|brody\_hamer]] suggests it could mask distracting classmates; joeevans1000 [[./thread#^c43532099|joeevans1000]] confidently attributes the benefit to white noise. No sound measurements or comparison separating acoustic effects from air cleaning adjudicates these positions.
- The classroom hardware is insufficiently identified for reproducing the intervention at home or work. aetherspawn [[./thread#^c43530780|aetherspawn]] asks whether a five-stage unit matches the purifier they are considering; amluto [[./thread#^c43531145|amluto]] identifies carbon filtration, while Aurornis [[./thread#^c43529895|Aurornis]] says the model was unspecified and some schools received carbon filters. tangjurine’s suggestion of broadly similar HEPA performance [[./thread#^c43530858|tangjurine]] is qualified by sokoloff’s reminder about differing grades [[./thread#^c43530913|sokoloff]]. No supplied specification resolves the actual media, airflow or installed performance.
- Carbon-filter effectiveness and exhaustion remain practical unknowns. aitchnyu [[./thread#^c43531471|aitchnyu]] owns pellets, impregnated sheets and a packaged purifier filter but cannot tell when they help or become exhausted. schiffern [[./thread#^c43532638|schiffern]] proposes VOC monitoring and regeneration; amluto [[./thread#^c43536346|amluto]] suggests suppliers and considers IKEA carbon filters plausible inexpensive options. Nobody reports measured removal, breakthrough, service life or regenerated-filter performance.
- The evidence threshold for spending on classroom air remains disputed. im3w1l [[./thread#^c43537762|im3w1l]] requires demonstrated benefits before broad rollout, whereas dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c43538480|dzhiurgis]] argues that children’s right to decent air should not await a large cohort study. When dzhiurgis characterizes classrooms as suffocating, im3w1l [[./thread#^c43543449|im3w1l]] asks whether that concern is about CO2 rather than particles. Both the justification for expenditure and the exposure being targeted need clearer specification.
- Coverage is limited to this supplied story part. PaulKeeble discusses PM2.5 [[./thread#^c43529933|PaulKeeble]], but there are no firsthand numerical PM2.5 or NO2 measurements, and no direct NO2 or wildfire-smoke accounts. Cooking-related material consists mainly of food-odor/VOC remarks from PaulKeeble and dishwasher sensitivity from 7thaccount [[./thread#^c43530203|7thaccount]], without cooking-emission measurements. Sleep has no measured outcome or firsthand improvement account; d--b’s suggestion that children might have been more rested is speculation [[./thread#^c43530901|d--b]].
