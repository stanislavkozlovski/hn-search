# [Futuristic Helmet Has Its Own HEPA Filter and Fan](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=25848412)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion reacts to a powered helmet with HEPA filtration, weighing possible protection and comfort against price, appearance, ventilation, and everyday usability. Participants debate how it compares with masks, PAPRs, and DIY alternatives, including filtration standards and whether masks protect the wearer or others. Practical concerns include heat, moisture, hearing and speech, sneezing, face access, glasses fogging, and contamination while removing the device. Some see potential in high-risk or specialized settings, while others question its market appeal and whether it could replace inexpensive masks.
Tags: PAPR, respirators, HEPA-filtration, masks, COVID-19, product-design, wearability, public-health

## Relevance

Comments discuss a fan-powered filtered helmet as personal protection, including filtration, leaks, comfort, heat buildup, winter use, and the tradeoff between protection and wearability. Some users describe considering or using powered respirator designs, while the thread also questions the product’s filter rating and effectiveness. ## Arguments

- For personal respiratory exposure, yonran [[./thread#^c25849576|yonran]] argues that powered air-purifying respirators (PAPRs) offer better filtration, greater tolerance of leaks, and better comfort than surgical masks or N95s. These are general performance claims, not comparative measurements of the featured helmet. lallysingh [[./thread#^c25849891|lallysingh]] additionally suggests that its filter should capture allergens, without reporting allergy outcomes.
- Personal protection appeals to commenters who cannot control others’ precautions: syntaxing [[./thread#^c25849090|syntaxing]] would accept an unusual appearance to reduce the risk of bringing COVID-19 home to vulnerable family members, while ThrowawayR2 [[./thread#^c25849148|ThrowawayR2]] anticipates better comfort and filtration when commuting by bus. Both describe reasons to consider a purchase, not results from wearing this device.
- Transparent protection could improve communication for people who rely on lipreading, according to m-p-3 [[./thread#^c25848480|m-p-3]]. scotty79 [[./thread#^c25848951|scotty79]] proposes microphones, speakers, and audio processing to address communication difficulties; neither comment establishes that these benefits are realized by the featured product.
- Practical protection depends partly on tolerability and continued use: kube-system [[./thread#^c25849596|kube-system]], replying within the face-scratching discussion, argues that imperfectly used protection can be preferable to protection repeatedly removed or never worn. This is an argument about real-world adherence, without measured exposure or infection outcomes.

## Counterarguments

- Xavdidtheshadow [[./thread#^c25849403|Xavdidtheshadow]] disputes the idea that helmets could broadly replace ordinary masks: masks are cheap, accessible, potentially disposable, and require no charging. Their follow-up [[./thread#^c25850287|Xavdidtheshadow]] emphasizes the value of making protection available to everyone nearby. The argument concerns affordability and widespread use, not proof of equivalent filtration.
- adolph [[./thread#^c25849510|adolph]] predicts that the enclosure would become a greenhouse in summer, and \_abox [[./thread#^c25849462|\_abox]] expects stuffiness even with a fan. These are concerns about heat and comfort, not recorded temperatures, humidity, CO2 concentrations, or firsthand trials of the helmet.
- parliament32 [[./thread#^c25849564|parliament32]] anticipates muffled speech and unpleasant reflections of the wearer’s voice, extrapolating from speaking through retail plexiglass. kube-system [[./thread#^c25849246|kube-system]] also raises fan noise and blocked external sounds. EamonnMR [[./thread#^c25849355|EamonnMR]] explicitly values hearing clearly more than seeing faces clearly, preserving a competing accessibility preference.
- ogre\_codes [[./thread#^c25849479|ogre\_codes]] and falcolas [[./thread#^c25849285|falcolas]] predict unpleasant internal droplets from coughing, sneezing, or talking. The face-access objection receives a concrete qualification from kweks [[./thread#^c25849237|kweks]], who says the hood includes inverted gloves, but \_abox [[./thread#^c25849462|\_abox]] questions how those gloves remain clean when the device is removed. These comments identify maintenance and usability concerns without documenting contamination measurements.

## Assumptions

- The claimed benefits of PAPRs cannot automatically be assigned to this particular consumer helmet. yonran [[./thread#^c25849576|yonran]] explicitly qualifies their endorsement after finding that BioVYZR reportedly uses KN95 filters. Their comment supplies no completed-device filtration or leakage test that would establish the performance implied by the headline.
- maxerickson [[./thread#^c25850439|maxerickson]] proposes airflow resistance as a rough indication that an imported KN95 is better than cloth. This is an unvalidated heuristic in the supplied discussion. It follows ThrowawayR2’s [[./thread#^c25850122|ThrowawayR2]] question about which imported products actually meet the standard; availability alone, asserted by maxerickson [[./thread#^c25849536|maxerickson]], does not resolve that question.
- sgt [[./thread#^c25849154|sgt]] reports choosing a more comfortable biker buff despite believing it substantially less effective, and assumes that keeping greater distance compensates. The comment establishes a personal behavioral tradeoff, not equivalent protection or a measured reduction in inhaled particles.
- The alternative designs discussed are not interchangeable evidence. chongman99 [[./thread#^c25922567|chongman99]] expressly declines to assess the cloth-heavy featured product before describing bunnyPAPR users. Their observations cannot establish BioVYZR’s airflow, temperature, filtration, or comfort.

## Conditions

- For smnrchrds [[./thread#^c25849208|smnrchrds]], the advertised \$199 price is attractive compared with buying a car to avoid public transit. They report encountering absent or improperly worn masks despite a provincial mandate, but working from home limits their need to commute. Their unnamed province is cold, making uncertain fogging performance a possible reason not to buy.
- Costs vary substantially across the proposals: wl [[./thread#^c25849146|wl]] recalls PAPRs costing about \$1,000 early in the pandemic and choosing to leave them for medical and industrial users. yonran [[./thread#^c25849576|yonran]] links a DIY design advertised at \$15 when purchased in quantity. These figures concern different products and circumstances; they do not demonstrate equivalent performance or total ownership costs.
- Geography and airflow direction matter in the maker’s account: chongman99 [[./thread#^c25922567|chongman99]] says bunnyPAPR users did well during a New York City summer, reports airflow of 30–60 liters per minute, and describes air aimed at skin as cooling. They say winter use requires directing air toward the shell instead, and that use is mostly indoors. The airflow figure is reported without a measurement method, calibration, or accompanying pollutant readings.
- Certification and institutional acceptance constrain adoption. In response to jariel’s [[./thread#^c25850035|jariel]] speculation about hospital and airport uses, chongman99 [[./thread#^c25922412|chongman99]] reports that their own projects struggled to attract users, that hospitals required certifications, and that airlines were not supportive. This is experience promoting alternative helmets, not evidence about the featured product’s certification status.

## Lived experiences

- wl [[./thread#^c25849146|wl]] actually shaved their beard to obtain a properly fitting particulate respirator. A hood that accommodated facial hair was a hypothetical alternative; the account does not describe retaining a beard at the expense of protection or testing the advertised helmet.
- foobarian [[./thread#^c25849337|foobarian]] reports severe frustration with glasses fogging and considers heated frames. lhoff [[./thread#^c25849390|lhoff]] says foam tape around the mask’s nose area stopped their fogging, while tynpeddler [[./thread#^c25849515|tynpeddler]] describes pulling a cloth mask beneath their glasses so the glasses hold it down. These are firsthand comfort improvements, without measured fit factors or particle exposure.
- syntaxing [[./thread#^c25849371|syntaxing]] reports slight dizziness when a stronger KN95 or N95 nose bridge pushes their glasses outward. The stated mechanism is altered glasses positioning, not measured CO2 or impaired cognition from air pollution. EamonnMR [[./thread#^c25849379|EamonnMR]] describes using ski goggles before the pandemic to separate lenses from breath while keeping their face warm during a walking commute.
- chongman99 [[./thread#^c25922373|chongman99]] reports that the viral helmets they make have fans roughly as loud as laptop fans: users must speak louder but can still hear. This is a maker’s qualitative account, with no decibel measurements and no direct test of BioVYZR. Their identification as the operator of viralhelmets.com is explicit in chongman99 [[./thread#^c25922557|chongman99]].
- Ductapemaster [[./thread#^c25849485|Ductapemaster]] describes personally using diluted baby shampoo to prevent diving-mask fogging and finding commercial defog no better. They also explain a transfer limit: diving treatments remain wet after rinsing, whereas a dried coating on ordinary glasses would behave differently. Their suggestion about Rain-X is explicitly untried.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Ordinary masks’ wearer protection is disputed. wl [[./thread#^c25849475|wl]] says most masks do not protect the wearer, and Moru [[./thread#^c25849553|Moru]] invokes a Danish test involving 3,000 people to support limited wearer protection while allowing possible source-control benefits. Replying to wl, chongman99 [[./thread#^c25922103|chongman99]] cites a Linsey Marr preprint as evidence of substantial protection, including from bandanas and neck gaiters. These are commenters’ descriptions of research; the supplied material does not establish comparable methods, endpoints, or effect sizes.
- The featured helmet’s advantage over other protection remains unquantified. yonran [[./thread#^c25849576|yonran]] offers general PAPR benefits while flagging its reported KN95 filters, and nunez [[./thread#^c25854927|nunez]] asks whether two masks would be equally effective without receiving an answer in this export. There are no supplied comparative exposure results or firsthand health outcomes establishing the helmet’s superiority.
- Fogging is disputed as a diagnostic of leakage. kps [[./thread#^c25849381|kps]] attributes fogged glasses to leaks around the nose or cheeks; chongman99 [[./thread#^c25922362|chongman99]] replies that water vapor can pass through well-sealed filtration and reports moist exhaust from their helmets. sa1 [[./thread#^c25887033|sa1]] explains anti-fog treatment as producing a thin film instead of droplets. The discussion therefore does not establish that visible moisture measures particle leakage or poor air quality.
- The hygiene tradeoff remains unsettled: Someone1234 [[./thread#^c25849380|Someone1234]] warns that touching the mask or face can cross-contaminate, and TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c25849502|TeMPOraL]] argues for immediately discarded gloves. kube-system [[./thread#^c25849596|kube-system]] instead emphasizes handwashing and the practical value of keeping protection on. No comparative contamination or infection data resolve these competing recommendations.
- Thermal and acoustic concerns are not settled for the featured product. adolph [[./thread#^c25849510|adolph]] predicts overheating and parliament32 [[./thread#^c25849564|parliament32]] predicts unpleasant sound, while chongman99 reports acceptable summer use [[./thread#^c25922567|chongman99]] and manageable fan noise [[./thread#^c25922373|chongman99]] for other designs. Differences in enclosure, airflow, weather, and use setting prevent treating those accounts as a direct rebuttal or confirmation.
- For the approved question, this source unit provides no PM2.5, NO2, or CO2 concentration measurements, air-quality sensor validation, or documented effects on sleep or cognition. It also supplies no cooking, wildfire-smoke, traffic-pollution, room-ventilation, or window-opening comparison. yonran’s [[./thread#^c25849576|yonran]] filtration claims, chongman99’s [[./thread#^c25922567|chongman99]] reported helmet airflow, and \_abox’s [[./thread#^c25849462|\_abox]] anticipated stuffiness cannot establish when outdoor air would improve indoor exposure or make it worse.
