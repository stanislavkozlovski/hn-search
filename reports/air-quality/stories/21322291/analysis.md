# [London Ultra Low Emission Zone cuts toxic air pollution by a third](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=21322291)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

The reported reduction in toxic air pollution under London’s Ultra Low Emission Zone prompts discussion of the evidence, which pollutants were measured, and whether traffic or pollution shifted beyond the zone. Participants consider compliance, effects on commercial fleets and vehicle replacement, and the remaining role of buses, trains, taxis, and black cabs. The thread also explores expanding car restrictions, pedestrianizing streets, accessibility and deliveries, electric vehicles, tire and brake particulates, and the costs of transport infrastructure.
Tags: Ultra Low Emission Zone, London, air pollution, vehicle emissions, public transit, urban planning, electric vehicles

## Relevance

The discussion concerns measured outdoor NOx pollution changes, possible effects of the emissions zone, and concerns about displacement and other pollution sources. It includes firsthand reports of polluted air and exhaust exposure, plus debate about whether the measurements support the headline. PM2.5 is mentioned as a gap in the reported results, so relevance is strongest for NO2/NOx rather than particles. ## Arguments

- The strongest quantitative evidence discussed is reported monitoring, not a commenter's own measurements. tomgp [[./thread#^c21324057|tomgp]] says the underlying report shows a 36\% reduction in NO2 readings, an acceleration of an existing downward trend that its modelling accounts for. They regard the press release as reasonable while acknowledging the report's call for further monitoring to rule out an anomaly.
- Cleaner vehicles could reduce emissions without eliminating journeys. lorenzhs [[./thread#^c21323097|lorenzhs]] reports vehicle compliance rising from 39\% in February 2017 to 61\% in March 2019 and 77\% in September. tialaramex [[./thread#^c21323639|tialaramex]] illustrates how a logistics company could allocate its compliant trucks to London and older trucks elsewhere; this is a hypothetical mechanism, not a documented company case. tialaramex [[./thread#^c21329138|tialaramex]] clarifies that compliant modern diesel trucks qualify, so electrification is not required for that mechanism.
- Health concerns extend beyond unpleasant smells, but the supporting health claims are secondhand. rbradford [[./thread#^c21323013|rbradford]] summarizes a book's discussion of PM0.1 entering the bloodstream and a possible cardiovascular connection. growlist [[./thread#^c21323321|growlist]] invokes estimates of tens of thousands of excess UK deaths associated with diesel promotion. Neither supplies the underlying analysis, and the PM0.1 discussion does not establish a PM2.5-specific effect in these commenters.
- Electrification is defended as a way to reduce exposure where people spend time. Responding to edf13's concern about moving pollution elsewhere [[./thread#^c21328653|edf13]], movedx [[./thread#^c21328711|movedx]] emphasizes the benefit of removing emissions from a densely populated city centre. Brakenshire [[./thread#^c21323590|Brakenshire]] separately argues that replacing combustion vehicles eliminates their local NOx emissions. These arguments concern local exposure benefits; they do not demonstrate that every pollution source disappears.
- Some proposals target traffic volume and proximity directly. jon889 [[./thread#^c21323441|jon889]] advocates restricting cars on many central London backstreets, with timed commercial loading, while dabeeeenster [[./thread#^c21325874|dabeeeenster]] proposes bollards to stop through traffic. These are suggested exposure-reduction measures, without before-and-after pollution measurements in the thread.
- Anti-idling enforcement is presented as an immediately available source-control measure. jeffwass [[./thread#^c21322773|jeffwass]] says TfL requested location, time, registration and ideally a photograph when reporting idling taxis, and describes borough-level restrictions on parked vehicles running their engines. jeffwass [[./thread#^c21322803|jeffwass]] also suggests asking taxi-stand drivers to switch off. The thread provides observations of compliance, but no quantified NO2 or particulate reduction.
- Rail electrification could reduce emissions at crowded terminals. vertex-four [[./thread#^c21322912|vertex-four]] and lorenzhs [[./thread#^c21322886|lorenzhs]] describe electric and bi-mode replacement trains intended to use electric power near London and diesel on unelectrified sections. This offers a mechanism for reducing local exhaust exposure, although neither supplies station-air measurements.

## Counterarguments

- Causal attribution is contested. easytiger [[./thread#^c21322771|easytiger]] [[./thread#^c21322970|easytiger]] argues that private cars were already a small share of central traffic and questions how ULEZ could produce the headline reduction so quickly. Their Euston Road objection concerns congestion-charge geography, which should not be silently treated as proof of ULEZ coverage. lorenzhs [[./thread#^c21323151|lorenzhs]] replies that ULEZ also affects commercial vehicles; easytiger [[./thread#^c21323264|easytiger]] then narrows the objection to whether those businesses would stop making journeys. The fleet-reallocation explanation addresses that narrower objection without requiring fewer deliveries.
- Electric vehicles do not remove every source of particulate pollution, but the thread also corrects an overly simple estimate of their benefit. jlnthws [[./thread#^c21323268|jlnthws]] initially interprets a publication as suggesting roughly half of relevant particles come from exhaust. Brakenshire [[./thread#^c21323590|Brakenshire]] objects that regenerative braking also reduces brake emissions, claiming it eliminates them. jlnthws [[./thread#^c21324265|jlnthws]] accepts that correction and revises their estimate to over 70\% local reduction, while identifying tyres and roads as remaining sources. These are interpretations and estimates, not measured PM2.5 reductions.
- A taxi's zero-emission capability does not mean continuous zero-emission operation. In reply to Angostura's statement that new taxis must be zero-emission [[./thread#^c21322617|Angostura]], mattlondon [[./thread#^c21322746|mattlondon]] identifies them as hybrids with petrol range extenders. cjrp [[./thread#^c21322972|cjrp]] gives approximately 80 miles of battery range and another roughly 300 using the extender. Those specifications alone cannot establish the exhaust exposure encountered by pedestrians or cyclists.
- Fuel economy and a modern-diesel label are challenged as evidence of cleanliness. MisterTea [[./thread#^c21322897|MisterTea]] [[./thread#^c21323465|MisterTea]] praises a friend's approximately 2009 diesel Passat for economy and driving experience. telchar [[./thread#^c21326205|telchar]] and mprovost [[./thread#^c21323552|mprovost]] point to Volkswagen's emissions-test cheating, while Brakenshire [[./thread#^c21323615|Brakenshire]] claims diesels of that vintage emitted 5–10 times regulatory limits in real conditions. These replies challenge certification-based confidence; the thread supplies neither measurements of that particular car nor enough evidence to assign the same multiplier to every diesel.
- A cleaner zone might displace exposure. lordnacho [[./thread#^c21322772|lordnacho]] asks whether pollution merely moved outside ULEZ; colinhowe [[./thread#^c21322818|colinhowe]] quotes the report as finding no increase around its boundary during the first six months. That quoted finding addresses the immediate boundary concern, but does not establish what happened at every destination of displaced vehicles. tialaramex's illustrative reassignment of an older truck to Derby [[./thread#^c21323639|tialaramex]] makes the distinction between local improvement and wider redistribution explicit.
- Replacing large delivery trucks with numerous smaller vehicles could have adverse tradeoffs. WhompingWindows [[./thread#^c21323712|WhompingWindows]] objects to large trucks serving narrow streets, but TylerE [[./thread#^c21325845|TylerE]] raises the costs of extra warehouses, vehicles and drivers, and chrisseaton [[./thread#^c21328641|chrisseaton]] questions whether total pollution or congestion would improve. stuaxo [[./thread#^c21342915|stuaxo]] suggests electric vans and reports seeing cargo delivery bikes in London [[./thread#^c21342931|stuaxo]], without a comparative emissions calculation.
- The Underground discussion corrects one proposed explanation rather than confirming it. tonyedgecombe [[./thread#^c21328654|tonyedgecombe]] repeats hearsay that Northern line dirt came from historic steam trains; pmyteh [[./thread#^c21329287|pmyteh]] replies that the Northern and other deep Tube lines have always been electric, unlike the Metropolitan and District railways. Together with mattlondon's account of Tube-associated black mucus [[./thread#^c21323284|mattlondon]], this leaves a dust-exposure concern that cannot simply be explained as residual steam exhaust.

## Assumptions

- Pollutants and metrics must remain separate. tomgp's reported reduction concerns NO2 readings [[./thread#^c21324057|tomgp]]; alex\_duf [[./thread#^c21324314|alex\_duf]] explicitly asks why PM10 and PM2.5 are missing. jlnthws's particle-source percentages [[./thread#^c21323268|jlnthws]] and rbradford's discussion of PM0.1 [[./thread#^c21323013|rbradford]] cannot substitute for measured PM2.5 concentrations, personal dose or a demonstrated health improvement.
- Smell, visible smoke and bodily sensations are exposure clues, not calibrated pollution measurements. bananapear [[./thread#^c21326508|bananapear]] explicitly distinguishes perceptibility from pollution levels, noting that NO2 and brake microparticles are not as readily noticed as cigarette smoke. Accordingly, the stronger fumes Havoc notices while jogging [[./thread#^c21322542|Havoc]] do not identify a pollutant, concentration or causal health effect.
- Benefits attributed to compliance assume that standards translate into cleaner real-world operation and that controls remain effective. tialaramex [[./thread#^c21329138|tialaramex]] notes that compliant diesel trucks remain permitted, while lorenzhs [[./thread#^c21323010|lorenzhs]] quotes the report as saying all TfL buses within the zone meet its standards. Neither statement means those vehicles emit nothing. jlnthws [[./thread#^c21323268|jlnthws]] additionally asserts high particle-removal efficiency but weaker low-speed performance, without supplying urban measurements.
- The proposed explanation for changing diesel symptoms is explicitly uncertain. onlingyding [[./thread#^c21325455|onlingyding]] repeats something they have heard: newer diesel designs might trade lower particulate emissions for higher NOx and finer particles. growlist [[./thread#^c21326086|growlist]] connects this discussion to personally experiencing immediate lung tightness, but also suggests ageing as an alternative explanation. That reply does not validate the proposed combustion mechanism.
- Observed hybrid engine use and explanations for it have different evidentiary status. londons\_explore [[./thread#^c21322627|londons\_explore]] reports rarely seeing the taxis operate on electricity alone, then attributes this to heating, charging downtime and battery preservation. Their further explanation involving braking certification and engine-speed choices [[./thread#^c21322810|londons\_explore]] is a technical claim, not a measured operating study. stuaxo's expectation that hybrids should run their engines more efficiently [[./thread#^c21342997|stuaxo]] is likewise an inference.

## Conditions

- The evidence is concentrated in central London during the initial 2019 ULEZ period. jeffwass [[./thread#^c21322795|jeffwass]] describes expansion to the North and South Circular roads as planned for October 2021, while aries1980 [[./thread#^c21338300|aries1980]] objects that residential streets and Tower Bridge remain outside the then-current zone. Benefits within that geography cannot establish improvements throughout London, and these comments do not verify what subsequently happened.
- Financial incentives depend on vehicle class, exemptions and available alternatives. lorenzhs [[./thread#^c21323151|lorenzhs]] reports a £100 daily charge for noncompliant lorries, buses and coaches, and says only 9\% of all vehicles were exempt, with exemptions including taxis and residents at that time. tialaramex's fleet-allocation example [[./thread#^c21323639|tialaramex]] assumes a business already owns enough compliant vehicles to rearrange its deliveries.
- Taxi replacement was described as a transition rather than an immediate removal of diesel exposure. tim333 [[./thread#^c21322491|tim333]] quotes the January 2018 requirement for new taxis to have zero-emission capability. andylynch [[./thread#^c21322646|andylynch]] describes reducing older taxis' maximum age from 15 to 12 years and payments of up to £10,000 to delicense vehicles. tialaramex [[./thread#^c21323859|tialaramex]] clarifies that the age-limit change is gradual, correcting the concern that roughly 20\% of the fleet would become obsolete overnight.
- Maintenance costs and enforcement can undermine vehicle-emissions controls. skocznymroczny [[./thread#^c21322859|skocznymroczny]] says filter removal is common in Poland because replacement is expensive and owners believe removal improves fuel economy. gambiting [[./thread#^c21322899|gambiting]] contrasts an asserted £5,000 UK garage penalty with weak penalties and enforcement in Poland. These are geographically specific claims without prevalence or emissions measurements.
- Traffic restrictions must accommodate people who cannot readily substitute walking or ordinary public transport. rtkwe [[./thread#^c21325987|rtkwe]] highlights limited mobility, while learnstats2 [[./thread#^c21327558|learnstats2]] proposes exceptional vehicle access and routes usable by powerchairs. In the taxi-ban branch, sails [[./thread#^c21323772|sails]] cites Dial-a-Ride as an alternative, but stordoff [[./thread#^c21328920|stordoff]] says it does not help visitors. stuaxo [[./thread#^c21342939|stuaxo]] describes using black cabs with a toddler in an unfolded buggy. These accounts establish practical access concerns without resolving the safest or cleanest overall transport arrangement.
- Street layout limits the applicability of blanket proposals. Responding to calls for more anti-rat-running bollards, barrkel [[./thread#^c21369056|barrkel]] draws on ten years of motorcycle commuting to say many London side roads already feed drivers back through one-way loops, while rivers, reservoirs and canals restrict alternatives to major-road bridges. This challenges the proposal's assumed opportunity in those areas, not the possibility of benefit on every street.
- Rail improvements face capacity and operational constraints. vertex-four [[./thread#^c21322912|vertex-four]] argues that attaching electric locomotives outside London would disrupt already busy services and lack suitable waiting locations. makomk [[./thread#^c21323752|makomk]] raises bi-mode complexity and repeats a rumour of cooling problems above roughly 30°C. Conversely, regular passenger youngtaff [[./thread#^c21325121|youngtaff]] reports only a slight power-switching delay at Swindon and otherwise satisfactory Cheltenham–Paddington journeys. That experience does not directly test the temperature rumour.

## Lived experiences

- Havoc [[./thread#^c21322542|Havoc]] says they live farther outside London with better air and found fumes much more noticeable when jogging in the city. growlist [[./thread#^c21322832|growlist]] singles out cycling on Marylebone Road as particularly unpleasant. These are firsthand or experiential descriptions of outdoor comfort, without concurrent readings or evidence separating higher exposure from greater awareness during exercise.
- growlist [[./thread#^c21326086|growlist]] reports that breathing diesel exhaust now makes their lungs immediately tighten, unlike earlier in life. They explicitly acknowledge that ageing might explain the change. This is a specific respiratory symptom account, but supplies no diagnosis, exposure measurement or controlled comparison of old and new diesel exhaust.
- flyGuyOnTheSly [[./thread#^c21326620|flyGuyOnTheSly]] describes increasingly avoiding rush hour and feeling 'off' after standing near a running car for a few minutes. They distinguish this sensation from being sick and wonder why they did not notice it previously. Their city, pollutant levels and medical explanation are unspecified; the account supports perceived discomfort and avoidance behaviour.
- epanchin [[./thread#^c21325564|epanchin]] recalls walking alongside Euston and noticing bad air and heat from continuously running diesel engines. aries1980 [[./thread#^c21338359|aries1980]] similarly describes repeatedly seeing idling multi-engine trains at crowded stations and worries about concentrated exposure. These observations identify proximity to a plausible source and a thermal-comfort effect, but do not quantify NO2 or particulate concentrations.
- mattlondon [[./thread#^c21322746|mattlondon]] describes roughly 20–30 diesel taxis idling or creeping forward immediately outside major station entrances such as King's Cross and St Pancras. The account highlights a concentrated source where pedestrians gather; it is an observed vehicle count and behaviour description, not an air-quality measurement.
- jeffwass [[./thread#^c21322773|jeffwass]] says they contacted TfL after regularly seeing taxis idle outside their workplace and subsequently observed fewer idling taxis. They report sometimes hostile responses when asking ordinary drivers to switch off. In a separate reply about taxi stands, jeffwass [[./thread#^c21322803|jeffwass]] says most taxi drivers comply without argument. These concern different driver contexts and do not establish either a universal response or a measured effect of the complaint.
- willvarfar [[./thread#^c21323122|willvarfar]] recalls regularly having black nasal mucus while working in central London in the 1990s; batmenace [[./thread#^c21323155|batmenace]] describes it as usually dark grey. mattlondon [[./thread#^c21323284|mattlondon]] associates the phenomenon mainly with the Tube and reports improvement over the previous eight to ten years, tentatively attributing it to stronger cleaning. These are bodily observations and an uncertain explanation, without particle identification or clinical interpretation.
- As a cyclist, londons\_explore [[./thread#^c21322627|londons\_explore]] says the number of times they observe newer hybrid taxis operating on electricity alone is 'near zero.' This challenges the practical significance of advertised electric capability from a nearby road user's perspective. It provides no systematic sample, verified operating-mode log or exhaust measurement.
- rwmj [[./thread#^c21333389|rwmj]] offers a clearly secondhand example: a flower-selling friend replaced an old delivery van through a monthly hire-to-buy arrangement for an electric van, with avoided ULEZ charges and petrol costs reportedly making the change cost-neutral. This illustrates a possible business improvement under that person's costs, not a universal affordability result or a measured pollution reduction.
- zeristor [[./thread#^c21322749|zeristor]] recalls seeing plumes from fires while travelling into Liverpool Street and suspects their aggregate contribution could be substantial. The sightings are personal observations; the citywide contribution is speculation. The comment does not identify the fires as wildfires or measure smoke exposure.
- tim333 [[./thread#^c21322409|tim333]] identifies as a ULEZ resident and welcomes the scheme, while chillydawg [[./thread#^c21322410|chillydawg]] describes an office on polluted Euston Road and appreciates the regulation. safgasCVS [[./thread#^c21322447|safgasCVS]] still considers London's air awful. Chicago resident thinkpad20 [[./thread#^c21329047|thinkpad20]] expresses hope that their own city's air could improve. These establish individual evaluations and concerns, not measured personal improvement or population-wide agreement.

## Unresolved disagreements

- How much of the observed NO2 decline was caused by ULEZ remains disputed within the supplied discussion. tomgp [[./thread#^c21324057|tomgp]] considers the reported modelling persuasive; easytiger [[./thread#^c21323605|easytiger]] maintains that plausible future vehicle replacement does not prove the headline claim. bobbydroptables [[./thread#^c21322955|bobbydroptables]] points to the supporting report, but the supplied comments do not reproduce its raw readings, absolute concentrations, monitoring-site details or modelling sufficiently to independently settle attribution.
- PM2.5 and PM10 outcomes remain a major evidence gap. alex\_duf [[./thread#^c21324314|alex\_duf]] explicitly asks why they are not mentioned, with no supplied reply resolving the omission. jlnthws's revised estimate of electric-vehicle particle reduction [[./thread#^c21324265|jlnthws]] cannot establish the actual change in ambient PM2.5, its composition or residents' exposure.
- Sensor reliability is not evaluated. tito [[./thread#^c21325804|tito]] recommends Purple Air's distributed-sensor map, and dylan604 [[./thread#^c21327410|dylan604]] imagines street-mounted sensors with visual displays. Neither provides personal measurements, calibration checks, reference-instrument comparisons, placement details or evidence about accuracy for PM2.5, NO2 or CO2. These comments identify monitoring ideas rather than validate them.
- The real-world emissions of newer taxis remain unsettled. stuaxo [[./thread#^c21342997|stuaxo]] directly asks how the hybrids compare with older models; londons\_explore [[./thread#^c21322627|londons\_explore]] reports frequent engine operation and proposes reasons for it. The supplied discussion contains no measured electric-driving share or comparative NOx and particulate results, so neither optimistic efficiency reasoning nor the cyclist's observations quantify the exposure benefit.
- Short, concentrated smoke encounters are not reconciled with ambient pollution summaries. bananapear [[./thread#^c21326508|bananapear]] asks how repeatedly walking through cigarette-smoke clouds affects effective toxicity and whether ordinary pollution measures capture it. No supplied answer quantifies that exposure or establishes the commenter's assumption that cigarette smoke contributes little to typical measurements.
- The source breakdown behind the quoted claim that vehicles produce around half of London's harmful NOx is unresolved. dredmorbius [[./thread#^c21323380|dredmorbius]] proposes high-temperature combustion, machinery and industrial processes, while tarjei [[./thread#^c21322813|tarjei]] refers to construction-machinery findings from Oslo. Claims that few diesel trains remain are challenged by leoedin's list of additional London terminals [[./thread#^c21322837|leoedin]]. None supplies a complete London inventory, and evidence from Oslo cannot establish London's proportions.
- The quoted annual pollution cost of up to £3.7 billion lacks an explanation in the supplied replies. WatchDog [[./thread#^c21323194|WatchDog]] asks where it comes from; overcast [[./thread#^c21323274|overcast]] guesses maintenance, cleaning and healthcare. That guess cannot establish either the accounting method or the health burden underlying the figure.
- The unit does not establish indoor CO2 effects, cognition or sleep outcomes, cooking exposure, or the performance of domestic ventilation and filtration. dredmorbius [[./thread#^c21323380|dredmorbius]] mentions heating and cooking emissions in a general source discussion, without indoor measurements; growlist [[./thread#^c21322846|growlist]] merely asks about unspecified active filters. Those comments cannot support conclusions about indoor safety or effective household interventions.
- There is no supported comparison of opening versus closing windows, including during wildfire smoke. epanchin's station-side experience [[./thread#^c21325564|epanchin]] and flyGuyOnTheSly's discomfort near idling cars [[./thread#^c21326620|flyGuyOnTheSly]] show why nearby outdoor sources deserve attention, but provide no paired indoor/outdoor readings or ventilation outcomes. zeristor's unidentified fire-plume sightings [[./thread#^c21322749|zeristor]] are not a wildfire-mitigation account. This source therefore cannot determine when window opening would improve or worsen a particular person's exposure.
