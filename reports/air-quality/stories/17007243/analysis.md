## Description

Participants debate the significance of rising atmospheric carbon dioxide, contrasting recent levels with much higher concentrations in Earth's distant past. The discussion focuses on the rate of change, effects on people and ecosystems, risks to civilization, climate mitigation and adaptation, nuclear and renewable energy, carbon pricing, and proposals to capture or reuse CO2.

## Relevance

The main discussion concerns global climate and atmospheric CO2, but a side discussion explicitly raises indoor CO2 concentrations, cognitive effects, and the limits of inferring indoor risk from ancient atmospheric levels. That overlap is relevant, though narrow. [[./thread#^c17007699|graeme]] [[./thread#^c17007749|Pilfer]] [[./thread#^c17010358|xupybd]] [[./thread#^c17012183|zaarn]]

## Arguments

- graeme [[./thread#^c17007699|graeme]], replying to Pilfer’s reassurance about ancient life thriving at elevated CO2 [[./thread#^c17007657|Pilfer]], argues that 2,000 ppm is already well above the threshold for negative cognitive effects. This is an uncited cognition claim; no study, exposure duration, cognitive test, or firsthand observation is supplied.
- graeme [[./thread#^c17007699|graeme]] also imagines indoor concentrations reaching 3,000–4,000 ppm against a hypothetical outdoor baseline of 2,000 ppm. This raises concern about additional indoor accumulation, but the figures are speculative estimates.

## Counterarguments

- In a separate branch, Pilfer [[./thread#^c17007749|Pilfer]] argues that humans could survive 1,000–2,000 ppm CO2 and says poorly ventilated warehouses or offices can reach those concentrations today. Pilfer also characterizes approximately 10,000 ppm as hazardous but nonfatal. These assertions lack supporting exposure evidence and duration information; the endpoint discussed is survival, leaving cognition and comfort unaddressed.

## Assumptions

- Pilfer [[./thread#^c17007749|Pilfer]] explicitly assumes that pharrington’s hypothetical inability to survive ancient Earth [[./thread#^c17007655|pharrington]] concerns CO2. The parent mentions environmental and ecosystem change more broadly, so the response addresses an assumed exposure mechanism.
- Pilfer [[./thread#^c17008271|Pilfer]] treats ancient life surviving at 1,000–2,000 ppm as reassurance about human survival. That analogy does not establish tolerability for present-day humans or demonstrate preserved cognition, health, sleep, or comfort.
- graeme’s hypothetical indoor range [[./thread#^c17007699|graeme]] assumes substantial accumulation above outdoor CO2, without specifying occupancy, ventilation rates, building characteristics, or a calculation supporting the projected difference.
