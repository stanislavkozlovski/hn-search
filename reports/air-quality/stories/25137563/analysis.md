# [How to better ventilate your home](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=25137563)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion explores practical ways to ventilate homes while balancing indoor air quality, comfort, energy use, climate, and outdoor pollution. Participants compare brief cross-ventilation, continuous exhaust fans, whole-house fans, and heat- or energy-recovery ventilators, sharing installation experiences and constraints in older or tightly sealed homes. CO₂ monitoring prompts a detailed exchange about sensor calibration, accuracy, and consumer products. Other topics include humidity, thermal mass, insulation, soundproofing, and whether ventilation systems are worthwhile across different climates and building types.
Tags: home ventilation, CO₂ monitoring, ERV, HRV, energy efficiency, indoor air

## Relevance

Comments cover home ventilation strategies, CO2 buildup, monitor calibration and sensor reliability, and tradeoffs among window airing, exhaust fans, and HRV/ERV systems. Firsthand accounts describe comfort, cold-weather limitations, wildfire filtration, noise, and energy recovery. ## Arguments

- Ventilation can change indoor CO2: mherdeg [[./thread#^c25138680|mherdeg]] reports measuring CO2 and watching it equilibrate after opening a window. This is firsthand evidence of a response to ventilation, but the comment supplies no concentrations, sensor specifications, or measurement timeline.
- DoofusOfDeath [[./thread#^c25138485|DoofusOfDeath]] argues that accurate CO2 monitoring matters for cognition and ventilation decisions, citing unspecified medical literature about differences between 800 and 1,200 ppm. Those concentrations illustrate a hypothetical disagreement between meters; they are not reported measurements or personally observed cognitive effects.
- ThePadawan [[./thread#^c25137966|ThePadawan]] describes five-minute, twice-daily wide-open airing as a practice promoted to prevent mold. rsync [[./thread#^c25139117|rsync]] believes ventilation is an underrated disease-prevention measure. These provide reasons participants favor ventilation, without measured mold or infection outcomes.
- doctorhandshake [[./thread#^c25138037|doctorhandshake]] describes HRVs and ERVs as supplying outdoor air while recovering thermal energy, with ERVs also retaining humidity. compumike [[./thread#^c25138496|compumike]] argues that this permits tighter, better-insulated construction while reducing indoor CO2 and humidity. These are explanations of the approach, rather than results from a monitored installation.
- kyledrake [[./thread#^c25139125|kyledrake]] proposes adapting an existing bathroom exhaust fan to provide scheduled ventilation, using their interpretation of ASHRAE 62.2 to determine a target flow and operating time. The practical argument is that an existing exhaust route can provide regular exchange without installing an entirely new system.
- throwaway9d0291 [[./thread#^c25147892|throwaway9d0291]] recommends choosing a documented NDIR CO2 sensor and checking whether an IAQ product actually measures CO2 or instead estimates it from VOC readings. They identify the Sensirion SCD30 as supporting recalibration at an arbitrary concentration and describe an ESP32/ESPHome implementation option.
- ChuckMcM [[./thread#^c25140027|ChuckMcM]] proposes checking meters across multiple concentrations using controlled nitrogen/CO2 mixtures. GrumpyYoungMan [[./thread#^c25143359|GrumpyYoungMan]] replies that premixed calibration gas is available and cites annual CO2 calibration for a professional Fluke meter. Neither describes performing these tests in this discussion.
- kyledrake [[./thread#^c25140250|kyledrake]] suggests a box fan with a MERV-13 filter as a relatively inexpensive filtration option, while distinguishing filtering indoor air from exchanging it with outdoor air. This is a proposed improvement, without reported particle measurements or testing of that configuration.

## Counterarguments

- More outdoor-air exchange can worsen exposure when the incoming air contains smoke. blacksmith\_tb [[./thread#^c25139956|blacksmith\_tb]] reports that wildfire conditions overwhelmed what their HRV's MERV-13 filters could handle, leading them to reduce ventilation to its lowest speed and use air purifiers. This limits an unconditional preference for maximum fresh-air intake.
- In response to shostack's question about using a central HVAC fan, kyledrake [[./thread#^c25140250|kyledrake]] argues that recirculation mainly redistributes indoor air and may dilute local concentrations without establishing outdoor-air exchange. westurner [[./thread#^c25140544|westurner]] identifies possible Nest scheduling features, but scheduling alone does not demonstrate that the system introduces outdoor air.
- Cooler outdoor air does not settle the efficiency question when humidity differs. fiddlerwoaroof [[./thread#^c25138928|fiddlerwoaroof]] expects opening windows to help cooling when outside temperatures are lower; nbutyllithium [[./thread#^c25145399|nbutyllithium]], answering smegcicle's humidity concern [[./thread#^c25139858|smegcicle]], explains that condensation consumes cooling capacity. No measured comparison establishes the net effect in the homes discussed.
- compumike's expectation that HRV/ERV installation generally requires new construction or a substantial retrofit [[./thread#^c25138496|compumike]] is challenged by amluto [[./thread#^c25139447|amluto]], who names paired ductless Lunos units and single-room Zehnder units. This establishes a claimed alternative installation approach, without a firsthand evaluation of its cost or performance.
- nickff [[./thread#^c25142641|nickff]] argues that calibration with CO2 alone would not resolve differences caused by other gases, asserting cross-sensitivity in consumer infrared sensors. This is a technical claim without supporting measurements in the thread; it should not be treated as an established explanation for errors in every NDIR device.
- Exhaust hardware and routing can undermine a simple ventilation recommendation. ballenf [[./thread#^c25139977|ballenf]] warns about dusty, noisy, inefficient bathroom fans and attic discharge. Answering war1025's suggestion that a drafty attic should suffice [[./thread#^c25140176|war1025]], tapvt [[./thread#^c25142402|tapvt]] argues that moisture can condense before escaping and contribute to mold.
- voisin [[./thread#^c25145286|voisin]] questions dependence on airtight construction plus mechanical ventilation, worrying about breakdowns, inadequate specifications, and changing uses. They prefer thermal mass, radiant heating, and ordinary leakage. This is a proposed alternative and reliability concern, rather than a documented failure of a Passive House.
- Ventilation is not presented uniformly as sufficient protection against infection. kyledrake [[./thread#^c25139125|kyledrake]] distinguishes their general-air-quality/VOC project from COVID-specific ventilation and cautions against treating increased airflow as permission to invite people over. This qualifies rsync's disease-prevention enthusiasm [[./thread#^c25139117|rsync]] without establishing either household's infection risk.

## Assumptions

- Calibration depends on a valid reference. DoofusOfDeath [[./thread#^c25138485|DoofusOfDeath]] describes outdoor air being assumed to contain roughly 400 ppm CO2. throwaway9d0291 [[./thread#^c25147892|throwaway9d0291]] explains that automatic routines can incorrectly assume daily exposure to fresh air in homes that remain closed; their preferred routine instead identifies repeated low-reading periods over two weeks.
- Brief window airing assumes enough airflow and consistent participation. olau [[./thread#^c25142887|olau]], responding to a question about still weather, says wind usually helps in Northern Europe and that temperature differences may also drive exchange. Their warning about the discipline required makes a twice-daily routine a behavioral assumption, not an automatic guarantee of continuous air quality.
- Claims of little cooling during short airing depend on stored warmth in the building and its contents. ThePadawan [[./thread#^c25138583|ThePadawan]] invokes thermal mass, and LeifCarrotson [[./thread#^c25140416|LeifCarrotson]] emphasizes radiant temperature in perceived comfort. ThePadawan's advice to leave heating unchanged during a five-minute airing [[./thread#^c25141811|ThePadawan]] additionally assumes slow heater response.
- Descriptions of stale air do not establish oxygen depletion. ThePadawan [[./thread#^c25138165|ThePadawan]] uses that explanation for classrooms, while marton78 [[./thread#^c25140857|marton78]] describes smell and a suffocating sensation on entering an occupied room. jkepler [[./thread#^c25147068|jkepler]] supplies a historical Charlotte Mason quotation about oxygen and children's health. None supplies oxygen measurements or demonstrates the proposed mechanism.
- A calculated exhaust schedule assumes that the installed fan delivers the intended flow and that replacement air actually enters. kyledrake [[./thread#^c25139125|kyledrake]] reports configuring approximately 12 minutes of operation per hour, but gives no measured delivered airflow or achieved air-change rate. turtlebits' broader claim that US houses are generally leaky [[./thread#^c25139471|turtlebits]] is not a measurement of any particular home.

## Conditions

- Outdoor temperature substantially changes the window-opening tradeoff. rsync [[./thread#^c25139117|rsync]] finds 20–30 minutes of winter airing easy in California; kyledrake [[./thread#^c25139125|kyledrake]] finds routine window opening impractical in Minneapolis winters. bluGill [[./thread#^c25138122|bluGill]] likewise describes months below freezing. timw4mail's explanation involving Japanese localized heating [[./thread#^c25138461|timw4mail]] remains an unmeasured geographic comparison.
- Humidity makes outdoor-air cooling seasonal. post\_break [[./thread#^c25139909|post\_break]] warns that Houston airing can introduce moisture and promote mold, while johnrgrace [[./thread#^c25141409|johnrgrace]] reports enjoying whole-house fans in Houston and New Orleans at suitable times of year. EricE [[./thread#^c25161077|EricE]] finds a Mid-Atlantic installation most useful in spring and fall, with summer usefulness depending on humidity.
- The ERV prices reported in this 2020 discussion vary with equipment and installation scope. dota\_fanatic [[./thread#^c25139093|dota\_fanatic]] found a several-thousand-dollar American Standard installation unaffordable during an addition. turtlebits [[./thread#^c25139349|turtlebits]] uses an approximately \$400 Panasonic unit in a 500-square-foot workshop and mentions a larger model around \$1,000. johnrgrace [[./thread#^c25141366|johnrgrace]] reports seeing units below \$1,000 and local vent-installation work below \$100; these are not comparable complete-system quotations.
- Energy economics depend on climate and existing leakage. tzs [[./thread#^c25146289|tzs]] summarizes a linked report as finding net HRV energy savings only in US climate zones 4–8, with decades to break even in zone 4. turtlebits [[./thread#^c25139488|turtlebits]] warns that continuous ERV operation in older houses can raise heating bills and recommends checking ACH50 with a blower-door test. Both are claims about applicability, without household energy measurements here.
- Available construction access affects retrofit feasibility. gwd [[./thread#^c25140130|gwd]] could run ducts because a major renovation exposed ceilings and a bathroom route, and reports resulting attic clutter. compumike [[./thread#^c25138496|compumike]] wishes for a renter-friendly window-box HRV, whereas amluto [[./thread#^c25139447|amluto]] identifies through-wall alternatives that still require wall penetrations.
- Window geometry and local wind affect the proposed airing practice. ThePadawan [[./thread#^c25147484|ThePadawan]] contrasts fully opening hinged windows with double-hung windows exposing at most roughly half their area. olau [[./thread#^c25142887|olau]] recalls wind being available more than 80\% of the time in Northern Europe, but explicitly presents this as an approximate recollection rather than supplied meteorological evidence.

## Lived experiences

- DoofusOfDeath [[./thread#^c25138485|DoofusOfDeath]] began considering CO2 after soundproofing made their home office nearly airtight. Their practical tradeoff is between stronger ventilation and a quieter workspace. They do not report a verified elevated concentration, symptoms, or an actual side-by-side meter test.
- mherdeg [[./thread#^c25138680|mherdeg]] reports monitoring indoor CO2 and observing equilibration after opening a window, motivating plans for a fresh-air ventilator. Separately, they describe summer occasions with approximately 50°F, dry outdoor air and 78°F indoors against a preferred 72°F, when their available solution was an open window and box fan.
- throwaway9d0291 [[./thread#^c25147892|throwaway9d0291]] says Sensirion's automatic calibration approach works well at home in their experience. This is an owner assessment without a published reference comparison, error estimate, or time series.
- Window airing appears in several personal accounts: ThePadawan [[./thread#^c25137966|ThePadawan]] reports signing Swiss apartment instructions intended to prevent mold; subpixel [[./thread#^c25138088|subpixel]] recalls a German host mother urgently opening bedroom windows; csunbird [[./thread#^c25138415|csunbird]] was taught morning airing in Turkey; and marton78 [[./thread#^c25140857|marton78]] describes the practice and stale-air discomfort in Hungary. These accounts establish habits and perceptions, not population-wide practice or measured health benefits.
- ChuckMcM [[./thread#^c25139938|ChuckMcM]] describes moving fresh air as producing comfort instead of vague tension. After spending a few thousand dollars sealing and insulating their home, they use cool summer nights and warmer winter days for air exchange, then close the house to retain the desired temperature. They acknowledge a possible conflict with minimizing household COVID transmission.
- heffer [[./thread#^c25139064|heffer]] reports feeling cold at 21.5°C in a Canadian forced-air-heated house, unlike their experience with radiant heating in Germany. They attribute the difference to warmer objects retaining heat during airing, but their account does not isolate heating type from insulation, thermostat placement, or other building differences.
- war1025 [[./thread#^c25140290|war1025]] reports that insulating areas near the thermostat allowed more distant rooms to become colder before heating activated. In the subsequent exchange, war1025 [[./thread#^c25145527|war1025]] describes large improvements after sealing and insulating previously exposed areas, then clarifies that the missing insulation was in exterior-wall sections between floors [[./thread#^c25150380|war1025]]. This clarification matters: they were not recommending insulation between interior rooms. skoskie [[./thread#^c25145277|skoskie]] separately reports feeling outdoor gusts from the middle of their home.
- gwd [[./thread#^c25140130|gwd]] is happy with a retrofitted ventilation system and notices a difference when it is switched off, but also reports a constant background hum and substantial ductwork. The perceived improvement is not accompanied by pollutant or health measurements.
- blacksmith\_tb [[./thread#^c25139956|blacksmith\_tb]] reports eight years of generally satisfactory use of a Zehnder HRV. Wildfires led them to run it at its slowest speed and add air purifiers because its MERV-13 filters were insufficient in those conditions. No smoke concentration, PM2.5 measurement, or quantified purifier benefit is supplied.
- kyledrake [[./thread#^c25139125|kyledrake]] adapted an existing bathroom fan to run about 12 minutes each hour. They also describe a furnace humidifier controlled by an Ecobee as a major winter quality-of-life improvement, while warning that humidity should fall with outdoor temperature to avoid moisture problems. No before-and-after CO2, VOC, or humidity readings are given.
- fatnoah [[./thread#^c25138072|fatnoah]] describes a whole-house fan producing an immediate breeze and estimates roughly 6–8 air changes per hour. They later report the house approaching outdoor temperature in about ten minutes when outside air was cooler [[./thread#^c25150877|fatnoah]], and typical timed runs of 10–20 minutes [[./thread#^c25162327|fatnoah]]. These are owner-reported estimates without a stated measurement method. war1025 [[./thread#^c25140201|war1025]] reports a more modest, improvised box fan in an attic access helping somewhat.
- EricE [[./thread#^c25161077|EricE]] reports a whole-house fan installation taking a few hours, with an initially misdirected fan disturbing attic insulation. They report approximately 640 W for the fan versus 2,400 W for each air conditioner and believe one favorable fall repaid the installation. At night, full-speed operation could make the house too cold, while switching it off allowed stored heat to warm the interior again; they wanted variable speed.
- jonheller [[./thread#^c25138646|jonheller]] sometimes runs air conditioning with windows open when outside air is around 65°F and indoors around 75°F. This is an actual operating practice and reported temperature difference, without energy-use or pollutant measurements establishing its benefits.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The meter-accuracy problem remains unresolved by testing. DoofusOfDeath [[./thread#^c25140111|DoofusOfDeath]] wants independent laboratory evaluation; throwaway9d0291 [[./thread#^c25147892|throwaway9d0291]] offers better-specified sensors and reference-based calibration; nickff [[./thread#^c25142641|nickff]] raises cross-gas sensitivity. The thread contains no reference comparison establishing accuracy across indoor concentrations, distinguishing these possible error mechanisms, or verifying the hypothetical 800-versus-1,200-ppm discrepancy.
- The magnitude and cause of cognitive or health benefits are not established here. DoofusOfDeath [[./thread#^c25138485|DoofusOfDeath]] invokes unspecified medical literature, olau [[./thread#^c25142887|olau]] asserts that elevated CO2 harms the brain, and rsync [[./thread#^c25139117|rsync]] believes ventilation prevents disease. None reports a controlled comparison or a measured personal change in cognition, illness, or sleep.
- Whether five-minute airing provides enough exchange remains open. shostack [[./thread#^c25139752|shostack]] explicitly asks for research, and BitwiseFool [[./thread#^c25139834|BitwiseFool]] asks about windless conditions. olau [[./thread#^c25142887|olau]] supplies a qualitative explanation involving wind and temperature differences, but no measured clearance curve or evidence that a particular schedule maintains acceptable concentrations between airings.
- The explanation for different winter comfort experiences is disputed. heffer [[./thread#^c25139064|heffer]] attributes their Germany–Canada contrast to radiant versus forced-air heating; CyberDildonics [[./thread#^c25139695|CyberDildonics]] suggests thermostat placement or insulation instead. war1025's account of changing temperature distribution after insulation work [[./thread#^c25140290|war1025]] makes the alternative plausible, but does not resolve heffer's particular case.
- The best balance between airtightness, mechanical ventilation, and uncontrolled leakage is unsettled. voisin [[./thread#^c25145286|voisin]] raises reliability and future-sizing concerns without receiving a supplied answer. gwd's successful but intrusive retrofit [[./thread#^c25140130|gwd]] and amluto's smaller through-wall alternatives [[./thread#^c25139447|amluto]] show differing implementation possibilities, without comparative long-term reliability or exposure data.
- Energy and financial returns are not demonstrated across approaches. tzs [[./thread#^c25146289|tzs]] questions HRVs where heat recovery does not save energy, while jonstewart [[./thread#^c25138438|jonstewart]] values continuous fresh air itself. EricE's reported whole-house-fan payback [[./thread#^c25161077|EricE]] is household-specific. fomine3 [[./thread#^c25146007|fomine3]] relays a manufacturer's claim favoring continuous AC operation, but that does not resolve the window-opening comparison without humidity and energy measurements.
- This unit cannot provide a pollutant-by-pollutant exposure comparison. blacksmith\_tb's wildfire account [[./thread#^c25139956|blacksmith\_tb]] has no PM2.5 values, and mherdeg's CO2 observations [[./thread#^c25138680|mherdeg]] have no concentrations or simultaneous smoke readings to establish an optimal ventilation compromise. There are no NO2 measurements or traffic-exposure accounts. kyledrake [[./thread#^c25140250|kyledrake]] mentions a stove vent as an exhaust route, without discussing measured cooking emissions. EricE's nighttime temperature complaint [[./thread#^c25161077|EricE]] concerns comfort and does not establish a sleep effect.
