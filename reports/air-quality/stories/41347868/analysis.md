# [Study: Air purifier use at daycare centres cut kids' sick days by a third (2023)](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41347868)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants consider reported reductions in children’s sick days after air purifier use at daycare centers, questioning the study design, purifier details, and whether ventilation or other factors explain the result. The discussion ranges across HEPA and DIY filters, clean-air delivery rates, ERV/HRV systems, filtration of outdoor air, and UV treatment, including concerns about maintenance, cost, noise, and safety. A long thread challenges the idea that childhood illness is needed to strengthen immunity and distinguishes pathogen exposure from symptomatic infection, with discussion of vaccines, immune effects, and the limits of the hygiene hypothesis. Other topics include masks, daycare illness burdens, school conditions, and an anecdote about suspected radioactive contamination in a home.
Tags: air purifiers, daycare, ventilation, infectious disease, immune system, indoor air quality

## Relevance

The discussion addresses purifier effectiveness, ventilation, CO2 and cognition, indoor and outdoor pollution tradeoffs, and firsthand experiences with filtration and ventilation systems. It also raises limits of the study and uncertainty about the measured health effects. ## Arguments

- PaulKeeble [[./thread#^c41349045|PaulKeeble]] claims earlier school and hospital studies found HEPA filtration reduced infections by as much as two-thirds. thinkcontext [[./thread#^c41348299|thinkcontext]] argues that, if the daycare result holds, benefits could extend to parents’ illness, caregiving absences, and seasonal transmission. These are reported research claims and anticipated spillovers, not outcomes independently established by this export.
- rockinghigh [[./thread#^c41349847|rockinghigh]] supplies a specific response to the CO2-causality question: a quoted study reports increased viral stability at 800 ppm and roughly ten times as much infectious virus remaining after 40 minutes at 3,000 ppm versus clean air. The proposed mechanism is CO2 reducing droplet alkalinity and slowing viral inactivation. This concerns experimental viral survival, rather than a directly measured increase in household infections.
- retrochameleon [[./thread#^c41350806|retrochameleon]] argues that clean-air delivery rate matters more than the HEPA label alone. schiffern [[./thread#^c41351158|schiffern]] recommends targeting 6+ air changes per hour for respiratory pathogens and warns that advertised coverage often assumes much less. PaulKeeble [[./thread#^c41349066|PaulKeeble]] presents inexpensive Corsi–Rosenthal boxes as a practical way to obtain substantial filtered airflow; these recommendations are not accompanied by comparative test results in the supplied text.
- amluto [[./thread#^c41352692|amluto]] argues for combining ventilation with appropriate filtration: ventilation can remove internally generated contaminants while its incoming air introduces outdoor particles. The commenter distinguishes repeated recirculation through MERV 13 filters from single-pass outdoor-air treatment, recommending much stronger intake filtration in polluted or smoky conditions. bradlys [[./thread#^c41358704|bradlys]] adds that standalone units make it easier to increase cleaning capacity in individual rooms.
- rajnathani [[./thread#^c41354748|rajnathani]] advocates ventilation partly on the basis of a linked claim that elevated CO2 reduces cognitive performance. stevenwoo [[./thread#^c41348444|stevenwoo]] points to educational benefits following classroom filter installation even where outdoor air reportedly was not particularly poor. Neither comment supplies firsthand cognitive testing or enough study detail to quantify the benefit or identify the responsible pollutant.
- \_dain\_ [[./thread#^c41348396|\_dain\_]] argues that immune-system training cannot protect against traffic, industrial, or forest-fire pollution. alliao [[./thread#^c41351569|alliao]] similarly emphasizes tyre and brake dust and other introduced contaminants. Separately, actuallyalys [[./thread#^c41348486|actuallyalys]] and jph00 [[./thread#^c41349741|jph00]] distinguish potentially beneficial microbial exposure from harmful airborne infections, challenging the premise that cleaner air necessarily deprives children of something beneficial.
- swagasaurus-rex [[./thread#^c41348242|swagasaurus-rex]] presents UV treatment as another possible means of reducing airborne infection. PaulKeeble [[./thread#^c41349147|PaulKeeble]] advocates well-fitted N95/FFP2 masks as an additional protective layer. These comments support multiple interventions, but their strong efficacy claims do not come with directly reproduced comparative evidence.

## Counterarguments

- photochemsyn [[./thread#^c41348409|photochemsyn]] questions the daycare result’s statistical significance and possible ventilation confounding, quoting 43 children in two intervention units against 607 elsewhere in Helsinki. thrance [[./thread#^c41348349|thrance]] requests sample-size, variant, and p-value information. Retric [[./thread#^c41348542|Retric]] counters that a large effect can compensate for methodological weaknesses, but the supplied discussion does not contain the analysis needed to settle significance or attribution.
- readthenotes1 [[./thread#^c41348621|readthenotes1]] fears that reduced infection could weaken immunity and cause later harm. jimberlage [[./thread#^c41351484|jimberlage]] supports reducing illness in workplaces and hospitals but questions whether daycare children benefit from more minor infections. nradov [[./thread#^c41350671|nradov]] specifically argues that numerous common respiratory viruses will cause exposure elsewhere, distinguishing this position from opposition to vaccination. These are hypotheses about later outcomes, not demonstrated consequences of filtration.
- icelancer [[./thread#^c41349834|icelancer]] describes CO2 as merely a proxy for ventilation when discussing illness, while LordKeren [[./thread#^c41350508|LordKeren]] says they have not seen evidence that it causes disease. These parallel replies preserve skepticism about causal interpretation; rockinghigh’s experimental evidence [[./thread#^c41349847|rockinghigh]] complicates a proxy-only account without establishing how much CO2 itself changes real-world infection risk.
- retrochameleon [[./thread#^c41350806|retrochameleon]] challenges purifier marketing after returning a Medify unit whose advertised room coverage appeared inconsistent with its airflow. schiffern [[./thread#^c41351158|schiffern]] argues that maximum-speed specifications overstate useful everyday performance when that setting is too noisy to tolerate. Filter efficiency and advertised floor area therefore cannot, by themselves, establish effective exposure reduction.
- rajnathani [[./thread#^c41354748|rajnathani]] cites a study suggesting purifiers could increase airborne endotoxins and prefers ventilation partly for that reason. plasticchris [[./thread#^c41348947|plasticchris]] worries that neglected filters could become mold reservoirs. These are a linked research claim and a maintenance concern respectively; the export does not establish their frequency, magnitude, or applicability to every purifier.
- schiffern [[./thread#^c41349658|schiffern]] challenges the idea that a single arbitrary UV bulb will disinfect rapidly moving air: wavelength, intensity, and exposure time matter. bri3d [[./thread#^c41350160|bri3d]] corrects the blanket 1,000-hour replacement claim, distinguishing short-lived 222 nm lamps from 253.7 nm mercury lamps reportedly lasting about 10,000 hours in water treatment. schiffern [[./thread#^c41350243|schiffern]] accepts that correction while retaining the maintenance objection.
- tgsovlerkhgsel [[./thread#^c41349893|tgsovlerkhgsel]] argues that masks impose greater comfort and social-interaction burdens than room filtration, alongside recurring supply costs. roywiggins [[./thread#^c41349122|roywiggins]] and eleveriven [[./thread#^c41356928|eleveriven]] question consistent, effective mask use by daycare-age children. These practical objections concern sustained use and fit, rather than showing that filtration and masks cannot complement one another.

## Assumptions

- For story 41347868, part 0, the evidence is much stronger on general filtration experiences than on pollutant-specific exposure. amluto [[./thread#^c41352692|amluto]] reports particle-counter observations without numerical concentrations or particle-size results; elric [[./thread#^c41349500|elric]] discusses fine exhaust soot without measuring it. The unit supplies no numerical PM2.5 or NO2 concentrations, and its cooking account from amanaplanacanal [[./thread#^c41349717|amanaplanacanal]] does not identify fuel, appliance, or extraction arrangements.
- The analysis must distinguish evidence types: rockinghigh [[./thread#^c41349847|rockinghigh]] reproduces an experimental-study passage; elric [[./thread#^c41349500|elric]] describes personal symptom improvement; ceejayoz [[./thread#^c41350524|ceejayoz]] reports a household infection outcome. These support different conclusions. None makes the others a controlled demonstration, and the linked articles cannot supply additional findings beyond text actually reproduced here.
- The lower-dose argument assumes filtration can reduce symptomatic illness while leaving enough exposure for immune learning. DistractionRect [[./thread#^c41349023|DistractionRect]] explicitly presents this as a suspicion and notes uncertainty over whether fewer sick days mean fewer infections, milder infections, or both. taeric [[./thread#^c41348259|taeric]] raises continued asymptomatic carriage, while analog31 [[./thread#^c41351542|analog31]] asks for quantified exposure and benefit.
- Purification mechanisms should remain distinct. mdp2021 [[./thread#^c41350255|mdp2021]] describes their device as forced-air HEPA filtration rather than an air disinfectant; sbochins [[./thread#^c41350418|sbochins]] distinguishes VOC handling between products; photochemsyn [[./thread#^c41348409|photochemsyn]] raises CO2 accumulation despite particle cleaning. A favorable result for one device or contaminant cannot establish removal of gases, particles, and infectious agents alike.
- Coverage and performance advice assumes particular operating conditions. schiffern [[./thread#^c41351158|schiffern]] describes area ratings derived from airflow, assumed room height, and low air-change targets. amluto [[./thread#^c41352692|amluto]] distinguishes single-pass intake efficiency from repeated recirculation. Applying either recommendation requires knowing actual fan speed, room volume, pollutant entry, and whether air passes through the filter once or repeatedly.

## Conditions

- Opening windows helps only when the incoming air and weather make the exchange worthwhile. throw7 [[./thread#^c41350169|throw7]] favors outside air, while dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c41352403|dzhiurgis]] objects that heat, cold, or pollution often prevents this; their 80\%+ estimate is unsupported here. kjkjadksj [[./thread#^c41348808|kjkjadksj]] qualifies enthusiasm for outdoor California daycare by proximity to major roads, freeways, airports, and ports. thelastparadise [[./thread#^c41349620|thelastparadise]] additionally identifies diesel particles, cannabis smoke, and wildfire emissions as possible outdoor exposures.
- Operating cost can reverse an inexpensive purchase decision. Rebelgecko [[./thread#^c41353013|Rebelgecko]] reports calculating that a \$150 commercial purifier would repay its extra cost within months compared with their box-fan option under expensive California electricity. alliao [[./thread#^c41351619|alliao]] instead emphasizes Corsi–Rosenthal airflow and noise advantages over a particular premium purifier. The export supplies neither the wattages nor tariffs needed to generalize that comparison.
- Space and installation constraints matter. amluto [[./thread#^c41349049|amluto]] recommends thick, high-area filters and says a suitable holder can cost under \$200 but be difficult to retrofit, especially with ductless equipment. elric [[./thread#^c41362496|elric]] says their small home cannot accommodate the suggested additional filter. dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c41352205|dzhiurgis]] reports a NZD 1,500 DIY HRV installation requiring accessible roof space and more than half a day of work.
- Heat recovery does not eliminate the comfort and energy costs of ventilation. turtlebits [[./thread#^c41353819|turtlebits]] limits operation because additional ventilation introduces unwanted hot or cool air despite heat recovery. elric [[./thread#^c41354476|elric]] identifies competing concerns: sensor location can delay detection and response, and continuous operation may help prevent mold in a difficult-to-clean exchanger. The appropriate schedule depends on the building and operating conditions described.
- Institutional maintenance is a constraint even where purchase funding exists. plasticchris [[./thread#^c41348947|plasticchris]] worries about neglected filters in poorer US schools; erikvanoosten [[./thread#^c41355932|erikvanoosten]] recalls a school in affluent Haarlem where filters allegedly went unchanged for eight years. switch007 [[./thread#^c41348748|switch007]] clarifies that their enthusiasm for a Finnish rollout concerned affordability, not dispensing with consideration of health evidence.
- The radon branch is geographically and financially specific. LinuxBender [[./thread#^c41396196|LinuxBender]] describes an unnamed uranium-rich state and suspects older concrete, without confirming the source. ComputerGuru [[./thread#^c41348368|ComputerGuru]] says mitigation can be inexpensive and later suggests a US university extension investigation [[./thread#^c41348447|ComputerGuru]]. Vecr [[./thread#^c41348468|Vecr]] argues that professional risk assessment could be economical compared with demolition and rebuilding.

## Lived experiences

- elric [[./thread#^c41349500|elric]] reports installing a Zehnder ComfoAir Q with heat and moisture recovery, approximately 100 m³/hour airflow, and an F7 filter described as MERV 13. They experienced improved asthma, less sneezing, and almost no mosquitoes entering. These are firsthand symptom and comfort observations, without accompanying particle concentrations or a controlled comparison.
- amluto [[./thread#^c41352692|amluto]] reports using an actual particle counter and finding that a high-end ERV ventilated well but filtered outdoor pollution poorly. Adding a nominal 24×24×12-inch HEPA filter with a MERV 8 prefilter reportedly reduced supply particles to effectively zero with negligible added resistance. No counts, instrument details, detection limit, or pressure measurements are supplied; the expected multi-year filter life is a forecast.
- turtlebits [[./thread#^c41349754|turtlebits]] says their ERV operates roughly one-third of the time and recommends separate recirculating filtration. They describe CO2/VOC readings as sufficiently low and give a minimum airflow of 35 CFM, approximately 60 m³/hour [[./thread#^c41353819|turtlebits]], but provide no concentrations. dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c41352205|dzhiurgis]] separately reports a warmer home after HRV installation, occasional neighbor-fireplace odor, and continued mosquito entry.
- ceejayoz [[./thread#^c41350524|ceejayoz]] reports running several Corsi–Rosenthal boxes, improved allergies, and no additional household cases when one person had COVID. This cannot isolate the boxes’ contribution to transmission prevention. amanaplanacanal [[./thread#^c41349717|amanaplanacanal]] uses one box on each floor to address dust, pets, and cooking particles, but reports no measured reduction or health outcome.
- sbochins [[./thread#^c41350418|sbochins]] reports satisfaction with Austin Air purifiers while making broader claims about VOC removal that are not supported by personal measurements in the comment. abracadaniel [[./thread#^c41352429|abracadaniel]] bought a recommended REME Halo but explicitly cannot tell whether it helps. djmips [[./thread#^c41350314|djmips]] reports early box-fan failures during continuous operation and warns that failing bearings can aerosolize grease.
- LinuxBender [[./thread#^c41348297|LinuxBender]] reports two AirThings devices showing extremely high radon indications and an alpha detector reading 640 counts per minute about two inches from purifier filters. They later report alpha spikes in the attic and filters while three gamma/beta/X-ray instruments remained near background [[./thread#^c41356539|LinuxBender]]. These are instrument observations, not a quantified inhaled dose or a confirmed explanation of the contamination.
- Daycare-related illness accounts conflict substantially. wredue [[./thread#^c41348872|wredue]] recalls frequent illness and inadequate sick leave, while their separate claim of parents being sick 90\% of the time is attributed to unspecified studies. MikeRichardson [[./thread#^c41356289|MikeRichardson]] recalls a nurse mother who was not sick even 10\% of the time despite daycare and patient-care exposure. tuna74 [[./thread#^c41349770|tuna74]] reports children missing only 1–2 days annually while the parent felt frequently ill during the first year.
- BenoitP [[./thread#^c41348404|BenoitP]] describes repeated winter illness after Thursday–Friday daycare, disrupted nights, and two months planned around resting; this is an indirect sleep burden from illness, not a measured sleep effect of air quality. switch007 [[./thread#^c41348779|switch007]] reports worsening health after repeated COVID infections. Both accounts challenge treating infections as inconsequential, but neither tests an air-treatment intervention.
- encoderer [[./thread#^c41348489|encoderer]] reports that fully outdoor daycare in California experienced only brief COVID shutdowns, while acknowledging weather limitations; shutdown duration is not an infection-rate measurement. viraptor [[./thread#^c41352058|viraptor]] recalls after-hours UV treatment in an early-1990s Eastern European surgery and not noticing similar use in later healthcare settings across countries, without reporting efficacy measurements.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The daycare intervention’s precise effect remains unsettled within the export. photochemsyn [[./thread#^c41348409|photochemsyn]] quotes two intervention units and raises ventilation confounding; vhcr [[./thread#^c41348868|vhcr]] refers to four kindergartens and assumes the comparison addresses circulating variants. duxup [[./thread#^c41348703|duxup]] and DrNosferatu [[./thread#^c41356014|DrNosferatu]] ask which purifiers were used. The supplied text does not resolve device specifications, allocation, comparable ventilation, statistical uncertainty, or the frequency-versus-severity interpretation raised by DistractionRect [[./thread#^c41349023|DistractionRect]].
- The CO2 exchange partially resolves one question: lolinder [[./thread#^c41349792|lolinder]] asks for an experiment separating CO2 from poor ventilation and accepts rockinghigh’s response [[./thread#^c41350007|lolinder]]. It leaves open the size of that mechanism’s contribution in occupied rooms, useful intervention thresholds, and independently attributable cognitive effects. rajnathani [[./thread#^c41354748|rajnathani]] asserts cognitive impairment through a linked study, but no firsthand cognitive or sleep measurements are supplied.
- The classroom-learning mechanism remains uncertain. amluto [[./thread#^c41349400|amluto]] notes that filters in the study cited by stevenwoo [[./thread#^c41348444|stevenwoo]] were installed following a gas leak and criticizes the failure to distinguish particulate reduction from organic-gas reduction. The suggestion that the devices therefore contained carbon is itself an inference. The discussion cannot assign the reported educational benefit specifically to PM2.5, NO2, CO2, or VOCs.
- Sensor reliability and intervention magnitude remain incompletely documented. amluto [[./thread#^c41352692|amluto]] provides qualitative particle-counter results without calibration, size channels, or numerical before-and-after readings. turtlebits [[./thread#^c41353819|turtlebits]] supplies no CO2/VOC instrument details or concentrations, and elric [[./thread#^c41354476|elric]] raises placement and response-delay concerns. Consequently, effectively zero particles and sufficiently low gases cannot be translated into standardized exposure levels from this source.
- Whether filtration prevents lifetime illness or mainly delays it remains disputed. nradov [[./thread#^c41348617|nradov]] proposes shifting infections earlier and later emphasizes common-virus ubiquity [[./thread#^c41350671|nradov]]. coryrc [[./thread#^c41349038|coryrc]] contests a fixed illness total using contrasting family experiences, while analog31 [[./thread#^c41351621|analog31]] says beneficial exposure has not been quantified. The one-third sick-day claim does not answer lifetime immunity or later-infection questions.
- UV safety and effectiveness are unresolved for the proposed installations. amluto [[./thread#^c41348773|amluto]] worries about spectral leakage, dubious vendors, expense, and material damage. elric [[./thread#^c41349433|elric]] estimates approximately half a second of duct exposure rather than measuring delivered UV dose; thedougd [[./thread#^c41353878|thedougd]] requests independent testing of irradiation on captured particles. PaulKeeble’s qualified claim that 222 nm is not known to harm people [[./thread#^c41349102|PaulKeeble]] does not establish long-term safety for every device or installation.
- The radioactive-air source remains unidentified. LinuxBender [[./thread#^c41348403|LinuxBender]] suspects transport through the chimney and attic. rini17 [[./thread#^c41348752|rini17]] proposes accumulated radon decay products and later cites lead-210’s 22-year half-life [[./thread#^c41357980|rini17]]; amluto [[./thread#^c41348931|amluto]] disputes the accumulation explanation and suggests other possible legacy sources. ComputerGuru [[./thread#^c41348447|ComputerGuru]] urges investigation before rebuilding. Filter-associated radiation alone does not settle these competing explanations.
- Mask-related developmental tradeoffs remain disputed, without becoming evidence against room filtration. dumbo-octopus [[./thread#^c41349637|dumbo-octopus]] relies on conversations with rehabilitation personnel; maeil [[./thread#^c41349352|maeil]] reports no comparable concern in a highly masked country and requests research; terminatornet [[./thread#^c41350078|terminatornet]] offers a linked rebuttal and separately raises long-COVID symptoms affecting learning [[./thread#^c41364528|terminatornet]]. The reproduced material cannot quantify either developmental harm from masking or the competing benefit of preventing infection.
