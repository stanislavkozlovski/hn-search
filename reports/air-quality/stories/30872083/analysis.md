# [Fine Particulate Matter Exposure from Secondhand Cannabis Bong Smoking](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=30872083)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion examines a study measuring fine particulate exposure during indoor cannabis bong sessions, including how representative the experiment is and whether its findings can be compared with tobacco smoke, cooking emissions, or other particles. Participants debate secondhand exposure and the health evidence, then compare smoking with dry-herb vaping and edibles, including dosing, product labeling, and harm reduction.
Tags: cannabis smoke, secondhand smoke, particulate matter, indoor air quality, vaping, harm reduction

## Relevance

Directly discusses indoor PM2.5 from secondhand cannabis smoke, including the study's small and loosely controlled setting, uncertainty about particle toxicity by source, and reports of smoke entering neighboring homes. The discussion also compares smoking with non-smoking alternatives. ## Arguments

- throwawayboise [[./thread#^c30872286|throwawayboise]] questions why either firsthand or secondhand cannabis smoke should be presumed harmless when tobacco smoke is considered harmful. Their qualification is exposure frequency: cannabis and cigarette users may inhale different cumulative amounts. This is a qualitative risk argument, not a measured comparison of health outcomes.
- The discussion describes real-world PM2.5 sampling: ichugwindex [[./thread#^c30885211|ichugwindex]] quotes measurements before, during, and after eight cannabis social-smoking sessions in one 20 m² living room. et-al [[./thread#^c30872654|et-al]] quotes reporting that participants supplied their own cannabis and bongs and smoked without observers. SauciestGNU [[./thread#^c30874159|SauciestGNU]] argues that this natural setting could improve realism despite weak experimental control.
- h2odragon [[./thread#^c30872335|h2odragon]] recommends indoor filtration wherever smoke is present and identifies cooking as another airborne-particle source. kn0where [[./thread#^c30880468|kn0where]] similarly says kitchen monitors show large PM2.5 increases during cooking. Neither supplies a personal measurement series, concentrations, or a tested filtration result.
- vmception [[./thread#^c30872723|vmception]] advocates edibles and reports personally using gummies without smoking. lbotos [[./thread#^c30872336|lbotos]] also presents edibles as a way to reduce lung exposure. Their practical proposal removes the user's smoke-producing activity, although the thread supplies no before-and-after indoor-air measurements.
- whoopdeepoo [[./thread#^c30872752|whoopdeepoo]] proposes dry-herb vaping as another alternative. whimsicalism [[./thread#^c30872845|whimsicalism]] claims it produces substantially less tar and fewer carcinogens than smoking while explicitly acknowledging limited longitudinal research and rejecting an assumption of complete safety.
- Mountain\_Skies [[./thread#^c30872533|Mountain\_Skies]] argues that multifamily units and shared spaces exchange odors because they are not hermetically sealed. This supports treating neighboring smoke as an involuntary indoor-exposure problem; a resident's own decision not to smoke does not necessarily control their home environment.

## Counterarguments

- ichugwindex [[./thread#^c30885211|ichugwindex]] challenges the study's interpretation because the amount smoked and comparisons with normal tobacco use or incense are unclear. eyelidlessness [[./thread#^c30873494|eyelidlessness]] likewise regards prolonged social bong sessions as a real but potentially extreme exposure pattern. These objections concern representativeness and dose comparison, rather than demonstrating that the smoke is safe.
- kn0where [[./thread#^c30880468|kn0where]] questions whether equal PM2.5 levels imply equal danger across cooking, wildfire smoke, diesel exhaust, and cannabis. They also invoke unspecified statistics suggesting no significant cannabis-associated lung-cancer increase after accounting for tobacco. That is an unverified claim about users' epidemiology, not a reported result establishing the safety of secondhand bong smoke.
- twofornone [[./thread#^c30872671|twofornone]] suggests cigarette processing, additives, and possible radioactive contaminants could make tobacco and cannabis smoke differ. Maursault [[./thread#^c30881429|Maursault]] makes a stronger claim that intentionally added carcinogens explain much of commercial cigarettes' danger. Neither comment supplies comparative exposure or outcome data establishing these explanations or the safety of unprocessed plant smoke.
- epgui [[./thread#^c30872970|epgui]] disputes the suggestion that vaping avoids particulates, claiming it changes the particle-size distribution toward finer particles with different tissue effects. However, the subsequent exchange establishes that their linked evidence principally concerns liquid vaping, limiting its application to the original dry-herb suggestion.
- Avoiding smoke does not settle the overall safety or practicality of a substitute: 01100011 [[./thread#^c30872885|01100011]] reports severe distress after an edible dosing error, celdon25 [[./thread#^c30872757|celdon25]] reports hospitalization, and quickthrowman [[./thread#^c30873126|quickthrowman]] reports intoxication lasting into the next morning. These are tradeoffs of changing consumption route, not evidence of harm from ambient air.
- porbelm [[./thread#^c30872409|porbelm]] suggests mixing cannabis with tobacco could reduce nicotine intake and smoking frequency; gmfawcett [[./thread#^c30872668|gmfawcett]] answers with a sarcastic alcohol comparison. No particle measurements accompany the proposal, so reducing nicotine cannot be treated here as demonstrated reduction in inhaled particulate exposure.

## Assumptions

- Simple tobacco–cannabis comparisons depend on comparable cumulative exposure. throwawayboise [[./thread#^c30872286|throwawayboise]] assumes many cannabis users smoke less frequently than cigarette users, while amanaplanacanal [[./thread#^c30872876|amanaplanacanal]] suggests lower smoke volume but longer breath-holding. These are proposed behavioral differences, not quantified exposure adjustments.
- The vaping discussion relies on an explicitly uncertain extrapolation: after whimsicalism [[./thread#^c30873565|whimsicalism]] identifies the liquid-vaping focus of the supplied links, epgui [[./thread#^c30873661|epgui]] confirms it and expects similar results for dry herbs only conditionally on temperature, formulation, and absence of combustion. They also disclaim specific expertise and close tracking of the research.
- The claim that hot inhalation is especially damaging rests, in this exchange, on epgui's recollection of a pathology class and reasoning from first principles [[./thread#^c30873094|epgui]], following a source request from whimsicalism [[./thread#^c30872984|whimsicalism]]. Jeff\_Brown's suggestion that bong water or hookah tubing cools smoke [[./thread#^c30873085|Jeff\_Brown]] does not establish particle removal or a health benefit.
- Recommendations to manage edible risks through smaller portions and tolerance presume reliable information about the contents. pempem [[./thread#^c30873135|pempem]] emphasizes information and lower doses, while vmception [[./thread#^c30873807|vmception]] assumes use within tolerance; celdon25's later clarification [[./thread#^c30875567|celdon25]] instead alleges a major labeling error. mountainriver's proposed 1:1 CBD mitigation [[./thread#^c30880289|mountainriver]] is also unsupported by outcome data in the supplied discussion.
- Observable residue and apparent health are weak substitutes for exposure or outcome measurements. h2odragon [[./thread#^c30872335|h2odragon]] describes cannabis residue as easier to clean than tobacco stains, while mountainriver [[./thread#^c30880318|mountainriver]] says acquaintances who use edibles seem healthy. Neither observation establishes comparative particle toxicity, long-term safety, or a measured indoor-air improvement.

## Conditions

- The reported study concerns a particular off-campus apartment living room and student-selected cannabis and bongs, according to et-al [[./thread#^c30872654|et-al]], with eight sessions in one 20 m² room according to ichugwindex [[./thread#^c30885211|ichugwindex]]. This local setting limits extrapolation to other homes, room sizes, occupancy patterns, and smoking practices.
- Multifamily housing creates exposure constraints that differ from controlling one's own room. Mountain\_Skies [[./thread#^c30872533|Mountain\_Skies]] describes transfer into neighboring units and shared spaces. mschuster91 [[./thread#^c30872491|mschuster91]] proposes centralized air conditioning or toilet vents without backflow prevention as possible routes, but those building features were not established for the resident reporting the problem.
- Window advice depends on surrounding sources. Jeff\_Brown [[./thread#^c30873118|Jeff\_Brown]] suggests opening windows in response to indoor tobacco smoke; xeromal [[./thread#^c30872711|xeromal]] reports a neighbor's balcony smoke entering despite a closed door, and 8bitsrule [[./thread#^c30872463|8bitsrule]] describes smoking outdoors amid passing vehicle exhaust. Together these accounts make outdoor-air cleanliness relevant, but they do not measure whether opening a particular window improves or worsens exposure.
- Legal and social setting may affect ventilation behavior. Jeff\_Brown [[./thread#^c30873118|Jeff\_Brown]] suggests concealment encourages enclosed cannabis smoking, while eyelidlessness [[./thread#^c30873494|eyelidlessness]] reports fewer prolonged group sessions in more permissive environments and as acquaintances age. These are contextual explanations and anecdotes, not demonstrated geographic or population-wide patterns.
- The edible-substitution debate depends partly on California's changing supply system. celdon25 [[./thread#^c30873650|celdon25]] places the adverse experience in California; vmception [[./thread#^c30873715|vmception]] distinguishes 2014 from the later recreational framework. latchkey [[./thread#^c30875751|latchkey]] points to subsequently available testing, licenses, and traceable businesses. The exchange supplies no comparative failure rates establishing how much product reliability improved.

## Lived experiences

- ttyp3 [[./thread#^c30872373|ttyp3]] reports neighbors smoking roughly every two hours from morning to night, with smoke entering their living space in different buildings. They describe being unable to prevent it. This is a firsthand account of recurrent unwanted exposure, without pollutant readings or a reported medical outcome.
- xeromal [[./thread#^c30872711|xeromal]] reports smelling cannabis whenever a neighbor smokes on the balcony even with their own door closed. They find it annoying but tolerate it because they like the neighbor and believe alternatives are limited. This establishes a comfort complaint and perceived infiltration, not a measured PM2.5 dose.
- 8bitsrule [[./thread#^c30872463|8bitsrule]] says they always smoke outdoors, where the smoke mixes with gasoline and diesel exhaust. This documents their source-location choice; it does not demonstrate that outdoor smoking eliminates exposure to others or establish the relative risks of smoke and traffic emissions.
- Jeff\_Brown [[./thread#^c30873172|Jeff\_Brown]] recalls exhaling through paper-towel tubes filled with toilet paper in college and calls the method highly effective, without specifying a measured endpoint. h2odragon [[./thread#^c30872335|h2odragon]] reports easier removal of cannabis residue with diluted ethanol than tobacco staining. Neither anecdote measures airborne-particle removal.
- SauciestGNU [[./thread#^c30874246|SauciestGNU]] reports that their doctor strongly advised dry-herb rather than liquid cannabis vaping and explicitly limits this to one account. thomastjeffery [[./thread#^c30878428|thomastjeffery]] speculates that past cartridge-related lung injuries explain the advice, but does not establish the doctor's reasoning or provide a firsthand comparative health result.
- eyelidlessness [[./thread#^c30873494|eyelidlessness]] discloses past employment serving the legal cannabis industry, little personal consumption, and extensive social familiarity with users. They report that prolonged group smoking tends to decline with age and permissive settings and that sustained exposure of nonusers is uncommon in their experience. They explicitly describe this as anecdotal.
- celdon25 [[./thread#^c30872757|celdon25]] reports a 2014 hospitalization involving potassium depletion, loss of muscle control, and arrhythmia after an edible and sleeping through 90-degree heat. They later clarify that a product labeled 30 mg allegedly contained at least several hundred milligrams [[./thread#^c30875567|celdon25]]. The actual dose is not independently measured in the export, and this account concerns edible consumption rather than polluted-air exposure.
- 01100011 [[./thread#^c30872885|01100011]] reports nearly calling emergency services after an edible calculation error; when asked for the dose, they say they do not know and describe a pounding heartbeat and being unable to move in bed [[./thread#^c30874444|01100011]]. quickthrowman [[./thread#^c30873126|quickthrowman]] reports avoiding edibles after 5 PM on weekdays because they can remain intoxicated the next morning. These illustrate distress and duration tradeoffs when replacing smoking.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The export does not provide the numerical PM2.5 results, instrument model, calibration, or sensor-validation information needed to assess measurement magnitude or reliability. et-al [[./thread#^c30872654|et-al]] supplies reported study circumstances, and ichugwindex [[./thread#^c30885211|ichugwindex]] supplies the sampling outline while questioning dose comparability. SauciestGNU [[./thread#^c30874159|SauciestGNU]] sees ecological realism as a strength, whereas ichugwindex sees inadequate control and comparison as weaknesses.
- Whether equal PM2.5 mass from cannabis, cooking, wildfire smoke, and diesel exhaust implies comparable health risk remains unanswered. kn0where [[./thread#^c30880468|kn0where]] raises the distinction but provides no source-specific toxicity measurements. The discussion cannot establish a numerical health-risk ranking among these sources.
- Secondhand-smoke carcinogenicity is directly contested: 8bitsrule [[./thread#^c30872463|8bitsrule]] recalls a Lancet finding of immeasurably low carcinogenicity and questions cardiovascular confounding, while mechanical\_bear [[./thread#^c30879491|mechanical\_bear]] asserts that secondhand smoke causes cancer and links a CDC document. The supplied text does not reconcile their sources, populations, exposure definitions, or applicability specifically to cannabis.
- The magnitude of benefit from dry-herb vaping remains unsettled. emerged [[./thread#^c30872553|emerged]] believes the reduction exceeds 'slightly,' particularly at lower temperatures; lbotos [[./thread#^c30872597|lbotos]] agrees but awaits studies, and denkmoon [[./thread#^c30872651|denkmoon]] laments limited research. epgui's acknowledged reliance on liquid-vaping evidence [[./thread#^c30873661|epgui]] does not resolve that comparison. vmception's separate claim that hookahs are '100 times worse' [[./thread#^c30873466|vmception]] also lacks a defined exposure basis or supporting results in the export.
- The effectiveness of proposed air controls is unquantified: h2odragon [[./thread#^c30872335|h2odragon]] recommends filtration, Jeff\_Brown [[./thread#^c30873172|Jeff\_Brown]] endorses an improvised exhalation filter, and Jeff\_Brown [[./thread#^c30873118|Jeff\_Brown]] discusses opening windows. No supplied comment measures removal efficiency, room concentrations after intervention, or a ventilation–outdoor-smoke tradeoff.
- Accounts differ about how exceptional sustained involuntary cannabis-smoke exposure is. eyelidlessness [[./thread#^c30873494|eyelidlessness]] finds prolonged nonuser exposure uncommon in their social experience, while ttyp3 [[./thread#^c30872373|ttyp3]] describes frequent intrusion across multiple residences. Party attendance and neighboring-unit exposure are different contexts; neither account establishes prevalence.
- The edible debate leaves both product reliability and causation disputed. celdon25 [[./thread#^c30875567|celdon25]] insists mislabeling, rather than failure to know tolerance, caused the excessive dose, while latchkey [[./thread#^c30875751|latchkey]] argues current products are more reliably tested. dj\_mc\_merlin [[./thread#^c30873158|dj\_mc\_merlin]] questions the explanation for the hospitalization, and whimsicalism [[./thread#^c30879702|whimsicalism]] rejects continued intoxication as proof of that explanation. No testing records or clinical details resolve these disagreements.
- This source unit supplies no NO2 or CO2 readings and no measured relationship between airborne pollution and cognition or sleep. 8bitsrule's traffic discussion and NOx link [[./thread#^c30872463|8bitsrule]] are not NO2 measurements. quickthrowman's next-morning intoxication [[./thread#^c30873126|quickthrowman]] and celdon25's sleep-through-heat account [[./thread#^c30872757|celdon25]] concern edibles, so they cannot establish cognitive or sleep effects of indoor-air pollution.
