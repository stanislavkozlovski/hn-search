# [On Scale of 0 to 500, Beijing’s Air Quality Tops ‘Crazy Bad’ at 755 ](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=5051159)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants respond to reports of exceptionally severe smog in Beijing with firsthand accounts of poor visibility, smells, residue, and respiratory discomfort. The discussion considers likely contributors, including coal heating, power generation, factories, traffic, and regional dust, as well as differences between local and official air-quality readings. Comparisons with historical smog in London and Pittsburgh lead into debate about pollution controls, economic growth, energy access, and the costs of cleaner technology. The thread also examines China’s energy choices, industrial policy, international responsibility, and whether temporary restrictions or public pressure can produce lasting improvements.
Tags: Beijing, smog, air pollution, coal, energy policy, environmental regulation

## Arguments

- Outdoor pollution can remain noticeable indoors. bbuffone [[./thread#^c5051814|bbuffone]] smelled something resembling a campfire with burning plastic inside an apartment, while dmor [[./thread#^c5051299|dmor]] described persistent dust on clothing despite changing and washing at the entrance. These observations support concern about indoor exposure, but do not quantify indoor concentrations or the protection provided by staying inside.
- Opening windows has a reported comfort benefit and a possible pollution tradeoff. seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c5052748|seanmcdirmid]] opened a window because an insulated apartment overheated. In Buenos Aires, cardamomo [[./thread#^c5051373|cardamomo]] associated open street-facing doors and uncovered food sacks with dusty flavors and gray rinse water. Together these accounts suggest that cooling and limiting outdoor contamination can conflict; neither measures the resulting airborne exposure.
- Household filtration and tighter building seals are described as practical responses. tsotha [[./thread#^c5053607|tsotha]] reported wealthy households purchasing purification systems, and Mvandenbergh [[./thread#^c5054660|Mvandenbergh]], citing a friend in the business, described substantial filtration and seals in expensive buildings and portable filters among the urban middle class. These are reports of adoption, not measured demonstrations of effectiveness.
- MichaelSalib [[./thread#^c5051327|MichaelSalib]] argued that existing pollution-control equipment, including electrostatic precipitators and fabric filters, could substantially improve coal-related air quality, citing a father who designed and sold such systems. throwmeaway33 [[./thread#^c5053780|throwmeaway33]] also proposed cleaner plants, vehicle emission controls, filtration, and reduced coal use. These are technical claims and proposed interventions rather than reported exposure measurements.
- The health case for reducing pollution includes both immediate discomfort and claimed longer-term benefits. MichaelSalib [[./thread#^c5051553|MichaelSalib]] argued that avoiding childhood asthma and related harms could repay control costs. dredmorbius [[./thread#^c5053848|dredmorbius]] cited historical estimates of approximately 12,000 deaths and 100,000 illnesses during London's 1952 smog. Neither comment supplies an examined study or establishes the health burden of this Beijing episode.
- The Olympic-period contrast suggests that substantially clearer air was achievable under some conditions. fnordfnordfnord [[./thread#^c5051306|fnordfnordfnord]] personally observed a stark difference, and solox3 [[./thread#^c5051428|solox3]] described the pre-Olympic return of blue skies. The observations support the possibility of improvement, while leaving its causes and sustainable costs unresolved.

## Counterarguments

- Olympic-period improvement does not establish a permanent solution or isolate an intervention's effect. fnordfnordfnord [[./thread#^c5051360|fnordfnordfnord]] attributed it to temporary industrial shutdowns followed by catch-up production; seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c5052776|seanmcdirmid]] emphasized that late summer and fall normally have better air than winter. tellarin [[./thread#^c5051331|tellarin]] also pointed to subsequent growth in traffic, heating, and the city itself.
- Matching published ratings need not mean equivalent measurements. tellarin [[./thread#^c5051323|tellarin]] reported agreement between government and embassy figures at one moment. Replying directly, trevelyan [[./thread#^c5052217|trevelyan]] claimed that government figures used daily averages and peripheral equipment, whereas embassy figures were hourly and downtown. If accurate, that distinction complicates comparisons without by itself establishing either sensor's accuracy.
- The proposed electric-vehicle quick fix rests on disputed source attribution. brador [[./thread#^c5051285|brador]] asked whether vehicles caused the pollution; nekojima [[./thread#^c5051308|nekojima]] countered that electricity would still come from coal, and dmor [[./thread#^c5051304|dmor]] suspected factories and household heating were larger contributors. justin66 [[./thread#^c5053939|justin66]] went further, speculating that vehicle intake filters might make exhaust cleaner than ambient air. No measurements support these competing estimates or the car-as-air-cleaner claim.
- Coal's inconvenience does not necessarily create pressure to replace it when users receive district heat. FrojoS [[./thread#^c5051454|FrojoS]] hoped prosperity would end household coal handling, but ChrisClark [[./thread#^c5051509|ChrisClark]] explained that urban residents could receive piped hot water without handling fuel. ChrisClark's account of inadequate warmth was itself challenged by guard-of-terra [[./thread#^c5051632|guard-of-terra]], reporting strong heating in Russia, and seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c5052748|seanmcdirmid]], reporting overheating in a Chinese apartment.
- A societal health benefit does not automatically produce a private financial incentive. Responding to MichaelSalib's cost-effectiveness argument [[./thread#^c5051553|MichaelSalib]], chii [[./thread#^c5053184|chii]] argued that scrubbers cost plant operators money without necessarily increasing profits. corporalagumbo [[./thread#^c5053945|corporalagumbo]] additionally argued that economic-development-dependent valuations change the calculation; this was an unquantified economic argument, not evidence that health damage is smaller.
- Closing subsidized polluters may leave unresolved demand for their output. newbie12 [[./thread#^c5051401|newbie12]] and bcoates [[./thread#^c5051664|bcoates]] proposed removing support for uneconomic industry. archgoon [[./thread#^c5051480|archgoon]] questioned why private ownership would encourage expensive cleaner technology, while ars [[./thread#^c5051836|ars]] argued that continued energy and heating needs could lead to harmful substitutions. The thread supplies no comparison of the replacement sources or resulting exposure.

## Assumptions

- This analysis concerns only story 5051159, part 0, centered on a January 2013 Beijing pollution episode and several historical comparisons. tellarin's contemporary account [[./thread#^c5051240|tellarin]], dmor's 2009 visit [[./thread#^c5051299|dmor]], and fnordfnordfnord's 2008–2009 residence [[./thread#^c5051306|fnordfnordfnord]] have different observation periods. They cannot establish current conditions, population-wide experience, or an HN-wide consensus.
- Visible haze, odors, taste, and deposited dust indicate objectionable conditions but do not identify pollutant concentrations or particle sizes. dmor's gray residue [[./thread#^c5051299|dmor]], nickyoung's balcony deposits [[./thread#^c5053125|nickyoung]], and solox3's smokers' lounge comparison [[./thread#^c5051236|solox3]] are sensory observations, not PM2.5, NO2, or CO2 measurements.
- Source attribution frequently exceeds the evidence presented. TechNewb [[./thread#^c5051274|TechNewb]] blamed coal rather than cars, while dmor [[./thread#^c5051304|dmor]] suspected predominantly industrial and household coal emissions. dmor's estimate of near-universal coal dependence [[./thread#^c5051299|dmor]] and speculation about poor-quality coal and building heat loss [[./thread#^c5051745|dmor]] are not supported by source inventories, fuel tests, or building measurements.
- Several health explanations remain assumptions. firemanx [[./thread#^c5052091|firemanx]] explicitly did not know whether a two-packs-of-cigarettes-per-day comparison was true. sakopov [[./thread#^c5051380|sakopov]] relayed a friend's attribution of nosebleeds to airborne particles, and OGinparadise [[./thread#^c5053296|OGinparadise]] wondered whether historical pollution explained contemporary cancer prevalence. None supplies a clinical assessment or exposure-based risk calculation.
- Automatic improvement with wealth is a hypothesis here. wallflower [[./thread#^c5051379|wallflower]] invoked the debated environmental Kuznets curve, while FrojoS [[./thread#^c5051454|FrojoS]] hoped greater wealth would make inconvenient coal heating obsolete. Neither provides evidence that prosperity alone guarantees cleaner personal exposure or specifies when improvement would occur.
- Smog and CO2 must remain distinct in interpreting this source. throwmeaway33 [[./thread#^c5053780|throwmeaway33]] explicitly separated them. MichaelSalib's claim that CO2 would not cause short-term illness [[./thread#^c5051327|MichaelSalib]] occurred in a discussion of power-plant emissions, without concentrations or indoor conditions. Retric [[./thread#^c5055327|Retric]] discussed NOx generally; that is not a reported NO2 measurement.

## Conditions

- Season, weather, and exceptional combustion events matter. dmor [[./thread#^c5051299|dmor]] visited during winter heating, Chinese New Year fireworks, and a nearby skyscraper fire. seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c5052781|seanmcdirmid]] recommended fall rather than winter visits, while tellarin [[./thread#^c5051240|tellarin]] reported a forecast of improvement with wind. These are contextual observations and expectations, not a measured separation of each factor's contribution.
- Pollution was described as regional and geographically variable. dmor [[./thread#^c5051304|dmor]] suspected emissions arriving from outside Beijing, and mjn [[./thread#^c5053586|mjn]] added windblown Mongolian desert dust as a possible contributor. tellarin [[./thread#^c5051323|tellarin]] reported Beijing ranking 16th among roughly 50 monitored cities at one moment. firemanx [[./thread#^c5052091|firemanx]] and gbog [[./thread#^c5053273|gbog]] considered some other Chinese cities worse, based on their visits.
- Heating arrangements cannot be generalized across the housing discussed. ChrisClark [[./thread#^c5051509|ChrisClark]] described urban district heating with a scheduled start. When dmor attributed poor warmth to rudimentary rural construction [[./thread#^c5051745|dmor]], guard-of-terra [[./thread#^c5052000|guard-of-terra]] questioned whether those houses had central heating; dmor then clarified that they used a coal stove in the room [[./thread#^c5125177|dmor]]. That correction separates different exposure and comfort settings.
- Money and housing control affect available responses. tsotha [[./thread#^c5053607|tsotha]] and Mvandenbergh [[./thread#^c5054660|Mvandenbergh]] associated better indoor filtration with wealth. In Minneapolis, graue [[./thread#^c5051898|graue]] reported inadequate landlord-controlled heat and an inability to afford better housing. cbr [[./thread#^c5052735|cbr]] suggested a legal remedy, but graue [[./thread#^c5052947|graue]] described deposit losses and litigation costs as deterrents. These are local housing accounts, not universally applicable legal guidance.
- Pollution-control affordability is disputed across operating costs and upfront investment. MichaelSalib [[./thread#^c5051477|MichaelSalib]] guessed that plant controls might add only a few percent to electricity costs, while acknowledging higher-sulfur coal and substantial coal burning outside power plants. corporalagumbo [[./thread#^c5053776|corporalagumbo]] instead emphasized limited capital and urgent demand for generating capacity. No actual project budget or exposure reduction accompanies either assessment.
- Switching fuels depends on infrastructure and economically recoverable supply. In response to batgaijin's cited Chinese gas reserves [[./thread#^c5051470|batgaijin]], MichaelSalib [[./thread#^c5051488|MichaelSalib]] emphasized pipeline investment, and justincormack [[./thread#^c5052351|justincormack]] questioned geological suitability and economic extraction. protomyth [[./thread#^c5052666|protomyth]] likewise asserted that alternatives must compete on cost and reliability. These are arguments made in 2013, not present-day feasibility findings.

## Lived experiences

- During a 2009 Beijing visit, dmor [[./thread#^c5051299|dmor]] could not see the city from the arriving aircraft, encountered a strong coal smell outside the city, and reported gray nasal residue, gray shower runoff from exposed skin, and dusty clothing. Advice to avoid going outdoors conflicted with a spouse's commute. Changing and washing clothes did not eliminate the recurring dirt; no particle-size or concentration measurements were taken.
- Living in Shijingshan in 2008–2009, fnordfnordfnord [[./thread#^c5051306|fnordfnordfnord]] went a month before seeing a mountain approximately five miles away or a communications tower approximately one mile away. The commenter described diffuse yellowish daylight and an Olympic-period contrast. These provide approximate visibility landmarks, not calibrated pollution readings.
- During the reported episode, tellarin [[./thread#^c5051240|tellarin]] described foul-tasting air, nearly empty streets, and a dark sky at noon, alongside reported stay-inside warnings and school closures. solox3 [[./thread#^c5051236|solox3]] said the smokers' lounge comparison extended beyond appearance. These accounts document perceived severity rather than a verified exposure threshold.
- bbuffone [[./thread#^c5051814|bbuffone]], after two years in Beijing, reported unusually bad air on arrival and a burning-plastic-like campfire odor inside the apartment. nickyoung [[./thread#^c5053125|nickyoung]], after moving from California, observed pollution deposits accumulating on a city apartment's balcony over several days. Neither reported indoor monitoring or a filtration comparison.
- firemanx [[./thread#^c5052091|firemanx]] reported coughing up unusually unpleasant material after several weeks in Xi'an. weissguy [[./thread#^c5051548|weissguy]] said their lungs felt worse than ever during three days in Beijing. dmoy [[./thread#^c5052004|dmoy]] described tasting and smelling the air on arrival and an ongoing sinus infection after leaving before the worst spike. These are self-reported symptoms with differing exposure periods, not confirmed pollution diagnoses.
- gbog [[./thread#^c5053273|gbog]] reported a lingering smell sensation and headaches after the severe Saturday episode, explicitly questioning whether the effects were physical or psychosomatic. The same commenter considered Shijiazhuang and Chengdu potentially worse but wanted actual air-quality figures rather than relying solely on impressions.
- In Buenos Aires, cardamomo [[./thread#^c5051373|cardamomo]] repeatedly noticed off-flavored beans and grains, with gray rinse water, from a shop using open sacks and open street-facing doors. Goods from shops with closed bins and doors tasted better. This is a firsthand comparison involving several simultaneous storage differences; it does not isolate ventilation or measure airborne pollutants.
- FrojoS [[./thread#^c5051454|FrojoS]] recalled carrying household coal, tending the fire, and struggling to regulate warmth during harsh winters. guard-of-terra [[./thread#^c5051632|guard-of-terra]] reported Russian district heating capable of approximately 28°C indoors at −30°C outdoors, varying by house. seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c5052748|seanmcdirmid]] reported opening windows in an overheated, insulated apartment. These are contrasting heating and comfort experiences, not pollutant measurements.
- Some apparent personal accounts are relayed through others: sakopov [[./thread#^c5051380|sakopov]] described a friend's persistent nosebleeds during a visit, and mistagrrr [[./thread#^c5052173|mistagrrr]] quoted a Beijing teacher who associated pollution severity with the air's taste, including a copper-like taste. Neither is the posting commenter's own symptom or sensor record.
- ableal [[./thread#^c5051818|ableal]] personally saw the soot-encrusted facade of Pittsburgh's Carnegie Library before its late-1980s cleaning. The account of streetlights operating at winter noon in the 1950s came from photographs. This distinction separates a witnessed pollution deposit from historical ambient conditions the commenter did not claim to have experienced.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The numerical evidence does not establish sensor reliability. solox3 [[./thread#^c5051236|solox3]] quoted a reading of 755 at 8 p.m.; tellarin [[./thread#^c5051323|tellarin]] followed published ratings rather than presenting a personal instrument log. trevelyan's account of different averaging periods and station locations [[./thread#^c5052217|trevelyan]] raises comparability questions, but no instrument specifications, calibration checks, concentration units, or raw measurements resolve them. The photo and analysis links supplied by nekojima [[./thread#^c5052157|nekojima]], est [[./thread#^c5052642|est]], and davemaya [[./thread#^c5054216|davemaya]] contain no inspectable supporting material in this prompt.
- The PM10–PM2.5 question remains unanswered. novaleaf [[./thread#^c5053441|novaleaf]] asked how the metrics compare and whether PM10 should always have the higher number. corporalagumbo [[./thread#^c5053801|corporalagumbo]] responded only that smaller particles are more harmful. The supplied exchange provides neither paired readings nor enough reporting detail to settle the numerical comparison or quantify size-specific health risks.
- The efficiency-to-pollution argument contains an unresolved numerical dispute. Retric [[./thread#^c5055327|Retric]] interpreted an approximately ten-point thermal-efficiency improvement as roughly 30\% more electricity from the same coal and claimed reduced CO and NOx. ars [[./thread#^c5056868|ars]] acknowledged that the original 10\% figure was invented and challenged that interpretation. Retric [[./thread#^c5058820|Retric]] subsequently offered approximately 32–33\% average versus 42\% best thermal efficiency, but no measured emission factors establish the claimed pollution reduction.
- The net exposure effects of opening windows, sealing buildings, and using filters are unknown. seanmcdirmid's window opening improved overheating [[./thread#^c5052748|seanmcdirmid]], cardamomo's shop comparison suggested contamination associated with open storage and doors [[./thread#^c5051373|cardamomo]], and Mvandenbergh described sealed, filtered buildings [[./thread#^c5054660|Mvandenbergh]]. There are no paired indoor/outdoor PM2.5 readings, before-and-after filter results, gas-removal measurements, or indoor CO2 readings to determine which approach performed best under specific conditions.
- Health causation and several requested topics remain unsupported by this unit. gbog's uncertainty about headaches [[./thread#^c5053273|gbog]] illustrates the absence of objective outcome testing; there are no cognition or sleep assessments. MichaelSalib's CO2 statement [[./thread#^c5051327|MichaelSalib]] does not establish indoor CO2 effects, and Retric's NOx claim [[./thread#^c5055327|Retric]] supplies no NO2 exposure data. cardamomo discussed food storage rather than cooking emissions [[./thread#^c5051373|cardamomo]], while bbuffone's campfire description was an odor analogy, not a wildfire exposure account [[./thread#^c5051814|bbuffone]].
- Commenters disagree over whether continued pollution chiefly reflects unwillingness to buy available controls, familiar profit incentives, or constrained development choices. MichaelSalib [[./thread#^c5051327|MichaelSalib]] emphasized known technology and reluctance to spend; jacquesm [[./thread#^c5051366|jacquesm]] emphasized historical repetition of profit-driven pollution; corporalagumbo [[./thread#^c5053776|corporalagumbo]] emphasized immediate power needs and limited capital. The discussion provides no verified costs, implementation evidence, or exposure outcomes that resolve those explanations.
