# [New York to ban natural gas, including stoves, in new buildings](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=35746022)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion of New York’s plan to prohibit natural gas hookups in new buildings, including stoves, heating, and hot water. Participants debate the policy’s climate impact, methane leaks, indoor air quality, fire and outage safety, and whether ventilation rules or incentives could address these concerns. A major thread compares gas, resistance, and induction cooking, covering cost, cookware, culinary techniques, and accessibility for renters. Others discuss heat pump performance in cold climates, grid capacity and reliability, backup power, energy infrastructure costs, and the scope of government regulation. The thread also touches on political messaging, public trust, and whether phasing out new gas infrastructure is a practical transition strategy.
Tags: natural gas, building codes, climate policy, indoor air quality, electric cooking, induction stoves, heat pumps, power grid, energy infrastructure, government regulation

## Relevance

The discussion includes firsthand indoor monitoring of PM2.5, NO2, CO2, CO, and VOCs around gas cooking, along with claims about health risks and conflicting views on their strength. People discuss ventilation, recirculating versus externally vented hoods, opening windows despite cold-weather comfort costs, cooking-generated particles, and induction as a way to avoid combustion emissions. Most reported readings are personal accounts rather than controlled measurements; the reliability and effectiveness of ventilation remain disputed. ## Arguments

- Gas combustion and cooking food are presented as different pollution sources. amluto [[./thread#^c35758067|amluto]] distinguishes combustion-derived NOx from particles generated by high-temperature cooking; toss1 [[./thread#^c35757643|toss1]] notes that a gas burner adds combustion products even when merely heating water. Their argument is that substantial pollution from food does not make additional combustion exposure irrelevant.
- Filtration and ventilation address different pollutants in amluto’s account [[./thread#^c35758067|amluto]]: particle filters can remove cooking particulates, whereas NOx is harder to remove without replacing the air. They specifically argue that recirculating carbon-filter hoods do not adequately address this gas exposure. This is a technical claim in the discussion, without a reported filter-performance experiment.
- Outdoor extraction is proposed for both gas and electric cooking. oakwhiz [[./thread#^c35746670|oakwhiz]] advocates capturing exhaust with coordinated supply and return airflow; bombcar [[./thread#^c35762234|bombcar]] emphasizes a hood that overhangs the front burners and has makeup air. These proposals address food smoke and smells as well as gas combustion products.
- Source removal can avoid relying on occupants to operate extraction consistently. toss1 [[./thread#^c35758837|toss1]] argues that hoods help but introduce noise and expel heated or cooled air, making prevention preferable to dilution. amluto [[./thread#^c35758388|amluto]] similarly proposes either genuine outdoor extraction or a stove without combustion emissions.
- The respiratory-health case relies principally on reported research. TulliusCicero [[./thread#^c35756573|TulliusCicero]] quotes a claim that children in gas-cooking households are 42\% more likely to have asthma; bhickey [[./thread#^c35757905|bhickey]] claims gas explains 14\% of asthma cases; michael\_vo [[./thread#^c35762782|michael\_vo]] cites an estimate of roughly one eighth of US childhood asthma. These are different association and attributable-burden claims, not firsthand diagnoses or interchangeable measures of individual risk.
- Exposure is not necessarily limited to the person choosing the appliance. GuB-42 [[./thread#^c35758387|GuB-42]] emphasizes other household members, including children; Eric\_WVGG [[./thread#^c35757382|Eric\_WVGG]] argues that a young child cannot choose whether to inhale cooking fumes. This supports considering household exposure separately from the cook’s personal tolerance.
- Cognition appears as a concern rather than a demonstrated outcome. pathartl [[./thread#^c35757852|pathartl]] asserts that CO2 is bad for the brain, while anonu [[./thread#^c35761307|anonu]] recalls studies correlating school air quality with test scores and anticipates benefits from better ventilation. Neither supplies concentrations, effect sizes, or firsthand cognitive testing.

## Counterarguments

- Several commenters argue that food preparation dominates cooking pollution. pclmulqdq [[./thread#^c35757870|pclmulqdq]] asserts that cooking produces hundreds of times more harmful particles than the burner alone; nullc [[./thread#^c35760813|nullc]] argues for proper extraction over both gas and electric ranges and considers that sufficient to resolve the gas-air-quality issue. Neither supplies measurements establishing those claims across different pollutants or cooking methods.
- Switching to electricity does not establish that extraction is unnecessary. Responding to gwbas1c’s statement that their gas stove and oven require the fan whereas electric cooking would not [[./thread#^c35757547|gwbas1c]], catiopatio [[./thread#^c35757669|catiopatio]] argues that cooking aerosols justify using the fan with either fuel. This also limits danny\_codes’s broad claim that induction has no emissions [[./thread#^c35764529|danny\_codes]]: eliminating the flame does not establish pollution-free food preparation.
- Ventilation requirements are proposed as an alternative to prohibiting gas installations. iamdamian [[./thread#^c35757395|iamdamian]] says research they have seen suggests vented hoods mitigate health effects; apnew [[./thread#^c35767805|apnew]] proposes requiring effective hoods in rentals. These are arguments for mitigation, without supplied evidence that a particular installation eliminates the relevant exposure.
- The asthma evidence receives a specific confounding critique. tomp [[./thread#^c35756461|tomp]] quotes an external physician’s analysis saying some included studies did not adequately evaluate parental smoking, other indoor smoke, pets, or outdoor pollution. That challenges causal attribution to gas cooking; it does not demonstrate that gas contributes no exposure or harm.
- pclmulqdq [[./thread#^c35759867|pclmulqdq]] argues that NO and NO2 react readily and therefore accumulate less readily than CO or CO2, emphasizing dose rather than the mere presence of a hazardous substance. amluto [[./thread#^c35759578|amluto]] distinguishes relatively less concerning NO at the discussed concentrations from toxic NO2. Neither provides measured indoor decay rates or a demonstrated safe exposure level.
- One dispute concerns whether a paper correction undermines its findings. pclmulqdq [[./thread#^c35758287|pclmulqdq]] points to the correction notice when questioning the evidence; sonarone [[./thread#^c35758961|sonarone]] reports that the correction changes one figure’s r² from 0.59 to 0.58. pclmulqdq later acknowledges not having read the correction before objecting [[./thread#^c35759068|pclmulqdq]]. The exchange supplies a narrow correction, not a substantive refutation of the exposure results.
- The applicability of a WHO household-pollution source is contested. Pigalowda [[./thread#^c35762829|Pigalowda]] says its quoted focus is kerosene, biomass, coal, and inefficient fires; evandijk70 [[./thread#^c35763211|evandijk70]] quotes natural gas among cleaner alternatives. simplotek [[./thread#^c35771325|simplotek]] responds that being cleaner than those fuels does not establish harmlessness. The supplied excerpts do not quantify the risk of a modern, ventilated natural-gas kitchen.
- Operating during a blackout and operating with adequate ventilation are separate questions. orra [[./thread#^c35747225|orra]] warns that electric extraction stops during outages; christophilus [[./thread#^c35761664|christophilus]] recognizes this problem while describing relatives cooking on gas during blackouts. In response to JakeAl’s use of a stove for winter warmth [[./thread#^c35759942|JakeAl]], vel0city [[./thread#^c35765577|vel0city]] specifically warns about unvented NOx, CO2, and CO, including CO poisoning.

## Assumptions

- Some safety arguments assume ideal combustion adequately represents household exposure. SoftTalker [[./thread#^c35757753|SoftTalker]] invokes clean methane combustion producing water and CO2; pathartl [[./thread#^c35757852|pathartl]] objects that supplied gas is not pure methane, and Zak [[./thread#^c35756979|Zak]] identifies nitrogen oxides from high-temperature combustion. These comments expose differing assumptions about what an operating appliance actually emits.
- Claims that hoods solve the problem assume effective capture and actual use. MattRix [[./thread#^c35759850|MattRix]] recalls research suggesting poorer capture from front burners and questions how frequently occupants turn fans on; toss1 [[./thread#^c35758837|toss1]] identifies noise and conditioned-air loss as practical deterrents. MattRix’s suggestion that smelling food indicates pollution is explicitly intuition, not a validated exposure measurement.
- The measurements discussed represent different things. elif [[./thread#^c35761949|elif]] initially used CO2 as a proxy for ventilation, then monitored several pollutants; amluto [[./thread#^c35758403|amluto]] reports observations from a PM2.5 meter. A low particle reading alone cannot establish low gaseous pollution, and a CO2 ventilation proxy does not quantify every other contaminant.
- Comparisons between fuels depend on building conditions as well as cooking. endisneigh [[./thread#^c35756418|endisneigh]] suggests induction is associated with newer, better-ventilated housing; gwbas1c [[./thread#^c35756895|gwbas1c]] instead emphasizes reduced leakage in newer construction, estimating air replacement every eight to ten hours versus roughly four in older homes. These are unverified building-level generalizations here. throwaway049 [[./thread#^c35756557|throwaway049]] proposes controlled comparisons but supplies no experiment.
- Evidence about different gas appliances should not automatically be pooled. amluto [[./thread#^c35758067|amluto]] explicitly says a concerning linked graph concerns an oven, and later recalls differences in CO production between ovens and unloaded burners [[./thread#^c35759578|amluto]]. dschuler [[./thread#^c35757851|dschuler]] discusses combustion controls in newer furnaces. None of these comments establishes equivalent emissions from ovens, cooktops, and heating systems.
- A share of emissions is not an indoor exposure concentration. carabiner [[./thread#^c35762605|carabiner]] claims that leakage while stoves are off accounts for 76\% of their methane emissions and argues this matters for occupants. The figure is not a claim that 76\% of supplied fuel leaks, and the comment provides neither indoor concentrations nor a dose-based health assessment.

## Conditions

- The distinction between new construction and retrofits matters financially. wingspar [[./thread#^c35756999|wingspar]] and quags [[./thread#^c35760957|quags]] describe the proposal as applying to new buildings while existing installations remain. screwturner68’s approximately \$25,000-per-unit estimate [[./thread#^c35763706|screwturner68]] covers electrical upgrades, a stove, water heating, and HVAC in existing apartments; it is not a stove-only replacement price or a direct estimate of new-build costs.
- Renters describe different practical choices. ahoy [[./thread#^c35757781|ahoy]] reports gas stoves without hoods in every rental they have occupied and wanting electricity instead. apnew [[./thread#^c35758161|apnew]] deliberately excluded rentals without gas, while annexrichmond [[./thread#^c35757470|annexrichmond]] says both options were plentiful during their searches. jjj123 [[./thread#^c35763266|jjj123]] emphasizes that financial desperation can sharply restrict such choices. These accounts do not establish a common rental experience.
- Retrofitting extraction can require permission and expensive building work. iudqnolq [[./thread#^c35762052|iudqnolq]] anticipates landlords refusing an exterior penetration; john\_the\_writer [[./thread#^c35759472|john\_the\_writer]] anticipates costly ductwork being passed on to tenants; john\_shafthair [[./thread#^c35746882|john\_shafthair]] suggests some large older buildings may be difficult to upgrade. These are practical concerns and estimates, not documented retrofit quotations.
- Appliance prices vary by location and product category. Wowfunhappy [[./thread#^c35763520|Wowfunhappy]] reports buying a \$60 portable induction cooker; doikor [[./thread#^c35761903|doikor]] reports Finnish four-element induction cooktops around €200–250 with a small premium over resistance models. collegeburner [[./thread#^c35757724|collegeburner]] lists US integrated induction ranges starting around \$1,198, above the gas models they checked. Portable burners, separate cooktops, and full ranges are not equivalent purchases.
- Electrical installation costs can outweigh appliance prices, but the reported circumstances differ. hedora [[./thread#^c35755440|hedora]] describes three years and \$10,000 for utility work, later clarifying that service had been disconnected and the meter needed moving [[./thread#^c35756970|hedora]]. danans [[./thread#^c35755742|danans]] reports a roughly month-long, \$0 utility upgrade from 100A to 200A, while clarifying that the panel work itself was expensive [[./thread#^c35756965|danans]].
- Cookware compatibility can add expense or compromise comfort benefits. lobocinza [[./thread#^c35765380|lobocinza]] had to replace a steel pan that would not work with induction. cmclaughlin [[./thread#^c35764240|cmclaughlin]] says transfer plates can accommodate incompatible cookware but sacrifice efficiency and the reduced room heating they value; they suggest inexpensive compatible cookware instead. The tradeoff is specific to the pans and equipment involved.
- Weather changes the acceptability of ventilation. CydeWeys [[./thread#^c35757405|CydeWeys]] opens windows while cooking but dislikes doing so in winter; throwaway049 [[./thread#^c35756557|throwaway049]] reports opening windows in good weather and closing them in winter. amluto [[./thread#^c35758267|amluto]] emphasizes cold-climate heat loss from required extraction, while cmclaughlin [[./thread#^c35764259|cmclaughlin]] values reduced cooking heat in hotter climates.
- Regional outages make fuel choice a thermal-comfort and ventilation tradeoff. hn\_throwaway\_99 [[./thread#^c35756395|hn\_throwaway\_99]] describes a Texas gas fireplace helping prevent frozen pipes during prolonged outages; mardifoufs [[./thread#^c35757092|mardifoufs]] describes using a wood stove during a four-day Québec outage. Neither reports pollutant measurements. orra’s warning that powered extraction also fails during outages [[./thread#^c35747225|orra]] limits treating backup combustion as an exposure-neutral solution.
- Operating costs also depend on tariffs and billing arrangements. anonu [[./thread#^c35761382|anonu]] claims that NYC tenants may move from non-individually-metered gas to individually billed electricity; thebiss [[./thread#^c35779099|thebiss]] emphasizes substantial regional electricity-price differences. These comments complicate universal claims that switching cooking fuels is cheaper, without supplying comparable household bills for equivalent cooking.

## Lived experiences

- elif [[./thread#^c35761949|elif]] gives the broadest firsthand monitoring account: NO2, CO2, CO, PM2.5, and VOC readings reportedly reached levels they considered unhealthy despite a hood and open windows. They say pollution persisted for hours, spread throughout the house, and remained despite opening five or six windows and attempting to isolate the kitchen. This prompted switching to induction and a steam/convection oven, with better reported cooking control and speed. No numerical concentrations or measured post-switch exposure series are supplied.
- chokma [[./thread#^c35756811|chokma]] reports that searing meat raises PM2.5 in their flat by more than thirtyfold. This is a quantitative relative observation, but the comment gives no starting concentration, peak concentration, sensor model, duration, ventilation setup, or stove fuel.
- amluto [[./thread#^c35758403|amluto]] reports using a portable PM2.5 meter and finding little apparent particulate pollution from moderate-temperature induction cooking, including simmering stock for hours. They extend this to cooking below roughly 300 degrees, without specifying the temperature unit. No numerical readings are supplied, and the observation concerns particulates rather than all possible pollutants.
- 71a54xd [[./thread#^c35746578|71a54xd]] describes a luxury NYC apartment built in 2018 with a gas stove and only small bathroom vents exhausting to the roof. Extended stove use reportedly triggered an alarm, which they attribute to CO and particles. The account provides no detector identification or pollutant readings, so the alarm is an observation rather than a verified measurement of both substances.
- CydeWeys [[./thread#^c35757405|CydeWeys]] reports poor perceived kitchen air with a gas stove lacking outdoor extraction and regularly opens windows when cooking. crooked-v [[./thread#^c35758283|crooked-v]] describes an expensive home with a recirculating hood and enough gas-stove waste heat to open windows even apart from combustion concerns. Neither reports measured exposure changes from opening them.
- danbolt [[./thread#^c35759384|danbolt]] found gas-stove heat uncomfortable on their hands and preferred the cooler kitchen after switching to resistance cooking, especially in summer. cmclaughlin [[./thread#^c35764240|cmclaughlin]] similarly values induction for reducing unwanted house heating. These are firsthand comfort benefits, not measured health improvements.
- ravenstine [[./thread#^c35757868|ravenstine]] reports never feeling ill from cooking on gas and rejects the idea of feeling victimized by the appliance. That is a contrasting symptom account, but it contains no monitoring or long-term health assessment.
- azemetre [[./thread#^c35757783|azemetre]] says they had not encountered genuine residential extraction across roughly 800 homes, subsequently narrowing the claim to single-family homes and triple-deckers in the South and New England [[./thread#^c35758146|azemetre]]. Their own apartment’s hood reportedly feeds through a sponge without an outdoor path [[./thread#^c35763343|azemetre]]. mumblemumble [[./thread#^c35758263|mumblemumble]] likewise reports encountering only recirculating residential hoods across several US locations.
- Other hood experiences directly differ. grepfru\_it [[./thread#^c35759443|grepfru\_it]] reports real outdoor vents in homes in New Jersey, Virginia, Washington DC, and Texas; MrFoof [[./thread#^c35758212|MrFoof]] reports them in almost every New England home or apartment they occupied, including older buildings. fafqg [[./thread#^c35758156|fafqg]] reports hoods in every Spanish home they visited. These observations establish variation, not regional prevalence or verified capture performance.
- Acceptance of induction is mixed even among users. downut [[./thread#^c35755811|downut]] describes abandoning a roughly \$1,600 induction stove after three weeks because of cooking and cookware difficulties. hampelm [[./thread#^c35756013|hampelm]] reports performing many of the same tasks successfully on a lower-priced induction stove. incangold [[./thread#^c35792478|incangold]], who helps run a catering business, says their kitchens prefer induction while acknowledging a specialist-equipment issue for wok cooking.
- Some resistance to switching concerns particular cooking practices rather than unfamiliarity. apnew [[./thread#^c35768599|apnew]] reports three years using induction and roughly three using resistance cooktops, yet still values direct-flame finishing. uwagar [[./thread#^c35765654|uwagar]] identifies a personally important family aubergine preparation as a reason for retaining gas. Neither account measures the exposure associated with those techniques.
- Heating substitutions also produce conflicting comfort accounts. reisse [[./thread#^c35746729|reisse]] describes intrusive heat-pump noise in European apartments; plandis [[./thread#^c35759332|plandis]] reports inadequate heating on some cold days; achates [[./thread#^c35762309|achates]] reports greater comfort after switching to gas. Conversely, magicalhippo [[./thread#^c35757234|magicalhippo]] reports satisfactory heat-pump use in Oslo, with supplemental bedroom heating during the coldest periods. These are equipment- and building-specific experiences without indoor-pollutant measurements.
- gainda [[./thread#^c35747033|gainda]] reports feeling coated in grime after walking around NYC and speculates that a day outside is worse than years of gas-stove use. diordiderot [[./thread#^c35760685|diordiderot]] attributes the sensation to vehicle exhaust. The sensation is firsthand; the attribution and comparative health-risk ranking are not measured.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Sensor reliability cannot be evaluated from the supplied accounts. elif [[./thread#^c35761949|elif]], chokma [[./thread#^c35756811|chokma]], and amluto [[./thread#^c35758403|amluto]] provide no sensor models, calibration information, numerical concentration series, or adequate placement details. Their observations may be useful locally, but the excerpt does not establish measurement accuracy, comparable exposure thresholds, or what supports the label 'unhealthy'. It also supplies no evidence that their instruments were faulty.
- Even NOx composition is described inconsistently. Zak [[./thread#^c35756979|Zak]] recalls the relevant emissions being mostly NO2, whereas amluto [[./thread#^c35759578|amluto]] estimates NO2 at approximately one quarter of total NOx. Neither supplies firsthand speciation measurements. The discussion therefore cannot treat a total-NOx claim as a known NO2 dose.
- The magnitude and causality of chronic health effects remain disputed. TulliusCicero’s quoted asthma association [[./thread#^c35756573|TulliusCicero]] and michael\_vo’s attributable-case estimate [[./thread#^c35762782|michael\_vo]] confront tomp’s confounding critique [[./thread#^c35756461|tomp]]. The supplied comments do not resolve absolute risk by concentration, duration, ventilation, or cooking method, and the firsthand monitoring accounts do not establish clinical outcomes.
- Whether a properly designed, consistently operated hood is sufficient remains unsettled. iamdamian [[./thread#^c35757744|iamdamian]] considers hoods protective; MattRix [[./thread#^c35759850|MattRix]] questions capture, particularly at front burners; elif [[./thread#^c35761949|elif]] reports persistent pollution despite a hood. Because elif does not specify its outdoor routing or capture performance, that experience cannot establish that every effective extraction system fails.
- The supplied part does not establish when opening windows worsens exposure. pclmulqdq [[./thread#^c35758287|pclmulqdq]] recommends opening one, while elif [[./thread#^c35761949|elif]] reports inadequate clearance with several open. Outdoor comparisons remain speculative: gainda [[./thread#^c35747033|gainda]] supplies a grime impression and hatsune [[./thread#^c35838290|hatsune]] asserts greater harm from vehicle pollution. someonehere [[./thread#^c35747073|someonehere]] discusses wildfire-related outages, not smoke measurements. No paired indoor/outdoor readings or wildfire-smoke window experiment supports a blanket window-opening rule.
- CO2-specific cognitive and sleep effects are largely unanswered. pathartl [[./thread#^c35757852|pathartl]] makes a brain-health assertion, anonu [[./thread#^c35761307|anonu]] recalls school-performance correlations without identifying the pollutant, and elif [[./thread#^c35761949|elif]] describes CO2 monitoring without ppm values or cognitive outcomes. This source unit supplies no firsthand sleep improvement, sleep impairment, or concentration-response evidence.
- Exposure beyond the dwelling is disputed. CydeWeys [[./thread#^c35757378|CydeWeys]] asserts that poor apartment air affects neighbors, while amluto [[./thread#^c35758067|amluto]] does not consider stove NOx a particular outdoor or neighborhood threat. Deeedioo [[./thread#^c35770722|Deeedioo]] questions whether exhausting fumes into the city is acceptable. No measurements of inter-apartment transfer or outdoor concentrations settle these claims.
- Alternative flames are not demonstrated exposure solutions. uwagar [[./thread#^c35765671|uwagar]] asks whether a butane torch introduces more harmful chemicals; kerkeslager [[./thread#^c35773656|kerkeslager]] explicitly acknowledges uncertainty and unverified fuel-purity advertising. That exchange primarily concerns cooking and food contact, not measured inhalation exposure. adolph’s oxygen-fed combustion proposal [[./thread#^c35757386|adolph]] is also speculative and explicitly described as more expensive and hazardous.
- The effectiveness of additional air-treatment measures is barely documented. elif [[./thread#^c35761949|elif]] mentions learning about filters, dehumidifiers, plants, and ventilation schedules but supplies no separate performance results. amluto [[./thread#^c35758067|amluto]] argues for particle filtration while distinguishing gaseous pollutants, but gives no device comparison. This part cannot establish that plants improved air quality or rank filtration approaches for cooking pollution or wildfire smoke.
