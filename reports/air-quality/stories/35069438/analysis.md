# [Industry knew about gas stoves’ air pollution problems in early 1970s](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=35069438)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion reacts to evidence that the gas stove industry studied indoor pollution decades earlier. Participants debate whether this reflects failures of capitalism or government oversight, whether investigating product safety should be discouraged, and how industry research relates to public research; a practical side thread covers range-hood ventilation and makeup air.
Tags: gas stoves, indoor air pollution, industry accountability, capitalism, ventilation

## Relevance

The discussion connects gas stove emissions with indoor pollution and discusses range hoods, exhaust, and make-up air, including practical ventilation tradeoffs. [[./thread#^c35069723|zaroth]] [[./thread#^c35070092|hellisothers]] [[./thread#^c35070226|zaroth]]

## Arguments

- zaroth [[./thread#^c35069723|zaroth]] argues that indoor cooking produces airborne burnt food and grease particles and that installing and using an exhaust vent helps. This is a qualitative explanation, without measured pollutant reductions.
- politician [[./thread#^c35069575|politician]] quotes the article describing two approaches to gas-related indoor pollution: ventilation and range tops designed to emit fewer nitrogen oxides. This identifies proposed improvements, not demonstrated effectiveness; nitrogen oxides are not a specific NO2 measurement.
- zaroth [[./thread#^c35069723|zaroth]] presents automatic make-up air as protection against a powerful hood creating negative pressure and potentially backdrafting a chimney. His subsequent explanation describes an outdoor-air duct with an electrically controlled baffle [[./thread#^c35070226|zaroth]].

## Counterarguments

- Replying to zaroth’s installation account, hellisothers [[./thread#^c35070092|hellisothers]] raises two qualifications to choosing a very large hood: chimney backdraft and restricted exhaust airflow without an adequate inlet. These are concerns about a planned installation, not reported failures.
- zaroth [[./thread#^c35070226|zaroth]] supplies a comfort tradeoff against maximizing hood power: his three-speed hood is loud even at its lowest setting, and he wants a quieter setting for gas-oven baking. He does not say whether noise causes him to avoid using it.
- Against testybesty2’s distrust of profit-driven safety solutions [[./thread#^c35069598|testybesty2]], zaroth [[./thread#^c35069723|zaroth]] argues that industry incentives also supported safety experiments in model homes. Separately, politician [[./thread#^c35069575|politician]] defends research into product improvements. These arguments defend the value of research without establishing that particular gas appliances are safe.

## Assumptions

- zaroth’s attribution of cooking pollution mostly to burnt food and grease [[./thread#^c35069723|zaroth]] is unsupported by source-apportionment data in this discussion. The comment supplies neither particle sizes nor a comparison between cooking particles and gas-combustion pollutants, so it cannot establish their relative PM2.5 or NO2 contributions.
- Inferring an overall exposure benefit from zaroth’s outdoor make-up-air arrangement [[./thread#^c35069723|zaroth]] would require information about incoming air quality and pollutant capture. Neither outdoor measurements nor filtration details are provided; the stated purpose is pressure management.

## Lived experiences

- zaroth [[./thread#^c35069723|zaroth]] reports an installed gas-stove hood whose relay automatically opens a baffle admitting outside air into the HVAC return. This is firsthand equipment experience, without pressure measurements, pollutant readings, or documented prevention of backdraft.
- In explaining the system to hellisothers, zaroth [[./thread#^c35070226|zaroth]] describes a typical roughly six-inch outdoor-air connection to a furnace return and wiring from the hood to its baffle. He distinguishes more sophisticated controls that admit make-up air only at higher hood speeds; he does not establish that all these details describe his own installation.
- zaroth [[./thread#^c35070226|zaroth]] personally finds his hood’s minimum speed too loud and wants gentler ventilation while baking. The reported effect is acoustic comfort, with no stated health, cognition, or sleep outcome.
- hellisothers [[./thread#^c35070092|hellisothers]] reports remodeling a kitchen and wanting the largest hood that will fit to handle cooking smoke. This establishes a practical motivation and space constraint, but provides no completed-installation outcome or before-and-after measurements.
