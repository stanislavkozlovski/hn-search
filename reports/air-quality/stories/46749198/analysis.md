# [Adoption of EVs tied to real-world reductions in air pollution: study](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46749198)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion covers reported neighborhood air-quality improvements associated with EV adoption and debates how to interpret them alongside lifecycle emissions, electricity generation, battery production, oil supply, and the concentration of pollution away from populated streets. Participants compare tailpipe pollution with tire and brake particles, regenerative braking, vehicle weight, and emissions from poorly maintained or modified combustion vehicles. Other threads consider EV affordability, charging access and range, battery longevity and replacement, vehicle privacy and connectivity, and whether simpler cars are viable. The discussion also weighs EVs against hybrids, public transit, cycling, and reduced car dependence, with differing views on personal mobility, urban design, rural needs, and the pace of a transition away from combustion vehicles.
Tags: electric vehicles, air pollution, lifecycle emissions, energy, transportation, battery longevity, charging infrastructure, vehicle privacy, public transit, urban planning

## Relevance

The discussion includes firsthand reports of unpleasant exhaust exposure while walking, cycling, and in garages, plus discussion of vehicle-related NOx and particulate pollution. It also covers cabin filtration and the limits of inferring exposure from smells or visible smoke; most claims concern traffic emissions rather than measured indoor concentrations. ## Arguments

- The strongest quantitative evidence presented concerns neighborhood NO2. zahlman [[./thread#^c46750501|zahlman]] quotes a press release describing 1,692 California neighborhoods, vehicle registrations, and annual averages derived from the TROPOMI satellite sensor for 2019–2023. It reports a 1.1\% NO2 decrease for every 200 additional registered zero-emission vehicles. This is a reported study association, not a commenter's personal exposure measurement.
- Several commenters argue that removing combustion where people breathe has value independently of total emissions elsewhere. jordanbeiber [[./thread#^c46752448|jordanbeiber]] emphasizes densely populated neighborhoods; MBCook [[./thread#^c46750223|MBCook]] argues that centralized emissions are easier to control; KaiserPro [[./thread#^c46753963|KaiserPro]] specifically identifies stationary-plant nitrogen-pollution controls and electrostatic particulate capture. These are exposure and engineering arguments, without supplied measurements of the resulting population benefit.
- Source elimination can benefit both occupants and nearby people. didip [[./thread#^c46750378|didip]] values avoiding exhaust around their child in the garage, while unglaublich [[./thread#^c46751177|unglaublich]] emphasizes cyclists following vehicles. kalleboo [[./thread#^c46750335|kalleboo]] describes Japanese delivery fleets adopting electric trucks, vans, and motorcycles, highlighting neighborhood exposure from delivery vehicles otherwise left idling during summer.
- Repairing unusually dirty vehicles is proposed as another practical intervention. MBCook [[./thread#^c46750267|MBCook]] suggests finding and repairing the worst emitters, arguing that repair costs explain much visibly or noticeably dirty operation. Their proposed focus on the worst 0.1\% is a policy suggestion, not an empirically identified share or demonstrated intervention.
- Cabin filtration should match the pollutant being addressed. torginus [[./thread#^c46752084|torginus]] distinguishes HEPA particle filtration from removal of odor-causing molecules. formerly\_proven [[./thread#^c46753137|formerly\_proven]] recommends activated-carbon cabin filters, describes factory and aftermarket availability, and suggests annual replacement. The supporting firsthand reports concern reduced odors; they do not establish particular PM2.5 or NO2 removal efficiencies.
- Regenerative braking provides a mechanism for reducing brake dust despite vehicle weight. perlgeek [[./thread#^c46753469|perlgeek]] separates combustion, brake abrasion, and tire abrasion; catketch [[./thread#^c46755893|catketch]] reports very infrequent friction-brake use. ezfe [[./thread#^c46751271|ezfe]] clarifies that regeneration, rather than one-pedal driving itself, saves brake use, and AdamN [[./thread#^c46752996|AdamN]] notes that hybrids also have regeneration.
- Reducing vehicle size or car travel addresses sources that electrification leaves behind. dgacmu [[./thread#^c46750514|dgacmu]] favors lighter vehicles, e-bikes, and mass transit while describing EVs as a likely improvement across exhaust and non-exhaust emissions. davidw [[./thread#^c46750277|davidw]] likewise calls EVs a step forward while favoring e-bikes. These proposals concern remaining traffic pollution, rather than demonstrating that any transport mode produces no particles.
- Local source control needs to include heating and industry. MengerSponge [[./thread#^c46750434|MengerSponge]] identifies household coal heating as a potentially large aggregate source; DrProtic [[./thread#^c46753583|DrProtic]] recalls severe pollution despite a lockdown curfew; yanhangyhy [[./thread#^c46750406|yanhangyhy]] attributes Beijing's improvement partly to factory shutdowns or relocation. These comments challenge explanations based on traffic changes alone.

## Counterarguments

- A reduction in NO2 does not establish a reduction in every pollutant. chupasaurus [[./thread#^c46750372|chupasaurus]] emphasizes that the study concerns nitrogen oxides, whereas swsieber [[./thread#^c46750429|swsieber]] objects that its broader air-pollution framing invites consideration of other emissions. IngvarLynn [[./thread#^c46755277|IngvarLynn]] cites a report while claiming roughly half of PM10 pollution is independent of engine type; the comment does not clearly establish that figure's denominator or applicability to this study.
- Removing exhaust leaves tire and road wear. memen [[./thread#^c46752042|memen]] argues that greater weight and torque increase tire-derived microplastics and possibly PFAS, but supplies no exposure measurements. SideburnsOfDoom [[./thread#^c46752948|SideburnsOfDoom]] accepts a weight-related wear disadvantage while stressing reduced exhaust and brake emissions and the existence of heavy combustion vehicles. These are competing components of the comparison, not a demonstrated net PM2.5 result.
- Reports of severely malfunctioning vehicles do not describe every combustion vehicle. chrisbrandow [[./thread#^c46751101|chrisbrandow]], identifying themselves as a former California Air Resources Board employee, says modern vehicles are substantially cleaner than early-2000s models while acknowledging warm-up emissions. MBCook [[./thread#^c46750245|MBCook]] also distinguishes ordinary vehicles from particularly unpleasant malfunctioning ones. Neither supplies comparative emission measurements.
- Automatic stop/start is an incomplete answer to cyclists' complaints. memen [[./thread#^c46751931|memen]] says modern cars have no exhaust while stopped, but adev\_ [[./thread#^c46752544|adev\_]] replies that heating demand and a cold engine can disable shutdown. lukan [[./thread#^c46751990|lukan]] points out eventual engine restart, and amrocha [[./thread#^c46752173|amrocha]] notes that the fleet includes older vehicles. The objection therefore depends on operating conditions and fleet composition.
- Neighborhood improvement does not itself settle pollution displacement. tbrownaw [[./thread#^c46750181|tbrownaw]] explicitly distinguishes the study's local question from upstream pollution. freeopinion [[./thread#^c46756182|freeopinion]] raises electricity generated in Utah for Los Angeles; Rebelgecko [[./thread#^c46756277|Rebelgecko]] says California measurements would capture that pollution only if it reached California. This limits the study's geographic inference without negating a local benefit.
- High EV adoption is not presented as sufficient for clean urban air everywhere. In reply to the Beijing improvement account, rikima\_ [[./thread#^c46760823|rikima\_]] reports worsening smog in Chengdu and Chongqing despite substantial EV adoption and contrasts their industrial circumstances with Beijing. This is a contextual counterexample, not a quantified comparison controlling for other pollution sources.
- Pleasantness and healthfulness are different judgments. rubidium [[./thread#^c46750459|rubidium]] remembers liking some vehicle-exhaust smells as a child; margalabargala [[./thread#^c46750672|margalabargala]] replies that protecting children concerns health rather than enjoyment. Consequently, neither liking an odor nor becoming accustomed to it establishes low exposure or safety.
- The person who notices exhaust most acutely need not have the greatest overall exposure. scott\_w [[./thread#^c46752546|scott\_w]] cites reporting claiming cyclists inhale less pollution than drivers, replying within lukan's account of unpleasant roadside exposure [[./thread#^c46751657|lukan]]. The supplied comments contain no underlying dose measurements or route comparisons, so this does not resolve exposure rankings or invalidate the cyclist's experience.
- An individual's EV does not remove surrounding traffic emissions. digiown [[./thread#^c46750513|digiown]] observes that a child near school will still encounter other people's exhaust. That reply shifts location from didip's garage [[./thread#^c46750378|didip]]; it qualifies the reach of the personal intervention rather than disproving the reported garage benefit.
- The claim that nobody studies tire and brake pollution is directly challenged. lagniappe [[./thread#^c46750476|lagniappe]] raises cancer concerns near busy roads and says the subject is unstudied. dgacmu [[./thread#^c46750514|dgacmu]] supplies a report, and montalbano [[./thread#^c46752135|montalbano]] says tire dust has been studied for decades and links research suggesting some estimates were overstated. The links establish that commenters invoke research, but the underlying studies are not supplied for assessment.

## Assumptions

- This analysis covers story 46749198, part 0. Its substantive exposure evidence centers on the NO2 study excerpt supplied by zahlman [[./thread#^c46750501|zahlman]] and traffic or garage experiences such as didip's [[./thread#^c46750378|didip]]. dotcoma [[./thread#^c46750638|dotcoma]] asks about PM2.5 and PM10, but supplies no readings. This unit contains no household CO2 measurements, demonstrated cognition or sleep effects, cooking-exposure experiments, or wildfire-smoke accounts.
- The study's vehicle category should not be treated as exclusively battery-electric cars. zahlman's quoted passage [[./thread#^c46750501|zahlman]] includes battery-electric, plug-in-hybrid, and fuel-cell vehicles, while excluding heavy delivery trucks and semis. catketch [[./thread#^c46755893|catketch]] explains ZEV terminology as referring to tailpipe emissions; it does not mean the absence of tire particles or upstream emissions.
- Registrations are a proxy for activity, not a direct count of emitting miles displaced. omoikane [[./thread#^c46750411|omoikane]] quotes the study's ZEV registration share increasing from 2.0\% to 5.1\% during 2019–2023. ZeroGravitas [[./thread#^c46751968|ZeroGravitas]] argues that newer cars travel more miles, which could make registration share understate their traffic presence. The export supplies no mileage-weighted comparison or plug-in-hybrid charging behavior for these neighborhoods.
- Smell and visible plumes are observations, not pollutant assays. rootusrootus [[./thread#^c46756241|rootusrootus]] describes repeatedly misattributing nearby vehicles' odors to their own car. cucumber3732842 [[./thread#^c46754320|cucumber3732842]] identifies cold-weather water condensation as one explanation for white plumes, while mrj [[./thread#^c46754986|mrj]] emphasizes persistent bad odors and black grime in the original complaint. These observations cannot identify PM2.5 mass, NO2 concentration, or a specific mechanical fault by themselves.
- Large claims about individual gross emitters are mostly estimates here. HPsquared [[./thread#^c46753712|HPsquared]] cites a remembered Volkswagen emissions discrepancy and speculates that a badly modified vehicle could emit over 100 times as much pollution. nasmorn [[./thread#^c46752613|nasmorn]] gives a conditional calculation in which 1\% of vehicles emitting 100 times as much NOx contribute about half the total. Neither establishes the relevant fleet prevalence or a measured personal exposure multiplier.
- The small-engine comparison is explicitly uncertain. Analemma\_ [[./thread#^c46756551|Analemma\_]] says they lack hard numbers for a remembered claim that California lawn equipment exceeds cars and trucks in certain emissions. anamax [[./thread#^c46761682|anamax]] questions the characterization of US lawnmowers as two-stroke. This is a lead about another outdoor source, not a verified emissions inventory.
- Tire replacement intervals are an uncertain proxy for airborne particle emissions. windexh8er [[./thread#^c46754468|windexh8er]] argues that EV-specific compounds complicate comparisons; Tostino [[./thread#^c46754317|Tostino]] explicitly does not know how those compounds affect particle emissions. CraigJPerry [[./thread#^c46756955|CraigJPerry]] asks whether weight and wear scale linearly and how tire design changes the relationship. Equal service life therefore does not establish equal PM2.5 exposure.
- Reply ancestry matters when interpreting criticism. davidw initially calls EVs a step up [[./thread#^c46750277|davidw]] and repeats that qualification when accused of opposing improvement [[./thread#^c46756322|davidw]]. lagniappe objects to being characterized as making a disingenuous anti-EV argument [[./thread#^c46751229|lagniappe]]. Their concerns about residual pollution should not be rewritten as advocacy for keeping combustion vehicles.

## Conditions

- Repair affordability constrains reductions from existing vehicles. MBCook [[./thread#^c46750267|MBCook]] describes a possible \$1,500 repair as unaffordable even when continued operation wastes fuel, and doubts that fines would help. SoftTalker [[./thread#^c46750319|SoftTalker]] reports keeping a car over 20 years old and refusing a \$30,000–40,000 replacement. These accounts explain why replacement or maintenance recommendations may fail despite acknowledged pollution.
- EV affordability varies by market, vehicle, and household. leguminous [[./thread#^c46756393|leguminous]] reports buying a Kona Electric below \$35,000 and seeing used Bolts below \$15,000 and ID.4s below \$20,000, while acknowledging the absence of a \$5,000 beater equivalent. jillesvangurp [[./thread#^c46752461|jillesvangurp]] cites European options at €15,000–20,000, but otikik [[./thread#^c46753314|otikik]] replies that even €10,000 is a stretch for many buyers. These are not interchangeable affordability contexts.
- Charging access affects whether switching vehicles is a practical exposure-reduction option. matt3210 [[./thread#^c46752649|matt3210]] reports being unable to install a charger in an apartment. SirMaster [[./thread#^c46759286|SirMaster]] describes frequent work driving without shopping stops that conveniently accommodate charging. pornel [[./thread#^c46774317|pornel]] reports using chargers at travel stops and discusses street charging, while recognizing that no home, workplace, or destination charging creates a difficult case.
- Cold weather affects the emissions encountered around traffic. adrianN [[./thread#^c46750343|adrianN]] argues that catalysts may not reach operating temperature during short winter trips. adev\_ [[./thread#^c46752544|adev\_]] reports frequently seeing more than half of cars with engines running at cold-weather traffic lights and links this to heating and warm-up needs. These are an operating-condition claim and an informal observation, not a measured winter emissions factor.
- Inspection regimes and enforcement vary geographically. MBCook [[./thread#^c46754862|MBCook]] describes an unnamed US state without recurring emissions checks. realityking [[./thread#^c46752271|realityking]] describes German biennial checks against original vehicle specifications, with formerly\_proven [[./thread#^c46753151|formerly\_proven]] noting an oldtimer exemption. aziaziazi [[./thread#^c46752767|aziaziazi]] recalls certificates issued despite their parents' vehicles being noncompliant, and encom [[./thread#^c46753624|encom]] describes ineffective enforcement and inspection evasion.
- Cleaner household heating can impose costs on residents. In the Beijing-related coal-heating discussion, yanhangyhy [[./thread#^c46750582|yanhangyhy]] says local authorities lowered gas prices and increased subsidies after reports of hardship, but believes gas still costs more than coal. MengerSponge's question [[./thread#^c46750434|MengerSponge]] connects the air-quality intervention to villagers' ability to afford heat; the reply is not a household expenditure study.
- The location and control of electricity-generation emissions matter to people near those sources. KaiserPro [[./thread#^c46753963|KaiserPro]] makes benefits conditional on a country's generation and pollution-control practices. kakacik [[./thread#^c46757382|kakacik]] objects to treating rural areas as consequence-free destinations for pollution. trimethylpurine [[./thread#^c46768031|trimethylpurine]] disputes using national grid averages to describe a particular location, although the export supplies no local exposure calculation validating their broader conclusion.
- Adoption and apparent traffic composition vary within a region. justaboutanyone [[./thread#^c46750770|justaboutanyone]] says EVs seem more common than one in twenty on California's 101/280 corridors; omoikane [[./thread#^c46750993|omoikane]] points to higher Santa Clara County sales. The 5.1\% figure quoted by omoikane [[./thread#^c46750411|omoikane]] belongs to the study period ending in 2023, not a current traffic count for every California neighborhood.
- Replacing car trips depends on available transport and household needs. JamesTRexx [[./thread#^c46753677|JamesTRexx]] reports living without a car for ten years with Dutch public transport. cbeach [[./thread#^c46759801|cbeach]] describes preferring an EV in a countryside town for carrying children and shopping. SideburnsOfDoom [[./thread#^c46752985|SideburnsOfDoom]] emphasizes the difference between dense areas with abundant alternatives and dispersed communities. These contexts limit universal prescriptions to reduce traffic emissions through mode changes.

## Lived experiences

- MBCook [[./thread#^c46750211|MBCook]] reports encountering strongly smelling or visibly smoky vehicles even in an affluent area after five years of EV ownership. Their clearest intervention account is activated-carbon cabin filtration: they describe not smelling fresh asphalt or a dirty vehicle until lowering the window [[./thread#^c46750245|MBCook]], followed by odors returning as the filter wore out [[./thread#^c46754844|MBCook]]. This is firsthand evidence of improved odor comfort and a window-opening tradeoff, without pollutant readings.
- didip [[./thread#^c46750378|didip]] describes their child no longer smelling gasoline and exhaust in the garage before school. kristofferR [[./thread#^c46754578|kristofferR]] reports that Oslo parking garages have become much more pleasant over ten years. Both accounts concern perceived conditions in enclosed or partly enclosed vehicle spaces; neither includes ventilation rates, gas concentrations, or health measurements.
- lukan [[./thread#^c46751657|lukan]] describes winter cycling behind combustion vehicles as unpleasant and following EVs as a relief. rpozarickij [[./thread#^c46755776|rpozarickij]] reports habitually holding their breath briefly when a smelly vehicle passes, despite living in a comparatively clean city. These accounts demonstrate discomfort and exposure concern, not measured dose reductions from breath-holding or documented illness.
- 1970-01-01 [[./thread#^c46749908|1970-01-01]] reports that a passing diesel truck spoiled the air around a frozen lake for several minutes while they were skating. dpc050505 [[./thread#^c46758891|dpc050505]] describes dust attributed to tires beside four lanes of heavy traffic as comparably unpleasant to exhaust, and xnx [[./thread#^c46773471|xnx]] singles out bus exhaust. The experiences are sensory; pollutant identity and concentration were not instrumentally established.
- srmarm [[./thread#^c46752760|srmarm]] contrasts a home city with a low-emission zone against a recent visit to Athens, where old vehicles' pollution was noticeably unpleasant. They compare the change in sensitivity to noticing cigarettes more after public smoking restrictions. This illustrates perceived baseline changes, without isolating regulation, vehicle mix, or meteorology.
- yanhangyhy [[./thread#^c46750406|yanhangyhy]] describes moving to Beijing in 2015, buying an air purifier, preparing winter masks, and once wearing a gas mask outdoors during an exceptionally bad episode. By 2026 they report only a few bad winter days and improvement with wind. They attribute improvement to both industrial changes and EV adoption. The account conveys substantial improvement in daily comfort, but does not quantify purifier effectiveness or isolate the causes.
- DrProtic [[./thread#^c46753583|DrProtic]] recalls pollution readings of 250–300 during several weeks of an early-COVID curfew and attributes the persistence largely to home heating. They also report worse winters and relatively acceptable summers. The comment does not identify the location, pollutant, index, instrument, or units, so those numbers cannot be treated as PM2.5 concentrations or specifically as Beijing measurements.
- Mikasa1 [[./thread#^c46750342|Mikasa1]] reports an AQI of 3 near LHR and attributes it to a ban on gasoline cars. simonbarker87 [[./thread#^c46751975|simonbarker87]] replies that, assuming LHR means Heathrow, they recently drove and parked a combustion vehicle there. The air-quality observation remains a report with an unspecified source and scale, while its proposed explanation is directly contradicted by another firsthand account.
- Tire-maintenance experiences conflict. jwr [[./thread#^c46753224|jwr]] reports retaining original tires on a 2021 EV, while alternating winter and summer sets. cbeach [[./thread#^c46752067|cbeach]] reports two tire replacements over six years and original pads and discs after ten years. nielsbot [[./thread#^c46774525|nielsbot]] reports replacing tires at about 32,000 miles and guesses that is half an ICE tire lifetime. Mileage, compounds, driving style, and comparison vehicles are not standardized.
- catketch [[./thread#^c46755893|catketch]] reports that one EV can go days without friction braking and another occasionally applies the brakes automatically to prevent rust. petethepig [[./thread#^c46750709|petethepig]] similarly reports almost never using conventional brakes. These experiences support reduced friction-brake use in those vehicles, but do not measure brake-particle emissions.
- Some accounts concern noise and general outdoor comfort rather than measured air chemistry. apatheticonion [[./thread#^c46750089|apatheticonion]] describes Bangkok traffic pollution and engine noise loud enough to interrupt conversation. presentation [[./thread#^c46750104|presentation]] describes Shanghai's former French Concession as quieter with extensive electrification. baby [[./thread#^c46750810|baby]] reports a favorable contrast between Boracay and other Philippine islands. None supplies a PM2.5 or NO2 comparison.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Causal attribution remains unestablished by the supplied excerpt. braincat31415 [[./thread#^c46750259|braincat31415]] asks whether reduced COVID-era commuting was excluded. zahlman [[./thread#^c46750501|zahlman]] regards the quoted neighborhood-registration and satellite methodology as sufficient, and braincat31415 accepts the explanation [[./thread#^c46750641|braincat31415]]. However, that passage does not specify statistical controls or sensitivity analyses; the exchange cannot establish whether or how the full study handled the concern.
- Measurement reliability cannot be assessed in detail from this export. zahlman [[./thread#^c46750501|zahlman]] identifies TROPOMI but supplies no uncertainty estimates, calibration discussion, or comparison with street-level and indoor measurements. Mikasa1's AQI of 3 [[./thread#^c46750342|Mikasa1]] and DrProtic's 250–300 report [[./thread#^c46753583|DrProtic]] lack compatible scales and instrument details. There are no firsthand PM2.5 or CO2 sensor comparisons from which to judge consumer-sensor reliability.
- Health claims exceed the supplied outcome evidence. lagniappe [[./thread#^c46750476|lagniappe]] raises cancer and other disease near busy roads, while zdragnar [[./thread#^c46750773|zdragnar]] asserts substantial harm from inhaled exhaust particles. The export provides neither disease-rate estimates nor personal clinical before-and-after outcomes. Sensory relief reported by lukan [[./thread#^c46751657|lukan]] does not quantify health improvement, and this unit cannot resolve effects on cognition or sleep.
- The net PM2.5 and PM10 consequences of electrification remain unresolved here. dotcoma [[./thread#^c46750638|dotcoma]] asks whether heavier EVs increase brake-related particles; nielsbot [[./thread#^c46750706|nielsbot]] replies with regeneration. IngvarLynn [[./thread#^c46755277|IngvarLynn]] emphasizes substantial non-exhaust PM10, whereas dgacmu [[./thread#^c46750514|dgacmu]] describes a likely overall benefit and montalbano [[./thread#^c46752135|montalbano]] cites downward revisions to tire-pollution estimates. The comments do not provide matched measurements of particle mass, size distribution, airborne fraction, or inhaled dose.
- The regeneration dispute is specifically about driving behavior as well as hardware. margalabargala [[./thread#^c46771047|margalabargala]] argues that repeated braking and acceleration can replace slowing through air resistance, increasing tire wear; tzs [[./thread#^c46751389|tzs]] questions whether small variations materially matter. fafac [[./thread#^c46791076|fafac]] reports genuine coasting modes in a Polestar 2 and Mustang Mach-E, contradicting the earlier universal claim that none exist. margalabargala later concedes that very low automatic regeneration essentially prevents their proposed effect [[./thread#^c46755812|margalabargala]]. No comparative wear or airborne-particle experiment settles its magnitude.
- The export cannot determine whether local improvements redistribute exposure or reduce it across all affected communities. Rebelgecko [[./thread#^c46756277|Rebelgecko]] describes California-only measurement coverage, while rootusrootus [[./thread#^c46756210|rootusrootus]] answers the Utah-power-plant question by referring more generally to studies considering coal and better plant controls. feverzsj [[./thread#^c46751639|feverzsj]] additionally alleges lead and lithium exposure from battery recycling, but supplies no measurements or exposure pathway. These comments do not establish the health burden near generating, mining, or recycling sites.
- The prevalence and causes of gross emitters are disputed. MBCook [[./thread#^c46750324|MBCook]] mostly perceives old or poorly maintained cars; Braxton1980 [[./thread#^c46750857|Braxton1980]] emphasizes deliberate catalyst removal; ehaliewicz2 [[./thread#^c46761419|ehaliewicz2]] says removal is uncommon in their enthusiast circles. Der\_Einzige [[./thread#^c46750505|Der\_Einzige]] knows people who swap exhausts for inspections, while Aurornis [[./thread#^c46755071|Aurornis]] rejects extrapolation from that circle to Americans generally. No representative inspection or roadside-emissions sample resolves these accounts.
- Claims of safe indoor combustion are unsupported in the supplied evidence. ErroneousBosh [[./thread#^c46753572|ErroneousBosh]] says propane-powered equipment leaves only CO2 and water to ventilate away, and describes their own LPG conversion elsewhere [[./thread#^c46756181|ErroneousBosh]], but supplies no exhaust or indoor-air measurements. driverdan [[./thread#^c46750823|driverdan]] likewise asserts that a minute of garage idling causes no problems without specifying concentration or ventilation. These comments establish neither safe operating conditions nor a demonstrated ventilation requirement.
- The practical window and filtration tradeoff is only partly documented. MBCook [[./thread#^c46750245|MBCook]] reports traffic odors becoming apparent immediately after opening a previously filtered cabin, while torginus [[./thread#^c46752084|torginus]] distinguishes particle and odor filtration. There are no paired PM2.5, NO2, or CO2 readings showing the exposure change, filter breakthrough, or replacement interval. ErroneousBosh's proposal to ventilate combustion spaces [[./thread#^c46753572|ErroneousBosh]] is not a tested home intervention; this unit cannot establish when opening household windows helps CO2 or worsens traffic or wildfire-smoke exposure.
