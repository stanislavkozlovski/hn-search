# [Electric cars are not the answer to air pollution, says UK adviser](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=14927995)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants debate whether electric cars meaningfully address urban air pollution and how they compare with reducing car use through public transit, cycling, and shared vehicles. Topics include grid emissions, tire and brake particles, regenerative braking, vehicle weight, and the practical limits of autonomous car clubs, including empty trips, congestion, accessibility, storage, and vehicle cleanliness.
Tags: electric vehicles, air pollution, transportation, public transit, regenerative braking, car sharing

## Relevance

Although much of the discussion concerns transport choices, it addresses outdoor vehicle-related pollution, brake and tire particles, and the exposure benefit of moving combustion away from streets. It offers limited firsthand exposure evidence, so its connection to the question is narrower than the indoor-air discussions. ## Arguments

- dTal [[./thread#^c14947601|dTal]], replying to zamalek’s skepticism about electricity generated from fossil fuels [[./thread#^c14933784|zamalek]], argues that moving combustion away from homes, businesses, and sidewalks can improve urban exposure. stuaxo [[./thread#^c14935173|stuaxo]] adds that centralized generation may make pollution easier to control. These are arguments about pollution location and mitigation, without measured exposure reductions.
- anovikov [[./thread#^c14929987|anovikov]] and skybrian [[./thread#^c14930394|skybrian]] argue that regenerative braking reduces mechanical brake use and therefore should reduce wear pollution. mikeash [[./thread#^c14930430|mikeash]] emphasizes that rarely using mechanical brakes could provide much of the pollution benefit of eliminating them.
- stuaxo [[./thread#^c14935173|stuaxo]] advocates reducing car use while electrifying remaining vehicles. nkkollaw [[./thread#^c14929798|nkkollaw]] similarly favors walking or cycling where feasible and EVs where driving remains necessary. devy [[./thread#^c14929371|devy]] interprets the article as advocating fewer cars, especially in congested megacities, rather than opposing electrification itself.
- dade\_ [[./thread#^c14929855|dade\_]] proposes replacing hazardous tire and brake materials, while deckar01 [[./thread#^c14930062|deckar01]] suggests mechanical designs producing less friction. These are proposed approaches to non-exhaust pollution; neither reports a tested replacement or measured improvement.
- For smoke inside shared vehicles, kylehotchkiss [[./thread#^c14930082|kylehotchkiss]] raises a hypothetical concern about passengers smoking without a driver enforcing rules. Lio [[./thread#^c14930187|Lio]] proposes smoke alarms, interrupted journeys, and cleaning fines; DanBC [[./thread#^c14930225|DanBC]] proposes passenger identification and bans. These are enforcement proposals, without evidence of improved cabin air.

## Counterarguments

- Electrification leaves non-exhaust sources. hokkos [[./thread#^c14929296|hokkos]] supports EVs but acknowledges their greater weight; CalChris [[./thread#^c14929465|CalChris]] illustrates this with claimed curb weights of about 2,400 pounds for a Nissan Versa and 3,350 for a Leaf. These figures do not establish the resulting particle emissions or whether additional wear offsets reduced exhaust and brake pollution.
- Responding to dade\_’s comparison with bicycle tire wear [[./thread#^c14929855|dade\_]], zython [[./thread#^c14930144|zython]] argues that a bicycle-and-rider system weighing roughly 100 kilograms produces much less wear than a car. The rebuttal highlights vehicle mass and scale, but supplies no particle measurements.
- Lio’s suggestion that regeneration could eliminate brake discs and pads [[./thread#^c14930132|Lio]] is challenged by mikeash [[./thread#^c14930334|mikeash]]: batteries must accept very high braking power, and mechanical brakes provide inexpensive redundancy. Coincoin [[./thread#^c14933902|Coincoin]] proposes capacitors but still expects mechanical brakes for emergencies. Their continued presence does not establish how much dust they produce in ordinary driving.
- 6d6b73 [[./thread#^c14929287|6d6b73]] [[./thread#^c14929425|6d6b73]] argues that shared autonomous cars could increase pollution through empty repositioning trips. More broadly, strange\_quark [[./thread#^c14930264|strange\_quark]] counters mohaine’s prediction of substantially improved automated traffic flow [[./thread#^c14930202|mohaine]] with persistent bottlenecks and potentially increased car use. sokoloff [[./thread#^c14930989|sokoloff]] anticipates shifts away from transit and greater willingness to commute farther. None measures resulting air quality.

## Assumptions

- hokkos [[./thread#^c14929296|hokkos]] asserts that brake and tire particles are larger, and therefore less dangerous, than exhaust particles. The comment supplies no particle-size distribution, toxicological evidence, or exposure measurements. It cannot establish the relative PM2.5 contribution or health risk of these sources.
- The regeneration argument uses brake use and pad longevity as proxies for particulate emissions. simonbarker87 [[./thread#^c14929938|simonbarker87]] supplies driving impressions and a dealership anecdote, rather than airborne particle measurements. xiphias [[./thread#^c14930416|xiphias]] claims the linked paper omits regenerative braking, but the supplied export contains no methods or results with which to verify that criticism.
- The thread’s numerical fractions should not be treated as exposure estimates. sammoth [[./thread#^c14929347|sammoth]] uses halving pollution as a sarcastic example; AstralStorm [[./thread#^c14929625|AstralStorm]] asserts that cars account for a quarter of unspecified total pollution; sduclos [[./thread#^c14929364|sduclos]] presents an energy argument explicitly assuming away friction. None establishes a measured reduction in PM2.5, NO2, or CO2 exposure.
- Automation forecasts depend on demand assumptions. bryondowd [[./thread#^c14931598|bryondowd]] explicitly considers unchanged demand when arguing that closer vehicle spacing could reduce congestion. bogidon [[./thread#^c14930414|bogidon]] instead expects convenience and lower costs to increase car use. Cleaner air cannot be inferred from their competing traffic-capacity predictions alone.

## Conditions

- London’s ownership costs and transport alternatives matter: dm319 [[./thread#^c14928701|dm319]] attributes reduced car ownership to taxation, insurance, parking difficulties, and limited usefulness relative to public transport. chriswarbo [[./thread#^c14929815|chriswarbo]] interprets transport taxes as incentives translating environmental objectives into personal financial decisions. Neither demonstrates that environmental concern motivates the observed choices.
- Electricity supply and generation location change the comparison. dm319 [[./thread#^c14928701|dm319]] makes overall environmental performance conditional on the grid, while StillBored [[./thread#^c14933023|StillBored]] raises heavy EVs powered substantially by coal. dTal [[./thread#^c14947601|dTal]] and stuaxo [[./thread#^c14935173|stuaxo]] emphasize benefits from relocating combustion away from urban streets. The discussion supplies no comparison of exposure near roads versus near power stations.
- Alternatives depend on geography and service quality. devy [[./thread#^c14929371|devy]] emphasizes dense megacities; barrkel [[./thread#^c14929879|barrkel]] describes frequent underground services as acceptable but waiting and unreliable services as deterrents. baldfat [[./thread#^c14929584|baldfat]] identifies out-of-town trips, and Sharlin [[./thread#^c14929871|Sharlin]] identifies transporting bulky things, as reasons shared cars might supplement buses.
- Cost, exertion, and weather constrain cycling as a replacement for driving. giobox [[./thread#^c14929682|giobox]] sees e-bikes as cheaper and less burdensome to operate than cars. baldfat [[./thread#^c14931193|baldfat]] reports avoiding cycling in warmer conditions because of sweating, while stevekemp [[./thread#^c14934812|stevekemp]] describes people cycling locally on ice at −25°C. These concern transport feasibility and thermal comfort, without demonstrating pollution-related symptoms.

## Lived experiences

- simonbarker87 [[./thread#^c14929938|simonbarker87]] reports firsthand that a Model S test drive involved stronger deceleration and much less brake use than usual. The claim that roughly ten UK Model S cars had exceeded 150,000 miles without replacing brake pads came from the sales representative and is secondhand. Neither account measures airborne particles or health effects.
- simonbarker87 [[./thread#^c14929978|simonbarker87]] describes a business partner who disliked arriving sweaty after conventional cycling, then largely replaced car journeys under five miles with an electric bicycle. This is an account about another person’s behavior and comfort, without measured exposure or evidence that pollution caused the discomfort.
- 6d6b73 [[./thread#^c14929287|6d6b73]] uses a six-mile round-trip commute to illustrate the potential mileage penalty of sharing cars. After revelation questions driving such a short distance [[./thread#^c14930099|revelation]], 6d6b73 explains that the route involves a highway on which walking is unavailable [[./thread#^c14933131|6d6b73]]. The route constraint is personal testimony; the shared-car mileage comparison remains hypothetical.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The net particulate benefit remains unquantified. Brakenshire [[./thread#^c14930255|Brakenshire]] explicitly asks whether anyone has quantified the regenerative-braking difference, and the supplied replies provide no emissions measurement answering that question. hokkos [[./thread#^c14929296|hokkos]] acknowledges both reduced braking and greater vehicle weight, without resolving their combined effect on airborne particles.
- Whether sharing reduces on-road pollution remains conditional. headhunter [[./thread#^c14929329|headhunter]] proposes dispatching the nearest available vehicle; DiThi [[./thread#^c14929330|DiThi]] proposes shared per-mile costs to encourage pooling. Against concerns about extra mileage, DanBC [[./thread#^c14930250|DanBC]] claims journey starts are especially polluting and one longer journey can outperform two short ones. No supplied evidence compares occupancy, repositioning, powertrain, starts, emissions, and personal exposure together.
- This source unit provides no PM2.5, NO2, or CO2 concentration measurements, sensor calibration or reliability comparisons, or reported health, cognition, or sleep outcomes attributable to air pollution. hokkos [[./thread#^c14929296|hokkos]] offers a relative-hazard opinion, and devy [[./thread#^c14929371|devy]] invokes public health generally. Neither supplies health-outcome evidence.
- This unit cannot resolve when opening windows helps or worsens exposure, or compare household ventilation, filtration, cooking emissions, and wildfire smoke. Its enclosed-space smoke discussion is kylehotchkiss’s hypothetical shared-car concern [[./thread#^c14930082|kylehotchkiss]]. Lio’s proposed smoke alarms [[./thread#^c14930187|Lio]] have no reported detection accuracy, installation experience, or measured exposure benefit.
