# [U.S. safety agency to consider ban on gas stoves amid health fears](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=34310218)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants debate whether gas stoves should be restricted over indoor pollution and health concerns, with alternatives including effective outdoor-venting hoods, ventilation requirements, incentives, and induction cooking. They compare cooking performance, controls, cookware compatibility, installation costs, electrical capacity, household affordability, and resilience during power outages. The discussion also addresses evidence and risk, political framing and regulation, ventilation practices in different housing designs, and how pollution burdens may vary across communities.
Tags: gas stoves, indoor air quality, induction cooking, ventilation, public health, regulation, energy policy

## Relevance

The discussion directly covers indoor pollution from gas cooking, including NO2 and other combustion products, concerns about health, and disagreements over the strength and interpretation of evidence. It compares ventilation, induction, and gas cooking, with practical tradeoffs involving hood performance, retrofit costs, cooking preferences, and backup power. ## Arguments

- Removing combustion can reduce one source of indoor pollution while leaving cooking emissions to manage. Someone1234 [[./thread#^c34312644|Someone1234]] distinguishes particles produced by cooking food from additional gaseous byproducts of burning natural gas. Janalsncm [[./thread#^c34319784|janalsncm]] argues that an improvement need not eliminate every indoor pollutant to be worthwhile. Neither supplies a measured before-and-after fuel-switch comparison.
- Exterior exhaust, ideally activated automatically, is a proposed practical improvement. Someone1234 [[./thread#^c34310666|Someone1234]] recommends connecting gas-stove inspections with actual outdoor venting and a gas-on/fan-on habit. SAI\_Peregrinus [[./thread#^c34316440|SAI\_Peregrinus]] proposes automatic hood operation to remove dependence on that habit, consistent with FiloSottile's [[./thread#^c34310839|FiloSottile]] objection to relying entirely on behavior change. These are design proposals, not demonstrated exposure reductions.
- Cooking itself can justify ventilation regardless of fuel. Endisneigh [[./thread#^c34310752|endisneigh]] and peteradio [[./thread#^c34310720|peteradio]] argue that electric and induction cooking still need extraction; cute\_boi [[./thread#^c34318894|cute\_boi]] specifically identifies smoke from cooking oils. This supports treating fuel substitution and control of food-generated pollution as complementary improvements.
- Filtration is proposed as a broader intervention against particles from multiple indoor sources. Mensetmanusman [[./thread#^c34310774|mensetmanusman]] suggests subsidizing air filters partly to address dust from furnishings, clothing, and other materials. The comment supplies no particle measurements, filter specifications, or health outcomes, so this remains a proposed benefit rather than a demonstrated result.
- Indoor combustion is discussed as an addition to existing outdoor exposure. UtopiaPunk [[./thread#^c34310984|UtopiaPunk]] quotes an article linking vulnerability to asthma with proximity to roads and industrial facilities; spamizbad [[./thread#^c34310630|spamizbad]] interprets gas-stove pollution as another layer of a cumulative burden. These are secondary-source claims and explanatory arguments, not firsthand traffic or household exposure measurements.
- Induction's proposed benefits include kitchen comfort as well as air quality. Exclusiv [[./thread#^c34321052|exclusiv]] reproduces chefs' accounts of precise control, a less intensely hot kitchen, and fewer burns after adopting induction. These are quoted external accounts, not the commenter's own observations, and contain no pollutant measurements.

## Counterarguments

- Endisneigh [[./thread#^c34310580|endisneigh]] argues that inadequate exhaust use, rather than gas ownership alone, could explain much of the attributed harm, citing approximately 20\% proper ventilation and 13\% of childhood asthma attributed to gas stoves. They favor education or automatic outdoor exhaust. U801e [[./thread#^c34318391|u801e]] likewise argues that useful ventilation advice remains useful when delivered by an industry group. Neither establishes how much risk correct exhaust use actually removes.
- Recirculating hoods are not necessarily useless for every purpose. Responding to Someone1234's criticism, adrianmonk [[./thread#^c34319001|adrianmonk]] points to accumulated grease in the filter as evidence that it prevents some deposition elsewhere. That observation supports grease capture; it does not demonstrate removal of combustion gases or effective PM2.5 filtration.
- Particle filtration cannot simply be treated as a complete answer to gas-stove emissions. Someone1234 [[./thread#^c34312750|Someone1234]] specifically says PM2.5 filters do not remove combustion gases and VOCs. Falcolas [[./thread#^c34311932|falcolas]] makes the broader claim that air filters cannot remove NO2. The narrower particle-versus-gas distinction is clearer than a blanket conclusion covering every possible filtration technology.
- An ordinary extractor might not provide sufficient gas-pollutant control. ClumsyPilot [[./thread#^c34310740|ClumsyPilot]] claims domestic extractors cannot remove most NO2 and other emissions; jimhefferon [[./thread#^c34310766|jimhefferon]] immediately requests supporting information. No supporting measurements appear in that reply chain, so this is an unresolved challenge to ventilation-only proposals.
- A potential air-quality improvement does not establish that appliance replacement is every household's best health expenditure. Mhb [[./thread#^c34320797|mhb]] explicitly accepts that replacement could improve air quality while questioning its opportunity cost relative to other uses of the money. This disputes the preferred intervention, rather than establishing that gas exposure is harmless.
- R2vcap [[./thread#^c34318250|r2vcap]] offers a regional counterexample: widespread gas cooking, twice-yearly home gas-safety inspections, and a claimed national life expectancy of 83.4 years in an unnamed East Asian country. This describes a different maintenance context, but national longevity and inspection frequency do not isolate stove-related respiratory risk or establish indoor pollutant concentrations.

## Assumptions

- The evidence unit is story 34310218, part 0. Dang [[./thread#^c34317478|dang]] links other discussions, including one titled about measuring gas-stove pollution, but their underlying measurements and comments are not supplied here. Those titles cannot fill gaps in this unit's evidence.
- Endisneigh's percentages have different denominators: a proportion using ventilation properly and an attributed proportion of childhood asthma are not a comparison of asthma incidence in ventilated versus unventilated homes. Endisneigh [[./thread#^c34310580|endisneigh]] supplies no such cross-tabulation. Throwawaaarrgh [[./thread#^c34321073|throwawaaarrgh]] directly challenges the leap from simple Bayesian reasoning to identifying real causes and effective remedies.
- Multiplegeorges's description of induction as having no emissions [[./thread#^c34310613|multiplegeorges]] needs a source-specific interpretation. It does not establish that preparing food on induction produces no airborne pollution; endisneigh [[./thread#^c34310752|endisneigh]] explicitly argues that electric cooking still requires ventilation. Removing burner combustion and eliminating all cooking emissions are different claims.
- The word filter does not establish fine-particle performance. Someone1234 [[./thread#^c34312644|Someone1234]] includes recirculating hoods among examples of PM2.5 filtering, whereas adrianmonk [[./thread#^c34319001|adrianmonk]] describes basic grease capture. No specifications or fine-particle capture tests establish that the particular hoods discussed perform those functions equally well.
- Stove ownership is not a direct measure of exposure or unequal health burden. Wankle [[./thread#^c34310576|wankle]] cites personal gas-stove ownership while questioning demographic framing; IIAOPSW [[./thread#^c34310786|IIAOPSW]] explains that disproportionate exposure does not mean exclusive exposure. The supplied discussion contains no group-specific measurements that would quantify the contribution of appliance prevalence, ventilation, crowding, or outdoor pollution.

## Conditions

- Retrofit economics depend strongly on access and layout. Sbradford26 [[./thread#^c34310731|sbradford26]] describes a planned conversion with a \$900 induction cooktop, \$153 of wiring, an accessible unfinished basement, and an estimated additional \$500 for an electrician. In the same house, sbradford26 [[./thread#^c34324813|sbradford26]] estimates a basic exterior hood installation could cost under \$100 with six feet of duct, while different joist orientation could increase the expense. These are household-specific estimates, not completed comparative trials.
- Ventilation changes can trigger much larger projects. Seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c34320836|seanmcdirmid]] describes a potential panel upgrade, cabinet changes, microwave relocation, and a claimed 400-CFM limit without additional makeup air, escalating their contemplated renovation toward \$150,000. That figure covers a broader renovation, not just an exhaust hood. Amluto [[./thread#^c34310684|amluto]] also identifies makeup-air requirements and loss of conditioned air as ventilation costs.
- Price comparisons often concern different products and capacities. Jimhefferon [[./thread#^c34310811|jimhefferon]] reports roughly \$4,000 induction versus \$1,000 alternatives among their choices; duskwuff [[./thread#^c34318238|duskwuff]] points to approximately \$50 single-burner units. Smileysteve [[./thread#^c34310663|smileysteve]] distinguishes an existing portable induction burner from the electrical requirements of a desired five- or six-burner installation. These January 2023 examples do not establish a universal price premium or equivalent replacement capability.
- Local building requirements may constrain ventilation choices. Dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c34321665|dzhiurgis]] claims Lithuania's required A+ efficiency standard permits only filtered extractors, not exterior-vented ones; the code interpretation is not verified in the export. Dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c34321765|dzhiurgis]] also suggests a roughly \$200 mini-HRV, but provides no installation, airflow, suitability, or performance evidence.
- Room volume, occupancy, heating practices, and air exchange matter to the exposure argument. UtopiaPunk [[./thread#^c34310984|UtopiaPunk]] quotes claims that smaller, more crowded homes and stove use for supplemental heating increase NO2 risk. Derekp7 [[./thread#^c34311172|derekp7]] wonders whether draftier housing offsets buildup; falcolas [[./thread#^c34311717|falcolas]] replies that drafts can exchange air slowly and unevenly. Neither supplies measured ventilation rates for those housing categories.
- Separating kitchens has social and comfort tradeoffs. Positr0n [[./thread#^c34321234|positr0n]] deliberately chose an open kitchen to converse and supervise children while cooking; grandmczeb [[./thread#^c34319475|grandmczeb]] says San Francisco Victorian kitchens are often opened up to reduce isolation and claustrophobia. Wrp [[./thread#^c34333772|wrp]] describes a friend's Korean apartment combining an open kitchen with a second enclosed, windowed kitchen. None compares pollutant spread across these layouts.
- Conversion barriers include cookware and controls, and some claimed barriers are corrected within the discussion. Derekp7 [[./thread#^c34311137|derekp7]] reports owning nonmagnetic stainless cookware. Steltek [[./thread#^c34310841|Steltek]] eventually retracts an objection about reversible griddles; SAI\_Peregrinus [[./thread#^c34316543|SAI\_Peregrinus]] actually tests a similar griddle successfully on both sides. Sschueller [[./thread#^c34310715|sschueller]] objects to touch controls, while jackvalentine [[./thread#^c34320279|jackvalentine]] reports satisfactory induction knobs. These experiences establish product-specific compatibility and usability differences, not an air-quality comparison.
- Outages create a competing need for cooking and warmth. SAI\_Peregrinus [[./thread#^c34316401|SAI\_Peregrinus]] gives a secondhand account of Buffalo residents heating water on gas stoves during prolonged power loss. Oaktrout [[./thread#^c34318598|oaktrout]] questions the affordability and storm resilience of solar-plus-battery alternatives, and jacquesm [[./thread#^c34321128|jacquesm]] notes grid dependence of many grid-tied inverters. No exposure measurements establish the air safety of the emergency stove-heating arrangements.

## Lived experiences

- Cocoa19 [[./thread#^c34318822|cocoa19]] reports that an Airthings monitor showed VOC and CO2 spikes after cooking, worse when using the oven. This is the clearest firsthand air-monitor observation in the supplied part. It appears as a reply to another person's discovery of a recirculating hood, but supplies no concentrations, timing, sensor model, calibration checks, or associated symptoms.
- Bandyaboot [[./thread#^c34318151|bandyaboot]] had assumed their gas-stove fan exhausted outdoors, then checked and discovered air blowing out of the unit's top back into the room. They attributed its loudness to the restricted discharge space. The account demonstrates mistaken understanding of the installed ventilation, without measuring its pollutant-removal performance.
- Vanilla\_nut [[./thread#^c34318509|vanilla\_nut]] reports rentals with recirculating hoods, old proprietary filters, and exterior hoods they considered inadequate; they prefer their current electric stove partly because of air-quality concerns and the gas hookup charge. In direct reply, Tagbert [[./thread#^c34320358|Tagbert]] confirms their own US hood exhausts outside by feeling its airflow. The latter limits any inference that US homes universally lack exterior exhaust, but does not establish adequate capture.
- Foobarian [[./thread#^c34310876|foobarian]] describes visiting Italian apartments with separate kitchens where residents opened windows during smoky, high-heat cooking; their own centrally located stove has only a grease-filtering recirculating hood and appears difficult to retrofit. Seanmcdirmid [[./thread#^c34320836|seanmcdirmid]] adds their wife's experience of enclosed, window-adjacent kitchens in China. These are situated household observations, not representative comparisons between countries.
- Scoundreller [[./thread#^c34310847|Scoundreller]] reports that windows in a newer corner condo open only about two inches, with those on one side not opening at all. Their explanation involving building-wide HVAC savings is explicitly speculative. The account establishes limited personal control over window ventilation, not measured indoor pollution or failure of the building's mechanical system.
- Gigachad [[./thread#^c34318746|Gigachad]] avoids smoky home cooking because of a potential \$1,000 fire-department callout. In clarification, Gigachad [[./thread#^c34322748|Gigachad]] describes a high-rise alarm with roughly five minutes to clear smoke before wider evacuation and dispatch; they have triggered the initial alarm but say nobody reached dispatch during their years there. The charge is a reported potential consequence, not an expense they say they paid.
- Cooking preferences remain conflicting firsthand accounts. LargoLasskhyfv [[./thread#^c34314257|LargoLasskhyfv]] prefers induction for speed, control, cleaning, and absence of smell; peteradio [[./thread#^c34310751|peteradio]] prefers gas as a cooking experience. Kshahkshah [[./thread#^c34334439|kshahkshah]] considers induction plus a portable butane burner for wok cooking, while prioritizing replacement of their recirculating hood. That last account is a plan, not evidence of completed exposure reduction.
- Kylehotchkiss [[./thread#^c34321233|kylehotchkiss]] reports returning to a Southeast Asian apartment filled with a strong gas smell from a stove that allowed unlit gas flow. This concerns unburned fuel and acute safety, not measured NO2 or PM2.5 from combustion. Rlpb [[./thread#^c34320112|rlpb]] reports only encountering stoves with flame-failure shutoffs, then acknowledges possible country-dependent regulation [[./thread#^c34323482|rlpb]]. The contrasting accounts concern different equipment and settings.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Sensor reliability and exposure magnitude remain unassessed. Cocoa19 [[./thread#^c34318822|cocoa19]] provides no numerical VOC or CO2 readings, baseline, sampling duration, reference instrument, or calibration information. The account cannot quantify PM2.5 or NO2, separate burner emissions from food emissions, or establish how much a particular ventilation change would help.
- The supplied part does not connect measured exposure with firsthand changes in asthma, cognition, or sleep. Cocoa19 [[./thread#^c34318822|cocoa19]] reports instrument changes without symptoms; endisneigh [[./thread#^c34310580|endisneigh]] discusses a claimed childhood-asthma attribution estimate rather than a personal clinical outcome. Consequently, neither CO2-related cognitive impairment nor sleep improvement can be inferred from this unit.
- When opening windows helps versus worsens exposure is not established. Foobarian [[./thread#^c34310876|foobarian]] reports window use during cooking, UtopiaPunk [[./thread#^c34310984|UtopiaPunk]] quotes concerns about outdoor traffic and industrial pollution, and 2devnull [[./thread#^c34318777|2devnull]] asserts that outdoor NO2 is higher without supplying measurements. There are no paired indoor/outdoor observations or firsthand wildfire-smoke accounts here to resolve that tradeoff.
- The relative contribution of stoves, heaters, water heaters, and outdoor NO2 remains disputed. 2devnull [[./thread#^c34318777|2devnull]] explicitly asks for comparative quantities and suspects stove risk is overstated. Someone1234 [[./thread#^c34314175|Someone1234]] responds that other gas appliances must vent outdoors, but supplies no comparative exposure numbers. Different exhaust arrangements matter to the comparison without settling its magnitude.
- The importance of acute CO risk is contested separately from chronic air-quality concerns. Chongli [[./thread#^c34321646|chongli]] calls preventing dangerous CO buildup the primary purpose of a gas-stove hood; psd1 [[./thread#^c34322139|psd1]] considers hob-related CO risk small compared with a boiler; loandbehold [[./thread#^c34322269|loandbehold]] notes that boilers normally exhaust outside. No concentrations or incident-rate evidence adjudicate these claims.
- Adequate mitigation is not demonstrated for particular pollutants or cooking conditions. Someone1234 [[./thread#^c34312644|Someone1234]] says activated carbon can remove some gaseous byproducts but faces cost and lifetime limitations; mensetmanusman [[./thread#^c34313304|mensetmanusman]] proposes a desiccant column through links without performance results. Amluto [[./thread#^c34310889|amluto]] also exempts low-temperature electric cooking from needing the fan, in reply to peteradio's broader ventilation recommendation [[./thread#^c34310720|peteradio]]. No comparative tests establish these boundaries.
- The health-study disagreement cannot be resolved from the supplied material. Newbee123 [[./thread#^c34310777|newbee123]] questions the cited meta-study and institutional affiliations, while mstef [[./thread#^c34311036|mstef]] alleges confusion between correlation and causation. These objections do not themselves demonstrate a methodological failure, and the export does not reproduce enough study methods or results to evaluate causal claims or effect sizes independently.
- Future affordability is speculative and contested. Multiplegeorges [[./thread#^c34317931|multiplegeorges]] predicts greater supply and variety will reduce induction prices; driverdan [[./thread#^c34310726|driverdan]] predicts reduced competition and increased demand will raise them; Tagbert [[./thread#^c34320375|Tagbert]] expects a transition-period increase followed by later reductions. These forecasts provide no settled basis for choosing between an immediate ventilation retrofit and eventual appliance replacement.
