# [Drivers who keep their windows down are exposed to 80 percent more air pollution](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=24088517)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion ranges from the health effects of traffic pollution and unequal access to clean air to whether cleaner cities, electric vehicles, or less private car use are the best response. Participants debate exhaust versus particulate pollution from tires and brakes, the study’s percentage framing, and how much protection comes from closed windows, cabin filters, masks, and ventilation. Other threads weigh cycling’s health benefits against exposure, traffic design and stop signs, the costs of car dependence, and whether pollution is a larger or less visible public health burden than acute crises.
Tags: air pollution, traffic, public health, electric vehicles, particulate matter, transportation, ventilation

## Relevance

The discussion covers traffic-related particulate exposure in and around cars, compares open windows with recirculation, and raises tradeoffs: recirculation may reduce particles but can increase cabin CO2, while filters and masks have fit and comfort limits. Commenters also question the headline’s percentage comparison and the reliability of claims about particle sources. [[./thread#^c24089169|antisthenes]] [[./thread#^c24089570|Jonnax]] [[./thread#^c24089664|seszett]] [[./thread#^c24090476|guccihat]] [[./thread#^c24091127|k2enemy]] [[./thread#^c24121385|lliiffee]]

## Arguments

- Secondhand study results support reducing roadside particle exposure through recirculation. k2enemy [[./thread#^c24091127|k2enemy]] quotes an abstract reporting that, relative to recirculation, windows-open PM2.5 was up to 589\% higher in Blantyre and PM10 up to 1,020\% higher in São Paulo; fan-on increases reached 385\% and 390\%, respectively, in São Paulo. These are location-specific maxima, not firsthand readings or universal exposure ratios.
- Cabin filtration is presented as an accessible improvement. antisthenes [[./thread#^c24089169|antisthenes]] recommends regular filter replacement and recirculation in heavy traffic. lliiffee [[./thread#^c24121385|lliiffee]] proposes replacing compatible cabin filters with HEPA filters, describing modest additional expense and easy installation, but explicitly says they have not tested effectiveness. ta17711771 [[./thread#^c24091223|ta17711771]] separately suggests checking the exhaust system when fumes repeatedly enter a closed-window car.
- Several commenters favor controlling pollution at its source because cabin protection leaves other road users exposed. calibas [[./thread#^c24089496|calibas]] emphasizes preventing emissions, while bluejekyll [[./thread#^c24089094|bluejekyll]] advocates fewer combustion vehicles. Aachen [[./thread#^c24090036|Aachen]] asks why filtration should protect passengers rather than treat exhaust; davidmurdoch [[./thread#^c24091146|davidmurdoch]] points to catalytic converters and possible efficiency costs. These comments offer source-control arguments, not comparative filtration measurements.
- The health concern extends beyond immediately unpleasant breathing. generj [[./thread#^c24093874|generj]] invokes research linking fine particles with cardiovascular harm and shortened life expectancy, including claims of months lost in Europe and North America and potentially years in China. dustinmoris [[./thread#^c24090227|dustinmoris]] quotes a WHO estimate of seven million annual air-pollution deaths. These are commenters’ reports of external estimates, not verified findings or individual prognoses established by this export.
- Active travel can remain beneficial despite pollution exposure. kqr [[./thread#^c24089667|kqr]] and wongarsu [[./thread#^c24089628|wongarsu]] cite research arguing that cycling and walking benefits generally outweigh pollution-related harms. Their argument concerns net health outcomes under the research’s conditions; it does not establish that cycling reduces inhaled pollution or eliminates respiratory risks.
- Route selection offers another potential improvement. strogonoff [[./thread#^c24089890|strogonoff]] describes consulting AirVisual and Windy to choose routes upwind of busy roads and industrial sources, sometimes accepting extra distance. Mathnerd314 [[./thread#^c24093428|Mathnerd314]] highlights a linked article’s quieter 'green routes.' Neither supplies measured before-and-after personal exposure for such a route change.
- Particle-filtering masks receive qualified support. adrianN [[./thread#^c24089679|adrianN]] says Respro was the only brand for which they personally found fine-particle filtration studies. hnarn [[./thread#^c24090425|hnarn]] recommends ordinary N95 respirators, emphasizes the importance of a tight seal, and explicitly distinguishes particle protection from protection against gases. Finding studies for one brand is not evidence that competing products lack effective filtration.

## Counterarguments

- The headline’s percentage is challenged on arithmetic grounds. guccihat [[./thread#^c24090476|guccihat]] observes that 80\% less exposure with recirculation implies five times the exposure with open windows; k2enemy [[./thread#^c24091127|k2enemy]] agrees that the reverse comparison would be 400\% more, not 80\% more. Proven [[./thread#^c24092836|Proven]] minimizes the result by equating it to driving 80\% longer with closed windows, but that comparison inherits the disputed percentage and supplies no absolute-risk estimate.
- Continuous recirculation is not presented as an unconditional solution. antisthenes [[./thread#^c24089169|antisthenes]] warns of stuffiness, CO2 accumulation, and possible loss of concentration; globular-toast [[./thread#^c24089632|globular-toast]], replying to a complaint about cars defaulting to outside air, identifies CO2 buildup as a reason for that default. Neither reports measured cabin CO2, a concentration threshold, or an observed cognitive effect.
- Eliminating exhaust does not eliminate road particles, but the magnitude is disputed. rconti [[./thread#^c24089150|rconti]] raises brake and tire dust in reply to combustion-vehicle elimination. lowercase1 [[./thread#^c24092444|lowercase1]] claims approximately 30\% of car pollution would remain, while Fezzik [[./thread#^c24092953|Fezzik]] quotes a claim about over half of road-transport particle pollution. Those denominators differ. generj [[./thread#^c24093874|generj]] counters trickstra [[./thread#^c24093157|trickstra]] that comparing total fuel mass with tire and brake loss does not answer the particulate-specific question.
- The dramatic tire-emissions claim receives substantial methodological criticism. olivermarks [[./thread#^c24089357|olivermarks]] reports that tire particulate emissions can be 1,000 times tailpipe emissions. jjcm [[./thread#^c24089346|jjcm]] and malwarebytess [[./thread#^c24089421|malwarebytess]] argue that the quoted 5.8 g/km loss could not persist over normal tire lifetimes. TD-Linux [[./thread#^c24089424|TD-Linux]] says the underlying account involved weighing tires, questions possible track-testing conditions, and stresses that the fraction becoming PM2.5 is unknown.
- Regenerative braking’s practical benefit is contested rather than unanimously accepted. globular-toast [[./thread#^c24089937|globular-toast]] argues that low-speed braking, hard braking, and aggressive driving could preserve substantial friction-brake emissions. varjag [[./thread#^c24090656|varjag]] counters that most urban EV braking is regenerative, and rconti [[./thread#^c24092816|rconti]] describes much stronger deceleration through regeneration than through combustion-engine braking. The exchange contains driving experience and assertions, not direct brake-particle measurements.
- Claims that modern combustion engines are already clean depend on operating conditions. kqr [[./thread#^c24089508|kqr]] describes modern engines as relatively clean; speedgoose [[./thread#^c24089795|speedgoose]] challenges that characterization using winter diesel exhaust and some hybrids. Klinky [[./thread#^c24092743|Klinky]] distinguishes warmed-up gasoline engines with functioning catalysts. fileeditview [[./thread#^c24090220|fileeditview]] cautions that smell itself is not a dependable indicator of harmfulness, since dangerous gases can be odorless.
- Lower total commuting exposure is distinct from lower exposure per minute or a preferred health outcome. Responding to egman\_ekki’s linked cycling comparison [[./thread#^c24090544|egman\_ekki]], gruez [[./thread#^c24091354|gruez]] notes that cyclists finished in 11 minutes, approximately half the motorized travel time, and says the per-minute comparison favored cars and buses. seszett [[./thread#^c24089525|seszett]] notes increased breathing during exercise. monadic2 [[./thread#^c24089757|monadic2]] separately objects that longer life expectancy does not settle their preference about avoiding lung cancer.
- The forecast of an urban exodus is disputed. Barrin92 [[./thread#^c24092657|Barrin92]] and ImaCake [[./thread#^c24089485|ImaCake]] emphasize historical improvements and continued urban advantages, responding to vosper’s prediction [[./thread#^c24089088|vosper]]. dan-robertson [[./thread#^c24090428|dan-robertson]] notes rural agricultural and compost-related pollution, while dashundchen [[./thread#^c24091563|dashundchen]] argues that relocation can create traffic-heavy sprawl. These counterarguments undermine a simple city-bad/countryside-clean distinction without establishing that present urban exposure is harmless.

## Assumptions

- Exposure comparisons require consistent quantities. gruez’s distinction between trip duration and pollution per minute [[./thread#^c24091354|gruez]], together with seszett’s concern about exercise-related breathing [[./thread#^c24089525|seszett]], shows why ambient concentration, trip-integrated exposure, inhaled dose, and eventual health outcomes cannot be treated as interchangeable. The discussion does not provide enough measurements to calculate all four for any participant.
- The numerical EV comparison contains explicit modeling assumptions and an arithmetic inconsistency. Gibbon1 [[./thread#^c24094528|Gibbon1]] reports gasoline PM2.5 factors of 0.008 g/mile from exhaust, 0.003 from brakes, and 0.001 from tires. These sum to 0.012, although the comment subsequently uses 0.011. Its approximately 0.003 g/mile EV estimate assumes brake emissions are halved; it is not a firsthand EV emissions test.
- Alternative explanations for the tire result remain hypotheses. cptskippy [[./thread#^c24089391|cptskippy]] suggests unusually rapid shedding of manufacturing remnants from new tires; londons\_explore [[./thread#^c24089464|londons\_explore]] proposes road-surface abrasion; cameldrv [[./thread#^c24089871|cameldrv]] asks whether oxidation adds mass from air. None quantifies its contribution, and these possibilities do not establish the airborne PM2.5 fraction questioned by TD-Linux [[./thread#^c24089424|TD-Linux]].
- Effective filtration assumes adequate airflow, appropriate media, maintenance, and limited bypass. lliiffee’s HEPA recommendation [[./thread#^c24121385|lliiffee]] supplies no performance test, while beenBoutIT [[./thread#^c24089321|beenBoutIT]] questions filter size and air entering through vehicle gaps without measuring either. hnarn’s mask-fit explanation [[./thread#^c24090425|hnarn]] similarly makes protection dependent on the seal. The export does not establish that every cabin-filter retrofit performs as claimed.
- Broad mortality estimates cannot be added or ranked without resolving their definitions. dustinmoris [[./thread#^c24090227|dustinmoris]] quotes seven million annual pollution deaths; oehtXRwMkIs [[./thread#^c24090250|oehtXRwMkIs]] reports an 8.8 million estimate and compares it with hypertension; generj [[./thread#^c24140636|generj]] cites 4.2 million premature PM-related deaths in 2016. sjg007 [[./thread#^c24092081|sjg007]] replies that pollution can cause pulmonary hypertension, highlighting possible overlap between exposure-related and disease-related categories.
- Sensory observations are useful descriptions of discomfort but do not identify pollutant concentrations. fileeditview [[./thread#^c24090220|fileeditview]] explicitly rejects smell as a reliable toxicity indicator. kqr [[./thread#^c24093981|kqr]] is uncertain whether perceived hand dust reflects pollution or sweating, and emj [[./thread#^c24094098|emj]] offers a similar pollen-season sensation. Likewise, kqr’s suggestion of staying home to avoid transport exposure [[./thread#^c24089860|kqr]] is unsupported here by any comparison of household and outdoor air.

## Conditions

- Money and heat can constrain whether closing windows is practical. read\_if\_gay\_ [[./thread#^c24089774|read\_if\_gay\_]] reports being a student with broken AC and insufficient money to repair it, making open windows necessary during summer. jwilber [[./thread#^c24089487|jwilber]] identifies the same exposure disadvantage for people driving older cars without functioning AC. Advice to close windows therefore assumes access to tolerable cabin cooling.
- Mask choices involve price, exertion, and local temperature. adrianN [[./thread#^c24089679|adrianN]] calls Respro masks expensive, while hnarn [[./thread#^c24090434|hnarn]] gives an approximately five-dollar disposable-N95 alternative in this 2020 discussion. Aachen [[./thread#^c24090009|Aachen]] describes discomfort wearing a mask while walking in hot weather; danielheath [[./thread#^c24090085|danielheath]] finds low-effort commuting compatible with a mask but says a hotter climate might change that assessment.
- The study’s geographic applicability is questioned. netsharc [[./thread#^c24089673|netsharc]] characterizes its locations as poorer cities and asks whether EU/US emissions standards would change the findings, while acknowledging real-world emissions problems exposed by Dieselgate. speedgoose [[./thread#^c24089836|speedgoose]] disputes the realism of regulatory testing. Neither supplies a comparable cabin study establishing how much the reported ratios change in another country.
- Local sources and seasons matter. tamirzb [[./thread#^c24090835|tamirzb]] identifies crop burning in Punjab and Haryana as a possible major contributor to Delhi’s seasonal pollution; dagmx [[./thread#^c24092808|dagmx]] describes particularly difficult winter air there. seszett [[./thread#^c24089664|seszett]] attributes much local pollution to a harbor, alongside concern about European diesels. dan-robertson [[./thread#^c24090428|dan-robertson]] identifies muck spreading and compost operations as rural sources whose health significance remains unclear in the discussion.
- The relationship between wealth, geography, and mortality is not settled by regional totals. hnarn [[./thread#^c24090398|hnarn]] argues that poorer populations and non-Western regions bear disproportionate burdens. icebraining [[./thread#^c24090521|icebraining]], replying to the cited WHO material, says European regions rank highly per capita, including high-income Europe. This exchange limits any inference that affluent countries have negligible exposure or health burdens.
- Relocation and cleaner transport depend on affordability and timing. vosper [[./thread#^c24089088|vosper]] presents access to cleaner locations as a privilege and later notes that vehicle-fleet replacement can take decades [[./thread#^c24089551|vosper]]. derefr [[./thread#^c24092557|derefr]] proposes coastal cities with favorable winds and reduced local emissions, connecting them speculatively to housing demand. These are forecasts and proposed explanations, not demonstrated clean-air guarantees for coastal or expensive neighborhoods.
- Avoidance options depend on local transport geography. stefan\_ [[./thread#^c24090842|stefan\_]] argues that city residents may have to traverse busy roads before reaching an exercise route; mellosouls [[./thread#^c24092147|mellosouls]] says cars can be the only practical option outside urban areas. strogonoff’s route-selection practice [[./thread#^c24089890|strogonoff]] assumes usable alternatives and some knowledge of local sources and wind, sometimes at the cost of a longer trip.

## Lived experiences

- vosper [[./thread#^c24089088|vosper]] describes smelling or almost tasting exhaust, particularly at traffic lights, and finding Delhi much worse than photographs had conveyed. dagmx [[./thread#^c24092808|dagmx]], who spent the first half of their life there, reports improved health after leaving and breathing difficulty with migraines on returning. These are firsthand symptom and sensory accounts without pollutant measurements or established medical attribution.
- Causality1 [[./thread#^c24092638|Causality1]] reports nose and throat burning during a New York City visit and says rural friends perceived their smaller city’s air as poor. seszett [[./thread#^c24089664|seszett]] describes respiratory irritation during local pollution events and responds by walking or reducing cycling effort. Their preference for nose breathing is a personal coping practice, not demonstrated protection in the supplied evidence.
- Deposits and visible smoke made road pollution tangible for some participants. lostlogin [[./thread#^c24089610|lostlogin]] describes sticky, oily black material damaging cycling clothes and being difficult to remove from a bicycle. mcswell [[./thread#^c24094246|mcswell]] recounts driving through smoke from a motorcycle deliberately spinning its tire. Neither observation establishes airborne particle size, inhaled dose, or the usual contribution of tire wear.
- Jonnax [[./thread#^c24090062|Jonnax]] says a Respro Ultralight makes cycling behind large lorries less unpleasant and acknowledges that it does not block everything. They initially felt less able to cycle far with a mask but later adapted [[./thread#^c24090090|Jonnax]]; their explanation involving lung exercise is an interpretation rather than a tested mechanism. frandroid [[./thread#^c24089837|frandroid]] recalls only being able to commute along major Toronto roads during smog-alert periods with a mask.
- Owners provide concrete accounts supporting reduced friction-brake use. tobyhinloopen [[./thread#^c24090724|tobyhinloopen]] reports an EV retaining rusty brake discs after commuting, occasionally braking deliberately to clean them. Tade0 [[./thread#^c24092252|Tade0]] describes persistent rotor rust after five years of hybrid driving, including heavy traffic. robotresearcher [[./thread#^c24091996|robotresearcher]] often completes a commute using regeneration alone. These observations support reduced brake use in those vehicles, but do not measure emitted PM.
- tasssko [[./thread#^c24091042|tasssko]] says exhaust smells discourage sunroof use, that built-in HEPA filtration influenced their vehicle purchase, and that London roads seemed cleaner during school holidays over a decade of motorcycling. Their conclusion that school runs caused most pollution exceeds the supplied observational evidence. giardini [[./thread#^c24092266|giardini]] reports a cabin filter consistently dirtier than the engine filter, without measuring air concentrations or filtration efficiency.
- Opening windows has comfort and awareness benefits and costs independent of pollution. cortesoft [[./thread#^c24089068|cortesoft]] reports unpleasant ear-pressure effects above roughly 30 mph; viraptor [[./thread#^c24089098|viraptor]] says these depend on vehicle shape. at\_a\_remove [[./thread#^c24093313|at\_a\_remove]] prefers slightly cracked windows to hear surrounding cars. pacifika [[./thread#^c24090416|pacifika]] suggests opening a second window for the pressure problem, but provides no exposure measurement for that adjustment.
- intsunny [[./thread#^c24089332|intsunny]] reports only taking ride-hail trips with a window at least halfway open during the pandemic, explicitly treating the decision as competing risks. Terretta [[./thread#^c24093889|Terretta]] voices the same tension between recirculated AC, outside air, closed windows, and infection concerns. Neither reports infection outcomes or simultaneous particle and CO2 measurements.

## Unresolved disagreements

- No optimal ventilation strategy or switching interval is established. k2enemy’s quoted study results [[./thread#^c24091127|k2enemy]] favor recirculation for roadside particles, while antisthenes [[./thread#^c24089169|antisthenes]] warns about accumulated CO2 and concentration, and intsunny [[./thread#^c24089332|intsunny]] prioritizes outside-air exchange during shared rides. Opening windows may serve ventilation or comfort while worsening traffic-particle exposure; this unit does not quantify the combined tradeoff.
- Sensor reliability and absolute personal exposure remain largely unanswered. k2enemy [[./thread#^c24091127|k2enemy]] supplies relative results from an abstract, and strogonoff [[./thread#^c24089890|strogonoff]] uses public AQI and weather tools, but no commenter provides firsthand PM2.5, NO2, or CO2 concentration logs, sensor calibration, or instrument comparisons. generj [[./thread#^c24093874|generj]] discusses NO2 through a regulatory comparison rather than a personal measurement. Sensitivity to local plumes and correspondence with an individual’s inhaled dose cannot be assessed here.
- The net fine-particle benefit of different vehicles remains disputed. olivermarks [[./thread#^c24089357|olivermarks]] emphasizes EV tire wear, whereas timthorn [[./thread#^c24089412|timthorn]] questions whether reported problems concern Teslas rather than EVs generally, and saagarjha [[./thread#^c24091518|saagarjha]] contrasts a lighter Leaf with heavier, sportier cars. Combined with TD-Linux’s uncertainty about the PM2.5 fraction [[./thread#^c24089424|TD-Linux]], the thread does not resolve the balance among regeneration, vehicle weight, driving style, road wear, and particle size.
- Vehicle-interior emissions are raised but not answered. strogonoff [[./thread#^c24089853|strogonoff]] asks whether materials used inside cars create overlooked chemical exposure. lliiffee’s proposed HEPA upgrade [[./thread#^c24121385|lliiffee]] supplies no test addressing that concern, and hnarn [[./thread#^c24090425|hnarn]] expressly limits ordinary particle-mask protection by distinguishing gases. The discussion therefore does not establish that reduced particle exposure means comprehensively clean cabin air.
- Traffic-calming effects remain dependent on road context. rconti [[./thread#^c24092480|rconti]] describes a decade-long commute where additional stop signs allegedly increased stop-start pollution and reduced attention; diablerouge [[./thread#^c24089635|diablerouge]] reports that neighborhood stop signs divert through-traffic elsewhere. sveiss [[./thread#^c24089368|sveiss]] asks whether added emissions outweigh safety benefits. No supplied before-and-after pollution or safety measurements resolve these competing accounts.
- The effect of a short traffic reduction is not established. anxbcl [[./thread#^c24090551|anxbcl]] describes city air as 'perfect' during the strict lockdown phase. lrem [[./thread#^c24095050|lrem]], addressing a proposed single-day lockdown, cites an 83.5-day atmospheric-pollutant half-life without explaining its applicability to the pollutants under discussion. These comments concern different durations and do not supply comparable measurements establishing PM2.5 or NO2 responses.
- Several parts of the approved question lack evidence in this unit. Its quantified ventilation discussion concerns vehicle PM exposure, as quoted by k2enemy [[./thread#^c24091127|k2enemy]]; its CO2-related cognition claim is an unmeasured warning from antisthenes [[./thread#^c24089169|antisthenes]]. No sleep outcomes, household cooking-exposure accounts, or home-window ventilation trials are supplied. tamirzb’s agricultural-burning discussion [[./thread#^c24090835|tamirzb]] is not a firsthand wildfire-smoke account, so wildfire-specific filtration and window-opening conclusions cannot be drawn.
