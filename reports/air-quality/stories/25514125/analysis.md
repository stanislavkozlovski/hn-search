# [1\.7M premature deaths annually from air pollution in India](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=25514125)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion addresses severe air pollution in India, particularly Delhi, and disputes the relative contributions of traffic, crop-residue burning, and other sources. Participants discuss farmers' constraints and alternatives such as residue-management equipment, policy and subsidy enforcement, personal exposure and privilege, and broader agricultural practices, population, and governance.
Tags: India, air pollution, Delhi, crop-residue burning, agriculture, public policy

## Relevance

The thread includes firsthand accounts of severe Delhi pollution, eye irritation, asthma concerns, visible and smelled smoke indoors, and reliance on HVAC. It also discusses seasonal stubble-burning exposure and other pollution sources, while some proposed causes and remedies remain disputed. ## Arguments

- ghoomketu [[./thread#^c25514871|ghoomketu]], KorematsuFred [[./thread#^c25515352|KorematsuFred]], and amriksohata [[./thread#^c25515975|amriksohata]] identify crop-stubble burning in neighboring states, particularly Punjab, as a major source of Delhi’s outdoor pollution. KorematsuFred calls it larger than cars. These are attribution claims; none supplies personal monitoring data or a pollutant-specific source analysis.
- KorematsuFred [[./thread#^c25515352|KorematsuFred]] proposes additional subsidies for manual residue removal. Replying directly, jelliclesfarm [[./thread#^c25515447|jelliclesfarm]] argues that no-burn machinery can avoid manual labor. In another comment, jelliclesfarm [[./thread#^c25515408|jelliclesfarm]] quotes reporting about Happy Seeders and straw-management machinery, including claims that burning releases toxic gases and destroys soil nutrients. These are proposed source-control improvements supported by quoted reporting, without demonstrated changes in residents’ exposure.
- 11235813213455 [[./thread#^c25516094|11235813213455]] proposes lockdowns whenever pollution becomes excessive and considers pollution a larger long-term problem than coronavirus. This is a policy preference and comparative-risk assertion; the comment supplies neither an intervention threshold nor evidence of effectiveness or comparative mortality.

## Counterarguments

- Replying to ghoomketu’s attribution to stubble burning, hiyer [[./thread#^c25520911|hiyer]] says its substantial contribution is concentrated in early winter, while Delhi remains badly polluted throughout the year. Their reported contribution of over 40\% applies to that seasonal context; the comment does not specify the pollutant, calculation, or source. This challenges treating burning as a sufficient explanation of annual exposure.
- In response to wolfretcrap’s claim about having HVAC, octodog [[./thread#^c25515411|octodog]] points out that people still need to go outdoors and may not want or afford continuous air conditioning. Scapeghost [[./thread#^c25519836|Scapeghost]] additionally questions dependence on electricity and describes unreliable grids in other polluted regions. These replies challenge private indoor protection as a comprehensive solution; they do not measure the effectiveness of the particular Delhi installation.
- ghoomketu [[./thread#^c25514871|ghoomketu]] reports that conditions remained very bad despite odd-even driving restrictions and tree washing. That observation challenges the sufficiency of those measures in their experience, without establishing that they produced no improvement. Replying within that branch, gulabjamuns [[./thread#^c25515046|gulabjamuns]] alleges resistance to vehicle and firecracker restrictions and ministerial noncompliance, raising implementation as another possible limitation.

## Assumptions

- wolfretcrap [[./thread#^c25515366|wolfretcrap]] implicitly treats having HVAC as adequate protection from the pollution under discussion. The comment does not identify filtration, outdoor-air intake, operating conditions, indoor concentrations, or symptom changes. HVAC ownership alone therefore cannot establish effective particulate or gas removal in this account.
- amriksohata [[./thread#^c25515985|amriksohata]] associates Punjabi farming with heavier pesticide use; jelliclesfarm [[./thread#^c25519858|jelliclesfarm]] broadens the explanation to crop geography and Green Revolution practices. Replies from 11235813213455 [[./thread#^c25516114|11235813213455]] and jelliclesfarm [[./thread#^c25519820|jelliclesfarm]] reinforce that criticism without additional evidence. This branch supplies no measurements connecting pesticide use or the broader agricultural critique to personal airborne exposure.
- The population and national-behavior explanations advanced by ajfjrbfbf [[./thread#^c25516905|ajfjrbfbf]] and runawaybottle [[./thread#^c25516838|runawaybottle]] [[./thread#^c25517048|runawaybottle]] are unsupported generalizations, not exposure findings. Replying to runawaybottle’s traffic-image argument, OJFord [[./thread#^c25517847|OJFord]] offers an alternative interpretation—parked vehicles forcing cyclists around them—and reports encountering similar circumstances in the UK. The exchange does not establish a national behavioral cause of pollution.

## Conditions

- The machinery alternative has concrete adoption constraints in the reporting quoted by jelliclesfarm [[./thread#^c25515447|jelliclesfarm]]: approximately eight acres per day of capacity, insufficient subsidies, incompatibility with some potato-growing operations, lack of sufficiently large tractors, and moist residue prompting partial burning. These conditions complicate explanations based solely on farmers refusing an available solution.
- ghoomketu [[./thread#^c25514871|ghoomketu]] locates their experience in Ghaziabad within Delhi NCR, while amriksohata [[./thread#^c25515975|amriksohata]] attributes incoming smoke to Punjab. jelliclesfarm [[./thread#^c25519858|jelliclesfarm]] claims the south lacks the same problem and contrasts regional cropping conditions. The thread supplies no comparative monitoring to establish that southern air is generally cleaner or that these experiences represent India as a whole.
- The practical value of private indoor protection depends on resources, electricity, and time spent indoors: wolfretcrap [[./thread#^c25515366|wolfretcrap]] reports having HVAC, while octodog [[./thread#^c25515411|octodog]] and Scapeghost [[./thread#^c25519836|Scapeghost]] emphasize affordability, continuous operation, outdoor exposure, and power availability. No purchase costs, electricity bills, or measured protection levels are supplied.
- morpheos137 [[./thread#^c25519252|morpheos137]] asks whether reduced economic development would cause deaths through other pathways and whether India has emissions-control regulations. These introduce an economic and regulatory tradeoff for pollution control, but remain unanswered; the comment does not demonstrate that cleaner air necessarily requires less development.
- jelliclesfarm [[./thread#^c25515447|jelliclesfarm]] suggests small-acreage farm automation as a service, while acknowledging difficulty getting small-acreage robots operating in the United States. That is an implementation-related personal account, not evidence that the proposed service is affordable, available, or effective at reducing Delhi residents’ exposure.

## Lived experiences

- ghoomketu [[./thread#^c25514871|ghoomketu]] reports constantly red eyes while living in Ghaziabad, describes life there as extremely unpleasant, and says pollution motivated leaving. They also report that a friend with asthma wore a mask inside the home. The eye symptoms and departure are firsthand; the friend’s circumstances are secondhand, with no mask specification or demonstrated treatment benefit.
- hn\_throwaway\_99 [[./thread#^c25515102|hn\_throwaway\_99]] describes a business trip to Delhi as exceptionally unpleasant and says they could not wait to leave because the air felt persistently threatening. This establishes their reported distress and discomfort, without a concentration reading, clinical finding, or quantified health effect.
- Replying in agreement with hn\_throwaway\_99, serpix [[./thread#^c25515198|serpix]] reports seeing and smelling smog inside the airport terminal and finding outdoor conditions reminiscent of being in a wildfire. This is a firsthand sensory account of pollution extending indoors. The wildfire comparison is descriptive, not a report of exposure to an actual wildfire.
- wolfretcrap [[./thread#^c25515366|wolfretcrap]] reports having HVAC at their Delhi home. This is firsthand evidence of access to an indoor coping measure, but the accompanying suggestion that pollution is only a problem for poorer people is not supported by measurements or reported health outcomes.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The relative importance of burning versus other sources remains unresolved: KorematsuFred [[./thread#^c25515352|KorematsuFred]] ranks stubble burning above cars, whereas hiyer [[./thread#^c25520911|hiyer]] emphasizes its seasonality and substantial pollution outside that period. No annual source breakdown, traffic-exposure measurements, or measurements before and after residue-management interventions reconcile those claims.
- This supplied unit contains no PM2.5, NO2, or CO2 concentration readings and no sensor models, calibration checks, or reliability comparisons. serpix’s observations of visible and odorous smog [[./thread#^c25515198|serpix]] cannot identify individual pollutants or their concentrations, and hiyer’s seasonal percentage [[./thread#^c25520911|hiyer]] is a source-contribution claim rather than a personal exposure measurement.
- The reported health and comfort effects do not establish longer-term risks or mechanisms. ghoomketu [[./thread#^c25514871|ghoomketu]] reports eye irritation and an asthmatic friend’s indoor masking; hn\_throwaway\_99 [[./thread#^c25515102|hn\_throwaway\_99]] reports distress. Neither supplies evidence about cognition or sleep, and the comments do not substantiate the title’s premature-death estimate or 11235813213455’s coronavirus comparison [[./thread#^c25516094|11235813213455]].
- Whether opening windows helps or worsens exposure cannot be determined here. serpix [[./thread#^c25515198|serpix]] describes pollution indoors and outdoors, while wolfretcrap [[./thread#^c25515366|wolfretcrap]] supplies no ventilation details. There are no paired indoor/outdoor readings, window-opening experiments, CO2 measurements, or comparisons of ventilation with filtration. Cooking exposure is not described, and the wildfire reference provides no actual wildfire-mitigation experience.
- The constituency resisting pollution controls is disputed within the replies. gulabjamuns [[./thread#^c25515046|gulabjamuns]] distinguishes farmers resisting burning restrictions from the public resisting other measures; FooHentai [[./thread#^c25515305|FooHentai]] equates farmers with the population using a 60\% agricultural-employment claim. vijayr02 [[./thread#^c25516333|vijayr02]] cites 41.49\% of the workforce and asks which source is authoritative. The denominator and sourcing differences remain unresolved, and neither figure establishes public agreement about pollution controls.
