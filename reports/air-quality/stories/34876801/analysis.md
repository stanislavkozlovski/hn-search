# [Tell HN: Buy a CO2 monitor (not necessarily just a CO monitor)](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=34876801)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants discuss measuring indoor CO2, ventilation, and reported effects on alertness and headaches. Suggestions include opening windows, using fresh-air HVAC systems, building a DIY monitor, and finding reliable devices.
Tags: indoor air quality, CO2 monitoring, ventilation, DIY sensors

## Relevance

The discussion includes a report of a very high indoor CO2 reading associated with headaches, firsthand comparisons of ventilation and morning comfort, and practical window-opening and mechanical ventilation experiences. It also raises monitor reliability and describes a DIY sensor setup. ## Arguments

- Kelteseth [[./thread#^c34877140|Kelteseth]] recommends lüften, or airing rooms through open windows. In direct reply, 2rsf [[./thread#^c34879934|2rsf]] proposes adequate mechanical ventilation as an alternative. Their exchange concerns how to ventilate; neither supplies comparative measurements.
- Runjake [[./thread#^c34889475|runjake]] argues for measuring CO2 instead of assuming ventilation is sufficient. Nextos [[./thread#^c34887953|nextos]] provides additional anecdotal support: continuous outdoor-air supply coincided with lower measured CO2 and feeling fresher in the mornings.

## Counterarguments

- Runjake [[./thread#^c34889475|runjake]] reports elevated CO2 despite describing a newer home with good HVAC. This challenges using building age or a general assessment of HVAC quality as assurance of sufficient ventilation; the system's outdoor-air provision is unspecified.
- Nextos [[./thread#^c34887953|nextos]] says frequent window opening lowers CO2 in the current property but considers this impractical without heat recovery. That account places a practical limit on relying on repeated airing.

## Assumptions

- The opening post attributes headaches to a reported approximately 10,000 ppm reading and asserts more than 15\% impairment of brain function. These remain an unverified measurement and causal claim in this source. Nextos [[./thread#^c34887953|nextos]] offers subjective corroboration through improved morning freshness, without cognitive testing or evidence validating that percentage.
- Nextos [[./thread#^c34887953|nextos]] experienced filtration, continuous outdoor-air supply, a forest setting, and a different residence together. Attributing the improvement specifically to CO2 reduction assumes those other changes did not account for it; attributing lower CO2 to filtration itself is also unsupported.

## Conditions

- Runjake's [[./thread#^c34889475|runjake]] account concerns an approximately 1,800-square-foot home occupied by two adults and three children. Winter practice involves opening a door and window a couple of times daily; milder weather permits leaving them open much of the day.
- Nextos [[./thread#^c34887953|nextos]] describes outdoor air drawn from surrounding forest and calls filtered HVAC rare in the EU. 2rsf [[./thread#^c34879934|2rsf]] describes decent ventilation systems as common in modern Swedish properties. These geographic characterizations are commenters' claims, without supporting prevalence data.
- N8henrie [[./thread#^c34879745|n8henrie]] reports approximately \$20 shipped from AliExpress for an MH-719B sensor and \$1 for an ESP8266. These are component prices; a PCB and enclosure remained unfinished, and a complete system cost is unspecified.

## Lived experiences

- Nextos [[./thread#^c34887953|nextos]] reports feeling much fresher, especially in the morning, after years of sluggishness while renting a house with continuously supplied, filtered outdoor air. Subsequent CO2 measurements were substantially lower than in the current property unless windows were opened frequently. No numerical readings, sleep measurements, or controlled comparison are supplied.
- Runjake [[./thread#^c34889475|runjake]] reports CO2 readings around 1,800–2,200 in the occupied home and says opening a door and window brings them down. No post-airing readings, reduction times, sensor details, or associated health symptoms are reported.
- N8henrie [[./thread#^c34879745|n8henrie]] describes successfully assembling a CO2 monitor and finding integration with HomeAssistant through ESPHome unexpectedly easy. This is firsthand evidence of implementation feasibility; the comment provides no measured concentrations or accuracy checks.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Mdmglr [[./thread#^c34877758|mdmglr]] asks which brands make reliable monitors, with no answer in the supplied exchange. N8henrie's [[./thread#^c34879745|n8henrie]] working DIY setup does not resolve accuracy, calibration, drift, placement, or comparison against a reference instrument.
- The relative effectiveness, comfort, and cost of window airing versus mechanical ventilation remain untested. Kelteseth [[./thread#^c34877140|Kelteseth]] advocates airing, 2rsf [[./thread#^c34879934|2rsf]] offers mechanical ventilation, and nextos [[./thread#^c34887953|nextos]] raises heat recovery as a practical requirement. These positions are compatible under different circumstances, rather than a resolved comparison.
- Nextos [[./thread#^c34887953|nextos]] reports forest surroundings, and runjake [[./thread#^c34889475|runjake]] describes seasonal window opening, but neither measures outdoor pollution. This source supplies no PM2.5 or NO2 measurements, cooking, traffic, or wildfire-smoke experiences, or evidence establishing when opening windows worsens exposure. It also cannot establish objective improvements in cognition or sleep from the reported CO2 changes.
