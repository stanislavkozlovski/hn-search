# [Ask HN: Indoor air quality sensors and other IoT that's local-first and not DIY?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=33773786)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

The question prompts recommendations for indoor air-quality monitors that combine reliable measurements, local data access, and minimal hardware assembly. Participants compare products and protocols, including Bluetooth, Zigbee, Home Assistant, and local APIs, while distinguishing real CO₂ sensors from estimates based on VOC readings. DIY kits and pre-soldered options are weighed against turnkey devices, with discussion of calibration, data logging, sensor accuracy, privacy, cloud dependence, and the trade-offs between convenience and control.
Tags: indoor air quality, local-first IoT, CO₂ sensors, Home Assistant, data logging, sensor accuracy, DIY hardware

## Relevance

This discussion is directly about indoor air monitoring: sensor types, accuracy, calibration, local logging, and practical measurement of CO2, particles, and VOCs. It includes firsthand reports of readings changing with ventilation and a PM spike traced to a humidifier, while noting that VOC-based estimated CO2 readings can be unreliable. ## Arguments

- In story 33773786, part 0, air quality is treated as several distinct measurements. INTPenis [[./thread#^c33777465|INTPenis]] asks what specifically needs measuring; TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c33777694|TeMPOraL]] prioritizes particles and TVOC, followed by CO2, temperature and humidity. A device reporting one of these cannot automatically answer questions about the others.
- Actual CO2 sensing is distinguished from estimates derived from VOCs. Youden [[./thread#^c33777386|Youden]] says the BME680 cannot measure CO2 directly; Terretta [[./thread#^c33774695|Terretta]] recommends the Aranet4, described as using NDIR. The argument is to examine the sensing method rather than accept a CO2 label as evidence of direct measurement.
- Relative changes can still be useful when absolute concentrations are unreliable. TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c33777694|TeMPOraL]] explicitly accepts correctly detected relative changes, while ahaucnx [[./thread#^c33781877|ahaucnx]] considers VOC sensors useful for detecting cleaning-product and disinfectant spikes despite distrusting their absolute TVOC and estimated CO2 values.
- Reliable historical recording supports investigating exposures and evaluating changes. mvarrieur [[./thread#^c33776486|mvarrieur]] reports that direct Bluetooth integration made Airthings logging more dependable than relying on a background phone app; stevenhubertron [[./thread#^c33782659|stevenhubertron]] reports retaining three years of measurements locally. Neither supplies a corresponding quantified health improvement.
- Reducing assembly work need not mean abandoning local monitoring. akoutmos [[./thread#^c33774854|akoutmos]] describes plug-in Qwiic connections, while AirGradient maintainer ahaucnx [[./thread#^c33781691|ahaucnx]] describes tested, pre-soldered kits and argues that sensor selection, airflow and internal heating determine measurement quality. These are implementation proposals and manufacturer claims, not independent comparative accuracy results.
- The window-opening accounts suggest a tradeoff between different pollutants. akoutmos [[./thread#^c33776552|akoutmos]] reports a falling CO2-labelled reading after opening an office window, although the reading was not independently verified and arose in the proxy-sensor discussion. latchkey [[./thread#^c33783682|latchkey]] reports rising indoor AQI when opening windows in a filtered home. These observations concern different homes and measurements; they do not establish that opening windows improves or worsens every aspect of air quality.
- Shared outdoor measurements have a practical exposure-management role. wintermutestwin [[./thread#^c33777833|wintermutestwin]] uses nearby PurpleAir readings when deciding whether to exercise outdoors during California wildfire conditions. TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c33778065|TeMPOraL]] values public outdoor data but argues that indoor readings should not be mixed into an outdoor map as though they represented ambient conditions.

## Counterarguments

- Cheap sensors being useful for automation is disputed. nathan\_f77 [[./thread#^c33775178|nathan\_f77]] considers inexpensive Zigbee units sufficient for setting extractor-fan and purifier thresholds despite acknowledging poor accuracy. christiangenco [[./thread#^c33775329|christiangenco]] reports wildly wrong CO2 readings from two such units, and RobotToaster [[./thread#^c33775547|RobotToaster]] says their CO2 readings are, in their experience, VOC-derived estimates.
- Consumer-sensor experiences are not uniformly negative. bsder [[./thread#^c33779938|bsder]] reports consistently poor experiences with non-laboratory environmental sensors, whereas Terretta [[./thread#^c33774695|Terretta]] reports close agreement among several Aranet4 units and KaiserPro [[./thread#^c33779337|KaiserPro]] describes owned Awair units as well calibrated. These competing owner reports do not constitute a controlled comparison or demonstrate that either assessment applies to all consumer monitors.
- An unexplained spike need not be an instrument malfunction. Havoc [[./thread#^c33779670|Havoc]] reports unexplained changes in an apparently stable apartment; replying to that account, TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c33782912|TeMPOraL]] describes initially mysterious particulate spikes eventually attributed to an ultrasonic humidifier. Conversely, identifying one real source does not validate every unexplained reading.
- Integrated purifier sensors divide participants. troysk [[./thread#^c33777173|troysk]] prefers sensors in purifiers, and latchkey [[./thread#^c33776227|latchkey]] values combining monitoring with cleaning. TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c33778131|TeMPOraL]] objects that the purifier's immediate surroundings may not represent the room, later arguing that persistent sources can maintain concentration gradients [[./thread#^c33783054|TeMPOraL]]. latchkey's relocation check [[./thread#^c33783682|latchkey]] offers evidence for one small, open home rather than resolving representativeness in other layouts.
- Outdoor weather equipment should not be mistaken for pollution monitoring. rngname22 [[./thread#^c33776460|rngname22]] and anxrn [[./thread#^c33776202|anxrn]] point out that the proposed weather station lacks the requested indoor-air measurements; Terretta [[./thread#^c33777481|Terretta]] clarifies that the recommendation answered the weather-station subthread. Separately, shkkmo [[./thread#^c33780307|shkkmo]] eventually finds local UDP access, correcting the earlier suspicion that this weather system necessarily required cloud retrieval.

## Assumptions

- Havoc [[./thread#^c33779001|Havoc]] reasons that VOC-derived CO2 should correlate with actual CO2 if no other VOC sources are present, but explicitly lacks a reliable way to check. That assumed relationship is central to the disagreement: plausible responses to occupancy, breathing or outdoor air are being used as substitutes for reference measurements.
- Agreement between units is implicitly treated as evidence of quality in Terretta's Aranet4 report [[./thread#^c33774695|Terretta]]. It establishes reported consistency, but the supplied account cannot exclude shared calibration error. Similarly, gozdal [[./thread#^c33774618|gozdal]] owns recommended monitors but explicitly cannot assess their accuracy or precision.
- The office target of keeping CO2 below roughly 700–800 ppm is a practice reported by Terretta [[./thread#^c33774695|Terretta]], not a demonstrated health, cognition or sleep threshold. No study or outcome comparison supporting that particular target is supplied.
- HTresearch [[./thread#^c33777330|HTresearch]] proposes manually calibrating a forthcoming monitor against outdoor air assumed to be 420 ppm and advertises accuracy of ±(40 ppm + 5\%). Both the outdoor reference assumption and the accuracy specification remain developer claims; no independent validation is provided.
- Interpreting purifier throughput as whole-home exposure reduction assumes adequate air mixing. latchkey [[./thread#^c33780410|latchkey]] initially describes filtering all condo air several times hourly; TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c33783054|TeMPOraL]] challenges whether distant air actually reaches the purifier. Device-reported room air changes do not themselves demonstrate uniform concentrations throughout a dwelling.
- Treating a displayed AQI of zero as comprehensive air-quality assurance would assume that the relevant pollutants are represented. latchkey [[./thread#^c33783682|latchkey]] reports zero AQI, but oger [[./thread#^c33775378|oger]] explicitly distinguishes a PM2.5-only monitor from CO, CO2 and VOC sensing. The discussion provides no basis for translating a favorable particulate-related display into verified low concentrations of every gas.

## Conditions

- Exposure priorities differ geographically. wintermutestwin [[./thread#^c33777833|wintermutestwin]] describes wildfire-prone California; ahaucnx [[./thread#^c33781691|ahaucnx]] says AirGradient began with a Thai school's wildfire-season needs. TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c33782991|TeMPOraL]] reports no regular local wildfires and instead emphasizes family allergies, while already having substantial nearby outdoor-monitor coverage according to TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c33778065|TeMPOraL]].
- Housing design constrains interventions. TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c33785696|TeMPOraL]] describes a roughly 60 m² apartment with at least four poorly exchanging zones and a building designed for natural ventilation. The HOA prohibits adding fans to existing ventilation grilles because of effects on building-wide airflow. This limits applying another household's mechanical-ventilation or single-purifier arrangement.
- latchkey's favorable single-purifier experience concerns a small, open home near the ocean with already relatively low outdoor AQI [[./thread#^c33783682|latchkey]]. Their separate account of generally poor air in Vietnam [[./thread#^c33789421|latchkey]] concerns a different residence and period. Neither setting should be substituted for TeMPOraL's apartment or wildfire conditions.
- The late-2022 price examples span very different budgets: nathan\_f77 [[./thread#^c33775178|nathan\_f77]] cites approximately \$20 consumer sensors and a \$300 commercial alternative; HTresearch [[./thread#^c33777330|HTresearch]] anticipates approximately \$150 for an unfinished product. Confiks [[./thread#^c33774755|Confiks]] considers Aranet4 expensive, whereas xolox [[./thread#^c33782178|xolox]] considers it fairly affordable relative to other direct CO2 sensors.
- Second-hand pricing changes the practical comparison. omh [[./thread#^c33774700|omh]] reports buying an Awair Element for about £60 in the UK after a related cryptocurrency venture failed, despite high new-device prices. That purchase opportunity is geographically and temporally specific, and does not remove the support-dependency concerns raised by zapt02 [[./thread#^c33774737|zapt02]].
- Household budgets differ from industrial procurement. jvanderbot [[./thread#^c33774455|jvanderbot]] reports steep costs for small, high-quality production runs. TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c33776666|TeMPOraL]] rejects expensive specifications, proprietary equipment and procurement assumptions suited to industrial installations, while later considering costly offline-first industrial equipment or second-hand units [[./thread#^c33782810|TeMPOraL]].
- Filtration entails equipment, space, noise and replacement costs. latchkey [[./thread#^c33789421|latchkey]] reports that a homemade box-fan arrangement with three filters worked well but was larger and louder than the Mila. They describe commercial filters and accessories as expensive and expect a long filter life in their relatively clean home; that lifetime is an expectation, not a completed durability observation.
- Available skills and local assistance affect affordable monitoring options. TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c33782991|TeMPOraL]] accepts substantial software work but wants to avoid hardware assembly. thinkmcfly [[./thread#^c33774927|thinkmcfly]] offers free soldering only within the USA; igor47 [[./thread#^c33776577|igor47]] offers spare particulate sensors for Bay Area collection, which TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c33777859|TeMPOraL]] cannot use because of distance.

## Lived experiences

- Terretta [[./thread#^c33774695|Terretta]] reports using approximately six Aranet4 monitors in office meeting rooms, with agreement across their sensors. Empty rooms reportedly read 450–550 ppm CO2, and occupants are encouraged to remain below roughly 700–800 ppm. No associated cognitive, symptom or productivity measurements are reported.
- akoutmos [[./thread#^c33776552|akoutmos]] observed the project's displayed CO2 reading rise with exhalation and drop after opening a window, but had no reference instrument. nathan\_f77 [[./thread#^c33775490|nathan\_f77]] reports approximately 360 ppm in fresh air, increases in an occupied office with closed windows and door, and breathing-induced spikes. These are firsthand display observations, not validated CO2 concentrations.
- buran77 [[./thread#^c33778166|buran77]] reports substantial temperature dependence when using SGP30 and BME680 sensors: changes of only 1–2°C could double or triple readings near 50 ppb and increase readings near 500 ppb by approximately 50\%. The account identifies a potential measurement confound but supplies no independent VOC reference.
- ahaucnx [[./thread#^c33781877|ahaucnx]] reports testing many VOC modules and finding estimated CO2 values entirely wrong approximately 80\% of the time in their experience. They also distrust absolute TVOC values while finding spikes useful, and say newer Sensirion generations report an index instead. No sample count, reference instrument or definition of wrong is supplied, so 80\% is not a general failure-rate estimate.
- yobbo [[./thread#^c33779170|yobbo]] suspects an owned particulate monitor is approximately 20–30\% inaccurate and reports humidity differing by approximately 10–20\% from other inexpensive devices. They suggest device heating may affect the local humidity measurement. These are suspected errors and comparisons against other cheap instruments, not calibrated accuracy results.
- TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c33782912|TeMPOraL]] reports large indoor particulate spikes from a DIY Luftdaten monitor, traced over several days to an ultrasonic humidifier. Subsequent reading of blogs and scientific papers led them to attribute the particles to minerals and contaminants aerosolized from tap water. The sensor observation is firsthand; the explanation draws on outside reading whose methods and results are not reproduced.
- sliken [[./thread#^c33780469|sliken]] reports Airthings readings that track occupancy and use of a gas oven through CO2 peaks, plus radon falling after mitigation. No numerical concentrations, reference measurements or symptom changes are supplied. The gas-oven observation is about CO2, not a measurement of NO2.
- latchkey [[./thread#^c33783682|latchkey]] reports a Mila running at 20\% fan speed and displaying 4.2 room air changes per hour. Displayed AQI eventually reaches zero and remains zero after moving the unit elsewhere in the small home; visible household dust also decreases. Opening windows raises AQI and closing them lowers it. Their outdoor comparison comes from a third-party local reading in the app, rather than a supplied paired measurement dataset.
- TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c33785696|TeMPOraL]] reports dust-mite allergy and suspects similar sensitivity in their children. They hope HEPA filtration will reduce dust and cleaning work, but do not report having achieved symptom relief. Their assessment of four poorly mixing zones comes from observing heat and smells, with separate air-quality measurements planned rather than completed.
- latchkey [[./thread#^c33789421|latchkey]] reports getting sick less often after stopping use of wall-mounted AC while living in Vietnam, attributing the previous illnesses to accumulated dust and mold. There are no microbial measurements, diagnoses or controlled comparisons, so the account does not establish causation. Their proposed tradeoff was tolerating more heat.
- Comfort varies by device and household. TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c33782879|TeMPOraL]] found a previous particulate sensor's fan particularly annoying at night; AirGradient maintainer ahaucnx [[./thread#^c33783825|ahaucnx]] reports being unable to hear their PMS5003-based monitors from less than a metre away in a bedroom. These concern different equipment. TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c33777325|TeMPOraL]] also reports a household dislike of illuminated indicator lights at night; none of these comments measures sleep quality.
- Working local sensor systems are reported, but their existence is separate from accuracy validation. vonnieda [[./thread#^c33776347|vonnieda]] uses a CO2 Buddy variant and modified four IKEA particulate monitors in about an hour; oger [[./thread#^c33775378|oger]] logs modified IKEA sensors locally; sigio [[./thread#^c33774642|sigio]] operates two locally reporting classroom-project CO2 monitors. Their comments provide implementation experience rather than calibrated exposure results.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The useful-versus-misleading boundary for inexpensive proxy sensors remains disputed. nathan\_f77 [[./thread#^c33775178|nathan\_f77]] accepts them for household thresholds, while christiangenco [[./thread#^c33775329|christiangenco]] asks whether calibration could explain two unreliable units and ahaucnx [[./thread#^c33781877|ahaucnx]] distrusts their absolute outputs. The supplied comments do not establish a reproducible household validation procedure or show that their relative responses reliably track actual CO2.
- Whole-room and whole-home representativeness remains unresolved across layouts. latchkey [[./thread#^c33783682|latchkey]] supplies a favorable relocation check in one open home; TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c33785696|TeMPOraL]] describes several poorly exchanging zones needing separate monitoring. spockz [[./thread#^c33779832|spockz]] asks about purifier placement, including ceiling mounting, without receiving a supplied answer or comparative measurements.
- The window-opening tradeoff is not quantified. akoutmos [[./thread#^c33776552|akoutmos]] provides an unverified CO2-labelled response, while latchkey [[./thread#^c33783682|latchkey]] provides AQI changes without PM2.5 mass concentrations. wintermutestwin [[./thread#^c33777833|wintermutestwin]] discusses wildfire monitoring but not a window-opening experiment. This unit supplies no simultaneous validated CO2 and PM2.5 comparison from which to derive ventilation timing or thresholds.
- Health, cognition and sleep effects remain largely unmeasured here. TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c33782991|TeMPOraL]] describes allergy-related motivation, latchkey [[./thread#^c33789421|latchkey]] offers an uncontrolled illness anecdote, and TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c33782879|TeMPOraL]] raises nighttime noise concerns. No cognitive testing, quantified sleep outcome or controlled exposure–health comparison is supplied. The papers mentioned by TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c33782912|TeMPOraL]] concern the humidifier explanation rather than a reproduced clinical result.
- NO2 and traffic exposure are substantive evidence gaps in this part. sliken [[./thread#^c33780469|sliken]] reports CO2 peaks with a gas oven, but no commenter supplies NO2 readings, traffic-related measurements or cooking-extraction comparisons. Likewise, latchkey's favorable description of a substantial carbon filter [[./thread#^c33789421|latchkey]] includes no measured gas-removal performance. These accounts cannot establish how those interventions affect NO2.
- Measurement changes caused by altering hardware remain uncertain. TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c33782879|TeMPOraL]] asks whether running a particulate-sensor fan more slowly could reduce noise without compromising readings. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c33783825|ahaucnx]] responds that their chosen fan is already quiet, but does not answer whether changing fan operation preserves calibration or sampling performance.
- Durable local access is product- and setup-dependent. zapt02 [[./thread#^c33774737|zapt02]] warns that older Awair devices were disabled; cvp [[./thread#^c33775444|cvp]] distinguishes newer units with a local API, while zapt02 [[./thread#^c33775672|zapt02]] points to app dependence when changing Wi-Fi. mvarrieur [[./thread#^c33776486|mvarrieur]] cannot confirm cloud-free initial Airthings setup, and sliken's edit [[./thread#^c33780469|sliken]] identifies their device as a View Plus for which they found no non-cloud reader. Successful access to one model does not settle another model's independence.
- Historical access also remains uneven. dawnerd [[./thread#^c33775051|dawnerd]] reports Aranet history disappearing sooner than the advertised two weeks; bithaze [[./thread#^c33774955|bithaze]] exports Eve history manually through an iOS app, and TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c33777771|TeMPOraL]] remains unsure how to automate that access. HTresearch [[./thread#^c33778471|HTresearch]] describes a prototype QR code containing 40 prior CO2 and VOC-index values, with history only since power-on according to HTresearch [[./thread#^c33778516|HTresearch]]. These limitations matter for investigating longer-term exposure patterns.
