# [Dyson air purifier outperformed by cheap DIY box fan filter in Marketplace test](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=26101153)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

The discussion centers on a test in which a low-cost box-fan filter outperforms a Dyson purifier on particle-cleaning performance. Participants debate whether clean-air delivery rate is sufficient for judging a purifier, and how noise, size, appearance, sensors, heating, controls, energy use, and filter costs affect everyday value. They compare DIY fan-and-filter builds with commercial models, discuss HEPA and MERV filters, airflow, room size, wildfire smoke, and the limits of consumer air-quality sensors. Some favor high-performance filtration at low cost; others prefer quieter or more attractive devices that can run continuously. Broader threads question Dyson’s pricing and engineering across its product line, compare vacuum cleaners and hand dryers, and touch on product testing and review trust. A long side discussion ranges from child safety around fans to changing norms around risk and childhood play.
Tags: air purifiers, DIY filtration, box fan filter, CADR, HEPA filters, consumer products, product testing, Dyson, indoor air quality, noise and design

## Relevance

The discussion compares air purifiers and DIY fan filters for particle removal, including firsthand experiences with wildfire smoke, cooking, wood smoke, traffic-related pollution, and allergy or asthma comfort. It discusses PM2.5 measurements, CADR and filter performance, and limits of relying on blackened filters or consumer sensors. Participants describe practical tradeoffs involving filtration speed, room coverage, ventilation, positive-pressure systems, noise, energy use, cost, and sleep. It also notes that recirculating particle filters do not remove CO2, so fresh-air ventilation may still be needed; claims about health and cognition are mixed and not uniformly measured. ## Arguments

- Useful filtration depends on airflow through effective filter media, room size, and pollutant load. whateveryoua381 [[./thread#^c26114393|whateveryoua381]] quotes an expert explaining why high airflow with poor capture, or excellent capture with little airflow, yields low clean-air delivery rate (CADR). pyrophane [[./thread#^c26105280|pyrophane]] warns that advertised room coverage can be overstated. sdljfjafsd [[./thread#^c26107420|sdljfjafsd]] suggests sizing around four air changes per hour; this is a commenter’s rule of thumb, not a requirement established by this discussion.
- Room circulation can improve access to a purifier. timthorn [[./thread#^c26102987|timthorn]] and pjkundert [[./thread#^c26115263|pjkundert]] argue that repeated circulation can progressively filter a closed room even when much of the air moved bypasses the filter on each pass. clairity [[./thread#^c26103502|clairity]] reports using additional fans because their BlueAir units did not adequately collect material being disturbed farther away. These arguments distinguish moving room air from the rate at which air actually passes through filter media.
- Measuring performance in the occupied space is advocated over trusting branding. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c26105946|ahaucnx]], whose startup measures school air quality, reports encountering schools that bought poorly performing units and recommends comparing them with a PM2.5 meter. dyno-might [[./thread#^c26103610|dyno-might]] reports conducting comparative DIY-filter tests, although the supplied comment contains no results from the linked experiment.
- Particle removal and gas or odor removal require separate consideration. owenversteeg [[./thread#^c26110267|owenversteeg]] emphasizes airflow through HEPA media for particles and substantial carbon filtration for VOCs and odors, recommending much more carbon capacity for substantial smoke exposure. liminalsunset [[./thread#^c26103142|liminalsunset]] questions the Coway’s small carbon provision. These are technical claims and purchasing judgments; the comments do not supply comparative gas-removal measurements.
- Filtered outdoor-air supply is proposed as a way to address both incoming smoke particles and accumulating indoor CO2. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c26105677|ahaucnx]] describes positive-pressure systems that filter incoming air and discourage dirty-air infiltration. danans [[./thread#^c26106315|danans]] argues for fresh-air intake or a separate ventilation source alongside recirculating filtration. hedora [[./thread#^c26111529|hedora]] describes heat-recovering air exchangers as a way to ventilate CO2 while moderating incoming-air temperature.
- Larger filter area is presented as a practical improvement to DIY filtration. cjlars [[./thread#^c26105463|cjlars]] recommends two filters in a wedge or four around a box fan and reports success during forest fires. akeck [[./thread#^c26105593|akeck]] reports using cubes with three thick MERV-13 filters in large rooms. hedora [[./thread#^c26107349|hedora]] predicts that a four-filter cube would improve CADR, noise, and efficiency through lower resistance, but supplies no measurements of those improvements.
- A device’s tolerability affects how much protection it actually provides. turtlebits [[./thread#^c26104968|turtlebits]] preferred continuously running a quieter purifier to repeatedly switching a loud box filter on and off in response to an Awair monitor. reaperducer [[./thread#^c26104007|reaperducer]] argues that an attractive purifier accepted into the home delivers more benefit than an effective DIY device excluded from it. post\_break [[./thread#^c26102495|post\_break]] values bedside controls, automatic sensing, and a sound profile suitable for sleeping.
- Dust handling during cleaning is another potential exposure pathway. ashtonkem [[./thread#^c26106236|ashtonkem]] describes fine dust returning to the house when emptying a bagless vacuum. BHSPitMonkey [[./thread#^c26104547|BHSPitMonkey]] suggests careful outdoor emptying as a possible mitigation, while noting that many apartment residents lack that option. This supports considering containment and maintenance alongside purifier performance, without establishing the amount of inhaled dust.

## Counterarguments

- High advertised air movement does not establish high filtration throughput. ortusdux [[./thread#^c26102618|ortusdux]] questions Dyson’s air-multiplier design because, using the manufacturer’s claimed multiplication ratio, most moved air does not pass through the filter. Their later clarification [[./thread#^c26105244|ortusdux]] is that Dyson does filter, but allegedly delivers poor filtration value. The repeated-circulation replies challenge an inference of ineffectiveness, rather than proving equal cleaning speed.
- CADR alone does not settle overall product value. rconti [[./thread#^c26103478|rconti]] argues that quiet operation, automatic adjustment, and appearance can matter alongside filtration. ACow\_Adonis [[./thread#^c26108355|ACow\_Adonis]] adds carbon filtration, footprint, controls, and pollutant reporting to the comparison. These features can justify a preference, but their enumeration does not demonstrate superior exposure reduction.
- A low-ranked or slower purifier can still improve air quality. Havoc [[./thread#^c26108895|Havoc]] reports measurable improvement from the test’s bottom-ranked model, while declining to recommend it over a later purchase. cowmoo728 [[./thread#^c26103763|cowmoo728]] hypothesizes that adequate CADR plus good circulation could produce similar steady-state results under modest pollution loads. Their proposed 48-hour household comparison was not performed in the supplied account.
- Opening windows is not an unconditional substitute for filtration. rickyc091 [[./thread#^c26104609|rickyc091]] suggests that open windows can remove the need for a purifier, but JohnBooty [[./thread#^c26105039|JohnBooty]] replies that urban or otherwise polluted outdoor air can itself be the problem. IgorPartola [[./thread#^c26106339|IgorPartola]] reports that closed windows and an indoor filter help when outdoor conditions are poor.
- A dark filter, disappearing odor, and symptom relief are imperfect proxies for PM2.5 removal. nostrademons [[./thread#^c26107395|nostrademons]] used faster filter blackening and relief from smoke discomfort to judge a box fan superior. codemac [[./thread#^c26109269|codemac]] disputes blackening as a comparison of fine-particle capture. In the cigarette-smoke discussion, IgorPartola [[./thread#^c26106174|IgorPartola]] warns that smoke-related particles can remain after the smell disappears.
- Health benefits are disputed rather than uniformly accepted. nvarsj [[./thread#^c26105362|nvarsj]] questions the scientific support for allergy relief and wonders whether purifiers are largely expensive placebos outside severe pollution. No supporting study is supplied for that assessment. JohnBooty [[./thread#^c26104998|JohnBooty]] reports improved asthma and allergies but explicitly describes filtration as one component of care, not a cure or something everyone necessarily needs.
- The claim that Dyson purifier filters are washable is challenged by owners. cptskippy [[./thread#^c26104089|cptskippy]] says Dyson filters are reusable, but kelvie [[./thread#^c26104309|kelvie]] reports replacement notifications and a CAD\$99 replacement price. thesh4d0w [[./thread#^c26104601|thesh4d0w]] distinguishes washable vacuum filters from replaceable tower-fan filters, and qwertay [[./thread#^c26109059|qwertay]] makes the same distinction. Maintenance assumptions therefore need to be specific to the product and filter stage.
- The assertion that viruses are inherently too small for HEPA capture is contested within the thread. Bakary [[./thread#^c26104955|Bakary]] says filters primarily catch virus-carrying droplets because viruses themselves are too small; Maursault [[./thread#^c26136157|Maursault]] counters that filtration can be highly effective below as well as above 0.3 micrometers. Neither comment establishes a particular room’s infection risk or a safe time to remove a mask.

## Assumptions

- The argument that different filtration flow rates eventually reach the same target assumes a fixed initial contaminant load and sufficient operation, as pjkundert [[./thread#^c26115263|pjkundert]] explicitly specifies. It does not establish equivalent protection during continuing smoke infiltration, cooking, or repeated ventilation. hytdstd [[./thread#^c26107802|hytdstd]] instead emphasizes continual air exchange and cites an external claim that particle levels rebound after filtration stops.
- Consumer sensor readings are treated as informative, but their absolute accuracy is not demonstrated here. IgorPartola [[./thread#^c26112819|IgorPartola]] identifies PMSA003 particle and DS-CO2-20 CO2 sensors, distinguishes estimated eCO2 from direct CO2 sensing, and wants controlled testing of gas sensors. Their prototype revisions [[./thread#^c26112768|IgorPartola]] include hardware that had not yet arrived. Newer components and responsiveness to events are not, by themselves, calibration evidence.
- PM2.5 is not assumed to represent every relevant pollutant. When Karawebnetwork [[./thread#^c26106227|Karawebnetwork]] asks whether a cleared PM2.5 reading means everything is fine, IgorPartola [[./thread#^c26106339|IgorPartola]] explicitly answers that they do not know, citing possible false readings and concerns about PM1, PM10, CO, CO2, and VOCs. The discussion therefore does not support treating a favorable particle reading as proof of complete smoke removal.
- Published specifications and user measurements need different treatment. tareqak [[./thread#^c26102913|tareqak]] reproduces separate smoke, dust, and pollen CADRs for a Coway; liminalsunset [[./thread#^c26103142|liminalsunset]] identifies the units as cfm rather than m³/h. ph4te [[./thread#^c26105839|ph4te]] objects to the tested models’ differing sizes and prices. Comparisons require consistent units, operating settings, media, and room conditions, none of which are fully specified across the anecdotes.
- A box-fan purifier is not a standardized intervention. julianlam [[./thread#^c26102710|julianlam]] uses a single MERV-11 filter, akeck [[./thread#^c26105593|akeck]] uses multiple thick MERV-13 filters, and hansvm [[./thread#^c26103440|hansvm]] recalls approximately MERV-15 media with sealed edges. Outcomes from one construction cannot establish the performance, resistance, noise, or durability of every DIY design.
- Claims about external testing remain secondhand in this export. gonehome [[./thread#^c26108106|gonehome]] reproduces Wirecutter’s poor Molekule results and statements that advertising-review bodies rejected performance and health claims. victor106 [[./thread#^c26108883|victor106]] questions Wirecutter’s trustworthiness, while chipotle\_coyote [[./thread#^c26110314|chipotle\_coyote]] argues that affiliate links and isolated questionable recommendations do not establish systematic bias. The supplied material does not independently resolve those source-quality disputes.
- Personal comparisons can contain selection effects. stjohnswarts [[./thread#^c26105339|stjohnswarts]] associates a less sanitized childhood with fewer allergies than friends, while acknowledging possible observer bias. s5300 [[./thread#^c26105895|s5300]] suggests that people often seek filtration after developing symptoms. Neither account establishes that filtration causes immune problems or that childhood dirt exposure explains these particular differences.

## Conditions

- The February 2021 US purchase comparisons show substantial price differences. ortusdux [[./thread#^c26103679|ortusdux]] reports an \$800 Dyson versus \$30 tower fans and \$100 purifiers at Costco, buying two fan–purifier pairs for under \$300. nostrademons [[./thread#^c26107197|nostrademons]] reports roughly \$70–80 for a box fan and filter. These are historical, local purchases rather than universally available prices or matched performance comparisons.
- Replacement costs and discounts can change the value judgment. kelvie [[./thread#^c26104309|kelvie]] reports CAD\$99 Dyson replacements; thesh4d0w [[./thread#^c26104601|thesh4d0w]] bought \$30 imports they suspected might be counterfeit, with no filtration verification supplied. JohnBooty [[./thread#^c26103232|JohnBooty]] values affordable third-party Coway filters. turtlebits [[./thread#^c26104630|turtlebits]] considers a refurbished Dyson under \$200 attractive while rejecting a price above \$600.
- DIY economics depend on local component availability. arctangos [[./thread#^c26106223|arctangos]] reports difficulty finding flat box fans in the Netherlands, echoed more broadly for Europe by ricardobayes [[./thread#^c26112836|ricardobayes]]. Conversely, dis-sys [[./thread#^c26111445|dis-sys]] reports Shanghai availability of Xiaomi purifiers with sensing and remote control for about \$100, with \$40 replacement filters said to last six months. A US-style DIY recommendation therefore does not transfer automatically between markets.
- Room size and regional smoke burden change capacity needs. hedora [[./thread#^c26107508|hedora]] contrasts a reported New York apartment review that considered a large Medify excessive with their Bay Area house, which needed a high-MERV furnace filter plus two smaller purifiers during fires. Their larger replacement arrived after the last fires, so the comment does not establish its performance through that wildfire episode.
- Severe winter pollution can make filter consumption a significant cost. Tade0 [[./thread#^c26104005|Tade0]] reports buying a purifier for about 800 PLN, rated at 300 m³/h, and replacing its HEPA and carbon filters for under \$40 after the outlet began smelling bad. They describe four days of Polish smog above 120 µg/m³, peaking at 200 µg/m³; the source and location of those concentration readings are unspecified.
- Wildfire-season supply and consumable costs matter. carabiner [[./thread#^c26105709|carabiner]] reports West Coast filters selling out during summer and autumn fires, and s0rce [[./thread#^c26105013|s0rce]] recommends keeping a spare before California fire season. joshe [[./thread#^c26106995|joshe]] advocates standard furnace-filter media for temporary smoke events in places including California and Australia, claiming much cheaper replacements; the claimed tenfold saving is not independently documented.
- Housing and climate constrain the alternatives. tallanvor [[./thread#^c26111527|tallanvor]] notes that many apartments lack a furnace, while casion [[./thread#^c26110603|casion]] describes a noisy, visible central system that circulates rooms poorly. kyriakos [[./thread#^c26105799|kyriakos]] keeps windows partly open much of the year in a warm climate; rickyc091 [[./thread#^c26104609|rickyc091]] finds winter ventilation less practical. These circumstances affect whether central filtration, portable units, or window ventilation are usable.
- Operating cost is raised but not comprehensively measured. brody\_hamer [[./thread#^c26111013|brody\_hamer]] questions the efficiency of continuously running several box fans, and post\_break [[./thread#^c26109321|post\_break]] raises the electricity cost of overnight furnace circulation. liminalsunset [[./thread#^c26103142|liminalsunset]] recalls a video reporting Coway consumption of 2.2 W, 4.4 W, and about 40 W across settings. These comments do not provide a matched comparison of energy per unit of clean air.

## Lived experiences

- IgorPartola [[./thread#^c26106174|IgorPartola]] reports a DIY-sensor PM2.5 rise from below 12 to above 1,000 µg/m³ during one night, coinciding with the worst sleep everyone in the household experienced. The source of recurring spikes remained unidentified. Their comparison with smoking roughly 44 cigarettes is an interpretation, not a measured dose equivalence or demonstrated explanation of the sleep disturbance.
- IgorPartola [[./thread#^c26106339|IgorPartola]] reports detecting kitchen frying from a bedroom sensor and seeing pollution linger. They also report benefits from closed windows plus filtration during poor outdoor conditions and using sensor information to decide when opening windows might prevent headaches. No paired indoor–outdoor time series, headache log, or CO2 measurements are supplied for these decisions.
- ahaucnx [[./thread#^c26105677|ahaucnx]], living through Northern Thailand’s smoke season and identifying as AirGradient’s founder, reports classroom CO2 quickly exceeding 3,000 ppm. They cite 1,200–1,500 ppm as commonly recommended limits and associate high CO2 with impaired cognition, drowsiness, and headaches. The classroom concentration claim is a reported measurement; cognitive performance and symptoms were not reported as measured outcomes of that classroom work.
- dis-sys [[./thread#^c26111445|dis-sys]] reports owning four Xiaomi purifiers in Shanghai and maintaining indoor readings below 10 within minutes of switching them on during days described as PM2.5 above 150. The comment supplies no units, room sizes, independent monitor identification, or calibration details, so the numerical change is useful as a firsthand report but cannot be fully evaluated or directly compared with other accounts.
- mxfh [[./thread#^c26112180|mxfh]] reports adding a HEPA filter to a Synology NAS airflow path and seeing PM2.5 fall from 25 to 5, with negligible device-temperature increase. Units, room volume, duration, and sensor details are absent. Their follow-up [[./thread#^c26115305|mxfh]] stresses that the NAS’s airflow path and fan pressure characteristics differ from desktop cases with multiple openings.
- dwd [[./thread#^c26113320|dwd]] reports an Australian household exposed to neighbors’ fires and occasional bushfires. After an oven accident produced burnt-oil smoke, a Philips display exceeded a value they describe as 250 ppm and struggled to decline; a larger Inovaair reportedly brought the downstairs display to single digits in about 20 minutes. The stated unit is retained as reported and cannot be directly harmonized with the µg/m³ accounts.
- ortusdux [[./thread#^c26105244|ortusdux]] reports that a \$100 purifier returns their living room to its baseline in about 15 minutes after smoke escapes while tending a wood fire; they identify it as a sale-priced Winix C545 [[./thread#^c26110967|ortusdux]]. Karawebnetwork [[./thread#^c26104980|Karawebnetwork]] reports no cigarette smell on returning home and a Dyson app pollution peak subsiding within roughly an hour. These involve different sources, rooms, and unspecified metrics, so they are not a head-to-head clearance test.
- Karawebnetwork [[./thread#^c26103236|Karawebnetwork]] describes a large two-floor loft with few windows and no useful cross-draft, where a Dyson’s circulation seemed more effective than a previous Honeywell tower. Their clarification [[./thread#^c26104889|Karawebnetwork]] matters: the Honeywell was no longer present, and the reported improvement was from the Dyson alone. The improvement was perceived, without comparable concentration traces for the two devices.
- syntaxing [[./thread#^c26102813|syntaxing]] reports measuring '2.5um output' during wildfires and finding BlueAir purifiers better than their taped box-fan purifiers. burlesona [[./thread#^c26105219|burlesona]] also reports particulate measurements supporting BlueAir’s effectiveness. Neither supplies readings, sensor placement, instruments, or a reproducible comparative protocol.
- nostrademons [[./thread#^c26107395|nostrademons]] explicitly reports no objective data for their contrary preference: in an approximately 1,000-square-foot apartment, smoke discomfort persisted with BlueAir, improved after adding a box filter, and stayed improved after removing BlueAir. Their earlier comment [[./thread#^c26107197|nostrademons]] favors BlueAir for kitchen odors and the DIY unit for wildfire particles. This is a subjective household comparison, with the filter-blackening interpretation challenged by replies.
- JohnBooty [[./thread#^c26104998|JohnBooty]] reports that four Coways helped asthma and mild allergies associated with pets and pollen, reduced surface dust, and somewhat reduced odors. They also vacuum and describe the benefit as incomplete. pchristensen [[./thread#^c26105477|pchristensen]] reports household members with asthma appreciating a continuously running Coway, particularly during smoke season. - debaserab2 [[./thread#^c26105275|debaserab2]] reports not needing a purifier in the Midwest, then experiencing a return of childhood asthma symptoms after moving west, especially during wildfire season even when fires were distant. They mention humidity and climate differences, but provide no measurements that separate smoke, humidity, allergens, or other changes accompanying the move.
- s0rce [[./thread#^c26104993|s0rce]] reports maintaining indoor air quality with bedroom and living-area purifiers during severe California wildfire conditions. Their follow-up [[./thread#^c26111002|s0rce]] describes checking indoor air quality and increasing speed during winter inversions, summer fires, or cooking. In contrast, bredren [[./thread#^c26110073|bredren]] reports Coways showing red indicators and running at full speed for days during Oregon fires without being sufficient. - hansvm [[./thread#^c26103440|hansvm]] reports box filters resolving noticeable tobacco-smoke contamination from neighboring chain smokers in a cheap, leaky apartment, including smoke odor on clothing, and visibly clearing cooking smoke. This demonstrates perceived event responsiveness and usability, not calibrated particle accuracy or sufficient cleaning at the restricted speeds.
- Comfort and cognitive accounts vary. akeck [[./thread#^c26105632|akeck]] reports finding it easier to focus while working from home with a HEPA filter running, without pollutant or cognitive-test data. bloomingeek [[./thread#^c26104928|bloomingeek]] reports sleeping well with a box fan masking noise behind a closed bedroom door. hytdstd [[./thread#^c26107802|hytdstd]] finds a box-fan filter disruptive for work and nearly impossible to sleep with. - damontal [[./thread#^c26106640|damontal]] reports visible airborne dust whenever emptying a bagless canister after use. kevstev [[./thread#^c26107742|kevstev]] acknowledges some escape but considers their garbage-can emptying method mostly contained. - craftinator [[./thread#^c26102857|craftinator]] reports workplace hand dryers accumulating mold in difficult-to-clean crevices and describes droplets traveling up to ten feet, suggesting water-reactive paper to observe them. This is an account of contamination and splash dispersal, not a measurement of airborne bacteria, PM2.5, or resulting illness.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The best purifier depends on a comparison the discussion does not complete. nostrademons [[./thread#^c26107395|nostrademons]] favors DIY from subjective wildfire experience, while syntaxing [[./thread#^c26102813|syntaxing]] reports measurements favoring BlueAir. liminalsunset [[./thread#^c26103191|liminalsunset]] requests testing at comparable noise levels, and cowmoo728 [[./thread#^c26103763|cowmoo728]] proposes longer household steady-state testing. whateveryoua381 [[./thread#^c26114506|whateveryoua381]] questions Dyson’s alternative POLAR methodology. These sources do not establish one winner across room sizes, pollution loads, noise limits, and budgets.
- NO2 and traffic exposure remain major evidence gaps in this source unit. ACow\_Adonis [[./thread#^c26108355|ACow\_Adonis]] lists NO2 reporting among Dyson features but supplies no NO2 concentrations or validation. IgorPartola [[./thread#^c26112819|IgorPartola]] is still investigating gas sensors. There are no supplied firsthand traffic-specific measurements, NO2 health outcomes, or demonstrations of NO2 removal. The observed cooking particle spikes cannot establish which gases were present or attribute them to a particular cooking fuel.
- The causal size of health, sleep, and cognitive benefits remains uncertain. IgorPartola’s [[./thread#^c26106174|IgorPartola]] bad-sleep episode, akeck’s [[./thread#^c26105632|akeck]] improved focus, and JohnBooty’s [[./thread#^c26104998|JohnBooty]] allergy relief lack controls or quantified outcome comparisons. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c26105677|ahaucnx]] reports classroom CO2 measurements but does not report testing cognition there. These accounts support investigating exposure and symptoms together, while leaving pollutant-specific causation and population-level benefit unresolved.
- There is no universal window-opening rule or quantified ventilation prescription. IgorPartola [[./thread#^c26106339|IgorPartola]] uses measurements to choose between opening windows and filtering with them closed. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c26105677|ahaucnx]] proposes positive pressure and claims zero indoor PM2.5 even during extreme outdoor pollution, but the export supplies no detection limits, airflow rates, installation costs, or concurrent gas measurements. The claimed complete independence from outdoor pollution is therefore not established for all pollutants or buildings.
- Durability and HVAC compatibility remain incompletely resolved. hansvm [[./thread#^c26103440|hansvm]] reports DIY motor failures, whereas oblib [[./thread#^c26105510|oblib]] reports decades of using box filters continuously, without specifying individual fan lifetimes. Der\_Einzige [[./thread#^c26107031|Der\_Einzige]] broadly recommends high-MERV HVAC filters, while nateburt [[./thread#^c26106594|nateburt]] asks whether a bypass system can match room purifiers and handle local cooking bursts. thitcanh’s [[./thread#^c26111774|thitcanh]] question about AC units reintroducing mold receives no supplied answer.
- Dyson’s acoustic advantage is contested. odux [[./thread#^c26104094|odux]] finds it noisier for comparable perceived airflow; xyzzy\_plugh [[./thread#^c26104481|xyzzy\_plugh]] finds it exceptionally quiet. - Ozone’s effectiveness against odors or mold does not establish safe personal exposure.
