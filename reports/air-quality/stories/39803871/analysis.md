# [Safecast Open Radiation and Air Quality Map](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39803871)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants critique the Safecast radiation and air-quality map's visual clarity, information density, symbols, and coverage. They ask where the data comes from and whether sensors can be trusted; replies describe a volunteer measurement network and free data, while others question geographic coverage, sensor capabilities, baseline comparisons, and the map's representation of readings.
Tags: Safecast, radiation monitoring, air quality, citizen science, data visualization

## Relevance

The discussion raises the source, coverage, and trustworthiness of air-quality map data and homemade sensors, though some comments focus on radiation rather than air pollutants. ## Arguments

- rozzie [[./thread#^c39806276|rozzie]] describes a volunteer network collecting radiation and air-quality data through fixed and mobile sensors, with freely downloadable, CC0-licensed data. This establishes claimed accessibility and collection methods; the comment supplies no pollutant concentrations or sensor-validation results.
- anileated [[./thread#^c39807471|anileated]] argues that an air-quality map should include wind indicators. Their criticism concerns missing environmental context that could help interpret mapped readings.

## Counterarguments

- unsupp0rted [[./thread#^c39806513|unsupp0rted]] finds the map extremely difficult to interpret, and replying commenter btbuildem [[./thread#^c39807003|btbuildem]] describes the entire site as crowded and nearly unreadable. Separately, ACV001 [[./thread#^c39806467|ACV001]] identifies specific ambiguities: blue does not clearly distinguish radiation from poor air quality, and yellow represents higher values than red. These criticisms concern communication of measurements, not demonstrated measurement errors.
- Responding to rozzie’s account of the network [[./thread#^c39806276|rozzie]], defrost [[./thread#^c39806539|defrost]] tentatively characterizes the devices as total-count Geiger instruments, suitable for coarse results but lacking energy discrimination and useful source identification, including radon versus other sources. This criticism concerns radiation instrumentation and cannot establish the reliability of PM2.5, NO2, or CO2 sensors.

## Assumptions

- mike\_d [[./thread#^c39805280|mike\_d]] tentatively concludes, after investigating a device link, that volunteers drive around collecting data with homemade devices. Treating that description as the collection method for every air-quality layer would exceed the evidence: rozzie [[./thread#^c39806276|rozzie]] explicitly describes both fixed and mobile sensors and both radiation and air-quality measurements.
- Using map colors to guide personal exposure decisions would require knowing which quantity the selected layer represents. ACV001 [[./thread#^c39806467|ACV001]] reports uncertainty about that distinction; the discussion supplies no basis for translating the disputed colors into PM2.5, NO2, or CO2 exposure levels.

## Conditions

- Geographic usefulness depends on coverage. anileated [[./thread#^c39807471|anileated]] says limited coverage makes the map useless for many, while defrost [[./thread#^c39806539|defrost]] observes that Australian coverage largely follows occasional drives along a few roads. These are observations about map coverage, not representative measurements of Australian air quality.
- rozzie [[./thread#^c39806276|rozzie]] describes the data as free to download. defrost [[./thread#^c39806539|defrost]] also identifies a free Australian reference dataset, but specifies that it is radiometric; its availability does not establish an equivalent reference for the requested air pollutants.
