# [Ask HN: Any HNers have air quality sensors near-ish 'East Palistine, Ohio'?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=34783664)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion asks whether independent sensor readings or other evidence can clarify potential contamination following the East Palestine train derailment. Participants caution that common consumer air-quality monitors may not identify specific toxic compounds and discuss the need for laboratory analysis of air or water samples. Suggestions include using specialized analytical equipment and relevant local testing laboratories, while others point to EPA sampling reports and ways to request testing. The thread also considers sensor reliability, particulate-monitor deployments at varying distances, water-quality concerns, personal exposure, and the limits of anecdotal observations and available measurements.
Tags: East Palestine derailment, environmental monitoring, air quality sensors, chemical testing, water quality, EPA, public health, measurement limits

## Relevance

This discussion directly concerns measuring air after a chemical incident. It contrasts low-cost particulate and general gas sensors with specialized chemical analysis, mentions reported PM2.5 readings and possible combustion products, and includes a nearby sensor owner’s account with limits on location and setup. ## Arguments

- bfrog [[./thread#^c34784976|bfrog]] and colechristensen [[./thread#^c34784833|colechristensen]] argue that ordinary air-quality monitors cannot adequately identify the toxic chemicals potentially involved in this incident. Particle measurements, CO/CO2 sensors, and broad gas responses answer different questions from identifying specific hazardous compounds. bfrog proposes laboratory sample analysis, potentially using gas chromatography–mass spectrometry; neither commenter reports conducting such testing.
- Accujack [[./thread#^c34785205|Accujack]] says EPA static and roaming monitoring showed slightly elevated PM2.5 but no chemicals from the train. This is a secondhand interpretation of agency measurements: the comment supplies no concentrations, sampling locations, detection limits, or underlying results. Their statement that they have seen no evidence of deaths from the cloud is likewise an account of available evidence, not a measured health assessment.
- Answering a question about combustion products, Accujack [[./thread#^c34800573|Accujack]] lists CO, CO2, HCl, and trace phosgene, attributing the latter two to VCM. They identify HCl as the principal inhalation concern because of its claimed abundance and reaction with moisture in the lungs, while suggesting atmospheric moisture could intercept it. These are chemical-risk claims, without measured exposures or observed clinical outcomes.
- adamredwoods [[./thread#^c34786665|adamredwoods]] points to EPA sampled-air data and says residents can request sampling through the listed contacts. yuppie\_scum [[./thread#^c34785210|yuppie\_scum]] suggests city-data air-quality statistics. These are proposed information sources and practical avenues for investigation; their underlying data are not reproduced in this discussion.

## Counterarguments

- Replying directly to Accujack's reassurance, segmondy [[./thread#^c34788381|segmondy]] asks whether testing covers chemicals produced by burning the cargo, rather than only the original substances. Accujack [[./thread#^c34800573|Accujack]] answers that both are tested. The exchange identifies an important challenge to the reassurance, but the supplied comments contain no testing panel that independently establishes the claimed coverage.
- mdmglr [[./thread#^c34784847|mdmglr]] explicitly warns that sensors must be reliable and links an article without reproducing its findings. Together with bfrog's concern about chemical identification [[./thread#^c34784976|bfrog]], this distinguishes measurement reliability from measuring the relevant pollutant at all. The thread does not establish that any named participant's device is accurate, inaccurate, or suitable for detecting the incident's chemical hazards.
- Other replies to Accujack's reassurance provide skepticism rather than countermeasurements: lm28469 [[./thread#^c34787564|lm28469]] invokes historical hazardous exposures sarcastically; rcme [[./thread#^c34785290|rcme]] supplies only an NBC link; yieldcrv [[./thread#^c34786330|yieldcrv]] asks for more recent images. The link's contents cannot be inferred, and none of these comments supplies an air concentration or documented health outcome that resolves the dispute.

## Assumptions

- colechristensen [[./thread#^c34784833|colechristensen]] hypothesizes that any relevant problem would most likely involve a specific substance toxic at concentrations ordinary monitors cannot detect. This motivates specialized testing but does not demonstrate that such a substance was present. Their dismissal of CO/CO2 as the likely issue concerns this incident, not indoor air quality generally.
- Drawing broad reassurance from reported nondetections requires assumptions about which chemicals were tested, detection limits, sampling times, and geographic coverage. Those details are absent from Accujack's account [[./thread#^c34785205|Accujack]], and segmondy's combustion-products question [[./thread#^c34788381|segmondy]] exposes one such assumption. A lack of reported deaths also leaves less severe or delayed effects unaddressed.
- In reply to bfrog's laboratory-testing suggestion, samstave [[./thread#^c34883256|samstave]] claims cannabis-testing laboratories commonly have suitable equipment and guesses an Ohio laboratory would test for free. Availability, relevant analytical methods, and free service are unverified. The proposed sample is water, so the suggestion does not itself establish a way to measure personal air exposure.

## Conditions

- Cost could determine whether investigation is feasible: CSMastermind [[./thread#^c34784698|CSMastermind]] offers to deploy sensors if affordable, while colechristensen [[./thread#^c34784833|colechristensen]] speculates that specialized equipment could cost five to seven figures. These are willingness and rough cost claims, not quotations for a defined testing program.
- Geography and wind limit comparisons. roland35 [[./thread#^c34798787|roland35]] explicitly qualifies their unchanged experience near Akron by saying they are upwind. In a separate Tucson nitric-acid-spill account, jcrash [[./thread#^c34800880|jcrash]] contrasts a reported one-mile danger zone with approximately 20 mph winds and their own location 15 miles away. The discussion supplies no answer establishing safety at that distance.
- Sampling placement and duration constrain interpretation: 01arjuna's monitor is in a basement about 60 miles south/southeast of the incident [[./thread#^c34823205|01arjuna]], while mamabear19 describes a PurpleAir setup in northeast Ohio for only a few days [[./thread#^c34823915|mamabear19]]. Neither account supplies simultaneous indoor/outdoor measurements or a before-and-after series that would connect those locations to incident emissions.

## Lived experiences

They offer to install and record sensors, subject to cost. This establishes local connections and proposed participation, not completed monitoring or reported exposure effects.

They wonder whether it could detect the incident and have not developed a way to retrieve and publish readings. No numerical measurement or observed incident-related change is supplied.
- mamabear19 [[./thread#^c34823915|mamabear19]] reports setting up a PurpleAir while visiting northeast Ohio. Their brief, unclear description references indoor particulates, COPD, and natural gas. It does not establish measured gas-related pollution, a cooking exposure, symptom changes, or any benefit from monitoring.
- roland35 [[./thread#^c34798787|roland35]] reports noticing no change near Akron and explicitly labels the account anecdotal and upwind. This is a firsthand perception, with no instrument readings or specific health observations; it cannot establish conditions elsewhere.
- jcrash [[./thread#^c34800880|jcrash]] describes a nitric-acid highway spill near their Tucson workplace and says everything shut down. They ask about their own risk 15 miles away but report no measured exposure or symptoms. This is a separate incident, and the mention of nitric acid is not a measurement of NO2.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The adequacy of the reassuring interpretation remains unverified. Accujack [[./thread#^c34785205|Accujack]] reports elevated PM2.5 and no detected train chemicals, while segmondy [[./thread#^c34788381|segmondy]] questions combustion-product coverage and Accujack [[./thread#^c34800573|Accujack]] asserts that it is included. Without the actual results, the discussion cannot establish the magnitude, duration, source, or personal significance of the PM2.5 elevation, or verify coverage of other hazards.
- No participant supplies numerical firsthand PM2.5, NO2, or CO2 readings. The device descriptions from 01arjuna [[./thread#^c34823205|01arjuna]] and mamabear19 [[./thread#^c34823915|mamabear19]] establish monitoring intentions or ownership, while mdmglr's reliability warning [[./thread#^c34784847|mdmglr]] provides no device-validation results. Calibration, detection performance, and agreement between household monitors and specialized sampling remain unassessed.
- The unit does not connect measured exposure with cognition, sleep, comfort, or a documented health change. mamabear19's COPD reference [[./thread#^c34823915|mamabear19]] supplies no symptom history, and Accujack's inhalation-risk explanation [[./thread#^c34800573|Accujack]] supplies no measured dose. CO2 appears in sensor-capability and combustion discussions, including colechristensen's comment [[./thread#^c34784833|colechristensen]], rather than firsthand accounts of indoor CO2 effects.
- There is no reported comparison of ventilation, filtration, or opening versus closing windows. The indoor-monitoring accounts from 01arjuna [[./thread#^c34823205|01arjuna]] and mamabear19 [[./thread#^c34823915|mamabear19]] describe no intervention outcomes. Consequently, this unit cannot establish when opening windows would help or worsen exposure, or compare practical responses to cooking, traffic pollution, or wildfire smoke.
