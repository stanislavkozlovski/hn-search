# [A 45 year engineer clears up electric sauna ventilation](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39569077)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion unpacks recommendations for ventilation in electrically heated saunas, including inlet and outlet placement, air mixing, temperature distribution, humidity, and CO2. Participants compare electric and wood-fired sauna ventilation, consider heat recovery and moisture management, and clarify how sauna ventilation relates to whole-building systems. The thread branches into sauna construction advice, DIY builds, benches and materials, cooking in saunas, phone behavior in heat, and clarification of the title’s reference to an engineer’s years of experience.
Tags: sauna, ventilation, CO2, HVAC, heat-recovery, building-design, DIY, engineering

## Relevance

The sauna ventilation discussion directly concerns CO2 accumulation in a small occupied room and ventilation design. A firsthand account describes using a poorly ventilated sauna; replies discuss monitoring CO2, air exchange, heat recovery, humidity, and the differing drafts in electric versus wood-fired saunas. ## Arguments

- Ventilation is presented as serving both CO2 control and comfort. dharma1 [[./thread#^c39573171|dharma1]] identifies preventing excessive CO2 as its main purpose; gifvenut [[./thread#^c39570830|gifvenut]], quoting the article, adds comfortable temperature distribution and evenly distributed steam. These are design objectives, not demonstrated health, cognition, or sleep outcomes.
- For the electric-sauna arrangement discussed, xenonite [[./thread#^c39571086|xenonite]] recommends an inlet above the heater and an outlet near the opposite floor, claiming faster warming of incoming air, more uniform temperatures, and better steam distribution. vesinisa [[./thread#^c39571073|vesinisa]] explains this through cooler, denser supply air descending onto the heater before warming and mixing. Neither supplies measurements in the comment.
- golem14 [[./thread#^c39575225|golem14]] proposes a visible CO2 monitor and brief door opening when concentrations become excessive. This is a question about a potentially simpler alternative to continuous ventilation, not a reported successful installation; no trigger concentration or opening duration is established.
- adrianN [[./thread#^c39570879|adrianN]] proposes heat exchange as an obvious ventilation improvement. vesinisa [[./thread#^c39571073|vesinisa]] says building-level heat recovery can coexist with sauna circulation because recovery is incomplete and incoming air remains cooler than the sauna.
- Comfort also depends on room geometry and seating. vikaveri [[./thread#^c39572296|vikaveri]] argues that feet should be approximately level with the tops of the heater stones and that barrel curvature can prevent sufficiently high benches. jmacd [[./thread#^c39571765|jmacd]] mentions products intended to address design constraints, particularly ceiling height, but reports no comparative performance results.

## Counterarguments

- Aurornis [[./thread#^c39573024|Aurornis]] disputes the economics of a dedicated heat-recovery ventilator for a small, intermittently occupied, very humid sauna. Ekaros [[./thread#^c39571125|Ekaros]], replying within the heat-recovery discussion, adds that several litres of water may be poured onto the heater, plus perspiration, making condensation handling a material consideration. Neither provides costs or measured savings.
- Aurornis [[./thread#^c39573024|Aurornis]] also argues that allowing heat to escape helps regulate temperature. lukan [[./thread#^c39573962|lukan]] directly disputes that emphasis: ventilation should exchange stale air for fresh air, while temperature should primarily be controlled through heating, with door opening as a fallback. The thread does not experimentally resolve these competing descriptions.
- SoftTalker [[./thread#^c39576041|SoftTalker]] challenges the assumption that all saunas have substantial added steam: their gym prohibits water on the rocks, leaving occupants' perspiration as the stated humidity source. yencabulator [[./thread#^c39581736|yencabulator]] responds that this differs from the Finnish sauna designs under discussion, limiting how far either humidity description can be generalized.
- The electric-sauna layout is not treated as universally applicable to wood-fired rooms. In response to Maxion's question about combustion draft [[./thread#^c39571286|Maxion]], jmilloy [[./thread#^c39574258|jmilloy]] says an internally located wood stove can itself provide exhaust. However, falserum [[./thread#^c39571978|falserum]] reports stove inlets outside the sauna locally, and mikeyouse [[./thread#^c39573005|mikeyouse]] advocates direct venting. The draft argument depends on where combustion air actually comes from.

## Assumptions

- vesinisa [[./thread#^c39571073|vesinisa]] describes entering air as cold and low in CO2. That supplies the proposed dilution and mixing mechanism, but the comment contains no measurements of the replacement air or its other pollutants. Lower CO2 is therefore an assumed benefit of the supply air, not evidence that every aspect of exposure improves.
- Natural leakage and door traffic are assumed to provide potentially sufficient exchange. golem14 [[./thread#^c39575225|golem14]] wonders whether a non-airtight sauna could reach a high but non-dangerous steady-state CO2 level; SoftTalker [[./thread#^c39574260|SoftTalker]] suspects frequent door openings adequately ventilate their gym sauna. Neither establishes airflow, CO2 concentration, or a criterion for adequacy.
- The specific problem motivating the original work is partly inferred. jaktet [[./thread#^c39572741|jaktet]], replying to gifvenut's quotation of desirable sauna characteristics [[./thread#^c39570830|gifvenut]], argues that those characteristics do not identify a concrete failure and only assumes that the backyard sauna's owner was dissatisfied with ventilation. The supplied discussion does not establish a measured baseline problem.

## Conditions

- Building configuration changes the relevance of heat recovery. distances [[./thread#^c39573492|distances]] describes Finnish electric saunas as usually inside homes or apartments, accessed through bathrooms, and separate sauna buildings as usually wood-fired. vesinisa [[./thread#^c39590671|vesinisa]] describes ventilation shared at apartment, house, or building scale. Thorrez [[./thread#^c39573434|Thorrez]] points out that the article instead concerns a backyard sauna, so the integrated-building explanation may not describe that installation.
- Cold-climate practice and historical standards matter. vesinisa [[./thread#^c39571073|vesinisa]] describes building-level heat recovery as common in Finnish mechanized systems. Maxion [[./thread#^c39571284|Maxion]] says current Finnish requirements differ from those when the early-1990s sauna study was conducted and emphasizes that the study concerned sauna design rather than whole-house HVAC optimization. These are commenters' accounts of practice and regulations, not independently verified findings here.
- Availability may constrain practical implementation in the United States. nothercastle [[./thread#^c39581383|nothercastle]] complains about the lack of high-quality kits and the burden of sourcing materials; swagmoose [[./thread#^c39573528|swagmoose]] similarly reports finding few kits that follow their understanding of traditional best practices. These are individual market assessments, not a systematic survey.
- Budget affects enclosure and heater choices. dharmaturtle [[./thread#^c39575520|dharmaturtle]] reports a build costing under \$100, while salad-tycoon [[./thread#^c39580329|salad-tycoon]] asks about an approximately \$1,500 upgrade budget. dharmaturtle [[./thread#^c39584077|dharmaturtle]] identifies heater capacity, possible electrical-circuit work, wood costs, and limited time and money as constraints. Their discussion supplies no priced comparison of ventilation or heat-recovery systems.

## Lived experiences

- dharma1 [[./thread#^c39573171|dharma1]] reports using a sauna with a slightly ajar glass door and ventilation they consider inadequate, while postponing planned vent holes through the sauna and house walls. This is firsthand evidence of an improvised ventilation arrangement, without CO2 readings or reported symptoms.
- SoftTalker [[./thread#^c39574260|SoftTalker]] reports seeing no obvious vents in a gym sauna large enough to seat more than a dozen people, with frequent arrivals and departures. The room size and door traffic are observations; adequate air exchange is their inference. Their separate report that water is prohibited on the rocks [[./thread#^c39576041|SoftTalker]] establishes a different moisture regime from the steam-producing Finnish examples.
- dharmaturtle [[./thread#^c39575520|dharmaturtle]] reports approximately four years of every-other-day use of a very inexpensive homemade sauna and initially advises against copying it. In the follow-up, dharmaturtle [[./thread#^c39584077|dharmaturtle]] says a wooden enclosure leaked too much air for the homemade heater, whereas thermal foil and a sleeping bag allowed heating in about 10–15 minutes. This demonstrates their heat-retention tradeoff, without establishing adequate ventilation or safe CO2 levels.
- Comfort preferences differ. throwaway11460 [[./thread#^c39574093|throwaway11460]] prefers dry air and a somewhat lower temperature. nolroz [[./thread#^c39575306|nolroz]] prefers their gym's steam room because it is less crowded and speculates that phone use attracts people to the sauna. Neither account measures humidity or pollutants, and the crowding explanation is explicitly an interpretation.
- SOLAR\_FIELDS [[./thread#^c39577217|SOLAR\_FIELDS]] provides a temperature-linked device observation: in a barrel sauna, a phone approximately half a metre below the head-height thermostat rarely overheats when the thermostat reads 170°F, but typically produces a heat warning after about 15 minutes at 190°F. Position and insulation reportedly matter. These are thermostat readings and phone responses, not air-quality measurements or a mapped temperature profile.
- Cooking appears as practice rather than measured exposure. Permik [[./thread#^c39574091|Permik]] describes cooking foil-wrapped sausages on sauna stones in Finland; Moru [[./thread#^c39575026|Moru]], replying to that account, recalls relatives cooking meat, mainly in their smoky sauna. dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c39575972|dzhiurgis]] adds a general report about soy-marinated eggs in Korea. None quantifies cooking emissions, smoke concentrations, or health effects.

## Unresolved disagreements

- This source unit provides no numerical CO2, PM2.5, or NO2 concentrations and no demonstrated effects on health, cognition, or sleep. dharma1 [[./thread#^c39573171|dharma1]] supplies a CO2 concern, while gifvenut [[./thread#^c39570830|gifvenut]] supplies quoted design goals. The 1992 study and its claimed incorporation into Finnish guidance appear through vesinisa's description and link [[./thread#^c39590671|vesinisa]], without study measurements or methods reproduced here.
- It remains unknown whether leakage or occasional door opening can maintain acceptable CO2 under particular occupancy and session lengths. golem14 [[./thread#^c39575225|golem14]] asks about steady-state concentration and monitoring, while SoftTalker [[./thread#^c39574260|SoftTalker]] infers adequacy from door traffic. No supplied account compares these approaches with a specified continuous ventilation rate.
- CO2-sensor reliability in sauna conditions remains untested. golem14 [[./thread#^c39575225|golem14]] explicitly questions operation in humidity. Phone anecdotes vary: throwaway11460 [[./thread#^c39574093|throwaway11460]] and renewiltord [[./thread#^c39574165|renewiltord]] report little trouble, whereas vanattab [[./thread#^c39574122|vanattab]] and dharma1 [[./thread#^c39575446|dharma1]] report overheating, including higher-bench sensitivity. These devices and conditions differ, and none validates an air sensor. Moreover, 3-cheese-sundae [[./thread#^c39580194|3-cheese-sundae]] disputes SOLAR\_FIELDS' claim of harmless phone exposure [[./thread#^c39577217|SOLAR\_FIELDS]], citing battery risks rather than reporting a measured failure.
- The heat-recovery disagreement lacks a common installation scope and quantitative comparison. adrianN [[./thread#^c39570879|adrianN]] proposes an improvement, Aurornis [[./thread#^c39573024|Aurornis]] rejects the economics of a dedicated moisture-tolerant unit, and vesinisa [[./thread#^c39571073|vesinisa]] describes shared building systems. The discussion establishes neither payback nor the best balance between recovered heat, moisture management, and air mixing.
- Wood-stove ventilation cannot be resolved without knowing the air path. jmilloy [[./thread#^c39574258|jmilloy]] describes the stove as room exhaust, whereas falserum [[./thread#^c39571978|falserum]] describes an outside inlet. No measured exchange or combustion-pollutant data establish how either arrangement affects occupants' exposure.
- The discussion does not establish when opening windows improves or worsens total exposure. The openings described by dharma1 [[./thread#^c39573171|dharma1]] and proposed by golem14 [[./thread#^c39575225|golem14]] are sauna doors, without measured replacement-air quality. There are no comparisons involving filtration, traffic pollution, or wildfire smoke; the cooking and smoky-sauna account from Moru [[./thread#^c39575026|Moru]] supplies no particulate measurements that could fill that gap.
