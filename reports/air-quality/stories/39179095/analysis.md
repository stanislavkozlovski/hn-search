# [Experiments on a \$50 DIY air purifier you can make in 30s](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39179095)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants assess a low-cost fan-and-filter air purifier and debate how its measured performance compares with commercial units and Corsi–Rosenthal boxes. They raise questions about airflow, noise, static pressure, energy use, filter selection, and whether the experiment needs a fan-only control to establish causality. A substantial thread disputes California certification rules for air cleaners, weighing consumer protection and ozone safety against access to inexpensive DIY filtration. Other comments compare convenience, aesthetics, automatic controls, filter costs, and commercial alternatives.
Tags: DIY air purifier, air filtration, experimental controls, California regulation, MERV, HEPA, noise, commercial purifiers

## Relevance

Comments compare DIY and commercial air cleaners on particle removal, noise, power, cost, and convenience, including firsthand use. The discussion also reports indoor PM increases from ultrasonic humidifiers and considers practical filtration limitations. ## Arguments

- Basic particle filtration need not require expensive electronics. lispisok [[./thread#^c39180395|lispisok]] argues that a fan moving air through a HEPA filter provides the essential function; altairprime [[./thread#^c39180443|altairprime]] argues that a simple assembly can clean ordinary household air, although more slowly than a commercial purifier. These are effectiveness claims without numerical results in the supplied comments.
- Filter efficiency must be considered alongside airflow and repeated recirculation. legulere [[./thread#^c39183601|legulere]] argues that lower-grade filters can progressively reduce particles through repeated passes, while amluto [[./thread#^c39182530|amluto]] recommends MERV 13–16 over HEPA for cost and performance. amluto [[./thread#^c39186024|amluto]] explicitly emphasizes airflow multiplied by removal efficiency.
- Additional filters and alternative constructions are proposed to improve DIY performance. lamontcg [[./thread#^c39179409|lamontcg]] recommends a Corsi–Rosenthal box; StillBored [[./thread#^c39180513|StillBored]] suggests additional filters or a prefilter. Neither supplies a measured comparison in these comments, and StillBored labels the airflow explanation as theoretical.
- Automation and other features can have practical value. cheald [[./thread#^c39180839|cheald]] describes particle-responsive fan speeds that remain quiet most of the time and increase during particle spikes, with cooking as an example. jejeyyy77 [[./thread#^c39180728|jejeyyy77]] identifies monitoring, remote control, appearance, heating and rotation as additional reasons to choose a commercial device.
- Reducing an indoor particle source may matter alongside filtration. lispisok [[./thread#^c39180635|lispisok]] recommends replacing ultrasonic humidifiers with evaporative models after observing elevated PM. s0rce [[./thread#^c39181037|s0rce]] instead proposes distilled or deionized water to avoid mineral aerosols, and suggests a conductivity cutoff. These proposed remedies are not compared experimentally in the supplied discussion.
- Several commenters support certification as consumer protection for products marketed around health. SECProto [[./thread#^c39181331|SECProto]] emphasizes certification and ozone restrictions; mustacheemperor [[./thread#^c39181426|mustacheemperor]] argues that a sound general design does not guarantee an authentic, effective product from an online seller. Their claims about the regulation and counterfeit risk are interpretations, not documented product tests.

## Counterarguments

- Evidence for one filter configuration does not establish the performance of another. klipklop [[./thread#^c39184208|klipklop]] invokes EPA-backed evidence for DIY HEPA filtration against PM2.5 when defending the Lasko product. amluto [[./thread#^c39186024|amluto]] identifies its supplied filter as MERV 10 and questions its advertised smoke performance. The filter holder accepting replacements does not resolve the supplied configuration’s performance.
- Moving air through a restrictive filter is an engineering constraint. OJFord [[./thread#^c39180934|OJFord]] questions whether an inexpensive fan supplies sufficient pressure, and Casteil [[./thread#^c39183057|Casteil]] favors a commercial blower for that reason. This challenges StillBored’s [[./thread#^c39180513|StillBored]] suggestion that a box fan’s much higher nominal airflow should translate into faster cleaning.
- The price comparison does not automatically establish an automation advantage. conductr [[./thread#^c39180724|conductr]] initially argues that a roughly \$100 commercial purifier offers sensing over a roughly \$60 DIY build. neogodless [[./thread#^c39181117|neogodless]] corrects the specific comparison to \$100 versus \$54, with neither device sensing automatically. conductr’s [[./thread#^c39181293|conductr]] subsequent account of owning a different \$100 sensing model does not settle the featured comparator’s specifications.
- Certification could restrict access to inexpensive filtration during smoke events. manzanarama [[./thread#^c39182227|manzanarama]] argues that compliance costs could eliminate a low-margin product and that this harm might outweigh the regulation’s benefit. SECProto [[./thread#^c39183431|SECProto]] disputes the likely purchasing impact and points to inexpensive DIY alternatives. Neither supplies actual certification costs or evidence of resulting exposure changes.
- The reported California restriction concerns a particular assembled product, not the availability of every fan-and-filter combination. amluto [[./thread#^c39186076|amluto]] reports being able to buy fans, clips, MERV 13 filters and commercial purifiers in California. klipklop [[./thread#^c39184228|klipklop]] nevertheless values the restricted product’s integrated holder and standard replacement-filter size.
- Particle filtration does not guarantee noticeable symptom relief or whole-room hair collection. swozey [[./thread#^c39180387|swozey]] reports persistent symptoms and disappointing dog-hair capture despite multiple devices, while acknowledging that symptoms might be worse without them. oh\_sigh [[./thread#^c39181440|oh\_sigh]] argues that large hair must come very close to the intake and suggests a HEPA vacuum instead.

## Assumptions

- This analysis concerns only story 39179095, part 0. sp332 [[./thread#^c39179269|sp332]] dates the material to 2021, and Sporktacular [[./thread#^c39182244|Sporktacular]] [[./thread#^c39187494|Sporktacular]] identifies earlier posting. Reposting does not constitute an independent experiment; the supplied unit contains comments rather than the underlying experimental article or its data.
- The discussion principally concerns particles, humidification and device usability. It supplies no NO2 or CO2 concentrations, traffic-exposure measurements, cognitive testing or sleep outcomes. cheald’s [[./thread#^c39180839|cheald]] cooking example concerns particles without identifying the stove type; subpixel’s [[./thread#^c39183845|subpixel]] bedroom account concerns appearance and automatic operation.
- The firsthand pollution reports are qualitative rather than numerical PM2.5 datasets. lispisok [[./thread#^c39180635|lispisok]] reports an 'unhealthy' PM indication, and amluto [[./thread#^c39186024|amluto]] reports handheld particle-counter testing without readings. klipklop’s [[./thread#^c39184208|klipklop]] explicit PM2.5 effectiveness claim relies on a linked EPA source. Sensor calibration, particle-size results and measurement uncertainty are not supplied.
- A fair performance comparison would require comparable delivered clean airflow and operating conditions. StillBored [[./thread#^c39180513|StillBored]] discusses nominal fan airflow, while OJFord [[./thread#^c39180934|OJFord]] discusses pressure against filter resistance. Reported wattage or quietness alone cannot settle which arrangement delivers more clean air for the energy used.
- Persistent symptoms should not be treated as evidence of a documented high particle reading. swozey [[./thread#^c39181561|swozey]] explicitly says the device displays remain 'good'; turtlebits’s [[./thread#^c39181833|turtlebits]] subsequent advice about insufficient CADR is conditional on poor air quality. Likewise, lispisok’s [[./thread#^c39180635|lispisok]] humidifier measurement comes from a separate household and does not establish swozey’s humidifier type or exposure.

## Conditions

- Upfront costs depend heavily on components and local availability. turtlebits [[./thread#^c39181763|turtlebits]] estimates about \$30 using a roughly \$10, 20-by-20-by-1-inch filter; SECProto [[./thread#^c39183431|SECProto]] gives a \$30 fan plus \$10 filter example. OJFord [[./thread#^c39180934|OJFord]] questions whether the advertised budget is achievable where inexpensive North American-style box fans are uncommon.
- Commercial alternatives span more than the expensive devices criticized in the thread. mustacheemperor [[./thread#^c39181483|mustacheemperor]] considers approximately \$150 worthwhile for appearance and sound. RangerScience [[./thread#^c39180850|RangerScience]] also rejects treating price as the only reason to DIY. These are different preferences and purchasing conditions, not a shared judgment about the best product.
- Ownership costs include replacement filters and electricity. neogodless [[./thread#^c39181117|neogodless]] calls for both in the comparison; subpixel [[./thread#^c39183845|subpixel]] expects higher filter costs after buying a commercial bedroom unit. storyinmemo [[./thread#^c39180871|storyinmemo]] accepts a higher purchase price for low-power automatic operation. No complete lifetime-cost comparison is supplied.
- Wildfire conditions increase the importance of capacity and procurement. manzanarama [[./thread#^c39182227|manzanarama]] describes California potentially being smoked out for weeks. altairprime [[./thread#^c39180443|altairprime]] argues that filtration must keep up with particles arriving in outdoor air and recommends a stronger build when money and available parts permit. This qualifies the same commenter’s claim that a simple setup suffices under typical household conditions.
- Room size, placement and noise tolerance affect usable performance. turtlebits [[./thread#^c39181763|turtlebits]] questions whether \$100 purifiers provide enough CADR for a large living room. jerlam [[./thread#^c39181638|jerlam]] contrasts the clearance needed around a box fan with upward-exhaust commercial designs, and more\_corn [[./thread#^c39180472|more\_corn]] emphasizes the space occupied by Corsi–Rosenthal boxes.
- swozey [[./thread#^c39181561|swozey]] describes Denver hard water, renting and concerns about permanent connections as barriers to whole-house water treatment or humidification. These constraints qualify oh\_sigh’s [[./thread#^c39181440|oh\_sigh]] recommendation for whole-house equipment. They are household-specific accounts rather than evidence that every Colorado home has the same conditions.
- Window use has very limited evidence here. mikecoles [[./thread#^c39181363|mikecoles]] recalls window-mounted fans improving comfort before widespread air conditioning, without exposure measurements. altairprime’s [[./thread#^c39180443|altairprime]] concern about incoming wildfire particles implies that increasing outdoor exchange during smoke could undermine particle control. No supplied account measures whether opening windows improves or worsens PM2.5, NO2 or CO2.

## Lived experiences

- swozey [[./thread#^c39180387|swozey]] reports purifiers and humidifiers in every room but continuing sniffles and a dripping, scratchy throat; NAC reportedly helps. A large humidifier reportedly raises townhouse humidity from 10–20\% to 65\% and lasts about a week. swozey [[./thread#^c39181561|swozey]] later reports consistently 'good' indoor readings and a 'good' monthly outdoor display, whose data source is unspecified. There is no controlled symptom comparison.
- lispisok [[./thread#^c39180635|lispisok]] reports discovering elevated PM after buying an ultrasonic humidifier: a monitor showed an 'unhealthy' range despite a large HEPA purifier operating in the same room. This is a firsthand instrument observation, but no concentration, water composition, sensor model or intervention result is given. The linked research is not reproduced.
- amluto [[./thread#^c39186024|amluto]] reports personally testing filters in wildfire smoke with a handheld particle counter. That experience motivates insisting on defined specifications, but the comment provides no numerical readings or detailed comparison from those tests.
- cheald [[./thread#^c39180839|cheald]] reports owning roughly \$140 purifiers that normally operate almost silently and increase speed and noise when particles rise, with cooking offered as an example. The account describes sensor-responsive operation without reporting a cooking exposure concentration or health outcome.
- storyinmemo [[./thread#^c39180871|storyinmemo]] reports four years of Coway 400 use, approximately 5 watts at low operation and automatic ramping when particles increase. A separate bedside comfort fan reportedly draws 30 watts. These are reported operating figures for different devices, not a comparison at matched clean-air delivery or measured sleep quality.
- Casteil [[./thread#^c39183057|Casteil]] reports approximately three years of satisfactory, quiet Winix 5300 ownership. twisteriffic [[./thread#^c39182165|twisteriffic]] reports 5–8 watts at setting three of four for the 5500/5300, but later describes ticking or rattling in all three C535 units owned, unlike five 5500/5300 units [[./thread#^c39184427|twisteriffic]]. Similar-looking models did not provide the same noise experience.
- mbishop [[./thread#^c39183044|mbishop]] built a box-fan Corsi–Rosenthal purifier and considered it effective, but found it too loud to use with visitors. A replacement using computer fans was reportedly much quieter and accepted standard filters from Lowe’s, at greater cost. No particle measurements accompany the effectiveness judgment.
- \_neil [[./thread#^c39182378|\_neil]] uses a large-filter DIY assembly in an office and finds it loud. A Wi-Fi plug allows full-speed operation during vacuuming or brief absences. 'Seems to work well enough' is a subjective assessment without measured removal rates.
- subpixel [[./thread#^c39183845|subpixel]] built a large DIY purifier and then bought a commercial bedroom unit, accepting more expensive filters for better appearance and automatic operation. The account establishes a usability preference, not improved sleep.
- conductr [[./thread#^c39181293|conductr]] reports a roughly \$100 commercial purifier with sensing, very quiet operation and a reusable or washable filter that had not been replaced after about three years. No measurements establish filtration performance after that period.
- thaumasiotes [[./thread#^c39180480|thaumasiotes]], replying to swozey’s symptom account, reports persistent postnasal symptoms since a cold seven years earlier and dissatisfaction with medical responses. The account supplies no exposure measurement or purifier comparison, so it cannot identify an air-quality cause.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The experiment’s causal interpretation remains challenged. randa222 [[./thread#^c39182176|randa222]] requests a fan-without-filter control, proposing mixing, surface deposition or particles sticking to blades as alternative explanations for sensor changes; the commenter also flags surprising tape-versus-no-tape results. foobarian [[./thread#^c39181819|foobarian]] separately wonders whether sensor scaling exaggerates apparent changes, but provides no evidence that the measurements are logarithmic. Neither concern is resolved in the supplied replies.
- The optimal filter grade and particle-size performance remain disputed. amluto [[./thread#^c39186024|amluto]] questions MERV 10 for submicron smoke and favors higher grades; woleium [[./thread#^c39187932|woleium]] responds that intermediate particle sizes are the difficult ones. amluto [[./thread#^c39190620|amluto]] cannot locate the recalled supporting chart but maintains the higher-MERV recommendation. This exchange does not establish a measured winner between HEPA and MERV 13–16 at comparable airflow.
- The cause of swozey’s symptoms remains unknown. oh\_sigh [[./thread#^c39181440|oh\_sigh]] suggests household allergens and raises medication concerns; turtlebits [[./thread#^c39181809|turtlebits]] suggests 40–50\% humidity instead of the reported 65\%; baq [[./thread#^c39181078|baq]] suggests investigating reflux. swozey [[./thread#^c39181561|swozey]] clarifies intermittent antihistamine use and mentions a possible respiratory illness. These are competing explanations and unverified advice, not diagnoses or demonstrated purifier failures.
- Distilled water does not settle the commenters’ humidifier disagreement. s0rce [[./thread#^c39181037|s0rce]] presents it as the solution to mineral particles. amluto [[./thread#^c39182557|amluto]] [[./thread#^c39185949|amluto]] additionally argues that frequent sterilization is necessary because wet containers develop biofilm and can aerosolize pathogens. s0rce [[./thread#^c39184616|s0rce]] reports not encountering the issue and asks whether it would appear as mold or odor. No microbial measurements or infection outcomes resolve the exchange.
- The California debate leaves both regulatory scope and net exposure effects unresolved. SECProto [[./thread#^c39181331|SECProto]] emphasizes certification and ozone, whereas foofie [[./thread#^c39184021|foofie]] [[./thread#^c39189883|foofie]] frames testing as assurance of purification efficacy. The supplied text does not establish which properties the relevant certification verifies. manzanarama’s [[./thread#^c39182227|manzanarama]] access-cost argument and SECProto’s [[./thread#^c39183431|SECProto]] skepticism also lack quantified costs, purchasing effects or resulting health outcomes.
