# [Airline cabin air quality is worse than before smoking ban (2016)](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=28406229)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants question whether the claim that airline cabin air worsened after smoking bans is well supported, and debate how to compare elevated cabin CO2 with tobacco smoke. The discussion covers limited evidence, cabin air exchange and recirculation, CO2 as a marker of ventilation and potential infectious exposure, and whether reduced fresh-air changes were driven by operating costs.
Tags: airline cabins, air quality, CO2, ventilation, evidence

## Relevance

The thread discusses cabin CO2 readings, ventilation and recirculated air, and whether elevated CO2 or tobacco smoke presents the greater concern. It distinguishes personal comfort thresholds from claims about research and leaves the comparative evidence disputed. ## Arguments

- ReGenGen [[./thread#^c28408931|ReGenGen]] cites a research figure of 1,350 ± 350 ppmv CO2 and calls that air unpleasant if the finding is correct, while explicitly disclaiming knowledge of the research quality. This is a reported research result and a comfort judgment, not a firsthand cabin measurement.
- xfitm3 [[./thread#^c28408550|xfitm3]], responding to skepticism about comparing CO2 with tobacco smoke, argues that high CO2 causes human problems and points to commercial-building sensors, minimum fresh-air requirements, and two documents. These support the commenter's concern about ventilation but do not establish the historical cabin-air comparison.
- ReGenGen [[./thread#^c28409465|ReGenGen]], replying to mrosett's discussion of direct CO2 effects, argues that elevated CO2 also indicates inadequate air circulation and exposure to other people's exhaled air, potentially spreading SARS-CoV-2, influenza, or measles. This introduces an infection-exposure argument distinct from CO2 toxicity; no firsthand infection outcome or cabin-specific transmission measurement accompanies it.

## Counterarguments

- sparky\_z [[./thread#^c28407446|sparky\_z]] questions whether the headline has support beyond a friend of a letter-to-the-editor author. In reply, throwawayswede [[./thread#^c28410358|throwawayswede]] acknowledges that hearing the same claim on QI is 'not exactly evidence.' The supplied discussion therefore leaves its central evidentiary challenge unanswered.
- mrosett [[./thread#^c28407850|mrosett]] [[./thread#^c28409088|mrosett]] argues that showing elevated CO2 is undesirable does not establish that cabin air was healthier when it contained cigarette smoke. magwa101 [[./thread#^c28407951|magwa101]] independently emphasizes tobacco's carcinogenic and particulate exposure. Their objection concerns the comparison of different hazards, rather than a claim that ventilation is unimportant.
- mrosett [[./thread#^c28409088|mrosett]] interprets xfitm3's linked documents as saying concentrations below 5,000 ppm have no ill-health effects except perhaps drowsiness, and says cabin concentrations appear typically around 1,000–1,500 ppm. These are the commenter's interpretations and reported estimates, not independently verified safety thresholds or firsthand readings.

## Assumptions

- throwawayswede [[./thread#^c28410397|throwawayswede]] recalls a pre-ban requirement to replace air every three minutes and claims current replacement uses half fresh and half reused air. The proposed explanation depends on those historical and operational claims being accurate. A replacement interval and a fresh-air fraction alone do not establish comparable ventilation rates, passenger exposures, or the treatment of reused air.
- throwawayswede [[./thread#^c28410358|throwawayswede]] describes difficulty finding information beyond apparent airline advertising and speculates that such material concealed savings from operating with 'half-packs.' The advertising characterization and concealment motive remain the commenter's interpretation, rather than demonstrated explanations.

## Conditions

- throwawayswede [[./thread#^c28410397|throwawayswede]] quotes the article's claim that heating subzero outside air to cabin temperature costs money and therefore discourages fresh-air replacement. This identifies a proposed financial and thermal tradeoff specific to the aircraft setting. No costs, operating records, or quantified exposure consequences are supplied, and the outside air's temperature says nothing here about its pollutant concentrations.

## Lived experiences

- ReGenGen [[./thread#^c28408931|ReGenGen]] reports opening windows when CO2 reaches roughly 800 ppm. This is a personal intervention threshold, separate from the cited research concentration. The comment provides no location, sensor details, before-and-after readings, or observed improvement in symptoms, cognition, sleep, or comfort; it also does not describe an intervention available to an airline passenger.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Whether cabin air became worse after smoking bans remains unresolved. mrosett [[./thread#^c28409088|mrosett]] asks for a comparison encompassing cigarette smoke, while throwawayswede [[./thread#^c28410397|throwawayswede]] answers with a proposed ventilation mechanism. The supplied comments offer no paired historical measurements or comparative health outcomes that settle that disagreement.
- Effects at the reported cabin CO2 concentrations remain disputed: ReGenGen [[./thread#^c28408931|ReGenGen]] calls them unpleasant, xfitm3 [[./thread#^c28408550|xfitm3]] invokes general human harms, and mrosett [[./thread#^c28409088|mrosett]] reads the cited documents as allowing perhaps drowsiness below 5,000 ppm. No firsthand cognitive testing, sleep observations, or symptom-response measurements distinguish these positions. ReGenGen's infection argument [[./thread#^c28409465|ReGenGen]] concerns an additional exposure pathway rather than resolving the direct-effects dispute.
- Measurement reliability and practical improvement remain unassessed. ReGenGen [[./thread#^c28408931|ReGenGen]] supplies a research figure and a personal window-opening threshold without sensor type, calibration, placement, or measurement protocol. Neither that comment nor throwawayswede's recirculation claim [[./thread#^c28410397|throwawayswede]] establishes how much an intervention changes exposure or how filtration performs.
- The broader requested exposure comparisons are not addressed by this unit. magwa101 [[./thread#^c28407951|magwa101]] mentions tobacco particulate matter without identifying PM2.5 concentrations; no supplied comment measures NO2 or describes cooking, traffic, wildfire smoke, or outdoor pollution. ReGenGen's window-opening habit [[./thread#^c28408931|ReGenGen]] therefore cannot establish when opening windows improves overall air quality or brings in worse outdoor exposure.
