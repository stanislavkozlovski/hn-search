# [Low-cost open source device can measure air pollution anywhere](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=35186431)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants examine an open-source mobile air-pollution monitor intended to collect measurements while mounted on vehicles. They question its cost and complexity, identify what sensors are included, and compare it with cheaper monitors and existing community projects. Technical concerns include sensor calibration, humidity and temperature effects, airflow around the particle sensor, vehicle speed, placement, durability, and whether mobile sampling yields useful location data. The discussion also considers the value of public pollution maps and making local air pollution more visible.
Tags: air pollution monitoring, open source hardware, mobile sensing, PM2.5, sensor calibration, measurement accuracy, DIY electronics, community data

## Relevance

Comments address measuring PM and NO2 in mobile and street settings, sensor calibration and reliability, and how traffic pollution may affect people near schools. They distinguish the usefulness of low-cost indicators from limits in interpreting pollutant composition and readings. ## Arguments

- jl6 [[./thread#^c35187412|jl6]] argues that local monitoring makes pollution relevant to the air someone actually breathes. ggm [[./thread#^c35190180|ggm]] supports street-level data collection for exposure modelling and advocates measuring nitrogen oxides alongside particles and CO/CO2. These are arguments for better exposure information, without demonstrated health improvements from monitoring itself.
- Responding to objections about complex particle chemistry, ddulaney [[./thread#^c35188556|ddulaney]] argues that even a crude PM2.5 concentration remains useful. This distinguishes measuring particulate concentration from identifying every constituent; the usefulness of the former need not depend on achieving the latter.
- x3iv130f [[./thread#^c35190285|x3iv130f]] recommends monitoring temperature, humidity, CO2, VOCs, and PM2.5 together, asserting that elevated CO2 can affect sleep and critical thinking and that VOCs are associated with asthma and allergic reactions. The comment supplies no concentrations, study citations, or personal outcomes supporting those health claims.
- x3iv130f [[./thread#^c35190285|x3iv130f]] argues that filtration must match the pollutant: ordinary household HEPA filters allegedly capture little VOC pollution, while substantial charcoal filtration is needed. This is advice about gaseous pollutants, with no measured filter performance or firsthand account of improvement.
- gniv [[./thread#^c35187641|gniv]] identifies calibration, especially under changing humidity, as the project's apparent novelty. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c35193174|ahaucnx]], identifying an AirGradient affiliation, similarly argues that trustworthy measurements—not simply obtaining sensor output—determine whether such projects succeed.
- callalex [[./thread#^c35191293|callalex]] suggests checking whether nearby residents contribute to PurpleAir. theaussiestew [[./thread#^c35187587|theaussiestew]] proposes portable personal monitoring with optional contributions to a collective dataset. These offer possible routes to more local information, but neither establishes coverage or accuracy for another commenter's location.

## Counterarguments

- photochemsyn [[./thread#^c35188455|photochemsyn]] challenges broad claims that inexpensive devices can characterize air pollution adequately. They quote analytical-chemistry papers describing more than 15,000 chromatographic peaks in a PM2.5 sample and laboratory measurements of airborne metals. These illustrate compositional complexity and expensive analytical requirements; they do not directly test whether inexpensive PM concentration measurements are useful.
- ckocagil [[./thread#^c35188976|ckocagil]] describes PM sensing as straightforward laser particle counting. Elsewhere, ahaucnx [[./thread#^c35193174|ahaucnx]] raises substantial implementation concerns: the SPS30's stated maximum surrounding airflow is 1 m/s, and vehicle mounting introduces uncertain airflow, temperature, and humidity effects. These are questions about deployment accuracy, not demonstrated failures of the particular enclosure.
- B1FF\_PSUVM [[./thread#^c35190610|B1FF\_PSUVM]] concludes from shopping and buyer reviews that inexpensive multipollutant devices often fabricate readings. stephen\_g [[./thread#^c35194764|stephen\_g]] cites a video examination of an advertised CO2 meter apparently containing a VOC sensor that responded to alcohol vapour. Both accounts are secondhand; they support scrutiny of measurement channels without establishing that every inexpensive device is unreliable.
- x3iv130f [[./thread#^c35190285|x3iv130f]] says CO2 can only be removed by opening a window. In direct reply, stephen\_g [[./thread#^c35194815|stephen\_g]] corrects that exclusivity: mechanical ventilation can also exchange indoor air, including through heat- or energy-recovery systems. Thus opening windows is one proposed ventilation method, not the only one.
- shrx [[./thread#^c35187617|shrx]] initially concludes that the bill of materials lacks environmental sensors beyond temperature. detaro [[./thread#^c35187891|detaro]] explains that this overlooks the second spreadsheet sheet, and e44858 [[./thread#^c35189199|e44858]] identifies the SPS30 particulate sensor there. This resolves the specific missing-PM-sensor objection without resolving the separate gas-sensor questions.
- Severian [[./thread#^c35189536|Severian]] questions whether measurements atop automobiles represent surrounding exposure. jve [[./thread#^c35194326|jve]] replies that the height approximates a person's nose, but whiplash451 [[./thread#^c35194633|whiplash451]] distinguishes roadway pollution from conditions several metres away on the sidewalk. Similar sampling height therefore does not settle spatial representativeness.
- ggm [[./thread#^c35190301|ggm]] challenges the assumption that pleasant or apparently fresh outdoor air is necessarily clean, invoking traffic particles, adaptation to odours, and pleasant-smelling construction emissions. This qualifies enthusiasm for open windows: perceived freshness is not offered as a reliable measurement of PM or gaseous pollutants.

## Assumptions

- ckocagil [[./thread#^c35188976|ckocagil]] says some CO2 instruments automatically establish a baseline from their recent minimum, assuming periodic exposure to outdoor air. On that account, calibration depends on actually encountering suitable background conditions. Havoc [[./thread#^c35187700|Havoc]] separately warns about drifting CO2-equivalent sensors; the discussion does not establish that equivalent readings directly measure CO2.
- cactusplant7374 [[./thread#^c35188186|cactusplant7374]] hopes monitoring might establish whether pollution affects their asthma, but reports no observed pollution–symptom relationship. ggm [[./thread#^c35190301|ggm]] speculates that an asthmatic pupil's symptoms might signal shared classroom exposure. Neither establishes which pollutant caused symptoms or whether other occupants experienced effects.
- cactusplant7374 [[./thread#^c35188186|cactusplant7374]] questions whether city AQI values come from sufficiently local instruments and wonders about satellite estimation. That is a question, not a verified description of their city's data. callalex's PurpleAir suggestion [[./thread#^c35191293|callalex]] likewise does not establish that a nearby, representative sensor exists.
- x3iv130f [[./thread#^c35190285|x3iv130f]] assumes most household VOCs come from household chemicals and presents charcoal filtration as their exclusive removal route. Neither the source distribution nor that exclusivity is demonstrated through measurements or comparative interventions in the supplied discussion.
- WWLink [[./thread#^c35190271|WWLink]] reports that school air-conditioning retrofits coincided with windows remaining closed. This does not establish outdoor-air supply, filtration quality, or indoor CO2 afterward. ggm [[./thread#^c35190301|ggm]] stresses air replacement, but supplies no ventilation specification showing how either school arrangement performed.

## Conditions

- The March 2023 cost estimates refer to uncertain scopes: cinntaile [[./thread#^c35187518|cinntaile]] reports a paper figure below US\$2,500, while Karawebnetwork [[./thread#^c35187586|Karawebnetwork]] estimates roughly \$300 from component searches and explicitly warns about possibly incorrect SKUs. Karawebnetwork also notes large individual-versus-bulk connector prices. grozzle [[./thread#^c35187283|grozzle]] requests explicit price ranges, and cwkoss [[./thread#^c35189254|cwkoss]] rejects calling the higher figure low-cost.
- Cheaper options are not established equivalents. SECProto [[./thread#^c35190925|SECProto]] cites a \$10 IKEA monitor while acknowledging differing features; ClumsyPilot [[./thread#^c35191389|ClumsyPilot]] strongly objects to the comparison. louison11 [[./thread#^c35190931|louison11]] quotes AirGradient kits starting at \$67, while ddulaney [[./thread#^c35188556|ddulaney]] estimates under \$100 for a PM module and around \$100 for a packaged monitor. These prices do not demonstrate matching pollutant coverage or calibration.
- Commercial alternatives span different capabilities and markets: clnq [[./thread#^c35189558|clnq]] quotes an Atmotube at roughly half the tentative \$300 DIY price with a survey discount, without noise measurement, and an Atmocube around \$200–\$500. malfist [[./thread#^c35201295|malfist]] quotes a \$640 air-Q and a \$35 SEN55 module. stephen\_g [[./thread#^c35194764|stephen\_g]] considers a Davis AirLink costing over AU\$300. These are commenters' contemporary shopping claims, not current verified offers or comparative tests.
- Deployment requirements change the cost comparison. greggsy [[./thread#^c35190776|greggsy]] questions GPS, motion sensors, and solar circuitry for some uses; Johnny555 [[./thread#^c35191797|Johnny555]] points out the intended car-mounted application. Karawebnetwork [[./thread#^c35196822|Karawebnetwork]] wonders whether an open design might be sturdier or easier to repair in developing countries. That repairability advantage remains hypothetical, and AceJohnny2 [[./thread#^c35187155|AceJohnny2]] cautions about the gap between a prototype and a mass-market product.
- clnq [[./thread#^c35192062|clnq]] describes substantial street-level NOx and particulate variation with peak hours, weekdays, weekends, holidays, and events, while characterizing open roads as cleaner. No route measurements substantiate that broad contrast. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c35193174|ahaucnx]] asks how long a mobile reading should remain valid for a location, highlighting the dependence of a pollution map on sampling time.
- Building location and energy costs constrain ventilation choices. ggm [[./thread#^c35190301|ggm]] describes an older Brisbane school surrounded by later traffic growth and recalls Queensland geographically limiting air-conditioning provision for economic reasons. stephen\_g [[./thread#^c35194815|stephen\_g]] recommends heat- or energy-recovery ventilation to reduce the loss of previously heated or cooled air. Neither provides measured costs, energy savings, or pollutant reductions.

## Lived experiences

- sacnoradhq [[./thread#^c35191942|sacnoradhq]] reports building an Arduino, LCD, and Plantower PMS5003 monitor for \$35 when the air was described as unbreathable, with AQI around 900. This is the explicit numerical ambient-pollution account in the unit, but its location, AQI scale, reading provenance, pollutant concentration, and pollution source are unspecified.
- l1am0 [[./thread#^c35188539|l1am0]] reports building a German community-monitoring device roughly seven years earlier for under €50 and says it still runs. MandieD [[./thread#^c35189668|MandieD]] recalls assembling such devices during one evening at an Erlangen meetup without soldering, while observing that a newer temperature/humidity component appeared to require soldered pins. These establish accessibility and continued operation, not reference-verified accuracy.
- throwaway8451 [[./thread#^c35194242|throwaway8451]] says a friend's community sensor worked well enough that they and others built five more the previous year. They recommend window-screen material over enclosure openings to exclude insects and spiders. No numerical comparison or calibration accompanies the positive assessment.
- gsich [[./thread#^c35193286|gsich]] distrusts readings from an aging sensor because of accumulated dirt and lacks a calibration method. In a follow-up, gsich [[./thread#^c35213387|gsich]] notes that continued output would not reveal clogging and says they have not opened the device. Responding, throwaway8451 [[./thread#^c35194211|throwaway8451]] attributes limited lifetime to dust, describes intermittent sampling as a precaution, and personally stores unused equipment in a closed box. Actual clogging and its measurement error are not established.
- jrockway [[./thread#^c35201318|jrockway]] reports that their weather-station instructions call for calibration against a sling psychrometer. Their humidifier's humidity sensor is consistently '10\% off' relative to other sensors, while a BME280 and SHT45 appear similar. They explicitly acknowledge lacking a calibrated reference, so agreement between instruments does not establish absolute accuracy.
- Karawebnetwork [[./thread#^c35196822|Karawebnetwork]] reports that an Atmotube v1 did not last long when attached to a backpack and handled somewhat roughly. This is a durability anecdote about that device and use, not evidence that the proposed DIY alternative survives equivalent conditions.
- thewanderer1983 [[./thread#^c35190208|thewanderer1983]] reports using a small Sniffer monitor at home and finding its Home Assistant integration useful. alexeldeib [[./thread#^c35190498|alexeldeib]] reports building a similar device from Adafruit tutorials. Neither supplies measured pollutant levels, calibration results, or resulting health or ventilation changes.
- dariosalvi78 [[./thread#^c35187843|dariosalvi78]] describes a promising DIY project using Alphasense and cheaper metal-oxide sensors. They say long-term colocation studies were needed, but the project ended after a move to Sweden and loss of local-authority support. This illustrates a practical barrier to validation, without establishing the achieved sensor accuracy.
- WWLink [[./thread#^c35190271|WWLink]] remembers enjoying fresh air through open windows in suburban schools, while appreciating later air conditioning on hot days. In reply, ggm [[./thread#^c35190301|ggm]] describes their son's older inner-city Brisbane school between continuous flows of cars, buses, and trucks. The accounts concern different settings: subjective comfort in one and traffic proximity in the other, without measured classroom pollutant comparisons.
- ggm [[./thread#^c35190301|ggm]] recalls noticing a pleasant new-apartment smell on returning home throughout the first year. They attribute it to construction-material VOCs, including formaldehyde. The smell is firsthand; its chemical identification, concentration, and health implications were not measured in the account.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Reliable inexpensive NO2 measurement remains unresolved. henearkr [[./thread#^c35193032|henearkr]] asks whether the article's mentioned NO2 sensor was used only for calibration, and henearkr [[./thread#^c35194853|henearkr]] says it is absent from the bill of materials. adolph [[./thread#^c35189517|adolph]] identifies an analogue front end without the associated gas sensors. malfist's alternatives are described as including 'NO sensors' [[./thread#^c35201295|malfist]], which does not establish validated NO2 measurement. No firsthand NO2 readings are supplied.
- The relative value of mobile sampling and a dense stationary network is unsettled. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c35193174|ahaucnx]] raises airflow, thermal exposure, humidity, and time-of-day concerns. stephen\_g [[./thread#^c35194849|stephen\_g]] proposes using GPS to sample after a vehicle stops, but supplies no implementation or validation. The discussion does not establish whether that suggestion solves the measurement or coverage problems.
- Participants do not settle how accurate inexpensive PM devices remain across humidity changes and years of use. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c35193568|ahaucnx]] calls Sensirion SHT humidity sensors quite accurate, while jrockway [[./thread#^c35201318|jrockway]] stresses reference calibration; gsich [[./thread#^c35213387|gsich]] cannot determine whether aging hardware remains trustworthy. gniv [[./thread#^c35187641|gniv]] identifies calibration as the claimed innovation, but no numerical calibration errors or reference comparisons are reproduced.
- The health questions remain largely assertions and proposed investigations. x3iv130f [[./thread#^c35190285|x3iv130f]] links CO2 with sleep and critical thinking, and cactusplant7374 [[./thread#^c35188186|cactusplant7374]] seeks an explanation for asthma effects. This unit supplies no measured CO2 concentrations, exposure thresholds, controlled comparisons, or firsthand sleep or cognitive changes from an intervention.
- No measured comparison settles when opening windows improves overall exposure. x3iv130f [[./thread#^c35190285|x3iv130f]] advocates it for CO2, stephen\_g [[./thread#^c35194815|stephen\_g]] offers mechanical ventilation, and ggm [[./thread#^c35190301|ggm]] emphasizes polluted outdoor air near traffic. The discussion contains no paired indoor/outdoor PM2.5, NO2, and CO2 measurements, or before-and-after ventilation and filtration results, sufficient to choose between these options for a particular home or classroom.
- Cooking and wildfire-specific interventions cannot be assessed from this unit. photochemsyn [[./thread#^c35188455|photochemsyn]] lists wildfires among possible particle sources, but provides no personal smoke-exposure account. sacnoradhq [[./thread#^c35191942|sacnoradhq]] does not identify the source of the reported extreme AQI episode. There are no supplied cooking experiments or wildfire-specific window-opening or filtration outcomes.
- Broader household hazard coverage remains unanswered. rnk [[./thread#^c35191924|rnk]] wants CO monitoring and propane or natural-gas leak detection in addition to the recommended air-quality channels. Eisenstein [[./thread#^c35194234|Eisenstein]] responds that CO sensors degrade and impose a finite service life, but the thread identifies no validated all-in-one solution or replacement interval.
