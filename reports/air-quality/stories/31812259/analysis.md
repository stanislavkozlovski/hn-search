# [Contra Wirecutter on the IKEA air purifier](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=31812259)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion of a critique of Wirecutter’s assessment of IKEA’s Förnuftig air purifier. Participants debate HEPA and EPA filter classifications, particle capture across sizes, airflow and clean-air delivery, and whether higher filter efficiency meaningfully improves room-level purification. They scrutinize Wirecutter’s test methods and conclusions, including whether its comparison with other purifiers is supported by reproducible data, and disagree over whether affiliate links and ownership changes undermine editorial independence. The thread broadens into the reliability of consumer product reviews, sponsorship and search-driven content, alternatives such as specialist review sites and user forums, and practical tradeoffs among purifier cost, noise, power use, filtration, and DIY box-fan designs.
Tags: air-purifiers, air-filtration, HEPA, product-reviews, Wirecutter, affiliate-marketing, testing-methods, consumer-trust, DIY

## Relevance

The purifier discussion covers particulate filtration, PM2.5, airflow and CADR, filter and sensor measurements, and practical tradeoffs in noise, energy use, and cost. Firsthand accounts describe wildfire-smoke exposure, allergy relief, purifier performance, and indoor particle readings; commenters also debate how reliable the reported tests are. CO2 and opening windows appear briefly, while NO2 and sleep effects are largely absent. ## Arguments

- For recirculating room purifiers, jefftk [[./thread#^c31812962|jefftk]] argues that airflow matters more than small efficiency differences once particle removal is already high. londons\_explore [[./thread#^c31815111|londons\_explore]] illustrates this with a hypothetical 90\%-efficient filter moving twice the air and removing more room particles than a 99.9\%-efficient filter. These are system-level arguments, not measurements establishing that every cheaper purifier performs equally well.
- phnofive [[./thread#^c31817713|phnofive]] explains that PM2.5 includes particles up to 2.5 microns, rather than particles larger than that. highwaylights [[./thread#^c31813546|highwaylights]] describes impaction and diffusion, explaining why filtration can improve on either side of the most penetrating particle size. Their argument rejects treating 0.3 microns as a sieve cutoff; addicted [[./thread#^c31816903|addicted]] also invokes a NASA study, whose underlying evidence is not reproduced here.
- GistNoesis [[./thread#^c31813765|GistNoesis]] recommends measuring the actual problem before choosing an intervention: temperature, humidity, particles, VOCs, and CO2 require different responses. They distinguish particulate filtration from ventilation for CO2 and recommend identifying VOC sources such as paints, glues, and chemicals. This is explicitly nonprofessional advice, without accompanying household measurements.
- GistNoesis [[./thread#^c31813765|GistNoesis]] argues that opening windows can improve indoor air when outdoor conditions are suitable, while filtration remains useful for indoor particle generation or polluted outdoor air. Their recommendation is conditional on location, temperature, humidity, and ventilation performance; it is not an unconditional recommendation to open windows.
- jfim [[./thread#^c31813820|jfim]] and daenz [[./thread#^c31812799|daenz]] argue that a fan with a suitable furnace filter can provide useful particle removal cheaply. jfim identifies quieter operation, appearance, and replacement-filter availability as reasons to buy a commercial unit. daenz links to particle measurements elsewhere, but those measurements are not supplied in this export.
- sumoboy [[./thread#^c31819243|sumoboy]] argues that allergies, pets, volatile chemicals, room layout, purchase price, and maintenance costs make a universal purifier recommendation inappropriate. enragedcacti [[./thread#^c31813304|enragedcacti]] similarly emphasizes pollution entering or being generated in the room, clean-air delivery rate, and noise tolerance.
- Havoc [[./thread#^c31815090|Havoc]] values live sensing and automatic fan adjustment over continuous maximum airflow. Ataraxic [[./thread#^c31812795|Ataraxic]] favors rapid cleanup followed by quieter operation. These arguments treat noise and operating behavior as part of practical effectiveness, rather than judging devices solely at their highest setting.
- addicted [[./thread#^c31816940|addicted]] quotes actual Wirecutter testing: after 30 minutes in a 200-square-foot room, Förnuftig reportedly removed 64.5\%/53.5\% of 0.3-micron particles on high/medium and 85.2\%/73.6\% of 3-micron particles. Levoit Core 300 reportedly removed 97.4\%/92.6\% of 0.3-micron particles and virtually all 3-micron particles. These are secondhand test results, not addicted's measurements, and their validity is contested.
- jefftk [[./thread#^c31812962|jefftk]] requests particle-decay curves instead of a single half-hour endpoint; csours [[./thread#^c31816131|csours]] wants multiple runs. nextos [[./thread#^c31817722|nextos]] adds dirty-filter performance, real electricity consumption, and fan behavior after years of use. Together these comments argue for testing conditions that resemble sustained household operation.

## Counterarguments

- The certification distinction is substantive even if its practical importance is disputed. yurodivuie [[./thread#^c31813871|yurodivuie]] distinguishes E12/EPA from H13/HEPA; tssva [[./thread#^c31822766|tssva]] quotes a US requirement of 99.97\% removal at 0.3 microns. Lazare [[./thread#^c31819884|Lazare]] says the European specification instead uses the filter's most penetrating particle size. stephen\_g [[./thread#^c31819401|stephen\_g]] accepts the classification distinction while disputing its importance for room recirculation.
- buildbot [[./thread#^c31817009|buildbot]] quotes a methodological challenge to Wirecutter's results: interpreting virtually all as 99\% implies a CADR of 236.2, approximately 75\% above the manufacturer's maximum claim, while another medium-speed test implies 98.1. This greater-than-2.4-fold discrepancy motivates skepticism, but the reconstruction itself depends on assumptions and is not an independent repeat experiment.
- etchalon [[./thread#^c31816817|etchalon]] emphasizes that 99.5\% versus 99.95\% removal leaves 50 versus 5 particles from an incoming 10,000. amluto [[./thread#^c31819256|amluto]] explains that this tenfold difference concerns single-pass outlet contamination, not the concentration throughout a recirculating room. jefftk [[./thread#^c31823047|jefftk]] also identifies different device CADRs—135 versus 82 CFM—so the quoted product comparison does not isolate that small media-efficiency difference.
- Filter specifications alone do not establish assembled-device performance. asojfdowgh [[./thread#^c31814235|asojfdowgh]] raises bypass through poorly fitted components; amluto [[./thread#^c31819329|amluto]] emphasizes sealing differences between consumer purifiers and industrial installations. amluto [[./thread#^c31825368|amluto]] also reports previously setting up a purifier incorrectly, while presenting setup error in Wirecutter's test only as a possibility.
- DIY has costs beyond purchase price. Rebelgecko [[./thread#^c31813556|Rebelgecko]] reports disadvantages in noise and electricity consumption, while turtlebits [[./thread#^c31813194|turtlebits]] reserves a bulky, noisy box fan for wildfire conditions. donohoe [[./thread#^c31812980|donohoe]] values appearance, and jeromegv [[./thread#^c31813192|jeromegv]] prefers paying to avoid construction, maintenance, and uncertainty. These are different household priorities, not evidence that either approach is universally preferable.
- KennyBlanken [[./thread#^c31813764|KennyBlanken]] considers the proposed 120-mm fan purifier too weak for ordinary rooms. breput [[./thread#^c31813953|breput]] counters with perceived dust reduction and a theoretical room-volume calculation. The exchange does not supply measured airflow through the installed filters or a controlled room-cleaning test, so neither the blanket dismissal nor the calculated coverage is experimentally established.
- Youden [[./thread#^c31814274|Youden]] challenges the recommendation to buy a cheap multifunction monitor: some report VOC-derived eCO2 rather than measuring CO2. They recommend checking for NDIR sensing and warn about calibration errors. Their assertion that eCO2 correlates poorly with actual CO2 cites a study, but no study data or firsthand sensor comparison is included here.

## Assumptions

- jefftk's [[./thread#^c31813049|jefftk]] thought experiment assumes a 1,000-cubic-foot room, 100 cubic feet processed per minute, and efficiencies of 99.5\% versus 99.95\%. Its 0.9005 versus 0.90005 values describe the fraction remaining throughout the room after one idealized minute. They are neither measured room concentrations nor a tenfold difference in room contamination.
- Room performance depends on continued pollution entry, indoor generation, mixing, and placement. stephen\_g [[./thread#^c31819239|stephen\_g]] explicitly conditions steady-state concentrations on leakage, outdoor pollution, and purifier throughput. jefftk [[./thread#^c31816444|jefftk]] distinguishes this situation from filtering all incoming air into a clean area; conclusions about recirculating appliances should not be transferred unchanged to that arrangement.
- The measurements use different quantities. addicted [[./thread#^c31816940|addicted]] quotes particle-counter results for 0.3- and 3-micron channels, whereas syntaxing [[./thread#^c31814526|syntaxing]] reports concentrations in micrograms per cubic meter. phnofive's [[./thread#^c31817713|phnofive]] PM2.5 definition also means that a 3-micron channel is not itself a direct PM2.5 measurement. These results cannot be treated as interchangeable exposure metrics.
- Automatic operation assumes that the sensor and its thresholds represent the exposure of interest. jansan [[./thread#^c31813208|jansan]] reports an IKEA indicator that always stays green; enragedcacti [[./thread#^c31813304|enragedcacti]] does not know the PM threshold governing their Starkvind. Neither a green light nor low automatic fan speed establishes independently verified air quality.
- Window-opening advice assumes outdoor air is advantageous for the pollutant being addressed. GistNoesis [[./thread#^c31813765|GistNoesis]] acknowledges outdoor pollution and weather constraints, while syntaxing [[./thread#^c31814526|syntaxing]] reports severe outdoor wildfire pollution. GistNoesis's broader assertion that VOCs and CO2 only grow indoors is not supported here by outdoor VOC measurements.
- Symptom improvement, pleasant smell, visible dust reduction, and particle readings are different outcomes. Der\_Einzige [[./thread#^c31813554|Der\_Einzige]] reports several improvements after installing multiple filters, but the contribution of each device is unidentified. dusted's [[./thread#^c31814410|dusted]] nighttime particle graph tracks sleep-related activity without establishing that particles caused awakenings or that filtration improved sleep.

## Conditions

- The economic comparisons are from 2022. daenz [[./thread#^c31816583|daenz]] compares a \$40 box fan with a \$70 IKEA purifier and calculates approximately \$70 versus \$13 yearly electricity costs from 73 W versus 14 W, continuous operation, and \$0.11/kWh. Filters are excluded. daenz still favors DIY on expected throughput and replacement availability, but supplies no comparison at equal achieved air quality.
- Usage duration and local electricity prices can reverse the purchasing decision. Rebelgecko [[./thread#^c31813556|Rebelgecko]] considers low upfront cost attractive for a few wildfire days, but estimates approximately \$100 yearly electricity savings from a dedicated purifier in their expensive-electricity area. This is a personal estimate, not a general payback period.
- Replacement costs materially affect ownership. mthoms [[./thread#^c31813106|mthoms]] reports a 40\% increase in Blueair 211+ filter prices over a year. bouvin [[./thread#^c31813661|bouvin]] bought two Förnuftigs with filters for less than one replacement-filter purchase for their previous purifier. latchkey [[./thread#^c31813624|latchkey]] reports roughly \$100 annually for a Mila filter and suspects regular prefilter cleaning extends its life.
- Future filter supply is a practical uncertainty. thadk [[./thread#^c31817197|thadk]] reports that replacement filters for an older IKEA Flimmer product were discontinued in 2015. daenz [[./thread#^c31816583|daenz]] expects commodity furnace filters to avoid proprietary-supply problems, but that is a durability argument rather than proof of future availability. alleskleber [[./thread#^c31821783|alleskleber]] also raises reported Xiaomi cartridge authentication as a purchasing constraint.
- Maximum wattage does not describe every operating mode. mbrubeck [[./thread#^c31813382|mbrubeck]] relays Wirecutter measurements of 34.6 W on medium and 31.8 W on low for the Core 300, alongside a 45 W manufacturer rating. Ataraxic [[./thread#^c31813785|Ataraxic]] questions comparisons that do not match operating settings. leguminous [[./thread#^c31818548|leguminous]] suggests standardized Energy Star data while noting that manufacturers submit those measurements.
- Wildfire emergencies change both exposure and availability. daenz [[./thread#^c31815001|daenz]] [[./thread#^c31815275|daenz]] describes local purifier sellouts and apartment hallways continuously hazy with smoke; the box-fan solution was what they could obtain. Their account distinguishes emergency access from ordinary shopping and explicitly says they were no longer low-income themselves.
- Climate and housing constrain ventilation. GistNoesis [[./thread#^c31813765|GistNoesis]] discusses heat loss in cold weather, heat recovery, humidity control, and potentially expensive or noisy retrofits in older buildings. Their suggestions to install ventilation or move presume resources and housing choices that are not evaluated in the discussion.
- Room-size claims are conditional. bobcostas55 [[./thread#^c31812812|bobcostas55]] quotes an 8–10-square-meter Förnuftig rating, while addicted [[./thread#^c31816940|addicted]] quotes testing in a larger, 200-square-foot room. enragedcacti [[./thread#^c31813304|enragedcacti]] argues that cleaner surroundings or lower pollutant entry can allow use in a larger room; the rating is not demonstrated here to be either a universal cutoff or safely ignorable.
- Monitoring has its own budget and usability tradeoffs. Someone1234 [[./thread#^c31814016|Someone1234]] describes the stock VINDRIKTNING as a \$13 traffic-light indicator, approximately \$25 with power accessories, without numerical display or logging. Someone1234 [[./thread#^c31816552|Someone1234]] cites a roughly \$200 AirThings CO2 option. Youden [[./thread#^c31814274|Youden]] suggests approximately \$100 or more for multifunction equipment with real CO2 sensing, with CO2-only devices as an exception.

## Lived experiences

- syntaxing [[./thread#^c31813859|syntaxing]] [[./thread#^c31814526|syntaxing]] reports Bay Area wildfire measurements using a \$50 uncalibrated laser sensor: outdoor concentrations above 200 µg/m³, Blueair outlet readings of zero, and indoor ambient readings around 10–15 µg/m³ versus 30–40 with other solutions. They tried Dyson, Winix, and a V-shaped Vornado DIY setup. syntaxing [[./thread#^c31814555|syntaxing]] identifies ownership of Blueair Pure and Classic models. Zero refers to an outlet reading, not particle-free household air.
- dusted [[./thread#^c31814410|dusted]] logs IKEA VINDRIKTNING readings through an ESP8265 and observes lower particle readings with the purifier running than when off. Readings rise at night and show bedtime, waking, and interrupted sleep. Moving the purifier farther from the bed makes its low-speed noise less noticeable. No calibration, numerical exposure levels, or demonstrated sleep improvement is supplied.
- pnathan [[./thread#^c31814819|pnathan]] says a Coway works well according to a Dylos particle counter elsewhere in the room, but gives no counts or protocol. In contrast, garmanarnar [[./thread#^c31813924|garmanarnar]] explicitly describes satisfaction with Bluepure without instruments. These are instrument-supported and purely subjective reports respectively, with neither providing a reproducible comparison.
- Der\_Einzige [[./thread#^c31813554|Der\_Einzige]] reports using an explicitly supported MERV 16 central filter, two approximately MERV 13 box-fan filters, and a quiet bedroom purifier. They say their wife's allergy and breathing problems disappeared, guests noticed a cleaner smell, and items left out took longer to mold. These are uncontrolled household observations following a combined intervention.
- highwaylights [[./thread#^c31813410|highwaylights]] owns three Förnuftigs and reports substantial help with a pollen allergy, alongside bothersome noise at maximum speed. Their interest in a larger Starkvind reflects both satisfaction with filtration and a desire for more capacity; they do not supply pollen counts or clinical measurements.
- thadk [[./thread#^c31817197|thadk]] reports the opposite practical experience with Förnuftig: they stopped using it after two months because its filter became dirty, lacked a recoverable prefilter in their account, and left the room dusty. Their seven- to eight-year-old Coway seemed much more capable. This is a household comparison without matched conditions or particle measurements.
- Androider [[./thread#^c31812912|Androider]] reports five years of satisfactory Coway operation and finds a three-times-more-expensive Alen less effective, noisier, and more costly to refilter. SrslyJosh [[./thread#^c31815823|SrslyJosh]] uses four Coways across two floors, praises quiet lower settings, and stresses cleaning prefilters every month or two; the highest setting is described as extremely loud.
- inferiorhuman [[./thread#^c31816229|inferiorhuman]] reports two Coways with noisy, unbalanced fans; a newer filter had an odor they attributed to VOCs, and warranty handling was unsatisfactory. No VOC concentration was measured. nextos [[./thread#^c31817722|nextos]] independently reports fan vibration developing in both purchased Coways, despite favorable impressions of dirty-filter performance and energy use.
- breput [[./thread#^c31813523|breput]] describes a quiet DIY purifier using an \$8 Arctic P12 and two supposedly H13 filters costing \$17, reporting noticeably less dust in a low-pollution area. breput [[./thread#^c31813953|breput]] calculates roughly three nominal room volumes per hour for a 1,152-cubic-foot office from a 56-CFM fan figure; this is theoretical, not measured filtered airflow.
- steamer25 [[./thread#^c31817889|steamer25]] bought a locally available, prefabricated fan-and-furnace-filter device for their son's allergies and perceived improvement throughout the upper floor. daenz [[./thread#^c31813510|daenz]] reports that a simpler box-fan construction worked well during wildfire smoke and remained attractive because parts were cheap and available, although its highest setting was noisy. Neither supplies indoor concentration changes.
- Mister\_Snuggles [[./thread#^c31814823|Mister\_Snuggles]] uses a box fan with an inexpensive furnace filter for cat-litter dust and automates it with a litter-box motion detector because even low speed is loud. nerdjon [[./thread#^c31813672|nerdjon]] places an IKEA purifier at the opening of an enclosed litter-box area and reports reduced dust and smell, explicitly limiting the endorsement to that local application.
- rkagerer [[./thread#^c31813496|rkagerer]] reports that an IQAir GC's lower three speeds are reasonably quiet and maximum speed clears the room quickly after cooking is burned. This is a firsthand cooking-cleanup impression without PM2.5, NO2, or odor measurements. rkagerer [[./thread#^c31814361|rkagerer]] also cites manufacturer testing of individual units and their seals, rather than providing their own quantitative room test.
- Mister\_Snuggles [[./thread#^c31815338|Mister\_Snuggles]] observes a Starkvind ramping up when particles are detected and down afterward, with a nearly inaudible lowest setting. Mister\_Snuggles [[./thread#^c31825886|Mister\_Snuggles]] additionally schedules maximum operation from 4 PM to 9 PM before bedtime regardless of the sensor. bouvin [[./thread#^c31813661|bouvin]] successfully controls Förnuftigs using smart switches and Eve sensors. These establish automation experiences, not validated exposure targets or improved sleep.
- Comfort reports vary by device and setting. brnt [[./thread#^c31815601|brnt]] finds Förnuftig nearly silent on low but does not know its effectiveness there; their Winix Zero is much louder at maximum airflow. fehrm [[./thread#^c31822662|fehrm]] finds Förnuftig too loud even on medium and prefers a stronger, quieter but bulky Coway Storm. swagasaurus-rex [[./thread#^c31816077|swagasaurus-rex]] considers RabbitAir the only truly quiet purifier among those tried.
- vl [[./thread#^c31820706|vl]] reports running a full-size Vornado continuously in a poorly circulating room and finding its first two speeds nearly silent. This is a fan-comfort and circulation account, without measurements showing outdoor-air exchange or pollutant removal.
- reaperducer [[./thread#^c31812821|reaperducer]] reports two expensive Levoit units becoming unresponsive within a year, attributing the failures to software. aceazzameen [[./thread#^c31817955|aceazzameen]] reports satisfaction with several Winix units, while pkulak [[./thread#^c31812866|pkulak]] prefers the perceived airflow and quietness of Winix and Coway. These accounts concern different models and do not establish comparative failure rates.
- Tade0 [[./thread#^c31816217|Tade0]] reports winter pollution routinely exceeding 100 µg/m³ and says a local-brand purifier makes the worst smog events manageable. They chose a numerical display, ionizer, and compact vertical arrangement. The pollutant fraction and measurement method are unspecified, and the quoted CADR uses inconsistent area-based units, so its capacity cannot be treated as a verified volumetric measurement.
- mh- [[./thread#^c31813789|mh-]] reports commercial-looking freestanding purifiers in their children's schools, possibly as an interim measure before central equipment. They explicitly have not seen performance data. This supports actual deployment, not demonstrated reductions in infection, CO2, or other classroom exposures.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The IKEA unit's actual small-particle performance remains unresolved. bscphil [[./thread#^c31813619|bscphil]] questions whether its advertised efficiency matches the observed room results. addicted [[./thread#^c31816774|addicted]] distinguishes being tested against a standard from meeting it, while stephen\_g [[./thread#^c31819342|stephen\_g]] reads the wording corresponds to EPA12 as a compliance claim. No independent media test or controlled replication in the supplied comments settles those interpretations.
- The airflow-versus-efficiency dispute has a partial concession rather than a thread-wide resolution. asojfdowgh [[./thread#^c31827183|asojfdowgh]] initially argues that repeated passes amplify the difference, then accepts jefftk's response with fair [[./thread#^c31829613|asojfdowgh]]. etchalon [[./thread#^c31828946|etchalon]] continues attributing the observed device gap to tenfold penetration differences combined with CADR, while amluto [[./thread#^c31829316|amluto]] disputes that explanation. The mathematical distinction does not itself establish which real device performs best.
- The cause of syntaxing's measured Blueair advantage is unidentified. syntaxing [[./thread#^c31814526|syntaxing]] emphasizes both cleaner output and a 350-CFM CADR; jefftk [[./thread#^c31814893|jefftk]] models equal airflow and suspects the alternatives moved less air. That model is not a replication of the wildfire account, and the requested model-by-model comparison is not supplied. Media efficiency, airflow, setup, and room conditions are not isolated.
- Sensor accuracy and automatic thresholds remain poorly characterized. jansan [[./thread#^c31813208|jansan]] asks why their IKEA indicator always stays green, while highwaylights [[./thread#^c31817353|highwaylights]] requests independent Starkvind particle tests rather than specifications. dusted [[./thread#^c31814410|dusted]] provides useful behavioral traces but no calibration. The discussion does not establish what these devices' reassuring indicators mean in absolute exposure terms.
- Affiliate incentives are disputed rather than demonstrated as the cause of the purifier rankings. Saint\_Genet [[./thread#^c31812485|Saint\_Genet]] suspects IKEA's rejection reflects missing commission income; sumeno [[./thread#^c31812890|sumeno]] points to IKEA recommendations in other categories, and dchest [[./thread#^c31814540|dchest]] says IKEA has an affiliate program. These comments do not resolve the commercial relationship or validate either side's performance claims.
- Health benefits remain anecdotal and pollutant attribution is uncertain. Der\_Einzige's [[./thread#^c31813554|Der\_Einzige]] reported breathing improvement follows several simultaneous filtration changes. highwaylights [[./thread#^c31813410|highwaylights]] reports pollen-allergy relief, whereas Lazare's [[./thread#^c31819610|Lazare]] suggestion that IKEA would be poor for pollen is speculative. Symptoms, visible dust, and particle counts are different endpoints; no controlled health or cognition study is supplied.
- The window-opening question receives conditional advice rather than a measured household experiment. rossmohax [[./thread#^c31814676|rossmohax]] wants CO2 monitoring to decide when to open windows but reports no results. GistNoesis [[./thread#^c31813765|GistNoesis]] recommends ventilation while accounting for outdoor conditions; syntaxing's [[./thread#^c31814526|syntaxing]] wildfire account illustrates why admitting outdoor air can increase particle exposure. No simultaneous indoor/outdoor PM2.5-and-CO2 experiment establishes timing or an optimum tradeoff here.
- For story 31812259, part 0, there are no supplied NO2 measurements, traffic-specific exposure comparisons, firsthand CO2 concentration series, or demonstrated cognitive improvements. CO2 appears principally in GistNoesis's advice [[./thread#^c31813765|GistNoesis]] and Youden's sensor warning [[./thread#^c31814274|Youden]]. dusted's sleep-related graph [[./thread#^c31814410|dusted]] does not demonstrate a sleep benefit, and mh-'s school account [[./thread#^c31813789|mh-]] supplies no measured health outcomes.
