# [How to Determine the Error of an Air Quality Sensor?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40020402)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion considers how to assess error in air-quality sensors, what reference instruments can and cannot establish, and which metrics help compare readings. Participants question whether the article explains calibration, discuss the effects of sensor placement, particle type, humidity, and measurement method, and debate whether low-cost sensors are useful for regulatory measurements or tracking local trends. The thread also covers product openness, local data access, Home Assistant integration, device noise, CO sensors, and practical reasons people monitor indoor air.
Tags: air-quality-sensors, measurement-error, calibration, reference-instruments, PM-sensors, open-source, indoor-air-quality, sensor-accuracy

## Relevance

The thread is directly about air-quality sensor error and limitations, including disagreement between reference instruments, calibration, humidity effects, and the purpose-specific value of low-cost PM sensors. Comments also discuss CO2 sensor reliability and firsthand uses of readings to adjust ventilation, cooking exhaust, filtration, or wildfire exposure. ## Arguments

- Monitoring can support simple manual changes. SECProto [[./thread#^c40033313|SECProto]] reports using a portable CO2 monitor to discover inadequate air exchange and adjust windows; jbm [[./thread#^c40034917|jbm]] uses readings to decide when to crack a window during parties. These accounts support practical usefulness without requiring home automation.
- Measurements can reveal overlooked pollution sources. Klathmon [[./thread#^c40034419|Klathmon]] reports unexpectedly high PM2.5 while cooking, and billfor [[./thread#^c40033234|billfor]] says cooking observations—including waffles—prompted more consistent hood use. billfor also uses outdoor monitoring to inform when to go outside during wildfires.
- Sensor usefulness depends on the task. Drawing on laboratory work developing and characterizing low-cost PM devices, riedel [[./thread#^c40033086|riedel]] argues that calibrated, dense networks can reveal relative and transient changes, while placement can matter more than instrument accuracy for some regulatory urban-background measurements.
- Sensor assessment should combine metrics. Responding to jddj's left-versus-right question [[./thread#^c40029842|jddj]], ahaucnx [[./thread#^c40030660|ahaucnx]] says the illustrated normalized RMSE values are 6\% and 66\%, favoring the former, but recommends considering correlation and other performance measures together.
- Local access and open designs can make monitoring easier to sustain. Borealid [[./thread#^c40029816|Borealid]] runs several AirGradient devices with personal software, while nsbk [[./thread#^c40032527|nsbk]] builds the open-design devices and integrates them through MQTT. These are arguments for usability and control over data, rather than demonstrations of measurement accuracy.
- Different pollutants call for different responses. nicolaslem [[./thread#^c40029915|nicolaslem]] describes a relatively trouble-free Fibaro CO alarm and argues that CO should normally read zero, with alerts taking priority over data logging. xandrius [[./thread#^c40031958|xandrius]] wants CO readings and alarms added to AirGradient; that is a requested capability, not evidence that existing devices provide it.

## Counterarguments

- Residential usefulness is disputed. quickthrowman [[./thread#^c40033181|quickthrowman]] questions whether household readings lead to action without automation, contrasting them with commercial systems that control ventilation. nox101 [[./thread#^c40032993|nox101]] owns a sensor but rarely attends to its unspecified PPM reading. The manual interventions reported by SECProto [[./thread#^c40033313|SECProto]] answer this objection for particular households, without establishing that every buyer benefits.
- Comparison with a reference does not eliminate measurement uncertainty. riedel [[./thread#^c40033086|riedel]] argues that optical and beta-attenuation instruments also depend on calibrated conversions to mass concentration; actual size-selected filter collection provides slower mass measurements. Different response times and measurement principles complicate interpreting high-frequency RMSE as sensor quality. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c40034549|ahaucnx]] acknowledges these complications.
- Reference instruments can disagree substantially. Luc [[./thread#^c40029829|Luc]] quotes the article's account of an EPA comparison finding over 25\% divergence between a BAM-1020 and T640 at some concentrations; this is a relayed study claim, not Luc's experiment. honk [[./thread#^c40032499|honk]] attributes particular difficulty at low concentrations to the BAM's noise floor and says even identical colocated BAMs can differ substantially there.
- An air-quality output can be difficult to interpret and may respond poorly to actual conditions. HankB99 [[./thread#^c40030847|HankB99]] cannot determine what their Raspberry Pi's BME680 readings mean. Havoc [[./thread#^c40034295|Havoc]] describes only vague correspondence with room conditions, while beryilma [[./thread#^c40032728|beryilma]] and beryilma [[./thread#^c40033144|beryilma]] criticize the derived metric, temperature/humidity dependence, and claimed 25\% variability. No controlled comparison supporting those criticisms is supplied.
- A ventilation intervention need not produce a noticeable health benefit. Klathmon [[./thread#^c40034419|Klathmon]] reports no conscious difference from opening the office window or door at elevated readings. jbm [[./thread#^c40034917|jbm]] found no major airflow problem using multiple CO2 meters and consequently looked elsewhere for ways to improve sleep.

## Assumptions

- CO2 is treated chiefly as an indicator of air exchange in these household accounts. SECProto [[./thread#^c40033313|SECProto]] uses it that way, but this does not answer nox101's [[./thread#^c40032993|nox101]] wish for a device that establishes whether chemicals, viruses, mildew, or other hazards make a building legally unsafe.
- Reported action thresholds are personal practices. Klathmon's approximately 1000 ppm trigger [[./thread#^c40034419|Klathmon]] and jbm's approximately 1200 ppm party trigger [[./thread#^c40034917|jbm]] are not presented with evidence establishing universal health or comfort boundaries.
- The pollutant and intervention must remain identifiable. Royce-CMR [[./thread#^c40033241|Royce-CMR]] says a purifier lowers an unspecified PPM reading, then separately discusses responses to CO2. This does not demonstrate that the filter removes CO2, or establish whether running the air system provides outdoor air rather than circulation alone.
- Symptom explanations remain hypotheses. Klathmon [[./thread#^c40034419|Klathmon]] links relatives' headaches to a high CO2 reading, and rinron [[./thread#^c40033371|rinron]] attributes better sleep to improved bedroom airflow. Neither account separates CO2 itself from other changing conditions or provides a controlled health assessment.
- Equal PM mass need not imply equal harm. riedel [[./thread#^c40033086|riedel]] argues that particle composition probably matters and that limits generally assume a typical urban mixture. Translating a sensor reading into an individual's health impact therefore requires assumptions not resolved here.
- Operational reliability and agreement are weaker evidence than verified absolute accuracy. Borealid's trouble-free devices [[./thread#^c40029816|Borealid]], dawnerd's agreement between household sensors [[./thread#^c40032929|dawnerd]], and poulpy123's confidence based on reported Paris-agency testing [[./thread#^c40037697|poulpy123]] each support a narrower conclusion than a documented calibration or independently inspected test report.

## Conditions

- Housing and season affect the ventilation problem. SECProto [[./thread#^c40033313|SECProto]] associates higher desk-area CO2 with moderate weather when the furnace runs less, and reports high overnight readings in an old, tightly sealed house without forced-air circulation. jbm [[./thread#^c40034917|jbm]] lives in Canada and ordinarily would not think to open windows during winter parties. Neither supplies heating-cost or temperature measurements for these choices.
- Opening windows has a conditional exposure tradeoff. SECProto [[./thread#^c40033313|SECProto]] reports lower indoor CO2 with a cracked window, while billfor [[./thread#^c40033234|billfor]] uses outdoor readings during wildfire conditions. Together these accounts suggest checking outdoor conditions before airing a room: admitting polluted outdoor air could undermine the benefit. However, no supplied account measures smoke entering through an open window or simultaneous changes in indoor PM2.5 and CO2.
- Budget and data access constrain device choice. poulpy123 [[./thread#^c40037697|poulpy123]] says AirGradient was the only monitor offering CO2, PM2.5, and VOC readings within their budget, and wishes it supported microSD storage. No purchase price or comparative accuracy results accompany that assessment.
- Outdoor network coverage and ongoing storage costs can affect practical value. ck2 [[./thread#^c40031992|ck2]] argues that PurpleAir has much greater map coverage and criticizes AirGradient's lack of free lifetime storage. They propose supplying US universities and existing weather stations with free devices and reporting; these are marketing and coverage proposals, with no implementation results or financial feasibility analysis.
- Geographic separation limits reference comparisons. anikra [[./thread#^c40038827|anikra]], identifying herself as the AirGradient article's author, suggests comparison with a nearby reporting station over at least a week, but warns that locations a few kilometers apart can experience different emissions and peaks. Expected broad correlation would not establish identical local exposure or precise calibration.
- Weather and seasonal geography complicate visual judgments. doodlebugging [[./thread#^c40034107|doodlebugging]] challenges the article's pollution labels because the photographs appear to show storm conditions; stavros [[./thread#^c40034468|stavros]] suggests rain was mistaken for smog. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c40034493|ahaucnx]] accepts the illustration problem and proposes Chiang Mai, where the company is located and reports large annual air-quality swings. These comments contain interpretations of imagery, not measured pollution contrasts.

## Lived experiences

- SECProto [[./thread#^c40033313|SECProto]] uses an Aranet4 and reports that cracking a window brings elevated home-office CO2 down to reasonable levels. During a visit to relatives, the bedroom reached 2500 ppm while they slept, prompting them to open the window for the remainder of the stay. No numerical post-intervention reading or sleep improvement is reported.
- Klathmon [[./thread#^c40034419|Klathmon]] uses a cheap AliExpress device, opens the office door or window above approximately 1000 ppm, and notices no conscious difference. The same device showed cooking-related PM2.5 increases and 4300 ppm CO2 at the in-laws' home. They interpreted the latter as explaining headaches, and the household began opening windows in the mornings; sensor validation and subsequent symptom outcomes are absent.
- rinron [[./thread#^c40033371|rinron]] reports better sleep after experimenting with bedroom window opening and leaving the door open. Monitoring also helped adjust an air filter's location and speed. They estimate occasional monitoring would capture most of the benefit and explicitly acknowledge that other people may live in better-designed homes; their broad claim about bedrooms exceeds the personal evidence supplied.
- jbm [[./thread#^c40034917|jbm]] reports that multiple CO2 meters suggested no major household airflow issue, redirecting their search for better sleep. During winter parties, readings above roughly 1200 ppm prompt a slightly opened window. They do not report a measured sleep benefit from this practice.
- jbm [[./thread#^c40034917|jbm]] also reports approximately six months of radon readings near a safety limit, leading to home mitigation; a friend borrowed the device and also chose mitigation. After gausswho [[./thread#^c40040816|gausswho]] asks which AirGradient measurement indicates radon, jbm [[./thread#^c40042927|jbm]] clarifies that the instrument was an Airthings Wave Plus and that they have never used AirGradient. The radon account must not be attributed to AirGradient.
- billfor [[./thread#^c40033234|billfor]] owns indoor and outdoor monitors. Outdoor readings help with decisions about going outside during wildfires; indoor observations revealed surprising cooking pollution, including from waffles, and encouraged more consistent hood use. No numerical particle concentrations or measured hood effectiveness are given.
- Royce-CMR [[./thread#^c40033241|Royce-CMR]] reports that a standing purifier rated for twice their floor area quickly lowers an unspecified PPM reading on maximum speed, while continuous low-speed operation keeps it down. They describe personal sensitivity to CO2 as improving, but provide no exposure values or evidence connecting that improvement to the purifier.
- nsbk [[./thread#^c40032527|nsbk]] has built AirGradient DIY devices for personal use and as gifts, integrates them through Home Assistant/MQTT, and applies fixed temperature and humidity corrections based on observed deviations. This is firsthand adjustment experience, without a described reference standard or validation of the corrected results.
- Noise experiences differ substantially by device. Havoc [[./thread#^c40030648|Havoc]] finds small PM-sensor fans objectionably whiny and prefers the lower pitch of sensors using purifier airflow. geerlingguy [[./thread#^c40031497|geerlingguy]] reports about eight AirGradient deployments, including a studio with a 31 dBA noise floor, and hears the sensor only with an ear almost touching it. ploxiln [[./thread#^c40032645|ploxiln]] likewise finds an AirGradient quiet but reports rattling from an apparently malfunctioning fan in another brand.
- Borealid [[./thread#^c40029816|Borealid]] reports several hassle-free devices running personal software. guiambros [[./thread#^c40031268|guiambros]] calls their devices reliable but finds the ESP32 integration setup finicky. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c40030607|ahaucnx]] describes local API, mDNS, and official Home Assistant support as work in progress, so those promises should be distinguished from the integrations users had already operated.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Long-term calibration reliability remains disputed. seanmceligot [[./thread#^c40031686|seanmceligot]] asserts that Airthings devices need at least annual calibration but cannot be calibrated and criticizes the company's response. dawnerd [[./thread#^c40032929|dawnerd]] counters that their device still agrees with other sensors after more than a year. Neither supplies traceable accuracy measurements that settle drift, calibration frequency, or the manufacturer's conduct.
- Readers wanted practical calibration methods beyond error calculations. m4x [[./thread#^c40029903|m4x]] and m4x [[./thread#^c40038547|m4x]] connect error determination to calibration; wezdog1 [[./thread#^c40030681|wezdog1]] notes the absence of calibration instructions, while somat [[./thread#^c40035717|somat]] wanted a homemade reference measurement. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c40030636|ahaucnx]] points to other correction articles and future PM/humidity coverage. seanmceligot [[./thread#^c40031704|seanmceligot]] had found search results without investigating them, and runjake [[./thread#^c40032211|runjake]] redirects to the author's reply. Those references do not themselves supply a demonstrated calibration procedure in this export.
- Preferred CO2 hardware is not quantitatively resolved. Havoc [[./thread#^c40030648|Havoc]] favors the SCD41 based on experimentation. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c40030679|ahaucnx]] also endorses it but reports that the similarly priced SenseAir S8 was somewhat more accurate in their experience. No numerical comparison or test protocol is provided.
- Historical reference data may depend on calibration changes. honk [[./thread#^c40032499|honk]] says updated T640 algorithms often read about 7\% lower and explicitly leaves the implications for older measurements unresolved. The discussion supplies no reconciliation of those historical readings.
- The commercial context is contested. thebruce87m [[./thread#^c40034277|thebruce87m]] characterizes the recurring articles as product promotion and cites an advertising disclaimer. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c40034389|ahaucnx]] says that disclaimer labels the banner below the article and that the article itself is scientific. This exchange establishes a relevant manufacturer affiliation but does not settle the validity of individual technical claims.
- This source unit provides no NO2 measurements, numerical PM2.5 mass concentrations, traffic-exposure comparisons, or cognitive testing. Its health evidence consists principally of accounts such as Klathmon's inferred headache explanation [[./thread#^c40034419|Klathmon]] and rinron's subjective sleep improvement [[./thread#^c40033371|rinron]]. Luc's EPA-study quotation [[./thread#^c40029829|Luc]] concerns instrument agreement, not health effects. Exposure-response relationships, clinical benefits, and the magnitude of the window-opening tradeoff therefore remain unestablished here.
