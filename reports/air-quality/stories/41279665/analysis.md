# [Air Quality Monitors Compared: AirGradient One vs. Airthings](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41279665)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants compare AirGradient, Airthings, IQAir, and other air monitors on measurement quality, price, usability, repairability, local data access, and Home Assistant integration. Discussion covers data ownership terms and cloud dependence, open hardware and firmware, the absence of affordable radon sensing, and possible PoE support. Some users question AirGradient’s self-promotion and firmware quality, while others value its hardware, research, and local integrations. Several comments distinguish useful CO₂, particulate, VOC, and radon measurements and debate how to interpret sensor readings.
Tags: air quality monitors, AirGradient, Airthings, sensor accuracy, data ownership, open source hardware, Home Assistant, radon

## Relevance

Comments discuss monitor accuracy and sensor limitations, indoor CO2 and PM readings, ventilation changes, cooking-related particles, and using filtration. The thread includes firsthand device and household experiences alongside disagreement about calibration and measurement quality. ## Arguments

- Monitoring can make an intervention assessable. rssoconnor [[./thread#^c41282714|rssoconnor]] describes using particle readings to identify poor performance from a range hood without outdoor exhaust, then to assess a kitchen air cleaner. This is the clearest measurement-linked improvement in this source, although it establishes neither a quantified exposure reduction nor a health benefit.
- Distinguishing direct CO2 sensing from VOC-derived estimates matters. DIY builder progbits [[./thread#^c41280830|progbits]] warns that some products estimate CO2 from other gases; AirGradient founder ahaucnx [[./thread#^c41282352|ahaucnx]] identifies its SenseAir S8 as an NDIR CO2 sensor. These comments support checking the sensing technology before interpreting a displayed CO2 value as an actual concentration.
- Reference comparisons offer a stronger basis for accuracy claims than product satisfaction alone. strogonoff [[./thread#^c41280198|strogonoff]] chose IQAir partly because of reported California AQMD testing. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c41280291|ahaucnx]] reports AirGradient co-location work with reference instruments at more than 25 sites, while explaining that AQ-Spec testing had not yet been undertaken. Neither comment supplies the underlying comparison results.
- Local access can make personal measurements easier to retain and use. ziml77 [[./thread#^c41280155|ziml77]] reports straightforward local-only Home Assistant integration, and chomp [[./thread#^c41280083|chomp]] describes sending a DIY device's data to Grafana for alerts. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c41280053|ahaucnx]] says newer AirGradient firmware provides native Home Assistant discovery and a local API, addressing montjoy's earlier need to flash alternative firmware [[./thread#^c41280017|montjoy]].
- A monitor's usefulness depends on the exposure question. anotherhue [[./thread#^c41280199|anotherhue]] considers a window-opening cue its main purpose, whereas rssoconnor [[./thread#^c41282714|rssoconnor]] values particle measurements during cooking and daft\_pink [[./thread#^c41310417|daft\_pink]] prioritizes detecting radon because of its claimed cancer risk. These are different monitoring objectives, not evidence that one measurement adequately represents every indoor hazard.

## Counterarguments

- The claim that an inexpensive CO2 meter is accurate enough for ventilation decisions remains qualified. anotherhue [[./thread#^c41280199|anotherhue]] recommends a roughly \$30 meter, but bhouston's separate favorable account of a roughly \$50 device [[./thread#^c41281743|bhouston]] ends with an acknowledgment that it probably estimates CO2 from VOCs [[./thread#^c41295917|bhouston]]. Apparent responsiveness in everyday situations does not establish CO2 accuracy.
- AirGradient's favorable reception is contradicted by a dissatisfied owner. emmanuel\_1234 [[./thread#^c41282132|emmanuel\_1234]] reports readings ranging from 200 to 2000 ppm, objects to automatic recalibration and VOC reporting, and unplugged the unit. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c41282352|ahaucnx]] disputes the claim that its CO2 sensor does not measure CO2, offers configurable calibration and VOC-learning settings, and invites support contact. No subsequent resolution from that owner appears.
- DIY savings do not necessarily include the same validation as a finished unit. illiac786 [[./thread#^c41281290|illiac786]] says the preassembled product undergoes testing against specific standards. ziml77 [[./thread#^c41283298|ziml77]] accepts missing that testing and assumes sensor manufacturing QC is sufficient for home use. The latter is an owner's tolerance for uncertainty, not a demonstrated equivalence between assembled and kit units.
- Higher prices and professional positioning do not by themselves establish better sensing. oloapiu [[./thread#^c41281014|oloapiu]] contrasts consumer sensor modules with professional indicative monitors, but ahaucnx [[./thread#^c41282886|ahaucnx]] replies that Clarity also uses a Plantower sensor. Conversely, sharing a component does not establish equal complete-device performance; no comparative results settle that question here.
- Claims that Airthings data can always remain local are model-dependent in these accounts. sliken [[./thread#^c41297607|sliken]] advocates avoiding its cloud service, and montjoy [[./thread#^c41280017|montjoy]] confirms local Bluetooth integration for a Wave+. Replying to sliken, View Plus owner mbStavola [[./thread#^c41312470|mbStavola]] says their newer model appears to require the cloud to retrieve data. The older-model experience does not resolve the newer-model limitation.
- Turning off a bright display does not meet every bedroom use case. Urgo [[./thread#^c41280263|Urgo]] recommends a nighttime display-off mode and later explains scheduling it [[./thread#^c41282713|Urgo]]. strogonoff [[./thread#^c41280839|strogonoff]] and [[./thread#^c41287148|strogonoff]] objects that checking a reading during the night still requires interaction and exposes them to an unpleasantly bright display. The disagreement concerns usable nighttime access, not whether scheduling exists.

## Assumptions

- For source unit 41279665, part 0, most evidence consists of owner impressions and manufacturer statements rather than controlled exposure measurements. For example, felixtany [[./thread#^c41280000|felixtany]], antirez [[./thread#^c41281954|antirez]], and lrhegeba [[./thread#^c41281953|lrhegeba]] praise accuracy, components, or overall performance without supplying reference comparisons. These accounts cannot establish population-wide satisfaction or accuracy.
- Manufacturer explanations have a disclosed commercial context. ahaucnx identifies as AirGradient's founder [[./thread#^c41282394|ahaucnx]]; haswell [[./thread#^c41282712|haswell]] questions repeated self-promotion while acknowledging that some content is good, and ahaucnx [[./thread#^c41282830|ahaucnx]] agrees to reflect on the criticism. This warrants distinguishing vendor claims from independent observations without treating promotion alone as proof that a technical claim is false.
- What counts as accurate enough depends on the intended decision. ziml77 [[./thread#^c41283298|ziml77]] explicitly tolerates readings being somewhat off at home, while anotherhue [[./thread#^c41280199|anotherhue]] wants a simple ventilation cue. Neither position establishes adequate accuracy for identifying a hazardous concentration, comparing small changes, or attributing symptoms to exposure.
- The proposed ventilation mechanisms contain untested assumptions. scire [[./thread#^c41282057|scire]], explicitly lacking radon experience, assumes bringing in fresh air will reduce both CO2 and radon. Xen9 [[./thread#^c41285017|Xen9]] proposes a mail-slot fan, small HEPA filter, and window exhaust, including an odor-removal claim. Neither supplies airflow, pollutant, odor, or before-and-after measurements demonstrating those outcomes.

## Conditions

- Upfront cost strongly influenced one multi-device purchase. felixtany [[./thread#^c41280000|felixtany]] reports approximately \$800 for two outdoor and four indoor AirGradient kits, excluding freight and shared with a friend, versus a quoted \$1,774 PurpleAir alternative. These are that purchaser's reported prices and configurations, not a verified current or universally equivalent comparison.
- Low-cost figures refer to different things. anotherhue [[./thread#^c41280199|anotherhue]] suggests a roughly \$30 complete CO2 meter; bhouston [[./thread#^c41281743|bhouston]] bought a roughly \$50 device later suspected to be VOC-based [[./thread#^c41295917|bhouston]]; progbits [[./thread#^c41280830|progbits]] cites \$30-plus for a true CO2 sensor component. A component price and a finished meter price are not directly interchangeable.
- Monitoring additional hazards changes the total-cost comparison. daft\_pink [[./thread#^c41310451|daft\_pink]] argues that the Airthings comparator already includes radon sensing and costs less than AirGradient plus a separate radon monitor, without giving totals. progbits [[./thread#^c41280830|progbits]] and ahaucnx [[./thread#^c41280079|ahaucnx]] describe difficulty sourcing affordable radon modules. daft\_pink [[./thread#^c41310417|daft\_pink]] also calls ventilation expensive, but provides no installation or running-cost breakdown.
- Access costs can restrict use of measurements after hardware purchase. Urgo [[./thread#^c41280263|Urgo]] reports free local file access for IQAir indoor units but says outdoor data requires either paid API access or consent to data resale. Their existing investment discourages switching despite dissatisfaction. This is an account of access restrictions, not evidence that the outdoor sensor measures poorly.
- Weather and surroundings can affect ventilation-related alerts. nov21b [[./thread#^c41280461|nov21b]] says the Wave+ warns when relative humidity exceeds 60\%, and gives plants, forest surroundings, and ventilation on rainy days as circumstances that can trigger it. The complaint concerns an alert that distracts from CO2, VOC, and radon priorities; it does not demonstrate that those circumstances worsen particle exposure or that humidity is harmless.
- The geographic references do not provide geographic exposure comparisons. strogonoff [[./thread#^c41280198|strogonoff]] references a California testing laboratory; ck2 [[./thread#^c41282271|ck2]] discusses US monitoring-map coverage; oloapiu [[./thread#^c41281014|oloapiu]] cites London's sensor network as an example. None supplies local outdoor concentrations from those places, so these references cannot establish where opening windows would improve air quality.

## Lived experiences

- Cooking and filtration: rssoconnor [[./thread#^c41282714|rssoconnor]] reports that AirGradient particle readings made a non-exhausting range hood look especially ineffective. They bought a Blueair cleaner, described as a HEPA filter, and found it effective according to the same monitor while cooking. No concentrations, particle-size breakdown, cooking fuel, exposure duration, or health outcomes are given.
- Ventilation prompted by a portable monitor: bhouston [[./thread#^c41281743|bhouston]] tested an inexpensive device in several situations, judged it relatively accurate, and changed household ventilation. They subsequently used occasional spot checks rather than continuous monitoring. Their later acknowledgment that it probably estimates CO2 from VOCs [[./thread#^c41295917|bhouston]] limits what those observations establish about actual CO2.
- Calibration needed attention even for a satisfied owner. rssoconnor [[./thread#^c41282714|rssoconnor]] says they changed CO2 recalibration periods to keep readings sensible and used curl commands to configure indoor and outdoor units differently. They valued the possibility of editing the firmware. This reports a workable configuration process, not independent verification of the corrected concentrations.
- IQAir durability accounts conflict. strogonoff [[./thread#^c41280198|strogonoff]] reports paying more than \$400 and having the micro-USB connector break after about four months. Urgo [[./thread#^c41280263|Urgo]] reports four indoor units and one outdoor unit still working, with the oldest approximately eight or nine years old and the others approximately three or four. These anecdotes establish variation in experience, not a failure rate.
- Bedroom comfort was affected by the instrument itself. strogonoff [[./thread#^c41280198|strogonoff]] found the IQAir display too bright for bedroom use and later preferred an e-ink display for convenient nighttime readings [[./thread#^c41280839|strogonoff]]. They considered the sensor probably functional but the device unpleasant enough that repair might not be worthwhile. This is a firsthand usability complaint, not a demonstrated effect of polluted air on sleep.
- Assembly and local setup were easy for several owners. ziml77 [[./thread#^c41280155|ziml77]] describes plugging in three modules and installing four screws; anotherhue [[./thread#^c41280199|anotherhue]] reports roughly 90 seconds of assembly and rapid Home Assistant discovery. ploxiln [[./thread#^c41281046|ploxiln]] was happy with a DIY Basic built about a year earlier and bought a ONE kit for their parents. In that reply chain, ahaucnx [[./thread#^c41281066|ahaucnx]] clarifies that the discontinued soldering kit was the DIY Pro, while the Basic was expected back in stock.
- Equipment comparisons were enthusiastic but unquantified. felixtany [[./thread#^c41280000|felixtany]] describes Sensibo accuracy as very poor, regards existing Aranet4 devices as highly accurate, and praises the purchased AirGradient system's setup and dashboard. antirez [[./thread#^c41281954|antirez]] similarly praises Awair Element, while lrhegeba [[./thread#^c41281953|lrhegeba]] likes air-Q's interface and integrations. None provides numerical accuracy results supporting those assessments.
- Portable monitoring exposed variation between indoor settings for eisa01 [[./thread#^c41280982|eisa01]], who values taking a battery-powered Airthings View Plus on vacation. The comment reports substantial variety in indoor air quality but identifies no pollutants, concentrations, locations, symptoms, or successful interventions.
- Historical data access had practical limitations for Urgo [[./thread#^c41280263|Urgo]]: the indoor IQAir files updated every five to fifteen minutes, with fifteen-minute updates during display-off mode, and the existing graphs were difficult to use. These are reported data-access intervals; the comment does not establish the underlying sensors' sampling frequency.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Health, cognition, and sleep effects are largely unanswered in this unit. daft\_pink [[./thread#^c41310417|daft\_pink]] invokes radon's cancer risk to explain their priorities, but provides no personal diagnosis or exposure-outcome measurements. strogonoff's bedroom complaint [[./thread#^c41287148|strogonoff]] concerns display brightness. No supplied account measures cognitive performance, sleep quality, or clinical improvement following a measured PM2.5, NO2, or CO2 change.
- When opening windows helps versus worsens exposure remains undetermined. anotherhue [[./thread#^c41280199|anotherhue]] and scire [[./thread#^c41282057|scire]] favor ventilation as a response to CO2, while nov21b [[./thread#^c41280461|nov21b]] identifies rainy-weather humidity alerts. There are no paired indoor/outdoor pollution readings, traffic or wildfire-smoke accounts, or measured NO2 results here. rssoconnor's cooking account [[./thread#^c41282714|rssoconnor]] supplies qualitative particle changes, not numeric PM2.5 concentrations or evidence about gaseous cooking pollutants.
- The cause and extent of sensor errors remain unresolved. emmanuel\_1234 [[./thread#^c41282132|emmanuel\_1234]] reports implausible or erratic readings, ahaucnx [[./thread#^c41282352|ahaucnx]] offers calibration controls and support, and rssoconnor [[./thread#^c41282714|rssoconnor]] reports improvement through configuration. No reference-controlled before-and-after data establishes whether particular discrepancies arose from settings, defective hardware, environmental conditions, or interpretation of the output.
- The inexpensive VOC-based device's usefulness as a CO2 proxy is disputed but not tested conclusively. bhouston [[./thread#^c41295917|bhouston]] still considers it useful in most cases after accepting that it probably estimates CO2. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c41282409|ahaucnx]] suggests checking whether ethanol produces a large apparent CO2 rise, but no result of that proposed check is reported. The thread supplies no conditions under which the proxy reliably succeeds or fails.
- CO2-guided ventilation versus direct radon measurement remains a substantive disagreement. scire [[./thread#^c41282057|scire]] and anotherhue [[./thread#^c41282243|anotherhue]] emphasize a shared ventilation response; daft\_pink [[./thread#^c41310417|daft\_pink]] wants direct knowledge of radon exposure before paying for ventilation. anotherhue's later reference to a radon test-kit search [[./thread#^c41321880|anotherhue]] supplies neither performance evidence nor a comparison with continuous monitoring. No paired CO2/radon measurements establish that one can safely stand in for the other.
