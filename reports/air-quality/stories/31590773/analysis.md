# [Ceiling Air Purifier](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=31590773)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

The ceiling-mounted purifier idea prompts comparisons with box-fan filters and Corsi–Rosenthal boxes, alongside discussion of airflow, filtration performance, noise, and the evidence needed to evaluate the design. Practical concerns include filter cost and replacement, mounting, structural support, dust, appearance, and whether a ceiling fan can push enough air through filters. Participants also debate MERV ratings, HVAC airflow restrictions and equipment wear, and how room filtration may reduce smoke, allergens, or airborne infection risk.
Tags: air purifiers, DIY filtration, Corsi–Rosenthal box, airflow, HVAC, filter performance, COVID-19

## Relevance

The comments assess practical particle filtration designs, airflow, filter efficiency, noise, cost, and maintenance, with firsthand reports of dust removal and reduced wildfire smoke. They debate how well filters work and how to measure their effect, and note tradeoffs between filtration, airflow, and HVAC performance. ## Arguments

- More filter area can make a box fan more useful for particle removal. kolencherry describes a five-filter Corsi–Rosenthal box [[./thread#^c31608821|kolencherry]], while alexpw favors four filters and a cardboard base [[./thread#^c31609160|alexpw]]. jefftk explains the proposed advantage over one filter: parallel filters reduce resistance, allowing a fan designed for airflow rather than pressure to move more air [[./thread#^c31615512|jefftk]].
- The ceiling prototype has a quantitative performance claim: jefftk quotes approximately 180 CFM clean-air delivery at 33 dB, compared with a commercial purifier delivering 233 CFM at 54 dB or 110 CFM at 36 dB [[./thread#^c31612377|jefftk]]. These are reported prototype and comparison results, not independently verified measurements within the supplied discussion.
- Filter efficiency alone may be a poor purchasing criterion. blagie argues that 70\% removal at 100 CFM supplies the same effective particle removal as 100\% removal at 70 CFM [[./thread#^c31609294|blagie]]. jefftk explains why this equivalence holds in a randomly mixed room model, while acknowledging that actual rooms fall between idealized mixing models [[./thread#^c31610162|jefftk]].
- Existing HVAC equipment may provide distributed filtration without a separate appliance. bombcar recommends running the blower independently of heating or cooling and notes that scheduled circulation can also balance room temperatures [[./thread#^c31607435|bombcar]]. kube-system argues that additional pleated media area can improve filtration without necessarily increasing total airflow resistance [[./thread#^c31611417|kube-system]].
- The intended health benefit is exposure reduction, not a sterile room. jefftk says the design primarily targets COVID but could also serve during wildfire smoke [[./thread#^c31611348|jefftk]]. Responding to skepticism about cardboard-and-tape construction, tgsovlerkhgsel argues that substantial risk reduction does not require a certified containment environment [[./thread#^c31614802|tgsovlerkhgsel]]. Neither comment reports clinical outcomes.
- Some commenters see the awkward construction as a prototype limitation rather than a reason to abandon the idea. blagie proposes better fan selection, framing and prefilters [[./thread#^c31608731|blagie]]. mreiner suggests that a ceiling fan already running for hot-weather comfort could provide useful additional filtration even if only part of its airflow crosses the filters, but presents this as a conditional assessment [[./thread#^c31608629|mreiner]].

## Counterarguments

- The reported PM2.5 decay does not establish how much improvement the filters themselves caused. tallanvor requests clearer controls and fan-speed comparisons [[./thread#^c31607470|tallanvor]]; SamBam identifies the missing fan-without-filters comparison and suggests bypass or increased outdoor-air exchange as alternative explanations [[./thread#^c31608950|SamBam]]. jefftk establishes that a commercial-purifier comparison exists [[./thread#^c31608866|jefftk]], which answers one criticism but not the missing filterless control.
- Upgrading HVAC filtration can compromise the airflow needed for heating and cooling. post\_break relays an HVAC-worker parent's advice to avoid restrictive filters [[./thread#^c31608483|post\_break]]. bombcar warns about insufficient airflow over heat exchangers and cooling coils [[./thread#^c31608455|bombcar]], while dr\_orpheus discusses coil icing and reports a family experience involving condensation and duct damage [[./thread#^c31609114|dr\_orpheus]]. These concerns qualify blanket recommendations to install finer filters.
- Commercial purifiers may offer practical advantages, but the claimed superiority is contested. KennyBlanken recommends them for lower electricity use, quieter operation and automatic speed adjustment [[./thread#^c31612240|KennyBlanken]]; flybrand expects comparable effectiveness and possibly comparable replacement costs [[./thread#^c31608589|flybrand]]. jefftk's reported ceiling-prototype sound and CADR figures challenge a blanket claim that commercial units are quieter at useful output [[./thread#^c31612377|jefftk]].
- The objection that virus size makes MERV-14 filtration largely ineffective receives specific rebuttals. aarongray initially makes that objection [[./thread#^c31609106|aarongray]], but jefftk cites filter ratings and the sizes of respiratory particles carrying viruses [[./thread#^c31609844|jefftk]]. aarongray subsequently accepts the filtration-rating correction while continuing to dispute other claims [[./thread#^c31609809|aarongray]]. The exchange supports distinguishing nominal particle capture from demonstrated infection prevention.
- Dust falling when the ceiling fan stops is questioned rather than demonstrated. leobg raises the concern [[./thread#^c31595668|leobg]]; jefftk replies that small particles become embedded and reports that PM2.5 did not rise again after shutdown [[./thread#^c31597539|jefftk]]. That observation addresses stopping the fan, not every possible disturbance or reversal of airflow.
- Duct cleaning is not an agreed route to cleaner indoor air. hinkley recommends filter maintenance and describes having ducts cleaned after moving [[./thread#^c31609813|hinkley]]. wang\_li counters by invoking EPA guidance and arguing that deposited material is not necessarily being carried into rooms [[./thread#^c31614732|wang\_li]]. Neither supplies a household exposure comparison before and after cleaning.
- Claims for molecular destruction technologies face contrary particulate-performance evidence. gonehome quotes Wirecutter tests describing very poor particulate removal by Molekule products and recounts challenges to the company's advertising claims [[./thread#^c31609298|gonehome]]. This is a quoted external test report, not gonehome's own experiment. aarongray acknowledges the background [[./thread#^c31609769|aarongray]], then argues that denaturing biological contaminants is a different objective and points to manufacturer-linked laboratory reports [[./thread#^c31610318|aarongray]]. Those later claims do not establish better household PM2.5 removal or health outcomes.

## Assumptions

- The efficiency-times-airflow argument assumes sufficiently mixed air and comparable operating conditions. jefftk explicitly contrasts sequential filtration with random recirculation and judges real rooms closer to the latter without demonstrating the mixing pattern of this installation [[./thread#^c31610162|jefftk]]. Nominally equal clean-air delivery therefore does not settle every room-layout or exposure question.
- Several observations are proxies for different things and should remain separate. ChoGGi reports less wildfire smoke smell [[./thread#^c31615878|ChoGGi]]; azth reports darkened outlet filters [[./thread#^c31623022|azth]]; jefftk reports PM2.5 behavior after shutdown [[./thread#^c31597539|jefftk]]. Odor reduction, accumulated visible material and airborne particle measurements do not, by themselves, establish the same exposure reduction or health benefit.
- Displaying a pollutant concentration is separate from removing that pollutant. mellavora describes an AirGradient build with both PM2.5 and CO2 sensors [[./thread#^c31608065|mellavora]], while jefftk's purification discussion concerns particle filtration [[./thread#^c31609844|jefftk]]. Nothing supplied demonstrates CO2 or NO2 removal by these systems.
- Automatic speed control assumes that the device can detect the condition requiring filtration. KennyBlanken treats automatic adjustment as an advantage [[./thread#^c31612240|KennyBlanken]], but jefftk questions how a smoke-responsive approach would identify COVID risk [[./thread#^c31612377|jefftk]]. The discussion supplies no validated sensor-based rule for deciding when airborne infection risk is low enough to reduce airflow.
- Longer service life from additional filters is plausible but not established as a simple multiplier. eli asks whether five times as many filters would last five times as long [[./thread#^c31607533|eli]]. sampo, explicitly drawing on something read online, describes a competing balance between capture efficiency, clogging, electricity and noise [[./thread#^c31609317|sampo]]. Neither supplies a measured lifetime-cost calculation.
- Repeated filtration does not establish equivalence to a hospital installation. jjeaff argues that recirculation matters more than achieving perfectly clean air in one pass and suggests little difference with the same filters [[./thread#^c31635651|jjeaff]]. The supplied comments do not establish equivalent airflow, installation quality or exposure control; tgsovlerkhgsel's narrower claim is significant risk reduction without a certified-safe environment [[./thread#^c31614802|tgsovlerkhgsel]].

## Conditions

- Prices are historical quotations from this 2022 discussion. sha256sum describes a \$25 box fan plus a furnace filter [[./thread#^c31607436|sha256sum]]; jefftk quotes \$110 for nine MERV-14 filters for the ceiling design [[./thread#^c31608893|jefftk]]; mgraczyk describes a \$200 Winix purifier [[./thread#^c31614219|mgraczyk]]. jstream67's concern about \$50–100 conventional replacement filters helps explain the cost objection [[./thread#^c31607503|jstream67]], but these figures do not establish comparable lifetime costs or performance.
- Monitoring cost depends on assembly and logging requirements. mellavora lists a \$13.89 Plantower PMS5003 particle sensor and a \$28 Senseair S8 CO2 sensor, plus other components [[./thread#^c31608065|mellavora]]. joelnc52 reports approximately \$150 for an Arduino-based sensor, display and logging setup [[./thread#^c31607848|joelnc52]]. jefftk selected a Temtop M2000 because it was the cheapest option they found with per-minute export, rather than claiming it was the cheapest or most accurate monitor overall [[./thread#^c31608391|jefftk]].
- Wildfire emergencies change availability as well as demand. hinkley describes a large filter cube and difficulty obtaining several filters after the sky had already turned orange [[./thread#^c31609931|hinkley]] [[./thread#^c31610110|hinkley]]. jefftk recommends buying and rotating unopened stock beforehand [[./thread#^c31610054|jefftk]]. hinkley accepts advance replenishment [[./thread#^c31610110|hinkley]], so this exchange reaches a limited agreement about preparation rather than leaving the stockpiling issue wholly unresolved.
- Local supply can make DIY filtration substantially cheaper or easier. bombcar reports finding unused furnace and HEPA filters cheaply at a local Goodwill [[./thread#^c31608252|bombcar]]. UI\_at\_80x24 describes recovering fans through former photocopier work [[./thread#^c31609742|UI\_at\_80x24]], and Kadin reports discarded furnace blowers in their area [[./thread#^c31610751|Kadin]]. These are location- and access-dependent opportunities, not generally available prices or equipment.
- The housing and installation matter. SigmundA describes a four-inch filter cabinet installed with an HVAC replacement and explains that it is not simply interchangeable with a one-inch return filter [[./thread#^c31607550|SigmundA]]. bombcar suggests testing and adjusting airflow for the installed filter [[./thread#^c31607679|bombcar]]. Havoc favors distributing clean air through rooms but notes that retrofitting such a system is difficult [[./thread#^c31607594|Havoc]].
- Aesthetics, space and maintenance access affect whether filtration is acceptable in daily use. bredren finds conventional filter cubes large and unattractive and is developing a hanging purifier/light [[./thread#^c31611829|bredren]]. russnewcomer raises support and adhesive-smell concerns for ceiling mounting [[./thread#^c31607966|russnewcomer]]. maxk42 objects to repeatedly accessing numerous overhead filters [[./thread#^c31608848|maxk42]], while jefftk's proposed easy replacement mechanism depends on a future product design and a reachable ceiling [[./thread#^c31608959|jefftk]].
- Window-based approaches depend on what windows exist and how the device is placed. euroclydon prefers a powerful bathroom-window exhaust fan during demolition [[./thread#^c31611350|euroclydon]], but sfteus says their bathroom had no window [[./thread#^c31687273|sfteus]]. Separately, happyopossum explains that a fifth bottom filter can be useful when a filter cube is elevated, hung or placed in a window [[./thread#^c31609621|happyopossum]]; that is a placement argument, not a measurement of outdoor-air benefits.

## Lived experiences

- sha256sum reports success with a single furnace filter inserted into a box fan [[./thread#^c31607436|sha256sum]]. sudden\_dystopia says they have built roughly a dozen such units and finds them effective but loud, with the noise useful at night [[./thread#^c31607869|sudden\_dystopia]]. These are firsthand usability endorsements without particle concentrations or controlled effectiveness measurements.
- Sleep preferences differ even among purifier users. post\_break says a Dyson's sound profile does not keep them awake and describes using a much rougher purifier near a 3D printer [[./thread#^c31622162|post\_break]]. This supports room-specific purchasing priorities and a noise-related sleep benefit; it does not demonstrate improved sleep physiology from cleaner air or measured printer-emission removal.
- During demolition of an approximately seven-cubic-foot concrete shower pan, sfteus used a box-fan filter and went through three or four filters, reporting substantial visible material capture [[./thread#^c31609937|sfteus]]. They explain that the bathroom lacked a window and they wanted to avoid venting dust elsewhere indoors [[./thread#^c31687273|sfteus]]. No PM2.5 concentration, respirable-dust exposure or health outcome is reported.
- ChoGGi reports that a newly installed furnace/AC system with a four-inch carbon filter costing CAD \$50, operated with just the fan, greatly reduced smoke smell during wildfire episodes originating in British Columbia [[./thread#^c31615878|ChoGGi]]. This is a firsthand comfort and odor observation, not a quantified PM2.5 or gas-removal result.
- azth uses furnace filters described as MERV-13 or MERV-14 and additional MERV-13 filters on bedroom supply vents. The outlet filters darken enough to prompt replacement every couple of months [[./thread#^c31623022|azth]]. azth interprets this as inadequate central filtration, but supplies no measurements distinguishing residual airborne particles, system leakage or another source.
- Successful HVAC-filtration accounts coexist with warnings. SigmundA reports more than ten years with an installer-recommended AprilAire cabinet and blower circulation independent of heating/cooling demand [[./thread#^c31607550|SigmundA]]. Johnny555 describes an installer-recommended deep pleated MERV-13 filter with a manufacturer warranty [[./thread#^c31610619|Johnny555]]. jjeaff reports using high-MERV and charcoal filters, replacing them about every three months, without a noticeable energy or runtime difference [[./thread#^c31636458|jjeaff]]. These are system-specific experiences rather than controlled compatibility tests.
- There are also equipment-problem accounts. dr\_orpheus says their parents developed condensation and rusting ductwork after adding filters [[./thread#^c31609114|dr\_orpheus]]. foobarian separately describes repairing an HVAC control relay and a blower start capacitor [[./thread#^c31609496|foobarian]]. These accounts illustrate reported failure modes but do not establish that filter restriction caused all such failures or quantify their frequency.
- Filter appearance and replacement intervals differ by setting. maxk42 reports changing near-floor filters every two to three months in a dry, dusty area [[./thread#^c31608848|maxk42]]. jefftk reports that an office filter cube running eight hours daily since November was only somewhat gray by June [[./thread#^c31615642|jefftk]], directly qualifying mxfh's prediction that the ceiling assembly could become black within roughly six weeks [[./thread#^c31610989|mxfh]].
- Several commenters describe actual monitoring setups, but few report numerical exposure results. jefftk uses a Temtop M2000 with per-minute export [[./thread#^c31608391|jefftk]]; joelnc52 reports more than two years of operation from an Arduino-based particle monitor with logging [[./thread#^c31607848|joelnc52]]; ryankrage77 sends Nova SDS011 readings through a Raspberry Pi and MQTT into Home Assistant [[./thread#^c31607822|ryankrage77]]; nerdbaggy uses an Airthings View Plus [[./thread#^c31607634|nerdbaggy]]. These establish ownership and operation, not comparative accuracy.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Sensor accuracy remains largely unexamined. jefftk explains the monitor's export capability [[./thread#^c31608391|jefftk]], mellavora lists sensor components [[./thread#^c31608065|mellavora]], and joelnc52 reports longevity while only tentatively identifying their sensor with one used by PurpleAir [[./thread#^c31607848|joelnc52]]. The supplied comments provide no reference-instrument comparison, calibration results or numerical household PM2.5/CO2 time series. Reliable operation and reliable concentration estimates remain separate questions.
- The prototype's airflow and placement remain uncertain. lkbm proposes possible short recirculation paths and asks for visualization experiments [[./thread#^c31608596|lkbm]]; ark03 labels their proposed smaller-filter geometry a guess [[./thread#^c31608973|ark03]]; fomine3 asks whether floor placement would better collect settling dust [[./thread#^c31637728|fomine3]]. SamBam also speculates that reversing airflow could release dust [[./thread#^c31608986|SamBam]]. No supplied experiment resolves these configurations.
- Box-fan overheating and lifetime are disputed. bredren requests comparative temperature and power data [[./thread#^c31611845|bredren]]. hinkley reads linked test charts as showing temperature rises above 10°C, reaching about 20°C for one model, despite the test author's reported conclusion of no problems [[./thread#^c31612378|hinkley]]. Cerium reports years without box-fan failures and argues that their indoor operating conditions are cooler than a hot window application [[./thread#^c31615012|Cerium]]. Neither the anecdote nor the reported chart interpretation settles long-term failure risk.
- The HVAC motor debate mixes different motor and control designs. bityard argues that restricting airflow reduces fan load and current [[./thread#^c31611426|bityard]], while foobarian argues that increased backpressure raises consumption on a conventional induction motor [[./thread#^c31609700|foobarian]]. malfist assumes essentially fixed-speed operation [[./thread#^c31608267|malfist]], which bombcar challenges with a variable-speed, constant-airflow furnace example [[./thread#^c31608929|bombcar]]. The discussion does not resolve these claims with measurements from the same equipment and operating conditions.
- Infection-risk claims go beyond the available household evidence. mistrial9 emphasizes that few infectious particles may matter [[./thread#^c31615320|mistrial9]]; blagie argues for dose-dependent risk and possible severity effects while acknowledging that some claims are not rigorously proven [[./thread#^c31610842|blagie]]; aarongray disputes the asserted evidentiary basis [[./thread#^c31609809|aarongray]]. No supplied account measures infection reduction, and PM2.5 cleaning performance alone does not settle that disagreement.
- Alternative treatment methods have unresolved benefits and emissions questions. Pxtl proposes ducted UV-C [[./thread#^c31611140|Pxtl]]; jryb cites a review while warning about ozone [[./thread#^c31613180|jryb]]; Pxtl disputes substantial ozone production [[./thread#^c31616100|Pxtl]]. The comments supply no wavelength-specific installation data or ozone measurements. harvie's suggestion of silent electrostatic dust collection likewise comes without performance or exposure measurements [[./thread#^c31609901|harvie]].
- The best maintenance schedule and lifetime cost remain undetermined. jefftk cites external filter-cube testing as finding good performance at six months and declining performance at ten months [[./thread#^c31608917|jefftk]]. sampo suggests that retaining loaded filters could save replacement costs while increasing airflow resistance, electricity use and noise [[./thread#^c31609317|sampo]]. Those claims do not establish an optimal schedule across the very different dust environments described by maxk42 [[./thread#^c31608848|maxk42]] and jefftk [[./thread#^c31615642|jefftk]].
- This source unit cannot resolve the broader question of when opening windows improves or worsens personal exposure. euroclydon proposes window exhaust for demolition [[./thread#^c31611350|euroclydon]], sfteus could not use that option [[./thread#^c31687273|sfteus]], and ChoGGi reports wildfire-smell improvement from filtration without stating window conditions or outdoor particle levels [[./thread#^c31615878|ChoGGi]]. CO2 appears as a monitoring capability in mellavora's comment [[./thread#^c31608065|mellavora]], not as measured ventilation outcomes. The supplied discussion contains no NO2 measurements, cooking- or traffic-exposure comparisons, cognition findings or direct pollution-related sleep outcomes; post\_break's sleep account concerns appliance noise [[./thread#^c31622162|post\_break]].
