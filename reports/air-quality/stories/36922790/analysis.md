# [Open Source Outdoor Air Quality Monitor](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=36922790)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion covers an open-source outdoor air-quality monitor, including sensor accuracy, calibration, enclosure heating, weatherproofing, power, wireless connectivity, and possible gas-sensor additions. Participants compare it with other monitoring projects and commercial devices, ask about data access and interoperability, and offer feedback on documentation, DIY options, and recurring product submissions.
Tags: air quality monitors, open source hardware, environmental sensors, sensor accuracy, air pollution data, DIY electronics

## Relevance

Discusses outdoor and indoor air-quality monitors, including PM sensing limitations, temperature and humidity effects, and the difficulty of accurately measuring NO2 at relevant concentrations. Firsthand reports also describe using sensors to respond to wildfire smoke and indoor CO2. ## Arguments

- Monitoring can support practical ventilation controls even when users distrust absolute readings. nathan\_f77 describes using kitchen PM2.5 spikes to trigger a range hood and bedroom/office CO2 readings to trigger bathroom fans, treating inexpensive sensors primarily as indicators of changing conditions [[./thread#^c36927226|nathan\_f77]]. This demonstrates automation, without establishing how much exposure the fans reduce.
- Two identical PM sensors can serve maintenance purposes beyond measurement accuracy. AirGradient founder ahaucnx says divergent readings can flag failure, while alternating operation can extend sensor life [[./thread#^c36924363|ahaucnx]]. cyberax proposes a 40\%/60\% duty split to reduce the chance of similar accumulated operating time causing simultaneous failures; this is a suggestion, not a tested result [[./thread#^c36924455|cyberax]].
- ahaucnx argues that component quality and enclosure design justify paying more: the monitor uses a SenseAir S8 NDIR CO2 sensor and Sensirion modules, while the larger indoor PRO enclosure separates temperature sensing from heat-producing components [[./thread#^c36924178|ahaucnx]] [[./thread#^c36923820|ahaucnx]]. These are specific engineering explanations, although the comments do not supply comparative accuracy results.
- Open firmware and accessible data can make monitoring useful for independent analysis and home automation. ahaucnx says users can export CSV through the company's platform or redirect firmware reporting to another server [[./thread#^c36924247|ahaucnx]]. ted\_dunning reports actually redirecting several household monitors into personal data systems [[./thread#^c36926685|ted\_dunning]].
- Denser geographic coverage is presented as a practical improvement in environmental monitoring. epaulson reports an ongoing deployment of 68 sensors, one per census tract, in an unnamed city [[./thread#^c36924972|epaulson]]. PeterStuer describes Sensorcommunity as having more than 10,000 active sites and being popular in Europe [[./thread#^c36929326|PeterStuer]]. Neither comment supplies pollutant readings or demonstrates how accurately these networks represent individual exposure.

## Counterarguments

- zerof1l challenges the accuracy rationale for duplicate sensors, arguing that two identical units offer little improvement and that a different sensor capable of directly measuring PM10 would be more useful. They also claim these sensors commonly measure the smallest particle class and statistically derive larger classes [[./thread#^c36924292|zerof1l]]. ahaucnx's reply addresses failure detection and longevity, leaving that particle-sizing criticism unanswered [[./thread#^c36924363|ahaucnx]].
- Detecting disagreement between two sensors does not automatically identify the faulty one, as codetrotter points out [[./thread#^c36926019|codetrotter]]. ted\_dunning replies that common failures are distinguishable in practice—for example, one sensor becomes stuck at a low value while the other varies normally—but explicitly limits this reasoning to realistic failure modes rather than every possible case [[./thread#^c36926710|ted\_dunning]].
- Open source is not itself evidence of measurement accuracy. Brosper urges checking measurement methods and multiple sources [[./thread#^c36924144|Brosper]]. ahaucnx agrees and cites comparisons against reference instruments, but the supplied comment contains a research link rather than the underlying methods or results [[./thread#^c36924214|ahaucnx]].
- The price premium remains contested. CommanderData points to advertised four-function monitors at roughly half the price and cheaper products with additional features [[./thread#^c36924049|CommanderData]]. Conversely, aequitas says AirGradient was the cheapest option they found supporting MQTT or a custom HTTP endpoint [[./thread#^c36928552|aequitas]]. These comparisons value different capabilities; neither establishes equivalent sensor accuracy.
- Practical openness also depends on discoverable documentation and manageable setup effort. frognumber says missing links, unclear specifications and unwillingness to pay for a service deter purchase [[./thread#^c36924930|frognumber]]. In reply, ahaucnx acknowledges missing links in the new copy and supplies build instructions and hardware documentation, partially addressing that complaint [[./thread#^c36926291|ahaucnx]].
- Wireless connectivity can undermine monitoring usability. KirillPanov reports substantial frustration with PurpleAir WiFi and advocates wired data [[./thread#^c36926518|KirillPanov]]. playa1 proposes Power over Ethernet but asks whether USB data would require a host computer [[./thread#^c36926605|playa1]]. The exchange identifies deployment tradeoffs without demonstrating a working wired alternative.

## Assumptions

- Inferring that the finished monitor outperforms cheaper alternatives requires assuming that component selection and claimed reference testing translate into better deployed performance. Those supporting claims come from founder ahaucnx, and no head-to-head results are reproduced in this July 2023 source unit [[./thread#^c36924178|ahaucnx]] [[./thread#^c36924214|ahaucnx]].
- Using an uncertain sensor as a binary trigger assumes that its relative changes reliably correspond to relevant pollution events. nathan\_f77 explicitly doubts the accuracy of the TuYa sensors but considers them sufficient for fan automation; the account does not validate their thresholds or false-alarm behavior [[./thread#^c36927226|nathan\_f77]].
- declan\_roberts's description of CO2 levels as healthy assumes a trustworthy reading and an appropriate interpretation, neither of which is documented with concentrations or thresholds. Their report of CO2 settling in a living room describes an observed pattern and personal interpretation, without establishing physical stratification or overall indoor air safety [[./thread#^c36925067|declan\_roberts]].
- Treating exhaust-fan activation as an exposure improvement assumes suitable replacement air. nathan\_f77's automation operates in a New Zealand location they describe as having good outdoor air, while their earlier Chiang Mai experience involved severe seasonal burning [[./thread#^c36927226|nathan\_f77]]. Applying the same strategy during outdoor smoke would require evidence absent from this account.

## Conditions

- Reliable gas sensing may impose a substantially higher budget than basic monitoring. KirillPanov says they have not found a sensor below \$500 that reliably measures near what they describe as the EPA's 100 ppb NO2 limit, and that options needing less frequent recalibration cost more [[./thread#^c36926547|KirillPanov]]. This is a reported purchasing limitation; the comment supplies neither a market survey nor the limit's averaging period.
- Air quality is part of a location and affordability tradeoff for nathan\_f77: Chiang Mai's burning seasons were sufficiently unpleasant that they would not live there again, while perceived clean air makes leaving New Zealand difficult despite housing costs and low salaries. Sydney and Melbourne appear as hypothetical wildfire-prone destinations where they would upgrade sensors, not as places where they report firsthand wildfire measurements [[./thread#^c36927226|nathan\_f77]].
- Monitoring coverage and procurement vary geographically. yosito wants a monitor for a poorly monitored area of Thailand and asks about \$20 shipping [[./thread#^c36928744|yosito]]; ahaucnx confirms and says the company is based in Thailand [[./thread#^c36929271|ahaucnx]]. For European purchasing, petemir asks about customs charges [[./thread#^c36928945|petemir]], and ahaucnx describes Switzerland as relatively strict, without guaranteeing any particular charge [[./thread#^c36929276|ahaucnx]].
- Unpowered locations constrain outdoor deployment. dano requests battery-backed solar power and scheduled WiFi or LoRa [[./thread#^c36925804|dano]], while weinzierl asks for a battery-powered LoRaWAN version [[./thread#^c36928046|weinzierl]]. T3OU-736 is considering independent power for a future camping van installation; this remains a plan rather than an operating setup [[./thread#^c36926688|T3OU-736]].
- Coastal salt and winter road salt raise unanswered durability questions. xnzakg asks how exposed USB connections and circuit boards withstand these environments [[./thread#^c36924050|xnzakg]]. ahaucnx says the device was then unsealed and conformal coating was under investigation for later batches, so the exchange does not establish long-term outdoor corrosion resistance [[./thread#^c36924158|ahaucnx]].
- Local economic interests may affect monitoring placement and operator safety. KirillPanov recounts a nearby paper-mill-dependent town allegedly pressuring a PurpleAir operator's landlord to cancel their lease, then installing monitors inside filtered, air-conditioned public buildings [[./thread#^c36926518|KirillPanov]]. This is an uncorroborated nearby-community account, not a reported personal exposure measurement; indoor readings would describe different conditions from the displaced outdoor station.

## Lived experiences

- nathan\_f77 reports TuYa TS0601 sensors in every room, kitchen PM2.5-triggered range-hood operation, workshop extraction, and CO2-triggered bathroom fans near bedrooms and offices [[./thread#^c36927226|nathan\_f77]]. They also describe an earlier closed-source Chiang Mai monitor that could not feed Home Assistant. No pollutant concentrations, post-ventilation reductions, stove fuel type or sleep outcomes are supplied.
- declan\_roberts has used an open-source stop-light monitor to identify living-room CO2 patterns. Flaky ESP-board networking could be disabled while retaining the local colored display, and perceived healthy daytime readings provided reassurance about living in a new sealed home [[./thread#^c36925067|declan\_roberts]]. The reported benefit is reassurance, without a quantified health or performance outcome.
- kingsloi operates a community monitoring application in Gary, Indiana, using a Sensit Technologies RAMP monitor and a PurpleAir II, and reports approximately four years of data at 15-minute intervals [[./thread#^c36923746|kingsloi]]. This is a substantial firsthand collection history, but the excerpt contains no actual concentration series, comparisons or calibration results.
- Temperature and humidity disagreements are more numerically concrete than the pollutant accounts. cyberax reports sensors off by about 10\% humidity and 2–3°C, with multiple purchases failing to establish which reading was correct [[./thread#^c36924409|cyberax]]. Havoc reports a five-degree difference after gluing a temperature sensor behind an ESP32 [[./thread#^c36928990|Havoc]], and RobotToaster reports the same heating problem in a Xiaomi device [[./thread#^c36925247|RobotToaster]]. In the parent exchange, ahaucnx attributes such errors to enclosure heating and describes months spent improving airflow [[./thread#^c36924582|ahaucnx]].
- trailbits reports outdoor sensors becoming contaminated with spiderweb fragments. Divergence between two sensors exposed a problem that could otherwise resemble dirty air, and vacuum cleaning restored agreement [[./thread#^c36925759|trailbits]]. This directly supports a maintenance benefit of redundancy, while restored agreement alone does not prove absolute calibration.
- Hardware failures occur independently of pollution changes. jhot reports a failed Plantower sensor and an unreliable power supply in a PurpleAir [[./thread#^c36923963|jhot]]. iampivot reports that a recently assembled AirGradient PRO worked well but its supplied USB-C cable did not [[./thread#^c36932092|iampivot]]; ahaucnx requests support contact, with no diagnosis or resolution supplied in the thread [[./thread#^c36932695|ahaucnx]].
- ted\_dunning reports successfully building a custom dashboard quickly, querying an archive with DuckDB, and keeping historical readings locally in Parquet format on a Raspberry Pi 4 [[./thread#^c36926755|ted\_dunning]] [[./thread#^c36926803|ted\_dunning]]. This substantiates accessible data ownership and analysis for that user, without showing that every purchaser would find the setup equally easy.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The central health questions remain largely untested in this unit. nathan\_f77 describes unpleasant seasonal burning and sensor-driven ventilation [[./thread#^c36927226|nathan\_f77]], while declan\_roberts reports reassurance from CO2 monitoring [[./thread#^c36925067|declan\_roberts]]. Neither supplies numerical PM2.5 or CO2 observations, objective health outcomes, cognition tests or sleep measurements. No health-study results are reproduced, so these accounts cannot establish causal benefits or population-wide effects.
- The available NO2 capability is unclear. ahaucnx advertises an optional indoor TVOC module that can measure chemicals and NOx [[./thread#^c36923652|ahaucnx]], but separately describes dedicated electrochemical NO/NO2 work as early laboratory testing, months from market [[./thread#^c36923789|ahaucnx]]. KirillPanov's question about reliable measurement near 100 ppb receives no supplied answer [[./thread#^c36926547|KirillPanov]]. declan\_roberts's interest arises from gas-stove news, not measured stove emissions; this unit also supplies no traffic-related NO2 measurements [[./thread#^c36925084|declan\_roberts]].
- No supplied account directly tests opening windows, compares ventilation with filtration, or quantifies exposure before and after either intervention. nathan\_f77's clean-outdoor-air New Zealand setting and severe Chiang Mai burning experience illustrate why location and episode matter, but do not establish when window opening helps or worsens exposure [[./thread#^c36927226|nathan\_f77]]. KirillPanov's report of filtered indoor monitoring sites likewise contains no filtration-performance measurements [[./thread#^c36926518|KirillPanov]].
- The duplicate-sensor dispute remains only partly resolved: zerof1l questions accuracy gains and independently measured particle-size information [[./thread#^c36924292|zerof1l]], while ahaucnx argues for fault detection and longer service life [[./thread#^c36924363|ahaucnx]]. Those are different evaluation criteria. No supplied reference comparison, particle-sizing test or lifetime study settles their relative practical value.
- Public availability and interoperability remain unanswered. PeterStuer asks whether AirGradient interoperates with Sensorcommunity [[./thread#^c36929326|PeterStuer]], legulere asks whether it can push data there [[./thread#^c36929298|legulere]], and toomuchtodo asks how that network compares with OpenAQ [[./thread#^c36935839|toomuchtodo]]. mcny asks whether the grant-funded city readings will be public near real time and shared with the EPA; their impression that AirNow only covers airports is not verified in the supplied discussion [[./thread#^c36926132|mcny]].
- Radon introduces a separate disagreement about continuous monitoring. cyberax questions its value because useful readings require long accumulation, later clarifying the low activity involved [[./thread#^c36924393|cyberax]] [[./thread#^c36926338|cyberax]]. simondotau replies that continuous monitoring seems well suited to measuring for a long time [[./thread#^c36926057|simondotau]]. The exchange does not resolve the product-use question, and ahaucnx identifies affordable off-the-shelf radon modules as an additional obstacle [[./thread#^c36923740|ahaucnx]].
