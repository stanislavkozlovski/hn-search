# [Ask HN: Can you help me build a 0 profit venture?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=15345031)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion develops a proposal to distribute low-cost box-fan and furnace-filter air cleaners to lower-income households, with transparent costs and testing. Participants recommend crowdfunding and nonprofit or manufacturing contacts, question distribution logistics, operating costs, filtration performance, and claims about asthma, and suggest cheaper bulk sourcing and publishing build instructions. The exchange clarifies that the project seeks practical support and a cost-neutral public awareness effort.
Tags: air filtration, DIY, public health, crowdfunding, nonprofit distribution

## Relevance

The proposal and replies discuss a low-cost box-fan and furnace-filter setup to reduce indoor particles, with firsthand claims about odor and dust alongside questions about PM2.5 effectiveness, testing, electricity use, and health evidence. ## Arguments

- classicsnoot [[./thread#^c15345888|classicsnoot]] argues that smoking, pet dander and particles aggravate existing asthma, explicitly clarifying that they did not mean these exposures cause asthma. Their broader claim of other bodily effects is unspecified and unsupported by health measurements in this discussion.
- classicsnoot [[./thread#^c15345036|classicsnoot]] presents inexpensive fans and filters as a partial household improvement and a way to spread awareness of affordable air cleaning. The proposal does not claim to eliminate regional pollution or establish equivalence with commercial purifiers.
- jklein11 [[./thread#^c15349498|jklein11]] suggests supplying filters for existing fans or air conditioners, potentially avoiding the purchase of complete units. segmondy [[./thread#^c15362138|segmondy]] proposes freely available instructions and videos. These are practical distribution suggestions, with no reported exposure outcomes.

## Counterarguments

- notahacker [[./thread#^c15353233|notahacker]] questions the central technical premise: does this particular device capture PM2.5, how much air does it filter, and how does it compare with other inexpensive filters? The comment raises unanswered questions rather than demonstrating that the device fails.
- notahacker [[./thread#^c15353233|notahacker]] warns that electricity costs could discourage recipients from operating even a donated \$30 device. They also consider identifying recipients and distributing equipment potentially harder than manufacturing, suggesting charities could help with delivery and funding.
- cjbprime [[./thread#^c15345080|cjbprime]] challenges implied claims about asthma causation, invoking the hygiene hypothesis and opposing historical trends in smoking and asthma. Their suggestion that smoking might protect against asthma is explicitly speculative and has no supporting study results here. classicsnoot [[./thread#^c15345888|classicsnoot]] responds by clarifying exacerbation rather than causation; that distinction resolves the stated misunderstanding.

## Assumptions

- The opening story invokes someone else’s reported testing of a barrel-fan/HEPA design while describing the author’s different box-fan/furnace-filter build as untested. Transferring that reported performance to the new device remains an assumption: classicsnoot [[./thread#^c15345036|classicsnoot]] still requests equipment and software for accurate, repeatable testing.
- The proposed benefit assumes recipients can operate the device sufficiently and sustain its running costs. notahacker [[./thread#^c15353233|notahacker]] directly questions electricity affordability; the discussion supplies no operating schedules, room-size comparisons or evidence of sustained household use.

## Conditions

- classicsnoot [[./thread#^c15345036|classicsnoot]] gives 2017 estimates of \$15–30 for a box fan and \$7–15 for a filter, with shipping materially affecting distribution costs. They propose bulk purchasing or donations, but the worked shipping calculation does not establish an unambiguous delivered price.
- debacle [[./thread#^c15348219|debacle]] reports seeing Alibaba bulk prices around \$12 per fan and \$6 per filter. dpeck [[./thread#^c15348357|dpeck]] argues US retail purchasing would be uneconomic and predicts single-digit-dollar FOB pricing through a manufacturer agreement with a suitable minimum order. These estimates do not establish delivered costs, running costs or equivalent filtration performance.
- classicsnoot [[./thread#^c15345032|classicsnoot]] targets low- or no-income households and describes proximity to factories, highways and landfills, alongside regional pollution and indoor sources. No locations or exposure concentrations are specified. Their limited personal funding and intended net-zero finances, reiterated by classicsnoot [[./thread#^c15345102|classicsnoot]], make outside support a condition of the proposed distribution effort.

## Lived experiences

- In the opening story, classicsnoot reports that their homemade device nearly eliminates litter-box odor, collects hair and particles, and makes spaces feel fresher, while explicitly acknowledging they have not tested it. In the follow-up, classicsnoot [[./thread#^c15345036|classicsnoot]] requests repeatable testing. This is a firsthand comfort and cleanliness report, without quantified PM2.5 removal or demonstrated health improvement.
- classicsnoot [[./thread#^c15345032|classicsnoot]] recounts encountering children with untreated conditions, especially asthma, during work travel through low-income communities. They report indoor smoking often but not always, frequent pet ownership and nearby pollution sources. These informal observations neither establish population prevalence nor isolate which exposures contributed to individual illnesses.

## Unresolved disagreements

- PM2.5 effectiveness remains unanswered. notahacker [[./thread#^c15353233|notahacker]] asks about particle capture and filtered-air volume, while classicsnoot [[./thread#^c15345036|classicsnoot]] seeks testing resources. No measured concentrations, before-and-after results, sensor models, calibration information or comparative performance data are supplied, so sensor reliability and filtration effectiveness cannot be assessed.
- jklein11 [[./thread#^c15349498|jklein11]] asks for evidence of asthma prevention; classicsnoot [[./thread#^c15345888|classicsnoot]], in a separate reply branch, limits the intended claim to aggravating existing conditions. Neither prevention nor symptom improvement is demonstrated here. There are also no reports of measured effects on cognition or sleep.
- The highway and regional-pollution references from classicsnoot [[./thread#^c15345032|classicsnoot]] identify possible exposure contexts without measuring traffic pollution. The filtration questions from notahacker [[./thread#^c15353233|notahacker]] provide no evidence about NO2 or CO2 removal. This unit contains no ventilation, cooking, wildfire-smoke or window-opening comparison and cannot determine when opening windows would help or worsen exposure.
- Implementation and household benefit remain prospective. 97-109-107 [[./thread#^c15347079|97-109-107]] says the requested assistance is unclear, and classicsnoot [[./thread#^c15345102|classicsnoot]] describes an initiative still seeking support. No follow-up establishes distribution, sustained use, operating affordability or recipient health outcomes.
