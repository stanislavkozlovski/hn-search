# [Ultra-fine particles emitted by commercial desktop 3D printers \[pdf\]](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=11006739)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants discuss research reporting ultrafine particle emissions from desktop 3D printers, especially differences among printing materials and the implications for ventilation and exposure. They debate how much confidence to place in preliminary findings, how enclosure and filtration may affect emissions, and whether PLA is safer than ABS. Suggested mitigations include exhaust ventilation, fume hoods, and air filters, with questions about particle sizes, filter standards, and disposal of captured material. The discussion also branches into laser printers, resin printing, workplace safety, and concerns about possible regulation of hobbyist printers.
Tags: 3D printing, ultrafine particles, indoor air quality, ventilation, PLA, ABS, air filtration, workplace safety

## Relevance

The discussion concerns ultrafine particle and VOC emissions from desktop 3D printers, possible health concerns, and practical controls such as ventilation, enclosures, and filtration. It includes reported testing and firsthand purifier observations, while acknowledging uncertainty about exposure and filter performance. ## Arguments

- Reducing emissions at the source and improving ventilation are recurring proposals. msds says PLA or TPU greatly reduced particulate in their testing and interprets adequate ventilation as making even ABS printing acceptable [[./thread#^c11007947|msds]]. That safety judgment is broader than the numerical evidence supplied in the comment.
- Several commenters favor capturing emissions near their source. samcheng recommends a fume hood for printing and soldering [[./thread#^c11006903|samcheng]]; Animats proposes maintaining negative pressure inside the build chamber and filtering its exhaust [[./thread#^c11007467|Animats]]. eru extends the extraction approach to cooking [[./thread#^c11008308|eru]]. These are recommendations, not measured installations reported by those commenters.
- samcheng argues that HEPA filtration can help despite concerns about ultrafine particles, quoting a vendor demonstration in which a printer generated over 190,000 particles and the filter outlet registered zero [[./thread#^c11007062|samcheng]]. This is a quoted equipment demonstration, not an independently documented room-exposure measurement.
- jrockway credits IQAir's substantial prefilter and better sealing with cleaner exhaust and longer useful filter life than other purifiers they owned [[./thread#^c11008198|jrockway]]. Their observation that competing machines still reduced room particle counts supports filtration generally; their suspicion that accumulated dust had ruined competing HEPA filters is an inference.
- jacobolus quotes the article comparing PLA printing emissions with an electric frying pan and ABS emissions with low-power grilling on gas or electric stoves [[./thread#^c11007550|jacobolus]]. This introduces cooking as a relevant indoor particle source, but supplies neither personal cooking measurements nor evidence about NO2.

## Counterarguments

- Comparable emission quantities do not establish comparable health risks. Responding to jacobolus's cooking analogy, NegativeK points to different particle types and potentially much longer printer operating times, using eight hours of printing as an example rather than a measured schedule [[./thread#^c11007649|NegativeK]].
- Air exchange within a building may distribute pollution rather than remove it. Responding to castratikron's printer room without a ceiling, kzrdude warns that particles could spread throughout the building and recommends a nearby ventilation outlet [[./thread#^c11007802|kzrdude]]. nkozyra likewise disputes that the open arrangement solves ultrafine-particle exposure [[./thread#^c11007153|nkozyra]].
- An ordinary enclosure is not demonstrated to provide strong protection. jeffdubin quotes a study reporting approximately 35\% lower median UFP emissions for an enclosed MakerBot–ABS configuration, with the difference within estimated uncertainty and visible gaps in the enclosure [[./thread#^c11006864|jeffdubin]]. Although jeffdubin introduces this as a VOC finding, the quoted numerical result concerns UFP emissions.
- PLA's relative safety remains contested. Palomides describes PLA emissions as generally considered safer [[./thread#^c11006909|Palomides]], while amenghra distinguishes ambient-temperature material safety from uncertain exposure to heated PLA emissions [[./thread#^c11007681|amenghra]]. Palomides replies that plastics commonly undergo heating and emitted particles cool before inhalation [[./thread#^c11008123|Palomides]]; that response supplies no inhalation-toxicology evidence.
- userbinator objects that a purifier advertised at 0.3 µm does not address the article's particles below 0.1 µm [[./thread#^c11006967|userbinator]]. samcheng acknowledges the size mismatch but maintains that HEPA can help, citing the vendor demonstration [[./thread#^c11007062|samcheng]]. The supplied discussion does not establish either a hard filtration cutoff or size-resolved performance for these devices.
- Water filtration creates a disposal tradeoff. ryandonner argues that capturing particles in water merely transfers the pollution [[./thread#^c11007628|ryandonner]]. a\_puppy counters that microbeads, described as 10 µm to 1 mm, differ substantially from UFPs below 100 nm, weakening that particular analogy without establishing harmless disposal [[./thread#^c11007769|a\_puppy]]. Animats adds that even supposedly biodegradable ABS may not degrade substantially in water [[./thread#^c11007738|Animats]].
- Premium-brand superiority is challenged. nextos reports reading a review in which cheaper Korean purifiers performed comparably to IQAir and BlueAir [[./thread#^c11008101|nextos]]. By contrast, memonkey claims roughly 98\% room-particle reduction and exceptional performance relative to competitors [[./thread#^c11008557|memonkey]], having disclosed employment at IQAir [[./thread#^c11007101|memonkey]]. Neither comment supplies an independently assessable comparative dataset.

## Assumptions

- This source unit primarily concerns printer-related ultrafine and submicron particles. msds describes examining particles from 10–500 nm [[./thread#^c11007947|msds]], while jrockway reports submicron particle-counter readings [[./thread#^c11008198|jrockway]]. These are not supplied PM2.5 mass-concentration measurements; the unit contains no NO2 or CO2 readings, cognitive testing, or objective sleep measurements.
- The meaning of adequate ventilation is unspecified. msds's conclusion that users are 'fine' does not include room volume, outdoor-air exchange, operating duration, exposure thresholds, or health endpoints [[./thread#^c11007947|msds]]. Their survey also considered HCN, but no HCN results are provided, so the comment cannot establish gas-exposure safety.
- A displayed zero does not establish particle-free breathing air. jrockway identifies a laser counter with submicron capability but gives no model, lower detection limit, calibration, or sampling protocol [[./thread#^c11008198|jrockway]]. samcheng's quoted vendor outlet test lacks comparable methodological detail [[./thread#^c11007062|samcheng]]. Neither establishes detection across the full ultrafine range, and an outlet reading is not a whole-room exposure measurement.
- Advertised filtration specifications and sensory impressions require separate treatment. memonkey's IQAir '0.003' claim is a product claim accompanied by an employment disclosure [[./thread#^c11007101|memonkey]]. patcheudor's objection to ABS odor [[./thread#^c11009139|patcheudor]] and jrockway's symptom changes [[./thread#^c11008198|jrockway]] do not themselves identify pollutants or establish their concentrations; no chemical or humidity measurements explain those experiences.

## Conditions

- Price affects the practical choice between equipment and extraction. samcheng describes the premium purifier as approximately \$1,000 and suggests installing proper exhaust ventilation before buying it for a printer-filled room [[./thread#^c11007120|samcheng]]. This is a price reference from the 2016 discussion, not a current quotation. nextos also raises purchase price and motor efficiency when comparing cheaper alternatives [[./thread#^c11008101|nextos]].
- Maintenance convenience can come with substantial recurring expense. jrockway says IQAir requires little intervention beyond replacement filters but explicitly advises against buying it to save money [[./thread#^c11008198|jrockway]]. Their later wish to buy a house and install a central system reflects a proposed way to move noise out of the bedroom, not an installation they had completed [[./thread#^c11009931|jrockway]].
- Access to surplus equipment can change affordability dramatically. patcheudor's repurposed cleanroom cabinet and filter originally cost about ten times their MakerBot printer, but they acquired the equipment cheaply at an electronics surplus store [[./thread#^c11009139|patcheudor]]. That favorable purchase does not establish an affordable general solution.
- Animats's water-filter proposal depends on low airflow making blower demand manageable; they acknowledge substantial blower power and repeated replacement of dirty water [[./thread#^c11007467|Animats]]. No operating costs, capture measurements, or disposal costs are supplied.
- Local exhaust restrictions may constrain options. memonkey quotes a clinical-laboratory case in which unnamed city officials prohibited outdoor discharge, prompting filtration instead [[./thread#^c11008499|memonkey]]. driverdan questions whether the account is promotional material [[./thread#^c11009604|driverdan]]. This is a secondhand, jurisdiction-specific example, not a general restriction on household ventilation.

## Lived experiences

- msds reports participating in an independent survey of five consumer machines using multiple materials, examining 10–500 nm particulate and HCN [[./thread#^c11007947|msds]]. In response to pen2l's equipment question, msds identifies Flashforge Creator Pros and Ultimakers [[./thread#^c11008004|msds]]. The underlying master's thesis was unreleased, and the comments supply no numerical exposure results or instrumentation details.
- jrockway reports that their first bedroom purifier reduced nighttime coughing; after switching to IQAir, chronic post-nasal drip appeared to disappear, accompanied by a dry, sore throat the next morning [[./thread#^c11008198|jrockway]]. They acknowledge possible imagination. These are personal symptom and comfort observations, not controlled evidence of improved sleep or a demonstrated mechanism.
- jrockway reports a bedroom laser-counter reading of zero, while also observing large particle increases when moving around in bed and rapid subsequent removal with the purifier running strongly nearby [[./thread#^c11008198|jrockway]]. Their own caveat limits any interpretation of the zero reading as continuous absence of exposure.
- Noise constrained usable airflow for jrockway: they ran the bedroom purifier high with earplugs and judged quiet settings ineffective, without presenting comparative measurements [[./thread#^c11008198|jrockway]]. A RabbitAir unit's high-frequency sound prevented maximum-speed use even with earplugs [[./thread#^c11009931|jrockway]]. memonkey separately reports running IQAir overnight and finding its fan-like sound less objectionable than other purifiers' hum, while also being an employee [[./thread#^c11008557|memonkey]].
- patcheudor disliked ABS odor but found PLA acceptable and installed their printer in a repurposed laminar-flow cabinet, reversing airflow to pull fumes through a filter specified at 0.1 µm [[./thread#^c11009139|patcheudor]]. This is an implemented intervention, but they provide no before-and-after particle, gas, or health measurements.
- castratikron describes a workplace room containing multiple printers and lacking a ceiling, expecting air exchange with the rest of the building to help [[./thread#^c11006867|castratikron]]. hueving interprets this as an opening above internal ceilings while remaining beneath the roof [[./thread#^c11010278|hueving]]. Neither supplies evidence of outdoor-air replacement or measured exposure reduction.
- dghughes describes their father's terminal IPF following decades of occupational exposure to sawdust, welding fumes, metal grinding, paint, and possibly asbestos, attributing the illness to accumulated damage [[./thread#^c11006997|dghughes]]. This is a family account of mixed occupational exposures, not a demonstrated causal finding or evidence specifically linking desktop printers to IPF.

## Unresolved disagreements

- HEPA labeling remains unresolved after a partial correction. memonkey initially claims there is no meaningful industry or federal minimum [[./thread#^c11008515|memonkey]]; jadence responds with examples of specifications [[./thread#^c11021290|jadence]]. memonkey then narrows the concern to modified HEPA labels and the applicability of DOE specifications [[./thread#^c11067128|memonkey]]. jadence's final question—whether unqualified HEPA labeling guarantees 99.97\% removal at 0.3 µm—receives no supplied answer [[./thread#^c11078863|jadence]].
- The effectiveness and usability of complete containment remain untested here. drostie proposes a clear plastic bag but worries about overheating [[./thread#^c11006810|drostie]]; aylons notes that existing printer enclosures help stabilize temperature [[./thread#^c11006817|aylons]]. snarfy asserts that airtightness makes a major difference [[./thread#^c11006913|snarfy]], but no sealed-enclosure exposure results or thermal assessment establish a workable solution.
- Particle release when opening an enclosure is disputed. dghughes imagines trapped particles becoming airborne like visible dust [[./thread#^c11006997|dghughes]], while YZF argues that very small particles adhere strongly and are difficult to resuspend [[./thread#^c11007038|YZF]]. The comments provide no measurements resolving release during opening, handling, or cleaning.
- Alternative technologies and control combinations lack exposure evidence. Sephr asks whether resin-based DLP printing is safer because it lacks a nozzle [[./thread#^c11008623|Sephr]], and justifier requests testing of photolithographic printing [[./thread#^c11012759|justifier]]. tonyarkles reports sticky resin and mucous-membrane irritation associated with handling, which does not establish airborne risk [[./thread#^c11009071|tonyarkles]]. FrankenPC's proposed enclosure with HEPA, carbon, and a fan, and their question about ionic filtration, likewise have no supplied performance results [[./thread#^c11008949|FrankenPC]].
- Claims about other electronics remain unsupported or contested. ausjke says laser printers have similar pollution issues without measurements [[./thread#^c11006932|ausjke]]. ekianjo warns about hazardous dust emitted by computers and monitors [[./thread#^c11006819|ekianjo]], whereas hackbinary argues that most accumulated computer dust originates in the surrounding environment, while allowing trace equipment contaminants [[./thread#^c11006998|hackbinary]]. Neither provides source-attribution measurements.
- The discussion cannot resolve when opening windows improves or worsens exposure. samcheng invokes Beijing residents' purifier use without supplying outdoor measurements [[./thread#^c11006903|samcheng]], and castratikron describes exchange with other indoor spaces rather than a window experiment [[./thread#^c11006867|castratikron]]. No supplied account compares indoor and outdoor concentrations, traffic pollution, or wildfire smoke, so the printer-ventilation advice cannot determine those tradeoffs.
