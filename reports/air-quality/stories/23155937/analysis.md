# [Rising CO2 levels may trigger cognitive impairment, says new study](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=23155937)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants debate claims that rising CO₂ may impair cognition, weighing studies reporting effects at indoor concentrations against research and operational experience involving submariners and other selected populations. The exchange focuses on study design, sample selection, exposure duration, oxygen displacement, and whether CO₂ itself or broader poor ventilation explains reported effects. Personal experiences with stuffy rooms and CO₂ monitors prompt discussion of sensor accuracy, calibration, privacy, and practical ventilation. Participants also consider fresh-air systems, filtration, plants, outdoor pollution, and the difference between rising global CO₂ and elevated indoor concentrations.
Tags: CO₂, cognition, indoor air, ventilation, research evidence, air-quality sensors

## Arguments

- KingMachiavelli [[./thread#^c23157267|KingMachiavelli]] quotes the article's interpretation of Satish et al. and Allen et al. as evidence that CO2 directly impairs cognition, beyond serving as a ventilation proxy. wh-uws [[./thread#^c23156286|wh-uws]] recommends monitoring indoor air and asserts a substantial cognitive benefit from improving it. These are claims about research and benefits; neither comment supplies a controlled personal experiment.
- bpodgursky [[./thread#^c23157110|bpodgursky]] argues that indoor ventilation deserves more immediate attention than a small change in outdoor background CO2, contrasting an outdoor increase from 350 to 400 ppm with claimed concentrations above 2,000 ppm in poorly ventilated schools. Those figures illustrate the commenter's prioritization argument, rather than measurements documented in that comment.
- inamberclad [[./thread#^c23156310|inamberclad]] describes ISS CO2 partial pressures of approximately 4 torr versus 0.3 torr at sea level and associates that environment with headaches, fatigue, and possible checklist errors. This is a secondhand claim about an unusual, chronically elevated exposure environment, not a firsthand residential observation.
- mleonhard [[./thread#^c23162269|mleonhard]] proposes an energy-recovering ventilator with a suitable filter as a way to bring in outdoor air while addressing other pollutants. pas [[./thread#^c23160853|pas]] considers bringing in and, where necessary, cleaning outdoor air more economical than household CO2 scrubbing or continually replacing oxygen tanks. Neither supplies installation results or a cost comparison.

## Counterarguments

- sarchertech [[./thread#^c23157422|sarchertech]] reports that a submarine study found no decision-making impairment at 15,000 ppm. learc83 [[./thread#^c23158721|learc83]] argues that this finding, together with submariners reportedly working around 5,000 ppm, makes claims of severe impairment around 1,500 ppm doubtful. These comments challenge the proposed magnitude of cognitive effects; they do not establish that every health outcome or population is unaffected.
- swsieber [[./thread#^c23157658|swsieber]] emphasizes that the reported failure to replicate concerned a submariner population. dmoy [[./thread#^c23158253|dmoy]] points to fitness and psychological screening, while maxbond [[./thread#^c23160532|maxbond]] stresses limits on generalization. Conversely, tree3 [[./thread#^c23160062|tree3]] objects to dismissing evidence simply because its sample is imperfectly representative. The disagreement concerns how much weight to give the result, not whether the population was randomly sampled.
- learc83 [[./thread#^c23161403|learc83]] adds three cited studies, describing null results from isolated CO2 exposure, including at 5,000 ppm, and argues that other features of poor ventilation may explain reported cognitive effects. Following further discussion, mleonhard [[./thread#^c23270807|mleonhard]] explicitly accepts that interpretation. That change of mind matters, but does not resolve every participant's objection or independently verify the papers.
- graeme [[./thread#^c23157529|graeme]] questions whether the headline actually describes new experimental evidence of impairment. pas [[./thread#^c23160961|pas]] characterizes the paper as model fitting or deduction from existing data rather than a trial. On the supplied discussion alone, the headline should not be treated as a newly demonstrated exposure-response relationship.
- jack12 [[./thread#^c23157325|jack12]] warns that many advertised CO2 monitors actually estimate equivalent CO2 from VOC measurements; they do not measure environmental CO2 directly. The commenter identifies the Awair 2 as apparently using an NDIR sensor, based on a linked teardown. Separately, xoa [[./thread#^c23157087|xoa]] cites EPA evaluations reporting poor agreement between some consumer air sensors and reference instruments. Neither comment validates all devices in a product category.
- hckr\_news [[./thread#^c23159621|hckr\_news]] calls an air purifier a worthwhile purchase, but mleonhard [[./thread#^c23162087|mleonhard]] distinguishes particle filtration from CO2 removal and warns about ozone-producing purifiers. hckr\_news [[./thread#^c23172827|hckr\_news]] clarifies that their device uses HEPA filtration. The exchange supplies no measured CO2 reduction, particle reduction, or health outcome from that purifier.
- thatcat [[./thread#^c23158045|thatcat]] proposes houseplants as the easiest CO2 remedy. greenshackle2 [[./thread#^c23158568|greenshackle2]] and kolinko [[./thread#^c23163936|kolinko]] counter that substantial removal would require extensive plant growth, while the8472 [[./thread#^c23158800|the8472]] notes nighttime CO2 production. totetsu [[./thread#^c23163028|totetsu]] links a sealed-greenhouse experiment, but provides no results in the supplied comment. These exchanges do not demonstrate effective CO2 control with ordinary household plants.
- The mask evidence has narrow and mixed implications. balnaphone [[./thread#^c23156494|balnaphone]] quotes approximately 17\% inhaled oxygen and 1.2–3\% CO2 for a surgical mask worn over an N95, while also quoting no obvious short-term functional change or discomfort; a separate prolonged-N95 account reports headaches, dizziness, and fatigue. Reason077 [[./thread#^c23156644|Reason077]] emphasizes that the combined-mask setup differs from an ordinary surgical mask. empath75 [[./thread#^c23156646|empath75]] extrapolates concern to all-day masking without supplying additional measurements. These are quoted research claims, not room-air measurements or firsthand experiments.

## Assumptions

- Attributing improvement after opening a window specifically to CO2 assumes that other changes accompanying ventilation are unimportant. TheAceOfHearts [[./thread#^c23158389|TheAceOfHearts]] reports feeling and playing better after opening windows, but supplies no experiment separating CO2 from other exposures. mleonhard [[./thread#^c23270807|mleonhard]] subsequently raises exhaled VOCs, listing isoprene, acetone, ethanol, and methanol from a cited source; their presence does not establish which, if any, caused the reported symptoms.
- Oxygen depletion is a proposed mechanism, not an established explanation in this unit. AtlasBarfed [[./thread#^c23161652|AtlasBarfed]] speculates about oxygen loss and fitness; learc83 [[./thread#^c23162537|learc83]] argues that displacement at 1,000–2,000 ppm would be too small to explain the claimed effects, and Tagbert [[./thread#^c23162996|Tagbert]] invokes altitude adaptation. maxk42 [[./thread#^c23157346|maxk42]] likewise doubts displacement, while frank2 [[./thread#^c23157339|frank2]] argues that CO2 can affect blood acidity independently of oxygen availability. No supplied household experiment establishes oxygen supplementation as a remedy.
- Selection and acclimation explanations remain hypotheses. tfehring [[./thread#^c23158633|tfehring]] suggests tolerance or an already-affected baseline, and floatingatoll [[./thread#^c23158940|floatingatoll]] asks whether subjects were experienced submariners. mleonhard [[./thread#^c23162593|mleonhard]] interprets reported psychological attrition and claustrophobia screening as possible selection against CO2-sensitive people. The quoted attrition figure and off-site training anecdotes do not identify CO2 as the cause of withdrawal or discomfort.
- The operational-history rebuttal assumes that effects as large as those claimed would become conspicuous in submarine work, including among newcomers. learc83 [[./thread#^c23158804|learc83]], marchenko [[./thread#^c23160035|marchenko]], and solveit [[./thread#^c23162142|solveit]] rely on that expectation. It is a plausibility argument about severe impairment, rather than a supplied longitudinal dataset showing the absence of subtler effects.
- The usefulness of an indirect sensor depends on the question being asked. jack12 [[./thread#^c23157325|jack12]] considers VOC-derived eCO2 useful for occupancy-related air-quality control, whereas throwaway9d0291 [[./thread#^c23164977|throwaway9d0291]] calls such readings useless for determining actual CO2. Their common ground is that eCO2 is not a direct CO2 measurement; their assessments differ over its practical value as a proxy.
- Broader cognitive attribution is especially speculative. cryptica [[./thread#^c23158743|cryptica]] wonders whether CO2 explains perceived population intelligence decline, without supplying exposure or cognitive measurements. non-entity [[./thread#^c23156640|non-entity]] reports a personal sense of declining ability but explicitly acknowledges other, larger influences. Neither account establishes an environmental cause.

## Conditions

- Outdoor air can offer lower CO2 while presenting other exposure concerns. graeme [[./thread#^c23157431|graeme]] reports approximately 450 ppm outdoors in an unspecified city and recommends measuring particulates before deciding whether opening a window is sufficient. throwquestion24 [[./thread#^c23156437|throwquestion24]] raises the practical constraint of a nearby freeway. The city's reported CO2 value cannot establish particulate or NO2 conditions at another window or location.
- Opening windows has a climate-dependent heating or cooling trade-off. bufferoverflow [[./thread#^c23158551|bufferoverflow]] points to winter heat loss, and TheAceOfHearts [[./thread#^c23158606|TheAceOfHearts]] accepts that trade-off for the perceived benefit. Cerium [[./thread#^c23158828|Cerium]] makes the same choice while wanting a future fresh-air exchange unit. vzidex [[./thread#^c23158693|vzidex]] notes that some locations rarely require active heating or cooling; the affected commenters' actual climates are not established.
- The DIY prices are historical estimates from this 2020 discussion. throwaway9d0291 [[./thread#^c23164977|throwaway9d0291]] estimates about \$70 for an ESP32/SCD30 setup and roughly one hour of initial assembly, including soldering a header, versus about \$30 total with an approximately \$20 MH-Z19. realharo [[./thread#^c23156561|realharo]] separately suggests an MH-Z19B and Raspberry Pi Zero W for window-opening reminders. These are component-level proposals, not current prices or independently checked complete-system costs.
- Privacy and price shape monitoring choices. In response to zbrozek's concern about sending household data outside the home [[./thread#^c23156412|zbrozek]], detaro [[./thread#^c23156607|detaro]] identifies the offline RAD-0301, also sold as the TFA Dostmann AirCO2ntrol Mini, with an unofficial USB interface. ohm [[./thread#^c23156718|ohm]] says the IQAir AirVisual Pro works offline but is expensive. xoa [[./thread#^c23157087|xoa]] additionally values local-network operation and integration support.
- Building configuration constrains remediation. graeme [[./thread#^c23157431|graeme]] favors buildings with effective internal filtration in heavily polluted areas and considers an equivalent individual-house setup difficult. mleonhard [[./thread#^c23162269|mleonhard]] proposes a wall- or window-installed filtered ERV, with the specific constraint that it must not obstruct a fire-exit window. The discussion provides no retrofit quotes or demonstrated installations to settle feasibility.

## Lived experiences

- Arainach [[./thread#^c23156939|Arainach]] reports office CO2 usually remaining below 800 ppm with the door open and climbing rapidly within one or two hours after it closes. They use a RAD-0301 connected to a Raspberry Pi and an alarm prompting window opening. They raised the alarm from 800 to 1,200 ppm because it triggered too frequently; this is a practical preference, not a demonstrated health threshold.
- TheAceOfHearts [[./thread#^c23158389|TheAceOfHearts]] describes longstanding stuffiness relieved by fresh air and a substantial perceived health improvement after buying a CO2 meter and keeping a window slightly open. They also report more mistakes and poorer decisions in high-level Heroes of the Storm play as the meter rises, sometimes improving after a window-opening alarm. No concentration threshold, recorded game comparison, or blinded test is supplied.
- After oxygenation concerns were raised, TheAceOfHearts [[./thread#^c23172967|TheAceOfHearts]] bought a pulse oximeter and reports readings usually at 99–100\%, with a lowest reading of 97\% after prolonged N95 use, returning to 100\% after deep breaths. Crucially, all measurements occurred while room CO2 was below approximately 600 ppm. The promised closed-room comparison is absent, so these readings do not explain the earlier high-CO2-associated symptoms.
- Tade0 [[./thread#^c23156399|Tade0]] associates difficulty working remotely with insufficient fresh air and says reaching 1,000 ppm is easy in a 25-cubic-metre room. This combines a personal interpretation with a concentration claim, but provides no instrument, calibration details, time series, or quantified work-performance result.
- kevingadd [[./thread#^c23157236|kevingadd]] reports running the central HVAC fan before bed to address poor bedroom airflow, with improvement indicated by a CO2 sensor. No before-and-after concentrations, fresh-air intake configuration, or sleep measurements are given. The observation concerns that particular home's airflow and sensor response.
- downerending [[./thread#^c23156596|downerending]] reports routinely leaving windows cracked in a well-insulated home to improve exchange with outdoor air. The comment supplies a ventilation practice, without measured concentrations or a demonstrated health outcome.
- Lammy [[./thread#^c23159196|Lammy]] describes local vehicles idling for approximately fifteen minutes while waiting to merge and prefers the prospect of traffic moving along a nearby freeway. They link a Central Freeway/Octavia Boulevard before-and-after report, but give no personal pollutant measurements. This is a neighborhood traffic experience, not evidence that freeway adjacency generally improves air quality.
- throwaway9d0291 [[./thread#^c23164977|throwaway9d0291]] says they tested several sensor components and found the SCD30 best for repeatability and residential automatic calibration. They report poor MH-Z19 automatic calibration and recommend fresh-air manual calibration every few months to address drift. This is firsthand comparative testing, but without reference-instrument results, error ranges, or a documented protocol.
- learc83 [[./thread#^c23161403|learc83]] says concern initially prompted buying a CO2 monitor, but subsequent reading of the research removed that concern. The comment supplies a firsthand change in risk assessment, rather than personal exposure measurements showing the absence of symptoms.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The unit does not settle whether typical indoor CO2 directly impairs cognition, how large any effect is, or whether other accumulated pollutants explain ventilation-associated symptoms. learc83 [[./thread#^c23161403|learc83]] argues for the latter interpretation. mleonhard [[./thread#^c23173530|mleonhard]] accepts that two cited papers support the null-effect argument but notes their shared authorship and asks about independent replication; the supplied replies do not answer that replication question.
- Population selection remains contested, with an important correction in the ancestry. mleonhard [[./thread#^c23173530|mleonhard]] initially describes one cohort as prospective astronauts; learc83 [[./thread#^c23179086|learc83]] says they were astronaut-like adults, including men and women in their thirties and forties, without a CO2 exposure screening requirement or routine submarine exposure. learc83 [[./thread#^c23161403|learc83]] also describes the original Harvard sample as mostly students. These claims complicate both sides' generalization arguments without establishing effects in the broader population.
- The relevance of cognitive testing to everyday work is not fully resolved. \_y5hn [[./thread#^c23159007|\_y5hn]] asks whether the submarine evidence covers unfamiliar situations with conflicting goals rather than routine procedures. learc83 [[./thread#^c23161196|learc83]] replies that the study used the same kind of decision-making test as studies reporting impairment. That addresses test comparability as described by the commenter, but the supplied discussion lacks task-level results or a connection to the gaming experience reported by TheAceOfHearts [[./thread#^c23158389|TheAceOfHearts]].
- The net benefit of opening windows depends on unmeasured outdoor conditions. Lammy [[./thread#^c23156879|Lammy]] recommends a breeze without worrying about AQI, whereas graeme [[./thread#^c23157431|graeme]] recommends checking particulates. Likewise, jaggederest [[./thread#^c23156808|jaggederest]] says any air movement is sufficient, while kevingadd's HVAC-fan observation [[./thread#^c23157236|kevingadd]] does not establish whether the system brought in fresh air or redistributed indoor air. No paired indoor/outdoor pollutant measurements settle these practical differences.
- Coverage of the broader approved question is limited in this source unit. graeme [[./thread#^c23157431|graeme]] mentions particulates without supplying PM2.5 or NO2 measurements, and hckr\_news [[./thread#^c23172827|hckr\_news]] identifies a HEPA filter without measured filtration performance. No cooking or wildfire exposure accounts are supplied. kevingadd [[./thread#^c23157236|kevingadd]] describes a bedtime ventilation practice, but provides no measured sleep outcome; this part cannot establish benefits for sleep or compare interventions during smoke events.
