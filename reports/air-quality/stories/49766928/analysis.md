# [Replacing gas stoves with electric stoves greatly reduces asthma symptoms](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49766928)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion of replacing gas stoves with electric ones focuses on household practicality and the health case for reducing gas exposure. Participants cite wiring and equipment costs, cooking preferences, backup power, grid reliability, rental restrictions, and landlord resistance. The thread also debates whether electrification policy is justified and compares attention to gas stoves with other environmental hazards such as asbestos.
Tags: gas stoves, electric stoves, indoor air, asthma, electrification

## Relevance

The discussion connects gas-stove replacement with asthma and indoor exposure, while raising practical tradeoffs involving electrical upgrades, outages, renting, and ventilation. Most comments debate feasibility and policy rather than report measured health outcomes. ## Arguments

- proc0 [[./thread#^c49767218|proc0]] presents stove replacement and better ventilation as options residents could pursue to address cooking-related health concerns. This proposes possible improvements but supplies no ventilation measurements or evidence that the alternatives produce comparable health benefits.
- k310 [[./thread#^c49767876|k310]] expresses willingness to convert but questions buying a replacement for an appliance they do not use. Their argument concerns the incremental value of replacement given their existing cooking arrangements, rather than disputing the asthma claim.

## Counterarguments

- Responding to proc0’s assumption of individual choice, bell-cot [[./thread#^c49767612|bell-cot]] argues that renters often cannot replace appliances without landlord opposition. bell-cot [[./thread#^c49770690|bell-cot]] explicitly extends this objection to installing substantial ventilation: occupants may lack control over either intervention.
- proc0 [[./thread#^c49767218|proc0]] raises electricity reliability as a reason to retain gas cooking; bell-cot [[./thread#^c49767612|bell-cot]] agrees that many gas stoves work during blackouts. These are resilience arguments, without personal outage accounts or comparisons of resulting indoor exposure.
- proc0 [[./thread#^c49768438|proc0]] questions prioritizing gas stoves while asbestos remains in California buildings. bell-cot [[./thread#^c49770690|bell-cot]] challenges the exposure comparison, distinguishing sealed, undisturbed asbestos from material released into the air. The exchange does not establish whether stove replacement reduces asthma symptoms.

## Assumptions

- proc0 [[./thread#^c49768438|proc0]] proposes a legal right for tenants to request replacement as a solution to restricted choice. bell-cot [[./thread#^c49770690|bell-cot]] argues that legislation would still leave installation expenses and landlord resistance unresolved.
- proc0 [[./thread#^c49768438|proc0]] predicts higher rent, higher electricity costs, and poorer reliability from broader electrification. These are forecasts relevant to household tradeoffs, not documented costs or outcomes of a particular stove conversion.
- proc0 [[./thread#^c49768438|proc0]] asserts that prolonged residence in an asbestos-containing building is high risk, while bell-cot [[./thread#^c49770690|bell-cot]] calls sealed, undisturbed asbestos completely harmless. Neither supplies exposure measurements; bell-cot’s claim is explicitly conditional on fibers remaining contained.
