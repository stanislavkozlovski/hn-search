# [The Ashrae Ventilation Recommendations Are Too Low](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=37519852)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion challenges or defends claims that ASHRAE ventilation recommendations are too low. Participants compare ventilation calculations with standards, discuss CO2 as a proxy for fresh-air exchange, and weigh respiratory health and cognition against heating, cooling, humidity, and energy costs. The thread covers building airtightness, energy-recovery ventilators, apartment installation constraints, fans and HVAC settings, and measurement accuracy. Contributors also disagree about whether professional standards account for newer concerns such as airborne disease transmission and how much evidence supports specific CO2 thresholds.
Tags: ASHRAE, ventilation, CO2, indoor-air-quality, HVAC, energy-recovery, building-standards, airborne-disease

## Relevance

The thread directly debates ventilation rates, CO2, cognition, and the tradeoffs of opening windows versus energy use. Firsthand sensor accounts describe high overnight CO2 and improved conditions with airflow; replies discuss when windows fail to provide exchange, outdoor temperature, humidity, sensor calibration, and energy-recovery ventilation. Claims about health and cognition are contested. ## Arguments

- Syzygies [[./thread#^c37524114|Syzygies]] argues that ventilation should clear occupants’ exhaled air before others inhale it, using CO2 as a proxy for that clearance. This supports an infection-control rationale for ventilation, but the comment supplies no measurements or study results establishing its claimed health benefits.
- J\_Shelby\_J [[./thread#^c37525861|J\_Shelby\_J]] argues for precaution despite limited research on CO2 and cognition: standards based on existing evidence might overlook poorly studied effects. Their suggestion that cognition was probably excluded from recommendations is speculation about the standards’ rationale.
- elil17 [[./thread#^c37522377|elil17]] recommends energy recovery ventilation to temper incoming outdoor air using outgoing indoor air. onetimeuse92304 [[./thread#^c37522822|onetimeuse92304]] describes separate air streams and claims approximately 80–90\% energy recovery, while quickthrowman [[./thread#^c37523523|quickthrowman]] favors enthalpy wheels. These are technical recommendations and efficiency claims, without measured performance from the proposed apartment installation.
- throwaway290 [[./thread#^c37522043|throwaway290]] proposes a strategically placed extractor fan to overcome weak morning airflow and says extractors seem to be required where they live [[./thread#^c37522157|throwaway290]]. onetimeuse92304 [[./thread#^c37522073|onetimeuse92304]] instead plans heat exchange to sustain ventilation during hot and cold weather. Both proposals address the practical limits of relying on open windows.
- bob1029 [[./thread#^c37523264|bob1029]] argues that comfort requires controlling humidity independently of cooling, proposing separate dehumidification or variable-speed heat pumps. LUmBULtERA [[./thread#^c37525184|LUmBULtERA]] similarly suggests longer operation at lower speed, although neither supplies a measured comparison demonstrating the proposed improvement.

## Counterarguments

- sporf [[./thread#^c37521731|sporf]] questions whether the article used the correct ventilation requirement, quoting ASHRAE 62.2 as Qtot \= 0.03 × floor area + 7.5 × (bedrooms + 1), in cubic feet per minute. Their example gives 90 cfm, approximately 45 L/s, for a 2,000-square-foot, three-bedroom house. This challenges the calculation’s premise rather than demonstrating health outcomes at that rate.
- terminous [[./thread#^c37524677|terminous]] favors recommendations developed by named professionals through public review over an apparently improvised calculation by a pseudonymous blogger. This is an argument about evidentiary authority; the comment does not independently establish which ventilation rate protects cognition, sleep, or health.
- Enginerrrd [[./thread#^c37521739|Enginerrrd]] argues that assuming an airtight building overestimates indoor CO2 because uncontrolled leakage also supplies outdoor air. They nevertheless personally prefer higher prescribed ventilation. quickthrowman [[./thread#^c37523523|quickthrowman]] challenges the applicability of the leakage argument to newer sealed buildings and commercial buildings without operable windows.
- Responding to a basement reading around 500 ppm, kadoban [[./thread#^c37523109|kadoban]] cites outdoor air around 420 ppm and argues that further reduction offers little practical benefit. This directly challenges treating 500 ppm as an alarming reading, without establishing a universal health threshold.
- throwaway290 [[./thread#^c37524010|throwaway290]] disputes onetimeuse92304’s assertion that any CO2 level is detrimental, counterclaiming that some CO2 is required and distinguishing CO2 from PM2.5 and CO. Their assertion that PM2.5 has no safe level is not accompanied by study results; neither side establishes an inhaled-CO2 dose–response relationship.
- J\_Shelby\_J [[./thread#^c37525680|J\_Shelby\_J]] speculates that climates permitting year-round open windows help explain intellectual productivity. AlotOfReading [[./thread#^c37526887|AlotOfReading]] offers the Pearl River delta and Iceland as counterexamples involving air quality and weather. Neither supplies exposure measurements or evidence connecting climate, ventilation, and regional productivity.

## Assumptions

- frankus [[./thread#^c37523402|frankus]] estimates CO2 production using a 2,400-calorie diet consisting entirely of glucose: approximately 32 grams per hour, requiring around 1,000 liters per minute of ventilation to maintain 400 ppm above ambient. This is a theoretical calculation under a particular metabolic assumption, not a measured breathing rate or an absolute indoor target of 400 ppm.
- onetimeuse92304 [[./thread#^c37521796|onetimeuse92304]] attributes morning CO2 accumulation despite open windows to indoor and outdoor temperatures converging and airflow stopping. The concentration spikes are reported observations; the airflow mechanism is their explanation. No simultaneous airflow or wind measurements are supplied to establish when that explanation applies.
- oooyay [[./thread#^c37522040|oooyay]] hypothesizes that CO2’s weight causes it to collect near the basement floor and proposes testing a continuously running low-mounted fan. The comment contains neither measurements at different heights nor results from that planned experiment.
- AnotherGoodName [[./thread#^c37524068|AnotherGoodName]] assumes indoor CO2 roughly doubles the outdoor concentration, making a higher outdoor baseline cross a personally noticeable range near 800 ppm. The comment does not establish that doubling rule, a universal symptom threshold, or a controlled comparison across decades.
- Interpreting CO2 readings as direct measurements of cognitive or sleep impairment requires assumptions absent from these accounts. onetimeuse92304 [[./thread#^c37521796|onetimeuse92304]] reports feeling better after changing airflow, while J\_Shelby\_J [[./thread#^c37525719|J\_Shelby\_J]] merely asks how many IQ points winter readings above 2,000 ppm might cost. Neither isolates CO2 from other changes accompanying ventilation or measures cognitive performance.

## Conditions

- Season matters in the Central European apartment account. Asked about location, humidity, and year-round conditions by throwaway290 [[./thread#^c37521967|throwaway290]], onetimeuse92304 [[./thread#^c37522012|onetimeuse92304]] describes humidity around 40\% during the day and 80–90\% in the morning, higher in rain. throwaway290 [[./thread#^c37523647|throwaway290]] initially interprets the mild day–night temperature pattern as making heating and cooling unnecessary. onetimeuse92304 [[./thread#^c37528876|onetimeuse92304]] clarifies that winters reach about −20°C, and throwaway290 [[./thread#^c37533410|throwaway290]] acknowledges having mistaken the earlier description for year-round weather.
- Iceland illustrates the limits of judging window-opening feasibility from a general climate label. tomjakubowski [[./thread#^c37534951|tomjakubowski]] calls its climate temperate and guesses windows could remain open much of the year. AlotOfReading [[./thread#^c37536837|AlotOfReading]], whose family lives there, describes winter blizzards, hurricane-force winds, frequent storms, and insects during warmer months. This is family-based contextual knowledge, not an indoor-air measurement.
- MichaelZuo [[./thread#^c37529958|MichaelZuo]] argues that energy recovery permits ventilation even in extreme climates, but identifies equipment cost, bulk, and maintenance as constraints. throw0101b [[./thread#^c37531089|throw0101b]] counters with a US\$500 ERV and examples of compact units; MichaelZuo [[./thread#^c37534390|MichaelZuo]] replies that a small-room device does not establish the cost or capacity needed for a whole house. Installed costs are not supplied.
- Apartment installation can be the binding constraint. mikestew [[./thread#^c37523507|mikestew]] questions whether the recommendation requires invasive work. Aspos [[./thread#^c37524301|Aspos]] describes approximately 10 × 30 cm cylindrical units that still require cutting a wall opening. onetimeuse92304 [[./thread#^c37528824|onetimeuse92304]] reports that both invasive installation and local restrictions on large wall holes obstruct their proposed solution.
- Window-mounted alternatives are suggested but not demonstrated in the affected apartment. samtho [[./thread#^c37523848|samtho]] reports seeing units resembling shallow window air conditioners, and elil17 [[./thread#^c37527164|elil17]] links a window-unit manufacturer. Neither establishes suitable airflow capacity, installed cost, or compliance with the apartment’s particular restrictions.
- Building stock and locality limit generalization. PeterisP [[./thread#^c37521973|PeterisP]] asks whether passive heat exchange addresses ventilation’s thermal cost; Enginerrrd [[./thread#^c37525710|Enginerrrd]] agrees but says such equipment is uncommon in their area. Enginerrrd [[./thread#^c37525723|Enginerrrd]] also objects to treating modern building systems as universal. TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c37532990|TeMPOraL]] dismisses nonurban dwelling as a population-level rounding error, but supplies no evidence resolving the actual distribution of building airtightness or ventilation equipment.
- BXLE\_1-1-BitIs1 [[./thread#^c37526530|BXLE\_1-1-BitIs1]] attributes inadequate ventilation to financial incentives, alleging that employer-owned premises and cash-strapped schools economize on ventilation while landlords seeking tenant retention have stronger incentives to provide it. Their claims about schools restricting CO2 monitors and institutional priorities are not independently substantiated in this unit.

## Lived experiences

- onetimeuse92304 [[./thread#^c37521796|onetimeuse92304]] reports monitor graphs showing morning CO2 spikes up to 1,500 ppm while asleep despite open windows. They subsequently slept with the room’s door open and felt much better in the morning. This is a useful account of an airflow intervention and subjective improvement, but no post-intervention concentration, objective sleep measurement, or controlled comparison is provided.
- onetimeuse92304 [[./thread#^c37523335|onetimeuse92304]] reports carrying an Aranet4 and encountering friends’ and relatives’ apartments that normally stayed above 2,000 ppm during winter. They also say they personally could not distinguish effects below 600 ppm. These are observations from particular homes and personal perception, without a representative sample or standardized symptom testing.
- oooyay [[./thread#^c37522040|oooyay]] reports basement readings usually around 500 ppm and says opening windows reduces higher concentrations, with diminishing returns near that baseline. In a follow-up, oooyay [[./thread#^c37524781|oooyay]] reports peaks around 1,000 ppm when someone sleeps there. The detector’s unfavorable classification prompted concern; the comment does not report accompanying illness or impaired sleep.
- J\_Shelby\_J [[./thread#^c37525635|J\_Shelby\_J]] reports operating a large radon-type fan to supply outdoor air when apartment CO2 rises around 700 ppm, alongside an exhaust fan, yet still seeing 700–1,000 ppm while people are home. They also describe experience with sensors that drift when never exposed to an outdoor-like baseline. Neither airflow rates nor independent reference measurements are supplied.
- tikkun [[./thread#^c37525073|tikkun]] reports that setting the HVAC fan to run continuously while occupied was their most effective way to lower CO2 in windowless rooms or when opening windows was impractical. No concentrations are given, and the comment does not specify whether the system introduces outdoor air or redistributes air within the home.
- LUmBULtERA [[./thread#^c37525184|LUmBULtERA]] reports that running the fan without active cooling substantially increases indoor humidity during humid summers with a single-stage AC system. bob1029 [[./thread#^c37523264|bob1029]] separately reports needing a central unit running continuously to maintain summer relative humidity below 55\%, with substantial energy devoted to dehumidification. These accounts emphasize comfort and operating costs rather than measured CO2 benefits.
- turtlebits [[./thread#^c37525188|turtlebits]] reports using a small ERV in a shed/office with minimal ducting and combined intake/exhaust arrangements. Their surprise at the apparent absence of portable, window-vented ERVs is a statement about their awareness of products, not proof that such products do not exist.
- AnotherGoodName [[./thread#^c37524068|AnotherGoodName]] recalls sleeping with windows closed when outdoor CO2 was approximately 300 ppm and now putting fans in windows with outdoor concentrations above 400 ppm. This documents a reported change in personal practice, not a measured longitudinal relationship between atmospheric CO2 and sleep.
- BXLE\_1-1-BitIs1 [[./thread#^c37526530|BXLE\_1-1-BitIs1]] reports leaving a Bell Canada data-center job after dissatisfaction with what they describe as ASHRAE-minimum air quality. They provide no pollutant readings, specific symptoms, or before-and-after health outcomes.
- schiffern [[./thread#^c37526548|schiffern]] reports intentionally running the fan after dehumidification to dry the coil, accepting some return of moisture to the room. Their motivation is a claimed mold risk; they do not report measured mold contamination or a demonstrated health benefit from this practice.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The magnitude and threshold of CO2’s cognitive effects remain unresolved. onetimeuse92304 [[./thread#^c37523335|onetimeuse92304]] asserts that even ambient concentrations have measurable nervous-system effects while personally noticing no difference below 600 ppm; throwaway290 [[./thread#^c37524010|throwaway290]] disputes the underlying claim, and J\_Shelby\_J [[./thread#^c37525861|J\_Shelby\_J]] describes the research as limited. No study methods, effect sizes, cognitive tests, or objective sleep results are supplied.
- Syzygies’s claim that respiratory transmission approaches zero below 500 ppm [[./thread#^c37524114|Syzygies]] is not substantiated by evidence in the discussion. The adequacy of ASHRAE recommendations likewise remains unsettled: sporf [[./thread#^c37521731|sporf]] challenges the calculation and links a 2022 standard, while J\_Shelby\_J [[./thread#^c37525861|J\_Shelby\_J]] characterizes the recommendations as pre-COVID. The supplied material does not reconcile the versions or establish a protective concentration or ventilation rate.
- Sensor reliability is disputed rather than resolved. J\_Shelby\_J [[./thread#^c37525635|J\_Shelby\_J]] questions the basement’s low readings, emphasizes cost and calibration, and suggests leaving a sensor outdoors for 24 hours. However, oooyay’s monitor model and calibration history are unspecified [[./thread#^c37522040|oooyay]], and kadoban [[./thread#^c37523109|kadoban]] considers 500 ppm plausible relative to an approximately 420 ppm outdoor baseline. No reference comparison establishes that this sensor is inaccurate or that the proposed calibration procedure applies to it.
- The fan-operation tradeoff remains contested. quercusa [[./thread#^c37525163|quercusa]] recommends intermittent circulation to avoid re-evaporating condensate and undoing dehumidification. schiffern [[./thread#^c37526548|schiffern]] argues that drying prevents mold; quercusa [[./thread#^c37528797|quercusa]] replies that condensate should drain, while schiffern [[./thread#^c37529138|schiffern]] distinguishes drained water from residual moisture on the coil. No comparative humidity, mold, energy, or health measurements settle which practice is preferable in a particular system.
- Indoor CO2 capture appears only as a speculative alternative to costly ventilation in unfavorable climates. panax [[./thread#^c37526924|panax]] suspects it would be more expensive, and ActionPlankton [[./thread#^c37529088|ActionPlankton]] says they have explored related ideas but supplies only a link. This unit provides no demonstrated device performance, operating cost, or evidence of equivalent broader air-quality benefits.
- This source unit cannot answer the approved question’s outdoor-pollution and filtration comparisons. PM2.5 appears only in throwaway290’s unsupported threshold claim [[./thread#^c37524010|throwaway290]]; there are no supplied NO2 readings, particle-filtration results, or firsthand cooking, traffic, or wildfire-smoke accounts. onetimeuse92304’s open-window experience [[./thread#^c37521796|onetimeuse92304]] concerns indoor CO2 and airflow, so it cannot establish when opening windows would worsen exposure to outdoor pollutants.
