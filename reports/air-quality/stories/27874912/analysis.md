# [Cities try to phase out gas stoves but cooks are pushing back](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=27874912)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

The discussion weighs efforts to phase out gas stoves against their small direct emissions, the larger role of gas heating and leaks, and the possibility that removing gas hookups could simplify building infrastructure. Participants debate whether electrification should be paired with a cleaner, more reliable grid and how it might affect energy costs, low-income households, and backup power during outages. Much of the thread compares gas, resistance electric, and induction cooking: people describe differences in speed, heat control, cookware compatibility, wok use, safety, cleaning, cost, and appliance controls. Other recurring themes include indoor air pollution and ventilation, methane leakage, wildfire and grid resilience, propane alternatives, and frustration over whether climate policy places too much burden on households instead of industry.
Tags: gas stoves, electrification, climate policy, induction cooking, indoor air quality, energy reliability, appliance tradeoffs

## Relevance

The discussion includes claims about gas-stove indoor pollution and asthma, the role of exhaust ventilation, and firsthand concerns about poorly vented or recirculating kitchen hoods. It also compares gas cooking with induction and filtration, while noting uncertainty about the evidence and the relative contribution of pollutants from cooking itself. ## Arguments

- Indoor exposure provides a reason to reconsider gas cooking independently of its share of energy use. ip26 [[./thread#^c27875685|ip26]] calls indoor air quality a compelling health concern, and mlinksva [[./thread#^c27879031|mlinksva]] lists it separately from infrastructure costs and explosion hazards. These are arguments for reducing a household source, not measurements of its contribution to anyone's exposure.
- tialaramex [[./thread#^c27880614|tialaramex]] distinguishes combustion beside the cook from a boiler exhausting outdoors: a gas stove releases CO2 and some CO where someone is standing, whereas induction does not generate combustion gases itself. The commenter also acknowledges cooking-related volatiles, especially from pan frying. No concentrations or exposure durations accompany the toxicity claims.
- Ventilation is presented as an important intervention, with a comfort and energy tradeoff. fomine3 [[./thread#^c27879733|fomine3]] specifically criticizes gas cooking without exhaust. loonster [[./thread#^c27878861|loonster]] argues that removing gas pollutants requires more exhaust than equivalent electric cooking, carrying additional conditioned air out of the home. Neither supplies airflow or pollutant-removal measurements.
- Respiratory-health arguments rely on references to research rather than firsthand diagnoses. hluska [[./thread#^c27879551|hluska]] claims decades of research associate growing up around gas cooking with substantially greater likelihood of severe respiratory ailments. spyder [[./thread#^c27879354|spyder]] links an article about gas-stove pollution, while adrianN [[./thread#^c27879666|adrianN]] interprets its studies as comparing homes with and without gas stoves. Study methods and effect sizes are not supplied.
- Reducing heat released into the kitchen is another argument for induction. minetest2048 [[./thread#^c27880821|minetest2048]] points to a comparison of a 5,000 W induction wok cooker and gas, conditionally accepting its claim of less waste heat and more feasible indoor cooking. phonypc [[./thread#^c27882271|phonypc]] argues that gas heat lost to surrounding air varies with vessel size and shape. These are a linked demonstration and a technical explanation, not room-temperature or air-quality measurements.
- Outdoor and neighborhood exposure enters through wood burning. Responding to enthusiasm for fireplaces, chriswarbo [[./thread#^c27880665|chriswarbo]] argues that their main problem is particulates released into the immediate surroundings and associated health harms. The comment does not identify particle sizes, provide PM2.5 readings, or quantify exposure inside neighboring homes.

## Counterarguments

- Changing the heat source does not eliminate cooking emissions. In reply to zaphoyd's concern about an unventilated kitchen, nate\_meurer [[./thread#^c27876825|nate\_meurer]] asserts that most indoor cooking pollution comes from food rather than gas combustion. mattst88 [[./thread#^c27879685|mattst88]], in a separate air-pollution branch, explicitly asks whether either source dwarfs the other and whether food-related pollutants can be mitigated. The asserted dominance of food emissions remains unsupported here.
- Electrifying an appliance does not necessarily remove intentional smoke production. Former chef moate [[./thread#^c27875420|moate]] says an electric smoker still requires burning wood for smoke, while blackening food could be done on induction. This distinguishes replacing a burner from changing a cooking process that deliberately produces smoke; the comment contains no exposure comparison.
- Cooking objections concern specific equipment and techniques as well as preference. nate\_meurer [[./thread#^c27876825|nate\_meurer]] describes small aluminum pans, lifting pans while sautéing, and tactile flame feedback as advantages of gas. However, Wistar [[./thread#^c27879400|Wistar]] finds a high-end induction unit more precise than gas and would relinquish gas first if necessary. Neither account establishes an air-quality benefit large enough to resolve the user's particular equipment tradeoff.
- An autism claim is challenged rather than substantiated. chasil [[./thread#^c27879464|chasil]] reports switching away from gas and repeats an explicitly described rumor associating gas burners with autism. boring\_twenties [[./thread#^c27879614|boring\_twenties]] rejects reliance on unsubstantiated autism rumors. This exchange provides neither a diagnosed outcome nor supporting research.
- Notanothertoo [[./thread#^c27880057|Notanothertoo]], replying directly to hluska's respiratory-health argument, claims that entering a car entails greater mortality risk. This expresses a competing judgment about acceptable risk, but supplies no comparable probabilities and does not refute the claimed respiratory association. It is also not evidence about traffic-related air pollution.
- Removing gas can sacrifice useful emergency capability in some households. wu\_187 [[./thread#^c27875538|wu\_187]] describes boiling drinking and bathing water during Houston's power outage. Conversely, gkop [[./thread#^c27875775|gkop]] reports a week-long gas interruption in northern New Mexico and reliance on wood. These accounts challenge universal assumptions about either fuel's reliability; neither establishes the indoor exposure incurred during emergency use.

## Assumptions

- The evidence is confined to supplied story 27874912, part 0. An apartment-search account such as kpozin's [[./thread#^c27882091|kpozin]], an equipment-use account such as nate\_meurer's [[./thread#^c27875514|nate\_meurer]], and a research claim such as hluska's [[./thread#^c27879551|hluska]] have different evidentiary status. They do not establish population-wide conditions or an HN consensus.
- Comparisons require distinguishing resistance cooking from induction. why5s [[./thread#^c27875475|why5s]] initially attributed coil warm-up delays to induction; dragonwriter [[./thread#^c27875753|dragonwriter]] identified the confusion, and why5s [[./thread#^c27878192|why5s]] acknowledged learning the distinction. OJFord [[./thread#^c27880410|OJFord]] additionally notes regional terminology. Some apparent opposition to induction therefore rests on experience with another technology.
- Housing quality cannot reliably stand in for stove type or cooking frequency. After retracting an objection, desine [[./thread#^c27879729|desine]] explains having extrapolated from cheaper rentals with electric stoves and nicer homes with gas, acknowledging a chain of incorrect assumptions. adrianN [[./thread#^c27879666|adrianN]] had challenged whether different cooking frequency could explain the reported study comparisons. The export does not establish those relationships or how studies handled them.
- The presence of a hood cannot be assumed to mean outdoor exhaust. kpozin [[./thread#^c27882091|kpozin]] describes fans that recirculate stove exhaust, and fred\_is\_fred [[./thread#^c27883524|fred\_is\_fred]] reports no external venting even in a house built in 2008. Conversely, neither account establishes what filters were installed or how much pollution they removed.
- Owning an electric or induction appliance cannot be equated with eliminating gas use. LightG [[./thread#^c27880610|LightG]] installed four electric and two gas hobs, retaining gas for selected dishes and backup. philips [[./thread#^c27880154|philips]] uses both technologies and keeps gas partly because it already works and some pans remain incompatible with induction. Any exposure-reduction estimate would need actual patterns of use.

## Conditions

- Space and building configuration matter to the proposed health benefit. fred\_is\_fred [[./thread#^c27883524|fred\_is\_fred]] emphasizes smaller spaces and older buildings, but also cites a relatively recent family home without external stove exhaust. kpozin's NYC search [[./thread#^c27882091|kpozin]] suggests that inadequate kitchen exhaust can constrain renters across different building ages; the search is not a citywide inspection.
- Local appliance availability and installation practices differ. CannisterFlux [[./thread#^c27879859|CannisterFlux]], without identifying the location, reports declining gas selection, specialist installation, recurring safety reviews, and rentals commonly equipped with electric stoves. That contrasts with kpozin's gas-equipped NYC search [[./thread#^c27882091|kpozin]]. Neither experience should be generalized to rental housing everywhere.
- Purchase costs vary substantially by country and product. Notanothertoo [[./thread#^c27880031|Notanothertoo]] quotes a \$400 gas stove; g8oz [[./thread#^c27882626|g8oz]] counters a much higher induction estimate with approximately \$1,200 for an entry-level induction range and \$3,000 for a higher-end brand. ants\_a [[./thread#^c27883871|ants\_a]] reports paying €689 including tax for a Bosch induction cooktop. These are 2021 prices for differing products, not comparable current installed-cost estimates.
- Electrical infrastructure can determine whether switching is straightforward. Gibbon1 [[./thread#^c27879540|Gibbon1]] reports testing two single-hob models topping out at 1,440 W and describes compact units using ordinary 120 V/15 A power. 10x-dev [[./thread#^c27888509|10x-dev]] says their stove requires 240 V, while dawnerd [[./thread#^c27879773|dawnerd]] considers the wiring cost prohibitive. Appliance-power reports do not measure indoor pollution.
- Cookware replacement costs and compatibility are equipment-specific. nate\_meurer [[./thread#^c27875514|nate\_meurer]] relies on small aluminum pots that their induction unit cannot recognize or heat. nmcveity [[./thread#^c27879347|nmcveity]] reports a different unit handling 4–11 inch cookware and no incompatible existing pans. fy20 [[./thread#^c27879317|fy20]] found a small compatible pot for €15 in Europe, and biztos [[./thread#^c27880701|biztos]] successfully uses a €20 adapter for a Moka pot.
- Wood is not presented as a simple indoor substitute. Responding to a proposed electric/wood hybrid stove, b3morales [[./thread#^c27884138|b3morales]] argues that wood needs substantially better ventilation than most kitchens provide, occupies storage space, and is cumbersome for small cooking tasks. In an emergency context, gkop [[./thread#^c27875775|gkop]] values wood's availability while acknowledging that it is dirty.
- Restaurant and regional cooking practices complicate replacement claims. kibwen [[./thread#^c27879519|kibwen]] cites induction's popularity in China and advocates partial adoption. deadfoxygrandpa [[./thread#^c27890994|deadfoxygrandpa]] counters that portable induction commonly serves hot-pot, boiling, or steaming tasks, while stronger gas burners and affordable restaurant meals sustain other cooking practices. These are competing contextual claims, not surveyed adoption or exposure data.

## Lived experiences

- kpozin [[./thread#^c27882091|kpozin]] reports that every apartment encountered during a recent NYC search had a gas stove, generally with no meaningful kitchen ventilation beyond an occasional small passive opening. Where overhead fans existed, they recirculated into the kitchen. chaorace [[./thread#^c27883045|chaorace]], replying, describes similar frustration with exhaust blown back toward the occupant. Neither reports pollutant readings.
- zaphoyd [[./thread#^c27875747|zaphoyd]] directly reports gas waste heat making the kitchen and pot handles hot, resulting in burns, alongside concern about combustion products in an effectively unventilated American apartment. In another branch, zaphoyd [[./thread#^c27875577|zaphoyd]] says a \$100 induction plate placed over the apartment's gas stove became the preferred cooking tool. No respiratory, cognitive, or sleep improvement is reported after that change.
- desine [[./thread#^c27879976|desine]] values the scent and warmth of wood burning and describes it as emotionally satisfying. This is a firsthand comfort preference, accompanied by broader speculative claims about human history. chriswarbo's reply [[./thread#^c27880665|chriswarbo]] raises particulate health concerns without showing that this commenter experienced a particular adverse effect.
- Induction performance varies within users' own histories. pkolaczk [[./thread#^c27880519|pkolaczk]] reports smooth operation and useful temperature control with a previous Bosch unit, followed by objectionable power cycling with a cheaper Samsung unit. ip26 [[./thread#^c27885251|ip26]] reports fine control from a 100-step hot plate and identifies it as a commercial Vollrath bought after disappointing consumer models [[./thread#^c27887015|ip26]]. These experiences make model choice relevant to sustained substitution for gas.
- Accounts of heating uniformity conflict. sp332 [[./thread#^c27875442|sp332]] reports a small overheated area when trying to sear steak on induction; konfusinomicon [[./thread#^c27875492|konfusinomicon]] reports successful searing with a GE Cafe induction stove. Wistar [[./thread#^c27879400|Wistar]] reports excellent precision from a Breville/PolyScience unit but would need additional circuits and different pans to replace gas fully. These are cooking outcomes rather than air-quality outcomes.
- LightG [[./thread#^c27880610|LightG]] describes a negotiated household compromise: four electric hobs for tasks such as boiling and two gas hobs for perceived taste differences in wok cooking, some teas, and frying. Backup during electrical outages also mattered. The account demonstrates partial adoption while leaving combustion in the kitchen.
- wu\_187 [[./thread#^c27875538|wu\_187]] reports gas allowing potable-water preparation and warm bathing water during Houston's freeze. pram [[./thread#^c27878981|pram]] similarly reports using a gas fireplace for warmth and a range to boil water. In contrast, gkop [[./thread#^c27875775|gkop]] recalls northern New Mexico losing gas for a week in 2011 and wood providing emergency heat. These establish practical benefits under different failures, without measuring smoke or combustion-gas exposure.

## Unresolved disagreements

- How much pollution comes from the burner versus the food remains unresolved. nate\_meurer's assertion that food dominates [[./thread#^c27876825|nate\_meurer]] is not accompanied by pollutant-specific measurements; mattst88 [[./thread#^c27879685|mattst88]] explicitly leaves the relative contribution open. adrianN's interpretation of gas-versus-nongas household studies [[./thread#^c27879666|adrianN]] does not supply the methods needed to resolve that attribution.
- This unit contains no firsthand PM2.5, NO2, or indoor CO2 concentration series, sensor models, calibration checks, or reliability comparisons. tialaramex [[./thread#^c27880614|tialaramex]] names CO2 and CO without readings; chriswarbo [[./thread#^c27880665|chriswarbo]] discusses unspecified particulates; Gibbon1's numerical testing concerns appliance power [[./thread#^c27879540|Gibbon1]]. These cannot establish pollutant levels, exposure thresholds, or sensor accuracy.
- The effectiveness of ventilation and filtration is unquantified. kpozin [[./thread#^c27882091|kpozin]] documents recirculation rather than outdoor exhaust, while loonster [[./thread#^c27878861|loonster]] predicts greater conditioned-air loss from gas-related extraction. No supplied account compares hood capture, filter performance, operating settings, or measured concentrations before and after an intervention. Adequate exhaust eliminating all gas-related risk is therefore not demonstrated.
- The window-opening tradeoff cannot be resolved from this unit. kpozin [[./thread#^c27882091|kpozin]] discusses missing external kitchen exhaust, and chriswarbo [[./thread#^c27880665|chriswarbo]] raises nearby wood particulates, but neither reports opening-window experiments or indoor/outdoor comparisons. There are no firsthand traffic-pollution or wildfire-smoke exposure measurements supporting advice about when outside air would improve or worsen indoor conditions.
- No supplied account connects measured air exposure or a stove change with cognition, sleep, or a documented respiratory-health improvement. zaphoyd's concrete effects concern heat and burns [[./thread#^c27875747|zaphoyd]]; hluska's respiratory argument invokes research without presenting results [[./thread#^c27879551|hluska]]. The discussion cannot quantify clinical benefits or establish which symptoms a particular household would experience.
- The remaining functional gap for wok cooking is disputed. tafda [[./thread#^c27878267|tafda]] points to shaped commercial induction wok cookers, while 1MachineElf [[./thread#^c27879067|1MachineElf]], drawing on family restaurant experience, values flames reaching food during tossing and reiterates that mechanism in a follow-up [[./thread#^c27890477|1MachineElf]]. Available appliances do not by themselves settle taste equivalence, affordability, or the exposure consequences of preserving that technique.
- A tangential infection-risk exchange also leaves ventilation's importance disputed. Dylan16807 [[./thread#^c27885035|Dylan16807]] argues that poor grocery-store airflow complicates reliance on distancing and requests evidence about transmission settings. clairity [[./thread#^c27886164|clairity]] instead calls airflow secondary to time, distance, and air volume. Neither supplies relevant exposure measurements or setting-specific transmission data here; this dispute should remain separate from the gas and particulate claims.
