# [CO2meter: A Python interface to USB CO2 monitor devices](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=22764488)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion of a Python interface for a USB CO2 monitor expands into choosing reliable sensors and connecting them to Linux, Raspberry Pi, MQTT, Home Assistant, and time-series dashboards. Participants distinguish NDIR CO2 meters from cheaper VOC-based estimated CO2 devices, compare sensor modules and consumer monitors, and explain calibration challenges such as automatic baselines, fresh-air exposure, altitude, temperature, and humidity. People describe monitoring bedrooms and meeting rooms to prompt ventilation, while others ask about particulate, VOC, and oxygen sensors.
Tags: CO2 monitoring, Python, USB devices, NDIR, calibration, Home Assistant, air-quality sensors

## Relevance

The thread is about CO2 monitoring devices and sensor reliability, including NDIR versus VOC-based estimates, calibration methods, and practical accuracy limits. ## Arguments

- This unit—story 22764488, part 0—supports using CO2 monitoring to investigate suspected poor ventilation in homes and meeting rooms. doitLP [[./thread#^c22766054|doitLP]] describes monitoring prompted by nighttime headaches and subsequently instrumenting workplace meeting rooms. losteric [[./thread#^c22765227|losteric]] proposes monitoring meetings because of suspected effects on discussion quality; that cognitive explanation is a hypothesis, not a measured result.
- Several commenters recommend NDIR instruments for measuring CO2 directly. joecool1029 [[./thread#^c22764771|joecool1029]] warns that inexpensive VOC-based substitutes respond to pollutants such as alcohol, while throwaway9d0291 [[./thread#^c22767368|throwaway9d0291]] rejects interpreting VOC-derived 'eCO2' as actual CO2. Their recommendation concerns measurement method; it does not establish that every NDIR instrument is accurate.
- Accessible logging can make exposure patterns easier to investigate. 411111111111111 [[./thread#^c22766628|411111111111111]] sends readings into HomeAssistant through MQTT, toasted\_flakes [[./thread#^c22767359|toasted\_flakes]] uses InfluxDB and Grafana, and doitLP [[./thread#^c22766054|doitLP]] graphs apartment readings. These are demonstrated monitoring workflows, although the comments provide neither exported time series nor independent validation.
- Calibration control offers practical alternatives to an unsuitable automatic baseline algorithm. gsich [[./thread#^c22764746|gsich]] says automatic calibration can be disabled on MH-Z14/MH-Z19 sensors, and lorenzhs [[./thread#^c22767616|lorenzhs]] points to the corresponding SCD30 option. throwaway9d0291 [[./thread#^c22767368|throwaway9d0291]] proposes periodic manual fresh-air calibration, which introduces maintenance and depends on knowing the reference concentration.

## Counterarguments

- Low price alone does not identify the sensing technology. Against joecool1029's [[./thread#^c22764771|joecool1029]] characterization of \$10 devices as VOC sensors, Hasz [[./thread#^c22766415|Hasz]] identifies an approximately \$20 NDIR MH-Z19 and describes other inexpensive sensors as electrochemical. This qualifies the broad price-based warning without establishing equivalent accuracy across products.
- Automatic calibration can conceal sustained high indoor CO2 rather than correct it. polskibus [[./thread#^c22768994|polskibus]] warns that the MH-Z19's remembered daily recalibration is problematic in continuously elevated rooms. throwaway9d0291 [[./thread#^c22767368|throwaway9d0291]] explains that even expensive sensors can assume daily fresh-air exposure that a residence never receives.
- An elevated TVOC number is not a straightforward identification of a hazardous compound or its concentration. Responding to platz's [[./thread#^c22765191|platz]] difficulty interpreting a Foobot reading, gh02t [[./thread#^c22769082|gh02t]] reports drastically different sensitivities to different compounds, inability to distinguish them, and responses dominated by minute amounts of a sensitive compound. Their experiments supported only a relative 'good / bad' interpretation.
- The health accounts support investigating ventilation, but do not establish CO2 as the independent cause. 411111111111111 [[./thread#^c22766628|411111111111111]] reports a concentration association, Arkanosis [[./thread#^c22773980|Arkanosis]] reports headache relief alongside increased window opening, and doitLP [[./thread#^c22766054|doitLP]] reports headaches and a high reading. None supplies controlled comparisons separating CO2 from other conditions associated with ventilation.

## Assumptions

- Fresh-air calibration assumes the assigned concentration matches the actual reference air. petsfed [[./thread#^c22764695|petsfed]] describes an automatic baseline of 450 ppm and explicitly conditions its usefulness on local accuracy and occupancy; throwaway9d0291 [[./thread#^c22767368|throwaway9d0291]] uses 400 ppm for manual calibration; toasted\_flakes [[./thread#^c22767359|toasted\_flakes]] expects about 415 ppm outdoors. These are differing reference assumptions, not interchangeable verified measurements.
- The claimed SCD30 advantage depends on its baseline-selection algorithm. throwaway9d0291 [[./thread#^c22767368|throwaway9d0291]] describes at least five days of minima within 50 ppm over seven days, then clarifies that the 50 ppm detail came from direct manufacturer contact and that initial calibration requires seven uninterrupted days with daily fresh air [[./thread#^c22767793|throwaway9d0291]]. Under that description, consistently elevated minima remain a possible false reference; the algorithm does not independently prove that fresh air was encountered.
- Reliable readings assume appropriate installation and environmental compensation. petsfed [[./thread#^c22764862|petsfed]] says NDIR measurements are affected by temperature, humidity, and pressure. throwaway9d0291 [[./thread#^c22767793|throwaway9d0291]] warns about enclosure-related temperature/humidity errors and direct airflow over the SCD30. This sensor-specific advice should not be confused with gh02t's [[./thread#^c22769082|gh02t]] endorsement of a built-in sampling fan in a particulate sensor.
- Advertised accuracy, resolution, and repeatability are different evidence. joecool1029 [[./thread#^c22764771|joecool1029]] gives a tentative approximately 100 ppm accuracy estimate and separately mentions expensive 5–10 ppm resolution sensors. Hasz [[./thread#^c22766415|Hasz]] cites an untested S8-0053's advertised ±40 ppm accuracy and questions room-air homogeneity. Neither supplies a comparative reference test establishing that finer specifications yield more useful household measurements.

## Conditions

- The reported prices are from this 2020 discussion and cover different levels of completeness: Hasz [[./thread#^c22766415|Hasz]] cites an approximately \$20 MH-Z19 module; petsfed [[./thread#^c22764862|petsfed]] puts typical core devices around US\$50–150 and explains that finished meters add a controller, display, power supply, and sometimes environmental sensors; xenonite [[./thread#^c22767661|xenonite]] reports paying €65 for a complete TFA monitor. These figures are not current quotations or equivalent packages.
- Availability depends on location and supplier. xenonite [[./thread#^c22791363|xenonite]] describes the TFA-branded device as an accessible German route to the RAD-0301. petsfed [[./thread#^c22764695|petsfed]] reports SCD30 stock at SparkFun while DigiKey was out. lhoff [[./thread#^c22764603|lhoff]] and mataug [[./thread#^c22764620|mataug]] nevertheless express difficulty finding an instrument or module they can trust; availability does not resolve calibration confidence.
- Elevation can materially affect readings. petsfed [[./thread#^c22770452|petsfed]] reports moving CO2 sensors between sea level and 2,000 metres and seeing differences of roughly 150 ppm when measuring reference 400 ppm gas. They recommend altitude or pressure compensation and suggest daily weather-service pressure updates are sufficient; that update frequency is advice, not a separately demonstrated result.
- Building occupancy and ventilation schedules affect whether automatic calibration is suitable. throwaway9d0291 [[./thread#^c22767368|throwaway9d0291]] describes daily fresh-air purges as a commercial HVAC use case, while a residential room left empty for only three or four hours with closed windows may never return to the assumed baseline. The recommendation is conditional on actual ventilation patterns, not simply whether a sensor is marketed for indoor use.

## Lived experiences

- 411111111111111 [[./thread#^c22766628|411111111111111]] reports being surprised by how closely their perceived concentration tracked CO2 readings at home after installing a TFA meter. No concentrations, cognitive tests, timing analysis, or calibration checks are supplied.
- Arkanosis [[./thread#^c22773980|Arkanosis]], replying to that TFA account, says the household began opening windows much more frequently and experienced recurring headache relief without coffee or paracetamol. They would prioritize ventilation when choosing their next home. There are no paired before-and-after CO2 readings or outdoor pollution measurements.
- doitLP [[./thread#^c22766054|doitLP]] reports waking with headaches every night after moving into an apartment, then receiving an immediate alarm at 3,000 ppm when installing a monitor. They subsequently equipped workplace meeting rooms with sensors and Grafana monitoring. The comment does not report whether an apartment intervention resolved the headaches or whether workplace health or cognition improved.
- toasted\_flakes [[./thread#^c22767359|toasted\_flakes]] reports an SCD30 reading at least 1,400 ppm continuously after a rough move, even outdoors where they expected about 415 ppm, and recommends protecting the sensor with an enclosure. This is a firsthand report of implausible readings, not proof that physical damage rather than calibration or installation caused them.
- gh02t [[./thread#^c22769082|gh02t]] reports sensible, consistent readings from DIY Plantower PMS7003 laser particulate sensors, but explicitly lacks a laboratory reference. Their cheaper Sharp infrared dust sensors were unstable and useful mainly for relative changes; several VOC sensors yielded only rough relative assessments. No actual PM2.5 concentrations or exposure-related health outcomes are provided.
- throwaway9d0291 [[./thread#^c22767368|throwaway9d0291]] says testing multiple sensors led them to regard the SCD30 as substantially more accurate than cheaper Chinese alternatives, without presenting the comparison data or reference procedure. ClumsyPilot [[./thread#^c22765682|ClumsyPilot]] describes MH-series readings as reasonable, while smcleod [[./thread#^c22764888|smcleod]] reports MH-Z19/Python experimentation without quantitative accuracy findings. These accounts have different evidentiary strength and do not establish a performance ranking.
- joecool1029 [[./thread#^c22764771|joecool1029]] confirms that the library works with their RAD-0301 on Linux, and xenonite [[./thread#^c22767661|xenonite]] reports immediate compatibility with a TFA AirCO2ntrol Mini. xenonite gives stated tolerances of ±100 ppm or ±7\%, whichever is larger, through 2,000 ppm, and ±10\% above that. Compatibility is firsthand; the tolerances are reported specifications rather than measured calibration results.
- In the oxygen-sensor reply branch initiated by bjelkeman-again [[./thread#^c22766627|bjelkeman-again]], foobarbecue [[./thread#^c22769946|foobarbecue]] describes using multigas instruments in volcanic caves. They preferred CrowCon's logging, interface, size, and water resistance, reporting water damage to competing units despite IP68 claims. They disclose free sponsored equipment. This is specialized field-usability evidence, not residential CO2 accuracy or health evidence.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The magnitude and cause of cognitive, headache, and sleep effects remain unresolved. losteric [[./thread#^c22765227|losteric]] suspects impaired meeting discussions, tito [[./thread#^c22765373|tito]] says meeting rooms easily exceed 1,000 ppm, and doitLP [[./thread#^c22766054|doitLP]] reports nighttime headaches alongside a 3,000 ppm alarm. These do not establish an impairment threshold or measured sleep benefit. gbear605 [[./thread#^c22765258|gbear605]] links an article advocating attention to indoor CO2, but its findings and methods are not supplied.
- What makes a reading trustworthy remains contested. petsfed [[./thread#^c22764695|petsfed]] says a reference gas is the only way to really trust readings when automatic-baseline assumptions cannot be guaranteed; throwaway9d0291 [[./thread#^c22767368|throwaway9d0291]] recommends outdoor calibration to an assumed 400 ppm. lhoff's [[./thread#^c22764603|lhoff]] concern about lacking a reference is not resolved by a shared validation exercise.
- The SCD30 failure account has no confirmed diagnosis or repair. toasted\_flakes [[./thread#^c22767391|toasted\_flakes]] wonders whether insufficient fresh-air exposure explains bad readings; throwaway9d0291 [[./thread#^c22767793|throwaway9d0291]] offers initial calibration, forced calibration, environmental offsets, and placement checks. toasted\_flakes [[./thread#^c22769227|toasted\_flakes]] acknowledges the explanation, but does not report restored accuracy. A helpful reply should not be treated as a demonstrated fix.
- Altitude compensation receives an explicit disagreement: throwaway9d0291 [[./thread#^c22767793|throwaway9d0291]] downplays its importance except in unusual circumstances, while petsfed [[./thread#^c22770452|petsfed]] counters with reference-gas measurements across a substantial elevation change. The latter supplies concrete evidence for that setting; the thread does not establish how much compensation matters over smaller elevation or routine pressure changes.
- This unit cannot compare ventilation with filtration, cooking controls, or protection from traffic and wildfire smoke. Arkanosis [[./thread#^c22773980|Arkanosis]] reports benefits from opening windows without characterizing outdoor air, while gh02t [[./thread#^c22769082|gh02t]] discusses particulate-sensor behavior rather than measured PM2.5 exposure. There are no NO2 measurements, indoor/outdoor pollutant comparisons, filtration trials, or cooking and wildfire accounts. When opening windows might worsen total exposure, and the associated comfort or energy tradeoffs, remain unanswered here.
