# [Air Purifiers as White Noise Machines](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50212507)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion ranges over using air purifiers, fans, and mechanical noise machines as sleep sounds, with people comparing sound quality, airflow, filtration, noise levels, and running costs. Participants debate indoor air quality, CO₂ ventilation, particle and VOC sensors, filter performance, and whether filtration remains useful with open windows. Other threads cover DIY and commercial purifier designs, filter availability and expense, noise sensitivity and hearing concerns, wildfire smoke and allergies, and personal preferences for silence, pink noise, or ambient sound.
Tags: air purifiers, white noise, sleep, indoor air quality, air filtration, CO₂ and ventilation, sensors, DIY purifiers, energy use, noise

## Relevance

The discussion includes indoor PM readings during cooking, gas-stove NOx concerns, CO2 targets and disputed cognitive effects, purifier performance and costs, and the tradeoff between filtration and ventilation or open windows. It also includes firsthand wildfire-smoke and sleep experiences. ## Arguments

- Purifiers can combine particle filtration with sound that some sleepers prefer. senderista [[./thread#^c50214151|senderista]] favors their deeper sound over small speaker-based machines; JohnBooty [[./thread#^c50215372|JohnBooty]], who owns four Coway and two Winix units, describes housings and filters muffling high frequencies. Their reported sleep advantage is primarily acoustic; these accounts do not isolate a benefit from cleaner air.
- Smoke and allergy accounts provide concrete reasons to filter indoor air. alnwlsn [[./thread#^c50214563|alnwlsn]] describes regional wildfire smoke irritating eyes and throats and entering leaky buildings. fellowniusmonk [[./thread#^c50214009|fellowniusmonk]] reports fewer allergy symptoms after sleeping in heavily filtered air. These support the practical motivation, while the allergy improvement remains an uncontrolled personal observation.
- Several commenters argue that room cleaning depends on airflow as well as single-pass efficiency. schiffern [[./thread#^c50214405|schiffern]] proposes low-resistance MERV-13 media for axial PC fans; gpm [[./thread#^c50214827|gpm]], using a claimed 75\% single-pass efficiency, estimates that one-third more airflow could match HEPA cleaning in a well-mixed recirculating room. jerlam [[./thread#^c50214210|jerlam]] cites testing favoring the linked PC-fan purifier, without supplying the test data in the comment.
- Quiet operation and substantial cleaning need not be mutually exclusive. jefftk [[./thread#^c50217382|jefftk]] claims the AirFanta 3Pro delivers approximately 2 versus 0.2 CFM per dollar and 213 versus 86 CFM at 45 dBA compared with IQAir HyperHEPA. These are commenter-reported comparisons that favor evaluating clean-air delivery at the intended noise level rather than relying exclusively on filter branding.
- Ventilation has a distinct role in addressing occupied-room CO2. JohnBooty [[./thread#^c50215372|JohnBooty]] warns that CO2 rises quickly in closed bedrooms. gpm [[./thread#^c50217083|gpm]] argues that elevated CO2 warrants ventilation whether it directly affects cognition or primarily signals other accumulated indoor contaminants.
- Opening a window does not necessarily eliminate a purifier's benefit. In direct disagreement with JohnBooty, schiffern [[./thread#^c50219399|schiffern]] models a room receiving 200 CFM through a window and 200 CFM of purifier cleaning, predicting approximately half the particle concentration. This supports combining ventilation and filtration under suitable flow conditions, although it is a model rather than a reported household experiment.
- Cooking-source capture and filtered replacement air are proposed as complementary improvements. amluto [[./thread#^c50216999|amluto]] argues that a good exhaust hood can prevent a detectable cooking-related particle increase, but warns that unfiltered makeup air can introduce outdoor particles. gpm [[./thread#^c50215666|gpm]] separately raises gas-stove NOx concerns and suggests effective ventilation, especially a used exhaust hood, could reduce exposure.
- Measurements can guide equipment operation. patrickk [[./thread#^c50219420|patrickk]] describes a DIY purifier whose speed follows Air-1 readings through Home Assistant. another\_bear [[./thread#^c50221248|another\_bear]] reports building ESPHome sensors and a HEPA purifier using standard replacement filters. These are firsthand implementations of monitoring and control, without accompanying before-and-after exposure or health results.

## Counterarguments

- JohnBooty [[./thread#^c50215372|JohnBooty]] challenges the claim that a purifier is usually the best noise machine: outdoor-air ventilation may reduce its relative particle-cleaning benefit, while electricity and replacement filters add costs. JohnBooty's stronger assertion that effectiveness approaches zero with open windows is disputed by schiffern's airflow example [[./thread#^c50219399|schiffern]]; neither supplies a measured window-opening comparison.
- Noise preferences conflict. johnea [[./thread#^c50216876|johnea]] finds even refrigerator and computer-fan sounds distracting, and JoshTriplett [[./thread#^c50217186|JoshTriplett]] wants purification and ventilation with minimal noise. c0nsumer [[./thread#^c50221053|c0nsumer]] prefers a mechanical Dohm to their Blueair's sound, while kazinator [[./thread#^c50216693|kazinator]] points out that good speakers can reproduce noise without purchasing a purifier. Purifier noise is therefore a benefit for some and an operating burden for others.
- Using a purifier principally at bedtime can conflict with continuous exposure reduction. turtlebits [[./thread#^c50213862|turtlebits]] prefers quiet filters running throughout the day. schiffern [[./thread#^c50214516|schiffern]] warns that advertised cleaning ratings generally describe maximum speed, while turtlebits [[./thread#^c50215241|turtlebits]] responds that their units are sized to clean adequately at quiet settings. Room size, device capacity and operating speed matter to this disagreement.
- Some commenters favor higher-efficiency media or question PC-fan designs. LTL\_FTC [[./thread#^c50214019|LTL\_FTC]] doubts their static pressure; bradknowles [[./thread#^c50228345|bradknowles]] emphasizes HEPA's claimed 99.97\% collection at 0.3 microns and IQAir's smaller-particle specifications, asserting an allergy advantage. Der\_Einzige [[./thread#^c50220228|Der\_Einzige]] recommends at least MERV-15 for virus-sized particles. These objections contest the airflow-based argument, but provide no comparative household allergy or infection outcomes.
- Living farther from a freeway does not establish lower indoor pollution. arjie [[./thread#^c50214811|arjie]] extrapolates from their low readings near a freeway that others probably have better air. kj4211cash [[./thread#^c50217509|kj4211cash]] directly counters with higher household readings despite living farther away. Neither account isolates building ventilation, filtration, indoor sources or outdoor concentrations.
- Direct cognitive harm from moderately elevated CO2 is contested. teravor [[./thread#^c50216987|teravor]] treats CO2 mainly as a ventilation proxy and believes direct effects require much higher concentrations. eastbound [[./thread#^c50219098|eastbound]] cites a study reporting no decision-making impairment at 5,000 ppm. These claims oppose gpm's recollection of adverse effects from experimentally added CO2 at comparatively low concentrations [[./thread#^c50217083|gpm]].
- Masking environmental noise with another sound may have costs. kakacik [[./thread#^c50219026|kakacik]] questions adding louder noise to highway exposure; tgv [[./thread#^c50219116|tgv]] argues that masking interruptions might improve sleep, notes conflicting research, and suggests earplugs. Alternatives also have comfort limits: fwlr [[./thread#^c50218413|fwlr]] reports major benefit from Etymotic plugs, whereas gnabgib [[./thread#^c50218452|gnabgib]] finds them particularly uncomfortable when lying down.

## Assumptions

- Sleep and freshness reports often combine sound, airflow, habit and expectations. serkanh [[./thread#^c50220046|serkanh]] used a purifier for years solely for noise; simonbuilds [[./thread#^c50214699|simonbuilds]] describes reassurance about bedtime air; diogenescynic [[./thread#^c50218001|diogenescynic]] acknowledges a possible placebo contribution to perceived freshness. These accounts cannot determine how much benefit came from reduced pollutant exposure.
- The numerical filtration arguments depend on their models. gpm [[./thread#^c50214827|gpm]] explicitly assumes good mixing between intake and exhaust and distinguishes room recirculation from supplying a cleanroom. schiffern's open-window example [[./thread#^c50219399|schiffern]] assumes specified competing airflows; the suggestion that a window opening under one inch maintains low CO2 is not accompanied by measured exchange rates, occupancy or weather conditions.
- Personal targets are not established safety boundaries. gpm [[./thread#^c50215666|gpm]] chooses CO2 below 800 ppm partly from perceived drowsiness and partly from remembered studies, and cites a WHO mean PM2.5 guideline of 5 µg/m³ while distinguishing brief excursions from average exposure. Their cumulative-risk and Alzheimer's remarks are claims about health evidence, not outcomes demonstrated in this discussion.
- Very low particle targets presume suitable equipment and measurement capability. amluto [[./thread#^c50216999|amluto]] says a zero-PM2.5 target is especially meaningful when supply air can be filtered. cassepipe's description of filtered air as particle-free [[./thread#^c50220945|cassepipe]] goes beyond the supplied measurements: the discussion does not establish complete particle removal or the instruments' limits near zero.
- CO2 is being used partly as a proxy under an assumed relatively stable outdoor baseline. teravor [[./thread#^c50216987|teravor]] gives roughly 400 ppm as that baseline. JoshTriplett's wish for air circulation to reduce CO2 [[./thread#^c50217186|JoshTriplett]] contains no measured result; the proposed remedy in gpm's account is ventilation [[./thread#^c50217083|gpm]]. No supplied account demonstrates CO2 removal through ordinary particle-filter recirculation.
- A VOC index is not an absolute chemical concentration or a universal health score. amluto [[./thread#^c50216999|amluto]] describes a history-dependent, filtered signal whose response can differ in sign across gases. amluto [[./thread#^c50221157|amluto]] personally experimented with an SGP41 but only guesses that arjie's device uses an SGP40. Interpreting the reported index therefore requires knowing the actual sensor and algorithm.
- Lifetime-cost claims assume how filters load and age. schiffern [[./thread#^c50214462|schiffern]] argues that doubling filter area doubles the replacement interval, cancelling the additional ongoing media cost. That proportionality is not demonstrated with service-life measurements. michaeld123 [[./thread#^c50220847|michaeld123]] reports rarely replacing filters and perceiving less dust, which likewise does not establish preserved airflow or particle-removal performance.

## Conditions

- Window opening depends on the outdoor air and competing exposure priorities. JohnBooty [[./thread#^c50215441|JohnBooty]] would sometimes accept higher indoor CO2 to avoid wildfire particles or allergens. amluto [[./thread#^c50216999|amluto]] warns that exhaust-induced infiltration can import pollution. Together with schiffern's simultaneous ventilation-and-filtration model [[./thread#^c50219399|schiffern]], these comments support a conditional tradeoff rather than an always-open or always-closed rule.
- Heating and cooling costs constrain ventilation. dgacmu [[./thread#^c50217131|dgacmu]] prefers occupied-room CO2 below 700 ppm but generally achieves below 750 ppm while balancing the energy cost of makeup air without an ERV. dgacmu [[./thread#^c50220968|dgacmu]] says improved air sealing reduced heating and cooling costs while increasing the importance of planned active ventilation.
- Smoke exposure is not confined to homes near a fire or to western summer conditions. jerlam [[./thread#^c50214142|jerlam]] reports northern Michigan fires affecting Chicago more than 350 miles away; rootusrootus [[./thread#^c50217058|rootusrootus]] recalls Canadian smoke affecting the East Coast. Local combustion adds another context: senderista [[./thread#^c50223551|senderista]] mentions neighbors burning refuse, and gib444 [[./thread#^c50219104|gib444]] describes neighborhood log burners. These are localized reports and claims, not a prevalence estimate.
- Electricity costs vary substantially with device, setting and tariff. JohnBooty [[./thread#^c50215372|JohnBooty]] estimates 50–100 W for their discussion of conventional purifiers, while jefftk [[./thread#^c50217400|jefftk]] gives approximately 5 W for an AirFanta on setting 2 and roughly \$10 annually, or \$15 in higher-cost US states. andrew\_eu [[./thread#^c50219992|andrew\_eu]] calculates about €22 annually at €0.50/kWh. brokensegue's claim that energy dominates total cost [[./thread#^c50214484|brokensegue]] is therefore contested.
- Replacement-media costs can be material. jerlam [[./thread#^c50215716|jerlam]] quotes \$57 for four AirFanta filters, \$60 for Coway Mighty and \$70–80 for Mighty2. JohnBooty [[./thread#^c50215372|JohnBooty]] anticipates \$100-plus annually for continuous use in their account. TedDoesntTalk [[./thread#^c50213583|TedDoesntTalk]] instead describes Austin Air replacements costing \$250–300 with a claimed five-year life. The discussion does not normalize these prices for delivered clean air or pollution load.
- DIY economics depend on geography and component equivalence. mattlondon [[./thread#^c50229396|mattlondon]] estimates roughly £60 in delivered retail parts against a £130 product price. schiffern [[./thread#^c50237569|schiffern]] points out different fan sizes and filter grades, plus US IKEA filter prices of \$18 and \$25 versus the cited £3. schiffern [[./thread#^c50222628|schiffern]] also quotes the manufacturer's explanation of assembly, shipping and sales-channel costs; this is an attributed business explanation, not an audited cost comparison.
- Consumable availability affects purchase decisions. logged4upvoting [[./thread#^c50219963|logged4upvoting]] chose IKEA partly to avoid dependence on an obscure brand that might disappear, and jerlam [[./thread#^c50215716|jerlam]] worries about model churn and discontinued filters. In the filter-slot box-fan branch, itintheory [[./thread#^c50220493|itintheory]] reports stock difficulty; bookofjoe [[./thread#^c50220844|bookofjoe]] supplies retailer links, but gonzalohm [[./thread#^c50231384|gonzalohm]] questions their legitimacy. The thread does not establish reliable current stock.
- Price judgments and room fit also differ. c0nsumer [[./thread#^c50221053|c0nsumer]] values cheaper replacement filters for a gifted Blueair, while lurking\_swe [[./thread#^c50223751|lurking\_swe]] responds in terms of \$150–400 purifier purchase prices, leaving some ambiguity about which expense is disputed. gopalv [[./thread#^c50213621|gopalv]] accepts a pricier, reportedly less capable IKEA purifier-table for its furniture function; kube-system [[./thread#^c50217105|kube-system]] prefers a polished living-room product while using a simple box-fan filter for project dust.

## Lived experiences

- arjie [[./thread#^c50214811|arjie]] reports AirGradient readings in a San Francisco high-rise about 100 m from a freeway: baseline PM2.5 2.6 µg/m³, VOC index about 97 and CO2 about 505 ppm; during gas cooking, PM2.5 10.8 µg/m³, reported AQI about 46, PM10 17.5 µg/m³, VOC index 122 and CO2 572 ppm. They report recovery about 15 minutes after cooking. After jefftk [[./thread#^c50217234|jefftk]] identifies the impossible baseline PM10-below-PM2.5 relationship, arjie [[./thread#^c50220220|arjie]] acknowledges a transcription error without supplying a corrected PM10 value.
- dgacmu [[./thread#^c50217131|dgacmu]] reports monthly kitchen AQI averages of 1–4, corresponding to PM2.5 around 0.1–0.8 µg/m³, and occupied CO2 generally below 750 ppm. They associate the higher particle level with Canadian wildfire smoke and describe having many filters left over from COVID. dgacmu [[./thread#^c50220968|dgacmu]] explicitly clarifies that the poor-air description concerned outdoor Pittsburgh air; the heavily filtered indoor air was good. Bedroom superiority was an expectation, not a supplied measurement.
- drop-table [[./thread#^c50220862|drop-table]] reports much higher outdoor winter PM2.5 in an unnamed location: roughly 200–250 µg/m³ during daytime, 400–800 and sometimes thousands in evenings, with a maximum observed value of 3,000. They say multiple sources corroborated the readings. The location, instruments and validation records are absent, and these outdoor values are not directly comparable with dgacmu's filtered indoor measurements.
- logged4upvoting [[./thread#^c50219963|logged4upvoting]] reports five years of satisfaction using an IKEA FÖRNUFTIG for noise and a current room PM2.5 reading of 1 µg/m³, without a before-and-after comparison. jefftk [[./thread#^c50217333|jefftk]] reports three AirFanta units normally running on setting 2, with approximately 425 CFM combined and \$40 annual power cost. Those are reported equipment-performance figures, not a measured household concentration reduction.
- kccqzy [[./thread#^c50213979|kccqzy]] finds their Austin Air HealthMate effective at removing odors while their PM2.5 meter barely changes. kccqzy [[./thread#^c50216287|kccqzy]] identifies the instrument as a discontinued Temtop model capable of measuring PM2.5 and formaldehyde. No numerical series is supplied, and the favorable chemical/VOC assessment is principally an odor observation.
- bradknowles [[./thread#^c50214773|bradknowles]] reports satisfactory particulate control with IQAir units plus whole-house AprilAire HEPA filtration, but continuing concern about VOCs near bathrooms and litter boxes; additional IQAir GC units are large and their carbon media expensive. 1potato [[./thread#^c50223443|1potato]] reports an IQAir with prefilter, charcoal and HEPA stages still working after 15 years, alongside pleasant sound and costly replacements. Neither supplies quantitative gas-removal results.
- fellowniusmonk [[./thread#^c50214009|fellowniusmonk]] reports that sleeping in highly cleaned air improved allergy symptoms throughout the day, including slower or absent progression to sneezing after daytime exposure. They explicitly do not know the mechanism and speculate about reduced inflammation or improved breathing during sleep.
- alnwlsn [[./thread#^c50214563|alnwlsn]] describes several days of nearby regional wildfire smoke resembling fog, with an orange sky and campfire-like smell and taste. They report eye and throat irritation after more than about 20 minutes outdoors and smoke odor inside leaky buildings. The fire was hundreds of miles away, and driving for hours could still leave someone within the smoke region; no PM measurement accompanies the account.
- senderista [[./thread#^c50223535|senderista]] reports that purifier noise helped with severe tinnitus-related distress and continues to help falling asleep after the tinnitus largely resolved. gopalv [[./thread#^c50213621|gopalv]] bought a purifier during the 2020 Santa Cruz wildfires and says turning it on became a sleep cue for their children. cosmic\_cheese [[./thread#^c50217197|cosmic\_cheese]] and dylan604 [[./thread#^c50218032|dylan604]] also recall pleasant naps around old fans, supporting an acoustic or airflow explanation without establishing a filtration effect.
- otterdude [[./thread#^c50217406|otterdude]] sleeps with a purifier blowing gently across their face, reporting cooling and masking of highway noise; no traffic-pollutant measurement is given. ghalvatzakis [[./thread#^c50221085|ghalvatzakis]] reports improved comfort using a Xiaomi purifier and adds a dehumidifier because of a humid location. bob1029 [[./thread#^c50217530|bob1029]] separately finds a summer dehumidifier's compressor sound useful for masking tinnitus.
- c0nsumer [[./thread#^c50221053|c0nsumer]] reports less bedroom dust with a Blueair but prefers a mechanical Dohm's sound; switching it off while lights gradually brighten helps them wake naturally. dekhn [[./thread#^c50213704|dekhn]] prefers the steady sound and continuous speed control of a soldering-air purifier to a cycling air conditioner. mistic92 [[./thread#^c50215299|mistic92]] reports that a Philips purifier's sound changed after extended use and became less pleasant.
- agumonkey [[./thread#^c50220451|agumonkey]] reports dry-throat sensations and light coughing within about a minute of using a Levoit Core 300S and subsequently sold it. BenjiWiebe [[./thread#^c50224041|BenjiWiebe]] disputes the inferred dehumidification mechanism, saying filters do not remove moisture and suggesting possible manufacturing-related irritation. agumonkey [[./thread#^c50225690|agumonkey]] accepts that possibility. The symptoms are firsthand; actual humidity reduction and the cause of irritation were not established.
- jefftk [[./thread#^c50217356|jefftk]] reports that an immunocompromised aunt with a lung transplant carries a directed-air purifier in crowds, holding it approximately 8–12 inches from her face because masks are difficult for her. This is a secondhand account of usefulness and tolerability, with no measured inhaled-particle concentration or infection outcome.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The CO2–cognition dispute remains unresolved. gpm [[./thread#^c50217083|gpm]] recalls experiments adding CO2 directly but does not provide the studies; teravor [[./thread#^c50218220|teravor]] invokes military exposure studies, and eastbound [[./thread#^c50219098|eastbound]] cites no decision-making impairment at 5,000 ppm. schiffern [[./thread#^c50219803|schiffern]] alleges that meta-analytic effects depend on one author and questions implied SAT-score effects. The supplied text cannot establish which findings are robust or applicable to bedrooms, ordinary occupants or prolonged exposure.
- NO2 exposure is a major evidence gap. arjie's gas-cooking account [[./thread#^c50214811|arjie]] contains PM, CO2 and VOC-index readings but no NO2 concentration. gpm [[./thread#^c50215666|gpm]] raises NOx-related asthma findings and infers possibly adequate ventilation from low CO2. That inference does not demonstrate low NO2 exposure, and the unit supplies no direct NO2 measurements or measured health response to gas-stove ventilation changes.
- Sensor recommendations do not establish accuracy. lockie [[./thread#^c50220244|lockie]] likes AirGradient's usability and reviews; zuzuleinen [[./thread#^c50221657|zuzuleinen]] calls Awair accurate without supplying validation; omert [[./thread#^c50262298|omert]] emphasizes an Odak device's standalone operation. Avamander [[./thread#^c50220377|Avamander]] says accurate particulate and CO2 sensors are expensive. There are no reference-instrument comparisons or low-concentration uncertainty estimates, and arjie's acknowledged transcription error [[./thread#^c50220220|arjie]] should not be treated as evidence of sensor failure.
- VOC-index usefulness depends on the model and timescale. amluto [[./thread#^c50228021|amluto]] finds SGP4x behavior unstable and history-dependent, useful mainly for changes over minutes to hours rather than days. GeoAtreides [[./thread#^c50229194|GeoAtreides]] agrees slow deterioration can be missed but cites configurable tracking intervals of 4–28 days for Bosch BME680 and up to 1,000 hours for Sensirion SEN66. They agree on important limitations while disputing how broadly short-timescale conclusions apply.
- Directed clean-air devices remain a conditional proposal for personal exposure. settsu [[./thread#^c50215012|settsu]] asks whether entrainment and polluted surroundings defeat a small stream aimed at a sleeper. jerlam [[./thread#^c50216736|jerlam]] cites testing suggesting the AirFanta 4Lite's clean zone is short and narrow, requiring close positioning and little movement. jefftk's aunt's favorable experience [[./thread#^c50217356|jefftk]] does not resolve effectiveness during sleep, movement or severe smoke exposure.
- The filter debate does not establish comparative clinical protection. CoffeeOnWrite [[./thread#^c50216719|CoffeeOnWrite]] asks whether repeated MERV-13 passes miss particles that HEPA captures; schiffern [[./thread#^c50217071|schiffern]] answers using a claimed worst-case efficiency, while bradknowles [[./thread#^c50228345|bradknowles]] emphasizes HEPA and HyperHEPA specifications. No comparative allergy or infection outcomes settle those claims. Smoke anecdotes also cannot substitute for infection evidence: grandpajoey [[./thread#^c50214564|grandpajoey]] explicitly clarifies that their geographic answer concerned smoke, while fwip [[./thread#^c50214776|fwip]] separately invokes airborne viruses.
- Ionizer and electrostatic-filter claims remain unsettled. manwe150 [[./thread#^c50217125|manwe150]] promotes low-power BlueAir designs said to avoid ozone production; Avamander [[./thread#^c50220384|Avamander]] argues for an activated-carbon stage with electrostatic filtration. Avamander [[./thread#^c50232651|Avamander]] and manwe150 [[./thread#^c50247143|manwe150]] continue disagreeing over voltage, filter use and terminology. No firsthand ozone measurements or controlled particle-and-gas comparison establish the proposed exposure advantages.
- Long-term acoustic effects are not resolved by reported comfort. idiotsecant [[./thread#^c50217314|idiotsecant]] asserts negligible human hearing risk below 70 dB but explicitly cannot connect their dog's early deafness causally to purifier use. tgv [[./thread#^c50219116|tgv]] cites mixed sleep research and a small seven-day laboratory study, while schiffern [[./thread#^c50219422|schiffern]] uses a dog's fireworks response to question whether noise effects operate only through disturbed sleep. Neither animal anecdote establishes harm from purifier noise, and no supplied household sound-dose measurements settle the issue.
