# [Cooking Air Quality](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=36820937)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants discuss how cooking can raise indoor particulate and gas readings, whether pollution comes mainly from food, combustion, or both, and how much consumer air sensors reveal about health risk. They compare induction and gas cooking, debate ventilation and filtration options, and share observations about windows, range hoods, purifiers, and sensor limitations.
Tags: indoor air quality, cooking emissions, particulate matter, air quality sensors, ventilation, induction cooking

## Relevance

Includes firsthand PM2.5 and CO2 readings during cooking, differing views on gas combustion versus food-generated particles, and practical experiences with range hoods, filters, ventilation, and opening windows. Sensor limitations and the health meaning of brief spikes remain debated. ## Arguments

- Cooking food can produce substantial pollution independently of the heat source. Alupis [[./thread#^c36821539|Alupis]] points to heated fats and oils; jefftk [[./thread#^c36822023|jefftk]] clarifies that the discussed cooking used an electric range; Aurornis [[./thread#^c36821780|Aurornis]] emphasizes that smoky food remains a PM2.5 source with electric cooking. Switching appliances therefore does not address every cooking emission.
- Removing combustion could still improve air quality even when cooking particles remain. rootusrootus [[./thread#^c36821832|rootusrootus]] distinguishes gas combustion byproducts from food smoke, while dgacmu [[./thread#^c36822360|dgacmu]] argues that induction benefits indoor air quality. These are arguments about an additional pollution source, not controlled measurements of the size of the improvement.
- Capturing fumes at the stove is presented as a practical improvement. germinalphrase [[./thread#^c36821360|germinalphrase]] recommends a hood exhausting outdoors, and nomel [[./thread#^c36821641|nomel]] describes an externally vented hood that automatically starts with the oven. Their comments support source extraction, but do not establish that every hood captures all emissions.
- Ventilation depends on airflow arrangement. samldev [[./thread#^c36821148|samldev]] cites a video recommending placing a fan one or two meters from a window; hoherd [[./thread#^c36822011|hoherd]] supplies another airflow video. rsync [[./thread#^c36821450|rsync]] tentatively suggests closing a window immediately beside an exhaust fan so replacement air travels from a more distant room. These are linked demonstrations and a layout hypothesis, not measurements supplied in these comments.
- Filtration and ventilation are proposed for different problems. jtriangle [[./thread#^c36821784|jtriangle]] recommends opening windows when outdoor air is good and using a purifier or HVAC-filter-and-box-fan arrangement when it is poor; jxramos [[./thread#^c36822283|jxramos]] suggests running HEPA filters during smoke. callalex [[./thread#^c36821557|callalex]] instead emphasizes outdoor ventilation for VOCs, arguing that small carbon filters saturate quickly. The supplied accounts do not demonstrate these devices removing CO2 or NO2.
- Brief cooking peaks could contribute materially to cumulative particle exposure. Using a claimed New York average of 12 μg/m³ and cooking concentrations above 500 μg/m³, dynm [[./thread#^c36821125|dynm]] calculates that approximately 35 minutes at the higher concentration matches a full day's concentration-time exposure at the lower one. This is an illustrative calculation, not a firsthand exposure measurement or proof of equivalent health risk.

## Counterarguments

- The most dramatic description of gas cooking is challenged as unrepresentative. Alupis [[./thread#^c36821539|Alupis]] and Aurornis [[./thread#^c36821780|Aurornis]] suggest that singed eyebrows and conspicuous unburned-fuel exposure could indicate a stove or cooking problem. Apotheos [[./thread#^c36822331|Apotheos]] reports never experiencing that despite cooking most meals from scratch. These responses challenge LesZedCB's experience as a general baseline, without disproving other reported emissions.
- Induction does not necessarily eliminate an alarming particle indication. blondie9x [[./thread#^c36821692|blondie9x]] reports red air-quality indications with both gas and induction and attributes them to food and fats; their follow-up [[./thread#^c36822743|blondie9x]] continues to prioritize cooking smoke. rootusrootus [[./thread#^c36821832|rootusrootus]] counters that this observation may miss the separate benefit of eliminating combustion pollutants.
- Participants disagree about how much concern short peaks warrant. bsuvc [[./thread#^c36821345|bsuvc]] observes cooking spikes but worries less because they subside; jtriangle [[./thread#^c36821784|jtriangle]] asserts that their importance is small relative to other risks. thathndude [[./thread#^c36821896|thathndude]] enjoys induction but considers gas concerns exaggerated. dragonwriter [[./thread#^c36822091|dragonwriter]] replies that the answer depends on the exposed person, resisting a universal reassurance.
- Broad safety claims provoke requests for quantified harm. wilg [[./thread#^c36821370|wilg]] says gas stoves are barely safe even with consistent fan use. Alupis [[./thread#^c36821573|Alupis]] challenges this through the absence of cooking-related CO2 poisoning deaths and subsequently requests cooktop-specific mortality evidence [[./thread#^c36821903|Alupis]]. adt2bt [[./thread#^c36821858|adt2bt]] invokes premature deaths from air pollution generally, while tedunangst [[./thread#^c36822043|tedunangst]] asks for harm per minute of cooking. Neither side supplies that exposure-specific estimate.
- CO2 interpretations conflict sharply. jtriangle [[./thread#^c36821833|jtriangle]] invokes purported OSHA limits of 5,000 ppm continuous and 30,000 ppm short-term exposure, dismisses 1,900 ppm, and recommends alerts no lower than 5,000 ppm. verelo [[./thread#^c36822278|verelo]] instead asserts cognitive decline above 1,000 ppm. The supplied discussion does not validate either residential interpretation or establish that an occupational exposure limit answers a cognition question.
- Equal PM2.5 mass is disputed as a measure of equal danger. lm28469 [[./thread#^c36821687|lm28469]] contrasts traffic-derived brake and tire particles with cooking-related water and oil droplets. jefftk [[./thread#^c36822121|jefftk]] challenges that benign characterization of cooking smoke and links a paper. The paper's findings are not reproduced, so neither relative toxicity nor a quantitative risk comparison is established here.
- The wok dispute narrows when absolute claims are separated from particular cooking requirements. conception [[./thread#^c36821658|conception]] says wok cooking does not work on induction; zopa [[./thread#^c36822093|zopa]] quotes López-Alt saying most recipes work without flame. mrpowelldev [[./thread#^c36821849|mrpowelldev]] stresses exceptions involving particular flavors and flame-based workarounds. slily [[./thread#^c36826234|slily]] accepts that induction can work well while preferring a hybrid stove for flexibility.

## Assumptions

- Treating reported numbers as accurate requires confidence in instrumentation that the comments largely do not document. petepete [[./thread#^c36821333|petepete]] explicitly doubts their meter's accuracy; dhritzkiv [[./thread#^c36821762|dhritzkiv]] gives monitor distance but not calibration or sensor technology; PaulKeeble [[./thread#^c36821561|PaulKeeble]] names several measured pollutants without describing how each is sensed. The export cannot establish measurement accuracy or comparability.
- An unchanged overall air-quality indication is assumed by some participants to mean unchanged total pollution. That inference requires the instrument to detect the relevant pollutants: blondie9x [[./thread#^c36821692|blondie9x]] reports a red indication under both fuels, whereas rootusrootus [[./thread#^c36821832|rootusrootus]] explicitly questions whether the measurement captures combustion byproducts. No pollutant-resolved comparison accompanies their exchange.
- An alarm's color or intensity is not itself a toxicity measurement. rubatuga [[./thread#^c36821192|rubatuga]] says dextersgenius's VOC sensor lacks specificity for chemical type and concentration. lm28469 [[./thread#^c36821723|lm28469]] raises possible water-droplet interference in particle sensing, but that reply does not establish why the parent's specifically described VOC alarm activated.
- Attributing every cooking-associated CO2 rise to either combustion or food requires stronger isolation of causes. dhritzkiv [[./thread#^c36821762|dhritzkiv]] suspects predominantly combustion but reports a smaller rise when merely boiling water. PaulKeeble [[./thread#^c36821561|PaulKeeble]] reports CO2 increases while browning bread electrically. These observations leave cooking method, burner operation, ventilation, and instrument response incompletely separated.
- dynm's exposure calculation [[./thread#^c36821125|dynm]] assumes a representative concentration and duration at the person's breathing location; it does not establish those conditions for a typical household. Extending it to equal health effects would additionally require resolving the particle-composition objection raised by lm28469 [[./thread#^c36821687|lm28469]].
- Temperature-based window automation assumes favorable conditions beyond temperature. jtriangle [[./thread#^c36821784|jtriangle]] describes monitoring window states and outdoor temperature, but reports no outdoor pollution measurement. Their suggestion that subsequent HVAC operation will remove humidity introduced overnight [[./thread#^c36822054|jtriangle]] also assumes sufficient later dehumidification; ct0's July humidity concern [[./thread#^c36821894|ct0]] shows why that may not be an acceptable tradeoff everywhere.

## Conditions

- Opening windows is conditional on outdoor air quality and weather. jtriangle [[./thread#^c36821784|jtriangle]] recommends it where outdoor air is clean, while ct0 [[./thread#^c36821894|ct0]] keeps windows closed during humid July weather on the US East Coast. jtriangle [[./thread#^c36822054|jtriangle]] acknowledges that imported humidity can increase subsequent HVAC demand and reduce thermal comfort.
- Housing layout affects exposure control. za3faran [[./thread#^c36823587|za3faran]] observes that many North American kitchens open directly into living areas, limiting the option of isolating cooking behind a door. morsch [[./thread#^c36824008|morsch]] demonstrates another layout constraint: a window directly above a stove does not guarantee outward airflow or effective fume capture.
- Conversion costs vary with the scale of the change. LesZedCB [[./thread#^c36821432|LesZedCB]] suggests an approximately \$60 IKEA portable induction unit, provided cookware is compatible. nomel [[./thread#^c36821641|nomel]] reports that replacing their range would require several thousand dollars of electrical work and electricity costing several times more than gas locally. These describe different entry points, not a universal conversion price.
- Operating-cost claims are contested and lack comparable bills. Alupis [[./thread#^c36821889|Alupis]] describes induction as expensive to operate; Turskarama [[./thread#^c36822423|Turskarama]] argues that it uses less electricity than resistance cooking and that typical stovetop sessions are short. throw0101a [[./thread#^c36822215|throw0101a]] likewise argues that replacing existing electric resistance stoves could reduce consumption. Those efficiency arguments do not by themselves establish savings against locally priced gas.
- Avoidable gas connection fees depend on removing other gas uses. In Pittsburgh, dgacmu [[./thread#^c36826176|dgacmu]] reports a roughly \$25 summer bill, including a \$14.50 monthly connection charge, with gas also serving water heating, drying, and winter heating. Their \$174 annual figure concerns eliminating the connection, not merely changing the stove. Alupis [[./thread#^c36850909|Alupis]] disputes net savings; dgacmu [[./thread#^c36869920|dgacmu]] describes difficult retrofitting and a cost comparison dependent on a very efficient heat pump and relatively cheap Pennsylvania gas.
- Propane comparisons contain different loads and local prices. cj [[./thread#^c36822467|cj]] reports a roughly \$300 fill of a 100-gallon tank lasting years for cooking, but approximately annual refills when also supplying a generator; they value cooking during outages averaging one or two days annually. doubleg72 [[./thread#^c36822570|doubleg72]] reports more than \$800 per fill in Pennsylvania and half-winter endurance. jefftk [[./thread#^c36825116|jefftk]] asks whether that also includes heating, so the accounts cannot establish a stove-only cost difference.
- Filter ownership involves maintenance and product constraints. callalex [[./thread#^c36821557|callalex]] claims meaningful VOC removal would require frequent, potentially weekly, carbon replacement and a large budget, without supplying supporting measurements. Havoc [[./thread#^c36834564|Havoc]] hesitates to recommend the Xiaomi H3 because of RFID-equipped filters and Wi-Fi, which they leave disconnected. gcheong [[./thread#^c36821461|gcheong]] likewise declines to recommend their aging Idylis unit merely because they own one.

## Lived experiences

- LesZedCB [[./thread#^c36821432|LesZedCB]] reports that switching to induction reduced perceived combustion fumes and wasted heat while making boiling much faster. rootusrootus [[./thread#^c36821810|rootusrootus]] reports previously singeing knuckle hair around gas burners and enjoying induction's responsiveness after adjusting cooking habits. These are firsthand comfort and usability accounts, without pollutant measurements.
- Rebelgecko [[./thread#^c36822649|Rebelgecko]] reports little change on their air-quality meter when boiling water or heating an empty pan over gas, followed by a PM2.5 spike when oil is added. blondie9x [[./thread#^c36821692|blondie9x]] reports red indications with both induction and gas. Neither provides numeric concentrations, but both describe cooking-dependent sensor responses.
- PaulKeeble [[./thread#^c36821561|PaulKeeble]] reports deterioration on a TVOC, PM2.5, CO, and CO2 monitor despite using a hood: browning bread in an electric oven increased particles, CO, and CO2, while gas frying produced concerning readings across the monitored pollutants. acidburnNSA [[./thread#^c36821579|acidburnNSA]] independently reports household measurements showing emissions from heating food regardless of energy source. Neither supplies detailed readings or controlled comparisons.
- With a gas range and a CO2 monitor 70 cm away, dhritzkiv [[./thread#^c36821762|dhritzkiv]] reports readings rising from 650 to 1,200 during cooking, or to 900 with the hood running, and returning to baseline about 60 minutes later. This is a reported within-household ventilation comparison; the comment does not explicitly state units.
- bvan [[./thread#^c36821291|bvan]] reports gas cooking raising upstairs CO2 by at least 1,000 ppm to approximately 1,500–1,900 ppm in a two-story house, despite a reportedly functioning hood above the ground-floor stove. This account indicates that the observed increase was not confined to the immediate cooking area.
- verelo [[./thread#^c36821342|verelo]] says a CO2/VOC meter prompted a desire to replace the gas stove, although competing priorities delayed it. They later report regular CO2 levels above 1,000 while working from home without an air exchanger, and roughly 1,500 within minutes of using the range [[./thread#^c36822278|verelo]]. Their concern about impaired cognition is not accompanied by a measured change in performance.
- During a renovation without a working extractor, petepete [[./thread#^c36821333|petepete]] reports IKEA VINDSTYRKA readings rising from their usual 1–5 to as high as 30 while cooking. The comment does not specify units and questions accuracy. Their wish to log readings in Grafana also indicates that a historical dataset is not supplied.
- gcheong [[./thread#^c36821115|gcheong]] reports that a living-room purifier stopped ramping up during smoky cooking after a kitchen remodel that included a range hood. They identify the purifier as an older Idylis and the hood as a generic Home Depot model, and say they also use induction [[./thread#^c36821461|gcheong]]. The observed outcome is purifier behavior, not a quantified exposure reduction.
- Havoc [[./thread#^c36821044|Havoc]] reports an automatic purifier noticeably accelerating during cooking, particularly pan-fried steak. dextersgenius [[./thread#^c36821124|dextersgenius]] reports a different response: bringing heated food into a room triggers the purifier's VOC indication within about a minute, sometimes reaching its highest setting, level 9. That account concerns food already being eaten, not ongoing combustion, and reports fear rather than a demonstrated health effect.
- morsch [[./thread#^c36824008|morsch]] reports that opening the window directly above their stove often blows fumes toward them, with no reliably outward draft. Their main complaint is fried-food odor lingering for hours or days, especially after salmon. A window fan remains an untested possibility because a permanent installation would be unattractive and a temporary one inconvenient.
- jtriangle [[./thread#^c36821784|jtriangle]] describes using window sensors, an outdoor temperature sensor, and HomeAssistant to identify opportunities for ventilation without additional cooling demand. They report indoor humidity held around 40–50\% by an HVAC humidification system requiring annual service [[./thread#^c36822054|jtriangle]]. They do not supply before-and-after pollution measurements demonstrating the automation's effect.
- syntaxing [[./thread#^c36822349|syntaxing]] reports trying several popular portable induction units and finding their high-pitched hum objectionable. Their unanswered request for a quiet 120 V model adds an acoustic-comfort constraint to adopting induction.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The health significance of intermittent cooking peaks remains unquantified. bsuvc [[./thread#^c36821345|bsuvc]] questions whether rapidly subsiding elevations matter, dragonwriter [[./thread#^c36822091|dragonwriter]] emphasizes individual susceptibility, and Alupis [[./thread#^c36821903|Alupis]] requests cooktop-specific harm data. akira2501's appeal to the long history of restaurant cooking [[./thread#^c36821251|akira2501]] supplies neither occupational outcomes nor reassurance about safety.
- Whether the reported residential CO2 levels impair cognition remains unresolved within this source. verelo [[./thread#^c36822278|verelo]] asserts a threshold above 1,000 ppm, while jtriangle [[./thread#^c36821833|jtriangle]] dismisses substantially higher readings through occupational-limit reasoning. No cognitive test results or reproduced study evidence adjudicate these positions, and no one reports a measured sleep outcome.
- The best ventilation arrangement is not established across homes. rsync [[./thread#^c36821450|rsync]] explicitly qualifies their proposed airflow change; samldev [[./thread#^c36821148|samldev]] points to an external fan-placement demonstration; morsch [[./thread#^c36824008|morsch]] describes unsuccessful natural ventilation. The comments provide no comparable capture-efficiency or pollutant-decay measurements across these arrangements.
- Portable treatment effectiveness remains uncertain. LouisvilleGeek [[./thread#^c36821113|LouisvilleGeek]] says they use an Air Hood, but supplies no measured outcome; op00to [[./thread#^c36821146|op00to]] replies that charcoal does nothing for PM2.5. callalex [[./thread#^c36821557|callalex]] separately questions carbon longevity for VOCs. These concern different pollutant-removal claims and do not constitute a tested comparison of portable filtration, HEPA filtration, and outdoor exhaust.
- Some proposed appliance alternatives remain unanswered. Gene06603538031 [[./thread#^c36821659|Gene06603538031]] asks whether an air fryer would make more sense, and BlackLotus89 [[./thread#^c36821918|BlackLotus89]] asks about newer induction technology tolerating more cookware. The supplied replies establish neither comparative emissions from air fryers nor the claimed cookware compatibility improvement.
- The extent of induction's culinary compromise remains disputed. morsch [[./thread#^c36825117|morsch]] invokes a 100,000-BTU professional burner in the wok discussion; slily [[./thread#^c36826169|slily]] responds that home cooking can use much less, and morsch [[./thread#^c36826864|morsch]] distinguishes adequate home cooking from restaurant-style wok hei. slily's proposed hybrid arrangement [[./thread#^c36826234|slily]] is not accompanied by an exposure comparison showing how much occasional flame use would change air quality.
- Coverage limits matter for story 36820937, part 0: there are no supplied NO2 measurements or firsthand wildfire-smoke accounts. Outdoor pollution appears mainly as a condition on ventilation in jtriangle [[./thread#^c36821784|jtriangle]], while traffic appears in lm28469's particle-composition comparison [[./thread#^c36821687|lm28469]], not a measured traffic-exposure account. The cooking measurements and conflicting opinions here cannot settle those broader questions or establish population-wide agreement.
