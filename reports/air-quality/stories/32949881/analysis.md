# [Wildfire smoke is exposing millions of Americans to extreme pollution levels](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=32949881)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants discuss wildfire smoke as a serious and recurring source of fine-particle exposure, comparing episodic smoke with historical urban pollution, traffic, and indoor sources. Practical exchanges cover masks, indoor air filters, particle monitors, ventilation, and the limits of different filter types. A large thread debates drivers of severe fires, including climate change, drought, fire suppression, prescribed burns, fuel buildup, land use, and political choices; commenters challenge one another’s evidence and the risks of oversimplifying a complex problem. Other comments touch on health effects, historical exposure to smoke, and the difficulty of interpreting mortality patterns.
Tags: wildfire smoke, air quality, health, air filtration, fire management, climate change, public policy

## Relevance

Commenters describe prolonged wildfire smoke exposure, personal PM2.5 readings, respiratory discomfort, and precautions such as masks, closing windows, and indoor filtration. They compare purifier designs and discuss sensor readings, airflow, noise, and the limits of carbon filters versus particle filters. ## Arguments

- Wildfire protection should target particles: woah [[./thread#^c32953963|woah]] identifies PM2.5 as the health concern, while hollerith [[./thread#^c32954436|hollerith]] observes that the recommended carbon filter advertises VOC removal, not particulate removal. cameldrv [[./thread#^c32954751|cameldrv]] recommends a particle filter and explicitly allows effective furnace filters as alternatives to HEPA.
- Particle removal and odor removal address different objectives. danans [[./thread#^c32955413|danans]] proposes MERV 13 for PM2.5 and carbon for odor-carrying VOCs. amluto [[./thread#^c32961630|amluto]] likewise distinguishes particulate filtration from gas filtration, although their suggestion that carbon might remove some ozone or NOx is tentative and unsupported by measurements here.
- Affordable, partial filtration can still be useful. dynm [[./thread#^c32954118|dynm]] argues that the first increment of cleaning capacity provides substantial benefit and encourages inexpensive DIY filtration. This supports starting with an attainable improvement; it does not establish that every inexpensive design performs equally well.
- Filtered indoor spaces can provide relief during outdoor smoke events. Syonyk [[./thread#^c32952129|Syonyk]] reports acceptable indoor air with a filter-box fan and furnace filtration in a fairly tight house. WalterBright [[./thread#^c32955300|WalterBright]] describes substantially better air at home after upgrading HVAC filters and clean-smelling supermarket air. These are practical reports, not demonstrated health-outcome improvements.
- Well-fitting respirators are presented as a way to preserve outdoor activity. tzs [[./thread#^c32954316|tzs]] reports immediate relief with a KN95 during a smoke episode, and Syonyk [[./thread#^c32952479|Syonyk]] finds a reusable half-face respirator with P100 filters comfortable enough for sustained outdoor work. Neither supplies measured inhaled exposure.
- Duration matters when comparing smoke sources. Replying to jjcon’s campfire comparison [[./thread#^c32952942|jjcon]], Syonyk [[./thread#^c32953187|Syonyk]] distinguishes a few hours near a fire from weeks or months of polluted outdoor air. bombcar [[./thread#^c32953875|bombcar]] similarly contrasts a dispersing plume with widespread smoke. These arguments challenge the comparison without quantifying equivalent cumulative doses.
- Participants connect air pollution with effects beyond respiratory irritation. FollowingTheDao [[./thread#^c32952300|FollowingTheDao]] links papers concerning PM2.5 and mood instability; mensetmanusman [[./thread#^c32953290|mensetmanusman]] links material concerning Alzheimer’s, cancers, fertility, autism, and mood disorders. These are commenters’ claims about associations in linked research; the supplied text does not establish study quality, causal effects, or effect sizes.
- Cooking is treated as an important indoor source. bombcar [[./thread#^c32952250|bombcar]] highlights cooking, particularly gas cooking, while humanistbot [[./thread#^c32963010|humanistbot]] reports very high particle readings during hot-oil wok cooking on a gas stove. That observation supports investigating cooking exposure, but it does not separate emissions from the burner, oil, and food.

## Counterarguments

- The original carbon-only recommendation was corrected within its reply chain. wintermutestwin [[./thread#^c32957046|wintermutestwin]] wondered whether capturing smaller VOC molecules implied capturing smoke particles. hollerith [[./thread#^c32957752|hollerith]] explained the gas-versus-particle distinction, after which wintermutestwin [[./thread#^c32958644|wintermutestwin]] proposed adding MERV 13 material and acknowledged being unable to measure the resulting airflow loss.
- Sealing every gap around a box-fan filter was disputed. hcurtiss [[./thread#^c32954801|hcurtiss]] argues that bypass undermines filtration and later questions the experimental design [[./thread#^c32956090|hcurtiss]]. dynm [[./thread#^c32956419|dynm]] reports that sealing reduced throughput enough to approximately cancel the increased filtered fraction with their particular equipment, while explicitly expecting different results with stronger fans or less restrictive media.
- Raw airflow and purchase price do not settle practical value. keneda7 [[./thread#^c32952989|keneda7]] and iancmceachern [[./thread#^c32958787|iancmceachern]] preferred more expensive domestic purifiers after finding carbon-filter fan setups noisy. amalcon [[./thread#^c32954971|amalcon]] also objects that describing one device as two-thirds the dBA misuses a logarithmic scale; kurthr [[./thread#^c32956597|kurthr]] adds that perception, distance, and background noise affect comparisons.
- The claim that masks do not help with PM2.5 was narrowed by its author. boomchinolo78 [[./thread#^c32954455|boomchinolo78]] edited the comment to distinguish ordinary face coverings from KN95s after flaviut [[./thread#^c32954942|flaviut]] invoked KN95 particulate filtration. The exchange does not support treating every mask, fit, or wearing pattern as equivalent.
- Longstanding human exposure to fire does not establish safety. JoeAltmaier [[./thread#^c32951356|JoeAltmaier]] acknowledges health repercussions but hopes humans have some resilience, later suggesting adaptation [[./thread#^c32956885|JoeAltmaier]]. dahart [[./thread#^c32951973|dahart]] counters with claims about illness associated with indoor combustion, while jacobolus [[./thread#^c32953228|jacobolus]] supplies firsthand descriptions of severe discomfort in a house with an open wood fire. The degree of adaptation remains unsubstantiated.
- Historical pollution provides context without making current exposure harmless. zamfi [[./thread#^c32954507|zamfi]] cites an account of 1980s Los Angeles exceeding a modern AQI equivalent of 200 on over 40\% of days, explicitly disclaims minimizing harm, and later rejects the interpretation that conditions were better then [[./thread#^c32965049|zamfi]]. spoils19’s separate claim that experienced Americans already know how to cope [[./thread#^c32955465|spoils19]] is challenged by amluto [[./thread#^c32955625|amluto]] and TedDoesntTalk [[./thread#^c32956271|TedDoesntTalk]] on health grounds.

## Assumptions

- Purifier comparisons assume that advertised fan flow, filtered airflow, and effective particle-cleaning capacity are interchangeable. They are not shown to be interchangeable in this discussion: wintermutestwin [[./thread#^c32953074|wintermutestwin]] distinguishes fan and filter ratings, while dynm [[./thread#^c32956419|dynm]] describes substantial changes caused by filter resistance and sealing. Comparisons require matching the pollutant, operating speed, and filtration configuration.
- The numerical reports require caution about units. humanistbot [[./thread#^c32963010|humanistbot]] mixes AQI and PM2.5 terminology without clearly specifying a common scale; Syonyk [[./thread#^c32952129|Syonyk]] gives PM2.5 and PM10 readings without units. Treating those numbers as directly comparable mass concentrations, or converting them into health risks, would add information absent from the source.
- Smell, visible dirt, and perceived relief are useful observations but incomplete exposure measures. WalterBright [[./thread#^c32955300|WalterBright]] judges supermarket air partly by smell, dahart [[./thread#^c32952417|dahart]] notices smoke when removing a respirator, and bombcar [[./thread#^c32953765|bombcar]] explicitly calls inspecting a dirty N95 unscientific. These observations do not quantify PM2.5 dose or establish removal of gaseous pollutants.
- A visible laser beam is not a calibrated PM2.5 measurement. pesfandiar [[./thread#^c32953817|pesfandiar]] distinguishes larger visible particles from finer particles they say can enter the bloodstream; mensetmanusman [[./thread#^c32955063|mensetmanusman]] replies that laser scattering can reveal very small particles. Neither establishes the size distribution or concentration of the dust in the proposed household demonstration.
- Historical longevity cannot isolate smoke’s health effects. dymk [[./thread#^c32951513|dymk]] invokes deaths before age 30, but lotsofpulp [[./thread#^c32951595|lotsofpulp]] points out that childhood mortality and other causes confound that inference. VoodooJuJu [[./thread#^c32951985|VoodooJuJu]] and gruez [[./thread#^c32951803|gruez]] also offer conflicting survival estimates. This demographic detour supplies no direct estimate of harm from household smoke.
- Mask-use observations do not establish population-wide motivations. tzs [[./thread#^c32954316|tzs]] describes a local contrast between widespread pandemic masking and little masking during smoke. puffoflogic’s claim that 99\% of use was signaling [[./thread#^c32954330|puffoflogic]] is disputed by zamfi [[./thread#^c32957474|zamfi]]. Neither percentage-based explanation constitutes a survey or evidence about filtration effectiveness.

## Conditions

- The price comparisons are from September 2022. wintermutestwin [[./thread#^c32952859|wintermutestwin]] quotes roughly \$500 for an industrial carbon-filter setup and suggests used growing equipment could be cheaper; dynm [[./thread#^c32954118|dynm]] describes a roughly \$100 DIY purifier; humanistbot [[./thread#^c32953797|humanistbot]] quotes roughly \$500 for a three-stage industrial scrubber. These are different configurations, and neither current prices nor lifetime operating costs are established.
- Replacement-filter availability affects affordability and convenience. laminarflow [[./thread#^c32954968|laminarflow]] wants quiet domestic equipment using standard retail HVAC filters, and ycombinete [[./thread#^c32955551|ycombinete]] reports difficulty finding it. andbberger [[./thread#^c32955937|andbberger]] suggests a box fan. Bloating [[./thread#^c32955382|Bloating]] recommends a particular deeper HVAC filter as less restrictive and longer-lasting, but supplies no comparative measurements.
- Location, elevation, and season limit generalization. marricks [[./thread#^c32953210|marricks]] describes Pacific Northwest autumn conditions changing from usually below 20 AQI to weeks or months around 50, explicitly as an anecdote. landemva [[./thread#^c32953805|landemva]] attributes Denver episodes to geography trapping pollution, while throwaway1777 [[./thread#^c32953452|throwaway1777]] notes Massachusetts wildfires. These comments do not establish uniform regional exposure.
- An expensive home need not provide low outdoor exposure: wonder\_er [[./thread#^c32953529|wonder\_er]] describes an expensive house beside heavy traffic. clsec [[./thread#^c32954176|clsec]] reports soot near the Bay Bridge, whereas jejeyyy77 [[./thread#^c32953897|jejeyyy77]] reports no purifier indication in a 30th-floor highway-adjacent apartment. Neighborhood, height, building conditions, and measurement methods differ, preventing a controlled comparison.
- Opening windows has a conditional tradeoff, inferred rather than tested here. humanistbot [[./thread#^c32963010|humanistbot]] reports outdoor readings around 100–150, cooking peaks above 500, and filtered indoor readings around 5–15. If those readings are comparable, outdoor air could dilute a cooking peak yet worsen the filtered baseline. clsec’s report of soot just inside windows [[./thread#^c32954176|clsec]] illustrates outdoor intrusion, but neither commenter reports a controlled window-opening experiment.
- Housing configuration constrains the available improvements. Syonyk [[./thread#^c32952129|Syonyk]] qualifies successful filtration by describing a fairly tight house; humanistbot [[./thread#^c32963010|humanistbot]] has one small kitchen window and a microwave hood that recirculates air. These accounts concern specific buildings and do not establish a complete ventilation strategy for smoke, cooking gases, or occupant-generated pollutants.
- Respiratory protection also has financial and transport constraints. sneak [[./thread#^c32952150|sneak]] reports domestic powered respirators costing over \$1,000 during the pandemic and mentions cheaper imports without performance evidence. Syonyk [[./thread#^c32952129|Syonyk]] sometimes must ride a motorcycle because the household lacks spare cars, while rejecting helmet additions that could impair visibility [[./thread#^c32952504|Syonyk]]. Their proposed powered filtration remained a proposal.
- Cooking and heating choices depend on infrastructure and resources. jacobolus [[./thread#^c32953228|jacobolus]] describes an indoor wood fire as warm and convenient despite miserable smoke, and says people switch when resources permit. stinos [[./thread#^c32955482|stinos]] recalls figures suggesting wood stoves can dominate pollution in small villages; this is a remembered source claim, not a measurement from their household.
- Prescribed burning presents a geographically specific exposure tradeoff. x86\_64Ubuntu [[./thread#^c32951946|x86\_64Ubuntu]] says local smoke complaints can impede burns intended to reduce later wildfire risk, while julienchastang [[./thread#^c32953896|julienchastang]] describes New Mexico controlled burns becoming uncontrolled fires. These comments identify immediate smoke and escape concerns without quantifying whether particular programs reduce residents’ lifetime exposure.

## Lived experiences

- humanistbot [[./thread#^c32963010|humanistbot]] reports using an adjustable industrial scrubber during cooking, smog, and wildfire episodes. With outdoor readings described as roughly 100–150, intermittent 5–15-minute runs reportedly maintain indoor readings around 5–15. After hot-oil wok cooking, a sensor across the kitchen reportedly drops from over 500 to about 10 in 10–15 minutes. This is a concrete household measurement report, but units, calibration, and repeatability are unspecified.
- Syonyk [[./thread#^c32952129|Syonyk]], in the Boise/Treasure Valley area, reports PM2.5 readings in the 150s and PM10 above 250, without specifying units. They describe painful outdoor exposure, eye irritation while riding, and greater smoke sensitivity after substantial outdoor work in 2020. The persistent change is self-attributed rather than medically established. They also report useful indoor filtration and relief with P100 respiratory protection.
- dynm [[./thread#^c32954118|dynm]] reports testing a roughly \$100 DIY purifier at about 104 CFM on high and 71 CFM on a quieter medium setting. In the sealing discussion, dynm [[./thread#^c32956419|dynm]] describes approximately offsetting effects of increased filtration fraction and reduced airflow, explicitly limits the result to the tested combination, and mentions concern about the box-fan motor.
- hcurtiss [[./thread#^c32954801|hcurtiss]] describes running three box fans with fully taped MERV 11 and MERV 13 filters during Oregon’s 2020 Labor Day fires. They felt the devices worked very well but explicitly had no numerical results. Their belief that this performed as well as dynm’s design was a suspicion, not a measured comparison.
- hollerith [[./thread#^c32954436|hollerith]] reports owning both VOC-removal and particulate-removal equipment since the 1980s and using HEPA units during Bay Area smoke events in November 2018 and September 2020. Despite describing unusually strong VOC sensitivity associated with chronic illness, they did not feel a need to use the VOC equipment for those fires. No pollutant concentrations or clinical outcomes are supplied.
- tzs [[./thread#^c32954316|tzs]] reports outdoor AQI around 150–200 in the Puget Sound area, unpleasant breathing, and marked subjective improvement after putting on a KN95. WalterBright [[./thread#^c32955300|WalterBright]], replying about those days, reports better air indoors after upgrading HVAC filters, avoids strenuous activity during pollution, and resumes running when air quality is reported as good.
- dahart [[./thread#^c32952030|dahart]] describes three or four months of smoke largely eliminating outdoor activity in the previous year, followed by a mostly smoke-free year. Cycling with a disposable N95 caused sweating and deterioration of the mask [[./thread#^c32952417|dahart]]. They later identify the particular model and report another brand felt more comfortable, with some eye stinging remaining [[./thread#^c32952559|dahart]]. This illustrates individual fit and comfort differences.
- MichaelCollins [[./thread#^c32955462|MichaelCollins]] reports that their preferred mask leaks beside the nose and provides no smoke relief, while masks that fit properly feel intolerably uncomfortable. This is an account of inadequate fit and usability, not a test showing that properly fitted respirators fail.
- clsec [[./thread#^c32954176|clsec]] describes frequent soot cleanup on window sills and just inside windows near San Francisco’s Bay Bridge and greater satisfaction after moving west. In contrast, jejeyyy77 [[./thread#^c32953897|jejeyyy77]] says a Dyson purifier never reported pollution in a 30th-floor apartment beside a major highway. wonder\_er [[./thread#^c32953529|wonder\_er]] describes worry about roadside housing and scooter exposure, but reports no measurements or diagnosed consequences.
- keneda7 [[./thread#^c32952989|keneda7]] preferred a Medify MA-40 after replacing a carbon-filter/canfan setup, especially for noise. iancmceachern [[./thread#^c32958787|iancmceachern]] similarly found the industrial setup wearing and preferred a more expensive IQAir unit, described as nearly silent and rated up to 320 CFM. Neither supplies matched-speed noise tests or comparative particle measurements.
- mensetmanusman [[./thread#^c32953290|mensetmanusman]] reports using filter-based purifiers as white noise around their children for almost ten years. subsubzero [[./thread#^c32953158|subsubzero]] moves two Oransi units between home offices and bedrooms and reports reduced cooking odors. These are accounts of sustained use and perceived usefulness, without measured improvements in sleep, cognition, or illness.
- jacobolus [[./thread#^c32953228|jacobolus]] describes firsthand nights in a small earth-floor house with a central wood fire: warmth and cooking convenience accompanied by eye and throat stinging, choking, and smoke they found worse than heavily cigarette-smoked rooms. Their claims about long-term disease extend beyond the immediate symptoms they personally describe.

## Unresolved disagreements

- NO2 and CO2 remain largely unanswered by this source unit. amluto [[./thread#^c32961630|amluto]] only tentatively mentions carbon removing some NOx; this is not a measurement of NO2 removal. humanistbot’s gas-stove account [[./thread#^c32963010|humanistbot]] measures particles, not combustion gases. The supplied discussion provides no direct CO2 measurements, CO2-related symptoms, or demonstrated ventilation outcomes for either gas.
- Cognition and sleep outcomes are not established. FollowingTheDao [[./thread#^c32952300|FollowingTheDao]] cites mood-related research, while mensetmanusman [[./thread#^c32953290|mensetmanusman]] cites Alzheimer’s associations and describes purifier white noise. Neither provides cognitive testing, sleep measurements, or evidence that filtration caused a neurological or sleep improvement.
- Sensor reliability cannot be resolved. humanistbot [[./thread#^c32963010|humanistbot]] and Syonyk [[./thread#^c32952129|Syonyk]] provide useful numerical observations without sufficient calibration or measurement details; jejeyyy77 [[./thread#^c32953897|jejeyyy77]] reports a non-response from a purifier beside a highway. The latter could not, from this account alone, distinguish low exposure at that location from limits of detection, placement, or reporting.
- The headline airflow comparison is internally inconsistent. hnuser123456 [[./thread#^c32952997|hnuser123456]] reports a 350 CFM specification, while wintermutestwin [[./thread#^c32953074|wintermutestwin]] calculates 109 CFM from 1,312 square feet, ten-foot ceilings, and two air changes per hour. Taking those inputs literally gives approximately 437 CFM. The claimed performance ratio therefore remains unsupported, independently of the carbon-versus-particle distinction.
- The furnace-versus-room-purifier argument remains contested. amluto [[./thread#^c32961603|amluto]] distinguishes protecting equipment from particles that bypass a filter from removing particles through repeated room-air circulation, and asserts an IKEA design is superior to HEPA. hcurtiss [[./thread#^c32969199|hcurtiss]] rejects their account of furnace filters and intentional gaps. No comparative testing establishes the categorical design claims.
- Whether carbon adds worthwhile wildfire protection beyond particle filtration remains unclear. hollerith [[./thread#^c32954436|hollerith]] considers it unnecessary for their wildfire exposure despite VOC sensitivity; danans [[./thread#^c32955413|danans]] considers it useful for smoke odors. These positions partly concern different endpoints—lung protection and odor comfort—and neither provides measured gas concentrations or added health benefit.
- Comparative long-term risks remain unanswered. guitarbill [[./thread#^c32952434|guitarbill]] asks about persistent traffic exposure versus wildfire episodes; jjcon [[./thread#^c32952942|jjcon]] asks about campfires and whether other burning materials matter; stinos [[./thread#^c32955482|stinos]] introduces wood-stove pollution. The replies discuss duration and source differences without supplying comparable cumulative doses or quantified health risks.
- Effective purification without disposable supplies remains unproven here. causi [[./thread#^c32953320|causi]] asks the question; the8472 [[./thread#^c32953515|the8472]] proposes water scrubbing and notes ozone concerns with electrostatic filters; Bloating [[./thread#^c32955437|Bloating]] suggests washable electrostatic equipment; saurik [[./thread#^c32968314|saurik]] points out dirty-water disposal. Proposed cleaning or regeneration schemes receive no measured effectiveness, durability, or byproduct assessment.
- The childhood-allergy objection is speculative and unresolved. MichaelCollins [[./thread#^c32955478|MichaelCollins]] asks whether removing pollen could promote allergies; anonAndOn [[./thread#^c32955765|anonAndOn]] suggests outdoor daytime exposure might suffice. mensetmanusman’s reply about indoor plastic dust [[./thread#^c32959592|mensetmanusman]] raises another concern but does not answer the allergy question. No relevant outcomes or studies are supplied.
- The proposed contribution to excess mortality is unsupported quantitatively. After raising the question, 2devnull [[./thread#^c32958330|2devnull]] clarifies that they mean mortality after accounting for COVID and suggests possible interaction with smoke. coding123 [[./thread#^c32954254|coding123]] asserts that nearly 95\% is COVID-related, while jonnycomputer [[./thread#^c32953633|jonnycomputer]] also mentions overdoses. None supplies an analysis separating those effects or estimating a wildfire contribution.
