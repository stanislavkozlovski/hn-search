# [Where does air pollution come from?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43553663)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants discuss sources of air pollution and its health burden, including combustion, traffic, industry, household heating, shipping, agriculture, tire and brake wear, and natural sources such as sea spray. They debate how pollution exposure affects life expectancy and quality of life, how harms fall across communities, and whether deaths or quality-adjusted life years best communicate the impact. Other threads address measurement gaps and local variation, sensor reliability, masks and home filtration, shipping-fuel rules and enforcement, regulation and economic tradeoffs, and the roles of energy systems, transport, and agriculture.
Tags: air pollution sources, public health, particulate matter, traffic emissions, shipping, agriculture, air-quality measurement, environmental justice, regulation, air filtration

## Relevance

Although much of the discussion concerns pollution sources generally, comments address local differences in exposure, respiratory and cardiovascular effects, and firsthand indoor filtration during wildfire smoke. They also raise indoor CO2 and the tradeoff between purifying air and ventilating. ## Arguments

- Several commenters describe pollution as causing chronic disease and increasing vulnerability, rather than merely accompanying terminal illness. thaumasiotes [[./thread#^c43554028|thaumasiotes]] uses the analogy of starvation increasing frailty; julianeon [[./thread#^c43554062|julianeon]] lists respiratory disease, cardiovascular damage, childhood developmental effects, and cancer. These are explanatory claims, not personal measurements or results established within the export.
- Sam6late [[./thread#^c43574666|Sam6late]] reports that studies associate PM2.5, PM10, and NO2 exposure with increased blood pressure and hypertension, with NO2 showing the strongest association in their summary. No effect sizes, study methods, or exposure histories are supplied. The accompanying account of a colleague's conversation with a doctor is secondhand, not a documented personal exposure assessment.
- tim333 [[./thread#^c43559029|tim333]] cites a reported 5.5-year life-expectancy difference associated with northern China's free coal-heating policy. This supports the thread's argument that pollution can cost substantial years of life, but the comment does not reproduce the study's methods or establish how that estimate transfers to other exposures.
- The discussion identifies multiple sources requiring different interventions. TimByte [[./thread#^c43554160|TimByte]] emphasizes combustion; vasco [[./thread#^c43554026|vasco]] highlights fireplaces, cooking, oil lighting, and candles; kmoser [[./thread#^c43554042|kmoser]] points to tire and brake particles. These source claims do not establish their relative contributions in a particular home or neighborhood.
- Affordable particle filtration is presented as a practical improvement. cassepipe [[./thread#^c43556478|cassepipe]] says a linked purifier discussion made effective filtration seem financially accessible; passwordoops [[./thread#^c43556535|passwordoops]] reports substantial subjective benefit from homemade Corsi–Rosenthal boxes during wildfires. The thread supplies neither purchase-cost comparisons nor measured particle-removal rates.
- Several comments favor checking local exposure instead of relying exclusively on city summaries. nomilk [[./thread#^c43554422|nomilk]] describes a mismatch between perceived air quality and published readings, while fedeb95 [[./thread#^c43555014|fedeb95]], replying to competing explanations for a smoky town, argues for installing sensors to establish what is happening.
- Personal protection is another proposed intervention: Jiahang [[./thread#^c43554257|Jiahang]] reports continuing to wear a mask after the pandemic, and rudolftheone [[./thread#^c43555027|rudolftheone]] recommends a well-fitted N95 for PM2.5 rather than relying on surgical or cloth masks. The recommendation is not accompanied by fit testing, exposure measurements, or a reported health outcome.

## Counterarguments

- johnthesecure [[./thread#^c43553996|johnthesecure]] asks whether pollution-attributed deaths sometimes represent relatively little lost life and proposes quality-adjusted life years; concordDance [[./thread#^c43554475|concordDance]] emphasizes the difference between months and decades lost. philjohn [[./thread#^c43554014|philjohn]] counters that asthma can affect people otherwise capable of long lives and cites a child's death. The discussion supports distinguishing mortality, disability, and years lost, but does not quantify that distribution.
- The London docks account is contested geographically and causally. noneeeed [[./thread#^c43554420|noneeeed]] attributes unusually high childhood asthma near docks to ships. nohuck13 [[./thread#^c43554673|nohuck13]] disputes the container-port geography, then acknowledges Greenwich cruise visits while questioning whether three or four ships monthly establish the claimed asthma burden [[./thread#^c43555945|nohuck13]]. noneeeed [[./thread#^c43556595|noneeeed]] narrows the claim to a deprived local pocket and acknowledges relying on older documentary and article coverage.
- Visible smoke and odor do not establish which source dominates exposure. Replying to xlii's town account, matsemann [[./thread#^c43554553|matsemann]] suggests that some apparent chimney smoke could be water and that traffic-generated dust at breathing height might matter more locally. This is an alternative hypothesis, not an identification of what xlii actually observed.
- Replacing combustion vehicles does not eliminate every traffic-related particle source. Against Calwestjobs's characterization of electric-car tire emissions as equivalent [[./thread#^c43555002|Calwestjobs]], NoahKAndrews [[./thread#^c43555604|NoahKAndrews]] argues that weight and acceleration can increase tire wear. xethos [[./thread#^c43555853|xethos]] expects regenerative braking to reduce brake dust. These comments identify competing mechanisms without measuring their net effect on nearby people's exposure.
- Ability to choose housing does not make wealth a reliable air-quality proxy. alcover [[./thread#^c43554675|alcover]] and ArnoVW [[./thread#^c43554830|ArnoVW]] argue that money allows avoidance of undesirable locations; graemep [[./thread#^c43555191|graemep]] notes expensive central-London housing. Lutger [[./thread#^c43554874|Lutger]] adds that wood burning can be a luxury practice in their unspecified country, potentially creating pollution in wealthier areas.
- Historical improvements and remaining exposure can coexist. spwa4 [[./thread#^c43554733|spwa4]] points to the removal of leaded gasoline, and pjc50 [[./thread#^c43555159|pjc50]] mentions smoking and urban coal-burning restrictions. Replying to the improvement argument, jajko [[./thread#^c43555723|jajko]] recalls much greater traffic than during childhood. These comments do not provide comparable measurements establishing the net trend in any particular person's exposure.
- Aeolun [[./thread#^c43555074|Aeolun]] questions whether childhood air-pollution mortality should take priority over malnutrition and violence. whyoh [[./thread#^c43555244|whyoh]] disputes the proposed ranking and argues that pollution is especially salient in India and Southeast Asia. Their linked comparisons, populations, and age groups are not sufficiently aligned in the supplied comments to settle the prioritization dispute.

## Assumptions

- The numerical household CO2 report and the cooking-related PM2.5 report have limited measurement detail. fvrghl [[./thread#^c43556525|fvrghl]] supplies approximately 1200 ppm with windows closed; stephen\_g [[./thread#^c43556327|stephen\_g]] reports raised PM2.5 during cooking without concentrations. Neither names a sensor, calibration procedure, reference instrument, sampling period, or uncertainty. No firsthand NO2 concentration measurement is supplied.
- Reported symptom changes do not isolate a pollutant or prove a causal mechanism. philjohn [[./thread#^c43554014|philjohn]] associates improved asthma symptoms with reduced lockdown traffic, while vladvasiliu [[./thread#^c43554392|vladvasiliu]] associates easier forest running and urban throat irritation with air quality. Neither supplies concurrent pollutant readings or a controlled comparison.
- Emission categories and quantities should not be substituted for personal exposure. tpm [[./thread#^c43557371|tpm]] quotes a claim that gas boilers produce approximately 72\% of central London's NOx emissions; that is not a measurement of someone's NO2 exposure. Sam6late's approximately 6.1-million-ton tire-dust figure includes atmosphere and waterways [[./thread#^c43574666|Sam6late]], and Calwestjobs's comparison of fuel burned with tire mass lost [[./thread#^c43557146|Calwestjobs]] does not establish relative inhaled particle dose or toxicity.
- The regional mortality arithmetic contains an acknowledged error. Using the populations and deaths quoted in the discussion, rdtsc [[./thread#^c43554333|rdtsc]] calculates an Asian per-capita rate roughly nine times Australia's, contradicting NhanH's initial Australia-higher claim. NhanH [[./thread#^c43556690|NhanH]] acknowledges the mistake. defrost's suggestion that reporting quality affects comparisons [[./thread#^c43554186|defrost]] remains an unquantified hypothesis.
- The childhood mortality calculations also require caution. pjsousa [[./thread#^c43554807|pjsousa79]] reports 2019 rates per 100,000 children under five; jajko [[./thread#^c43555526|jajko]] applies the Nigerian rate to an entire city's population, which cannot establish the stated number of child deaths. Aeolun's later approximately 600-to-one comparison [[./thread#^c43563611|Aeolun]] combines different-year statistics without documenting compatible denominators and time bases.
- Healthspan cannot simply be quantified from the mortality discussion. zemvpferreira [[./thread#^c43554866|zemvpferreira]] proposes that pollution affects lifespan and healthspan proportionately, making QALYs less necessary. That proportionality is an assumption in the comment, not an estimate supported by data reproduced here.
- Large source ratios and spatial claims remain assertions. Calwestjobs [[./thread#^c43555002|Calwestjobs]] proposes a 99\% street-to-beach reduction and motorcycle or lawnmower emissions equivalent to hundreds of cars, without specifying comparable pollutants, operating conditions, or measurements. Those figures cannot establish a particular beach's safety or an individual's exposure.

## Conditions

- Window opening involves a conditional tradeoff that this thread does not measure. fvrghl [[./thread#^c43556525|fvrghl]] proposes opening windows in response to closed-window CO2 accumulation. The wildfire account from passwordoops [[./thread#^c43556535|passwordoops]] and the neighborhood smoke account from xlii [[./thread#^c43554484|xlii]] identify circumstances in which incoming outdoor air could introduce particles. Whether ventilation helps overall therefore depends on the outdoor pollutants and the indoor problem being addressed.
- Geographic detail matters. nomilk [[./thread#^c43554422|nomilk]] contrasts motorcycle-heavy streets with a nearby beach in Nha Trang, and busy roads with greener spaces in Singapore; these are proposed explanations for perceived differences, not measured transects. dynm [[./thread#^c43555834|dynm]] adds that coastal sea spray can contribute to PM2.5, so a coastal location alone does not establish low particle readings.
- Purchase price, noise, electricity, and filter maintenance affect practical usability. cassepipe [[./thread#^c43558229|cassepipe]] favors an IKEA purifier's size, low-setting noise, prefilter, lower power demand, and optional charcoal filter. mfro [[./thread#^c43556712|mfro]] values standard HVAC filters but finds their Lasko unit loud. ajolly [[./thread#^c43563624|ajolly]] recommends computer-fan Corsi–Rosenthal boxes as quieter with lower total ownership cost, without supplying comparative tests.
- Cold-climate road safety complicates reducing road dust. originalvichy [[./thread#^c43554439|originalvichy]] describes studded tires and road sand producing dust on dry spring and autumn roads. Maxion [[./thread#^c43554502|Maxion]] explains the need for maintained snow or ice surfaces on Finnish dirt roads, while bjoli [[./thread#^c43554505|bjoli]] supplies Swedish observations of vehicles sliding off roads. These accounts describe a safety–pollution tradeoff, not measured particulate concentrations.
- Shore power requires compatible ships and substantial port infrastructure. noneeeed [[./thread#^c43555183|noneeeed]] identifies compatibility at both ends as a condition. macNchz [[./thread#^c43556040|macNchz]] reports that Manhattan needs a new substation, while Brooklyn installed an eight-figure system that some ships initially did not use; subsequent requirements applied to capable ships. This is a local implementation account, without before-and-after neighborhood exposure measurements.
- Local economic dependence can constrain source control. In response to the smoky-town discussion, Tuna-Fish [[./thread#^c43555359|Tuna-Fish]] proposes that employment and tax dependence can discourage enforcement without requiring corruption. rsynnott [[./thread#^c43555644|rsynnott]] counters from reading Irish EPA enforcement records that threatened closures often did not follow compliance. The industrial explanation is introduced by the replies; xlii [[./thread#^c43554484|xlii]] did not identify the ownership or precise sources of the town's chimneys.
- An agency's scope limits what its successes imply about city air. rsynnott [[./thread#^c43557846|rsynnott]] distinguishes Irish EPA industrial enforcement from Dublin's traffic problems, which they place outside that agency's remit. Thus their claims of cleaner industry do not establish clean air beside roads or across Dublin.
- Water-based filtration introduces different maintenance concerns. xnx [[./thread#^c43558972|xnx]] asks about visibly collecting pollution in water; stephen\_g [[./thread#^c43563546|stephen\_g]] identifies industrial wet scrubbers but knows of no suitable household unit and raises humidity, mold, and bacterial-growth concerns. This is a proposed alternative and a caution, not a tested domestic comparison.

## Lived experiences

- fvrghl [[./thread#^c43556525|fvrghl]] reports that their apartment sits around 1200 ppm CO2 with windows closed. They do not report symptoms, an open-window reading, outdoor particle levels, or a measured change in purifier performance.
- stephen\_g [[./thread#^c43556327|stephen\_g]] reports using several HEPA purifiers and measuring elevated PM2.5 when cooking with an electric oven or induction stove, even without burning the food. This is firsthand evidence of a detected cooking-associated increase, but its magnitude, duration, sensor reliability, and health significance are unspecified.
- passwordoops [[./thread#^c43556535|passwordoops]] reports making a couple of Corsi–Rosenthal boxes every few months and finding them especially helpful during wildfires a couple of years earlier. The improvement is qualitative: the comment does not distinguish reduced odor, symptoms, visible haze, or instrument readings.
- mfro [[./thread#^c43556712|mfro]] reports buying a Lasko Air Flex, finding it sufficiently loud to serve as white noise, and seeing accumulated dust over a year. Their power-consumption assessment comes from a linked review. Visible collected dust demonstrates collection of some material, but does not quantify PM2.5 removal or establish improved sleep.
- philjohn [[./thread#^c43554014|philjohn]], identifying as asthmatic, reports markedly improved symptoms during COVID-19 lockdowns and connects this with reduced road traffic. This is a firsthand symptom account, supplemented by a study link, without personal exposure measurements.
- vladvasiliu [[./thread#^c43554392|vladvasiliu]], who says they are not asthmatic, reports that forest runs near their parents' suburban home felt substantially easier despite running faster and longer than in their dense-city setting. They also recall a rough throat and short coughing bouts after returning to a congested city following lockdown. These are subjective respiratory and comfort observations.
- xlii [[./thread#^c43554484|xlii]] reports repeatedly driving through a town with a plastic-like smell and winter chimney plumes, despite the area appearing clean through a regional hilltop sensor. The shopkeeper's account that the council opposed sensors is hearsay within this firsthand observation; neither emissions nor the sensor's representativeness were independently measured.
- k\_\_ [[./thread#^c43554659|k\_\_]] recalls police driving through their small town advising residents to stay home because a tire yard was burning again. This documents an experienced smoke-related warning, but provides no indoor/outdoor readings or evidence of how protective remaining home was.
- aeroman [[./thread#^c43554606|aeroman]] says they worked on a study finding substantial compliance with 2015 shipping-fuel rules around the United States and Canada, including offshore despite limited monitoring. This is firsthand involvement in research, distinct from a personal air-quality reading; the comment supplies no methods, concentrations, or health outcomes.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The ventilation–filtration question receives no supplied answer. fvrghl's belief that a purifier might do nothing with windows open [[./thread#^c43556525|fvrghl]] is explicitly a supposition. There is no paired CO2/PM2.5 experiment, outdoor comparison, airflow information, or test establishing whether purification remains useful under those conditions.
- The source unit does not establish effects on cognition or sleep, or a health threshold for its reported CO2 level. fvrghl [[./thread#^c43556525|fvrghl]] gives a concentration without symptoms, while mfro [[./thread#^c43556712|mfro]] describes white-noise use without a sleep outcome. Likewise, cassepipe's mention of charcoal-filter compatibility [[./thread#^c43558229|cassepipe]] supplies no demonstrated NO2 or CO2 removal.
- The relative toxicity of different PM2.5 compositions remains open. stephen\_g [[./thread#^c43556327|stephen\_g]] wonders whether non-burnt cooking aerosols and sea spray are less harmful than combustion or tire particles. dynm [[./thread#^c43556874|dynm]] agrees that composition matters but says quantification is poor and cautions that wood smoke undermines an assumption that natural sources are safe. No source-specific risk estimates are provided.
- The spatial scale of exposure differences is unresolved. nomilk [[./thread#^c43554422|nomilk]] suspects major variation over approximately 100 meters; djrj477dhsnv [[./thread#^c43554816|djrj477dhsnv]] hypothesizes that larger particles and odorous chemicals vary more locally while PM2.5 spreads more evenly. Neither provides measurements sufficient to establish the pattern or explain the mismatch between perception and city readings.
- London's source attribution remains unsettled at the relevant neighborhood scale. pjc50 [[./thread#^c43555138|pjc50]] emphasizes cars and buses; tpm [[./thread#^c43557371|tpm]] introduces central-London boiler NOx emissions; noneeeed [[./thread#^c43556595|noneeeed]] maintains that docked ships create a concentrated additional burden. Different pollutants, geographic boundaries, and time periods prevent treating these claims as directly interchangeable estimates.
- The thread does not resolve the underlying mortality-estimation methods. vasco [[./thread#^c43554026|vasco]] characterizes attribution as guesswork; DeathArrow [[./thread#^c43554300|DeathArrow]] rejects linked claims as insufficient proof after OsrsNeedsf2P [[./thread#^c43554092|OsrsNeedsf2P]] points to the article's citations. hmottestad [[./thread#^c43554058|hmottestad]] quotes an explanation of modeled avoidable deaths. The supplied excerpts support identifying this methodological disagreement, but do not establish either that the estimates are arbitrary or that every cited estimate is reliable.
