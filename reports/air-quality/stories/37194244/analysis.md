# [AirGradient Air Quality Monitor Pro Kit Review](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=37194244)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion considers AirGradient monitors as part of broader interest in public air quality measurement, open hardware, and sensor networks. Participants discuss the shortage and funding of official monitoring stations, calibration and accuracy challenges, and the maker’s effort to compare devices with reference monitors. Other topics include cloud data defaults, privacy, Home Assistant and Zigbee integration, shipping delays, and personal experiences tracking smoke and indoor air leaks.
Tags: AirGradient, air quality monitoring, open hardware, Home Assistant, sensor networks

## Relevance

Commenters discuss monitor calibration and sensor limitations, plus firsthand experience using particle readings and HEPA filtration during wildfire smoke. One account describes finding and sealing air leaks to improve indoor conditions; another cautions that sealing a home without designed ventilation can be unsafe. ## Arguments

- jh00ker [[./thread#^c37196749|jh00ker]] argues that personal monitoring can reveal smoke entry points that closed windows and air purifiers leave untreated. Their account supports measuring the combined effect of locating drafts, sealing them, and running HEPA filtration, but does not isolate each intervention’s contribution.
- ck2 [[./thread#^c37195635|ck2]] advocates dense, publicly accessible monitoring in homes, cities, schools, and universities, preferring PurpleAir to what they describe as sparse AirNow coverage. This is a proposal and comparative opinion, without a supplied coverage analysis.
- ahaucnx [[./thread#^c37195821|ahaucnx]], speaking for AirGradient, describes a global project placing inexpensive monitors beside reference instruments to develop publicly available corrections for conditions such as high humidity. This offers a route toward better accuracy; the comment supplies no validation results.
- xvfLJfx9 [[./thread#^c37197671|xvfLJfx9]] favors ZigBee for lower battery consumption and avoiding stored WiFi credentials on a stolen monitor; infogulch [[./thread#^c37198562|infogulch]] agrees. These are practical preferences, without measured power comparisons. stavros [[./thread#^c37199126|stavros]] adds a time-specific caveat that, to their knowledge in 2023, relevant ESP32 support remained in beta.

## Counterarguments

- Responding directly to the wildfire-sealing account, BHSPitMonkey [[./thread#^c37200607|BHSPitMonkey]] warns that many homes, especially older ones without mechanical ventilation, depend on leakage for air exchange. This challenges treating comprehensive sealing as universally beneficial, without establishing that jh00ker’s particular intervention caused harm.
- gr33nman [[./thread#^c37283761|gr33nman]] questions metal-oxide VOC sensor accuracy because of drift, instability, and sensitivity to temperature, humidity, and inorganic gases. They explicitly base this on limited research, not measurements of this monitor, and ask about electrochemical or photoionization alternatives.
- FirmwareBurner [[./thread#^c37197331|FirmwareBurner]] condemns the default upload behavior reported by yannvon [[./thread#^c37197271|yannvon]]. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c37197368|ahaucnx]] counters that the open-source firmware can use another endpoint or ESPHome without sending data to AirGradient. stavros [[./thread#^c37199116|stavros]], explicitly not a product user, considers the default dashboard convenient. Configurability addresses compulsory dependence, but does not settle objections to the default.

## Assumptions

- jh00ker [[./thread#^c37196749|jh00ker]] calls the Wynd tracker seemingly accurate after comparing outdoor readings with PurpleAir. That is an informal consistency check, not reference-instrument calibration. Their reported “0 AQI” does not establish the absence of every pollutant, and no pollutant-specific concentrations accompany it.
- Havoc [[./thread#^c37197332|Havoc]] says auto-calibration generally requires exposure to outdoor air and recommends placement near an openable window. The advice leaves the sensor, calibration procedure, and suitable outdoor conditions unspecified; it cannot establish a universal window-opening procedure.
- The proposals for denser networks presume inexpensive monitors can provide useful local coverage. ck2 [[./thread#^c37195635|ck2]] supplies a coverage judgment, while jiggawatts [[./thread#^c37195826|jiggawatts]] estimates roughly \$100 per device; neither supplies network-wide accuracy results or a full deployment and operating budget.
- user3939382 [[./thread#^c37196716|user3939382]] treats several firmware updates as evidence that an Aqara particulate monitor with HomeKit integration is reasonably mature and stable. That does not independently establish measurement accuracy. Likewise, gerogerke’s [[./thread#^c37199396|gerogerke]] report that an IKEA monitor exposes a proprietary VOC value does not explain that value’s calibration or health interpretation.

## Conditions

- The successful smoke-control account concerns the Bay Area during the November 2018 Camp Fire, over a weekend. jh00ker [[./thread#^c37196749|jh00ker]] identifies contaminated outdoor air entering through a fireplace and a garage-door gap. Its applicability depends on outdoor conditions and building ventilation; BHSPitMonkey [[./thread#^c37200607|BHSPitMonkey]] specifically raises older homes that depend on leakage.
- gerogerke [[./thread#^c37199396|gerogerke]] quotes €40 for an IKEA Vindstyrka, plus the ZigBee/Home Assistant setup. yannvon [[./thread#^c37197271|yannvon]] received a free AirGradient PCB, so that favorable ownership account does not establish retail affordability or total installation cost.
- Availability constrained the options described: jh00ker [[./thread#^c37196749|jh00ker]] says the standalone Wynd tracker had long been sold out, although available in a purifier bundle. ghostpepper [[./thread#^c37204436|ghostpepper]] characterizes AirGradient’s lead time as six-plus weeks, but quotes one to three weeks before shipment and two to three weeks for delivery to most destinations in North America, Europe, and Australia—approximately three to six weeks combined. These are contemporaneous availability claims, not verified delivery experiences.

## Lived experiences

- During the Camp Fire, jh00ker [[./thread#^c37196749|jh00ker]] used a Wynd tracker to locate smoke infiltration, sealed the identified gaps, and ran several HEPA purifiers. They report reaching “0 AQI” throughout the home within a couple of hours and maintaining it through the weekend. They also recall coworkers having raspy voices on Monday despite, according to the commenter, keeping windows closed and using purifiers. Those coworkers’ exposures were not measured, and the observation does not establish a causal health comparison.
- jiggawatts [[./thread#^c37195826|jiggawatts]] recounts attending a meeting with their state’s air-quality service in an unspecified country. They initially understood a proposed deployment as 50,000 devices, then learned it meant 50. They say officials reported that a budget of a few million had already been consumed by planning. This is a firsthand account of a meeting, not an audited account of spending or proof that all public monitoring programs operate similarly.
- yannvon [[./thread#^c37197271|yannvon]] reports being happy with an AirGradient PCB received several years earlier, but was surprised that the default Arduino code requested WiFi access and uploaded collected data to AirGradient.
- infogulch [[./thread#^c37195810|infogulch]] found the Pro Kit easy to assemble but found local integration confusing: they report that the web portal disappeared after WiFi setup and were unsure whether Home Assistant required firmware or configuration changes. In reply, ahaucnx [[./thread#^c37195852|ahaucnx]] specifies flashing the D1 mini with ESPHome and an AirGradient configuration. That clarifies an available route, but the supplied exchange does not document infogulch completing it.
- gerogerke [[./thread#^c37199396|gerogerke]] reports actually running an IKEA Vindstyrka through Home Assistant, receiving PM2.5 readings and a proprietary VOC value. They provide no readings, exposure event, reference comparison, or resulting health or comfort change.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The central exposure tradeoff remains unquantified: jh00ker [[./thread#^c37196749|jh00ker]] reports successful smoke reduction after sealing leaks and filtering, while BHSPitMonkey [[./thread#^c37200607|BHSPitMonkey]] warns about losing necessary ventilation. There is no simultaneous particle-and-CO2 record or open-versus-closed-window experiment to show when additional outdoor air would help overall or worsen exposure. Havoc’s [[./thread#^c37197332|Havoc]] window suggestion concerns calibration, not demonstrated health benefits.
- The CO2 exchange supplies no evidence of cognitive injury: kortilla’s [[./thread#^c37196831|kortilla]] assertion that the smoke-account commenter had CO2-related brain damage reads as a sarcastic reply. stavros [[./thread#^c37199103|stavros]] responds by suggesting monoxide and asserting that excess dioxide can be felt. Neither supplies gas measurements, diagnoses, or study evidence; these comments cannot explain the reported raspy voices.
- For the approved question, this supplied part offers no NO2 measurements, numerical CO2 readings, numerical PM2.5 concentrations, cooking or traffic exposure comparisons, sleep outcomes, or measured cognition or comfort effects. Its concrete exposure result is jh00ker’s [[./thread#^c37196749|jh00ker]] reported AQI change; gerogerke [[./thread#^c37199396|gerogerke]] identifies available sensor outputs without reporting values. The linked review itself has no supplied text.
- Sensor reliability remains incompletely established. gr33nman’s [[./thread#^c37283761|gr33nman]] questions about VOC drift and alternative sensing technologies receive no supplied answer, while ahaucnx’s [[./thread#^c37195821|ahaucnx]] reference co-location project supplies an intended methodology without performance results. The discussion does not establish how accurately these devices perform across environments.
- Default cloud reporting remains a preference dispute. FirmwareBurner [[./thread#^c37197331|FirmwareBurner]] objects to the behavior itself, whereas stavros [[./thread#^c37199116|stavros]] values convenience and ahaucnx [[./thread#^c37197368|ahaucnx]] emphasizes user control. The technical ability to change firmware or endpoints does not resolve whether the initial default is acceptable or sufficiently easy to change.
- The proposed public-network expansion is only partly concrete. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c37195779|ahaucnx]] says WeatherSTEM integration remains on their to-do list, while separately reporting automatic sharing of public outdoor data with OpenAQ [[./thread#^c37201213|ahaucnx]]. That existing sharing arrangement does not demonstrate that ck2’s [[./thread#^c37195635|ck2]] desired geographic coverage or school-monitoring network has been achieved.
