# [Is Wildfire Smoke Making Our Fog More Persistent?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=24684626)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion covers whether wildfire smoke and aerosols contribute to unusually persistent fog, with reports of lingering fog along the Pacific coast and in the Pacific Northwest. Participants also compare smoke-related air-quality readings, question the accuracy of low-cost particle sensors, and discuss cloud brightening, reflective surfaces, and the source author's broader public commentary.
Tags: wildfire smoke, fog, air quality, sensors, geoengineering, climate

## Relevance

Firsthand wildfire-smoke measurements compare indoor air with filtration against severe outdoor PM readings, and commenters discuss sensor limitations and smoke exposure. The fog discussion also includes personal observations, though those observations alone do not establish a cause. ## Arguments

- Filtration appears useful in one wildfire-exposure account: hexbinencoded [[./thread#^c24686007|hexbinencoded]] reports indoor PM2.5 of 5 µg/m³ with two MERV 14 filters operating, versus 57 µg/m³ outside at the time. This supports a practical benefit in that setting, although it is an observational comparison rather than a controlled measurement of filter efficiency.
- Appearance alone can be misleading about outdoor exposure. hexbinencoded [[./thread#^c24686007|hexbinencoded]] describes elevated particulate readings in air resembling gray fog, while Tade0 [[./thread#^c24685807|Tade0]] finds local fog and smog difficult to distinguish by color. These accounts support caution about interpreting fog-like conditions as evidence of clean air.
- Commenters propose mechanisms connecting combustion pollution with persistent fog. seaish [[./thread#^c24685361|seaish]] suggests reduced sunlight, additional particles, and possibly water produced by combustion, explicitly questioning the water contribution. sjg007 [[./thread#^c24687567|sjg007]] invokes coal-smoke-induced London fog as an analogy. These are explanatory claims, not supplied experimental results establishing the wildfire–fog relationship.

## Counterarguments

- Replying directly to the Paradise measurements, jhpankow [[./thread#^c24687793|jhpankow]] claims the PMS5003 counts particles and estimates their size distribution mathematically. After shortandsweet [[./thread#^c24688213|shortandsweet]] asks what it actually measures, jhpankow [[./thread#^c24689378|jhpankow]] describes a fan, emitter, and detector counting particles large enough to interrupt light. This challenges interpretation of the separate PM size categories; it does not provide an independent calibration or alternative indoor/outdoor readings.
- Low particle readings do not establish that every wildfire pollutant has been addressed. Replying to hexbinencoded's measurements, guenthert [[./thread#^c24690062|guenthert]] warns that combustion chemicals associated with the smoke's smell are not captured by those readings. The comment supplies no chemical identification, concentration, or measurement demonstrating how much residual exposure remained.
- Persistent fog alongside smoke does not by itself establish causation. qchris [[./thread#^c24691337|qchris]] explicitly limits the concrete coastal comparison to one day; throwawaysea [[./thread#^c24684986|throwawaysea]] is unsure whether fog suppressed wind or calm conditions sustained fog; and cols [[./thread#^c24685323|cols]] notes that seasonal fog is already normal locally, although its occurrence during warmer weather seemed unusual.

## Assumptions

- Attributing the entire indoor/outdoor PM difference to filtration would require assumptions about measurement comparability, outdoor-air infiltration, and other indoor conditions. hexbinencoded [[./thread#^c24686007|hexbinencoded]] supplies the filter setup and readings but no unfiltered indoor baseline or controlled before/after comparison. The earlier outdoor peak of 893 µg/m³ should not be paired with the later indoor value to calculate filtration efficiency.
- Calling the gray outdoor haze 'UFPs' is hexbinencoded's interpretation [[./thread#^c24686007|hexbinencoded]]. The supplied PM mass readings do not separately establish an ultrafine-particle concentration or exclude simultaneous water fog, particularly given jhpankow's claim that particle-size distributions are estimated [[./thread#^c24687793|jhpankow]].
- Use Tade0's corrected seasonal PM2.5 figure of 200+ µg/m³ [[./thread#^c24685807|Tade0]]. After hexbinencoded [[./thread#^c24685949|hexbinencoded]] requests mass-concentration units, Tade0 [[./thread#^c24686075|Tade0]] says they checked the source and found micrograms. The original ppm notation and intervening speculative soot conversion should not be treated as validated measurements; this is a checked source report, not an identified personal sensor reading.

## Conditions

- The geographic scope is regional and heterogeneous. rob74 [[./thread#^c24685258|rob74]] identifies the article's 'our fog' as referring to Seattle, while WD-42 [[./thread#^c24688577|WD-42]] reports persistent, out-of-season fog in coastal California. Similar observations across these places do not establish identical pollution levels or mechanisms.
- Paradise and the Bay Area represent different reported wildfire conditions: hexbinencoded [[./thread#^c24686007|hexbinencoded]] supplies exceptionally high outdoor particulate readings in Paradise, while guenthert [[./thread#^c24690062|guenthert]] describes Bay Area conditions as less severe without numerical measurements. hexbinencoded was packing to move, but jeffbee's question about the final reason for leaving [[./thread#^c24688121|jeffbee]] receives no supplied answer; smoke-related illness or a specific relocation motive cannot be inferred.
- Pollution sources and locations also differ outside the wildfire accounts. Tade0 [[./thread#^c24685807|Tade0]] describes recurring high PM2.5 in an unnamed location; cyberdrunk [[./thread#^c24685904|cyberdrunk]], replying to Tade0, attributes recognizable local winter smog to coal and other burned materials. Neither location is identified, and their experiences should not be assigned to Seattle or California.

## Lived experiences

- hexbinencoded [[./thread#^c24686007|hexbinencoded]] reports indoor PM1.0/PM2.5/PM10.0 of 4/5/5 µg/m³ with two MPR 2900/MERV 14 filters and a fan at a 50\% duty cycle, compared with 31/57/75 outside. On September 28, outdoor readings were 815/893/920, with an obscured, dark sky and red sun. The stated method is a 15-minute sliding average from a Plantower PMS5003, identified by the commenter as the sensor used in the PurpleAir PA-II. These are firsthand instrument reports without supplied independent validation.
- throwawaysea [[./thread#^c24684986|throwawaysea]] describes Puget Sound fog lingering all day, unusually still water, and little apparent wind. The commenter explicitly leaves the direction of the fog–wind relationship uncertain. This is a weather observation, not a particulate measurement.
- In the Willamette Valley reply chain, malwarebytess [[./thread#^c24685092|malwarebytess]] recalls the thickest local fog they had seen, persisting for at least a couple of days during '400+ particle count' hazardous-air warnings. No unit, instrument, or clear index is supplied for 400+. cols [[./thread#^c24685323|cols]], also in the valley, reports unusually abundant fog with daytime temperatures described as '65-75deg'; the temperature unit is unstated.
- qchris [[./thread#^c24691337|qchris]] observed substantial coastal fog near Refugio State Park until at least 3 p.m., compared with an expected burn-off by noon, and noticed similar unusual conditions toward Ventura. They distinguish the specific one-day observation from their broader impression that fog had become more common since the fires.
- Sensory experiences differ by setting. Tade0 [[./thread#^c24685807|Tade0]] reports noticeably reduced sunlight during smog despite difficulty distinguishing it visually from fog. cyberdrunk [[./thread#^c24685904|cyberdrunk]] replies that local winter smog is easy to recognize because it stinks. guenthert [[./thread#^c24690062|guenthert]] likewise describes unpleasant Bay Area wildfire-smoke odor. These accounts document perceived light and odor, without quantifying toxicity or physiological effects.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The cause of persistent fog remains unresolved: seaish [[./thread#^c24685361|seaish]] offers several possible mechanisms, and throwawaysea [[./thread#^c24684986|throwawaysea]] questions the relationship with calm winds. malwarebytess [[./thread#^c24685092|malwarebytess]] wonders why scholarship appears absent, but that impression is not evidence that relevant research does not exist. No study results supplied in these comments settle the question.
- Sensor accuracy remains unresolved, including the reliability of the separate size-category estimates and the extreme reported concentrations. shortandsweet's measurement question [[./thread#^c24688213|shortandsweet]] receives jhpankow's description of the instrument [[./thread#^c24689378|jhpankow]], but no calibration results, reference-monitor comparison, or error bounds are provided for hexbinencoded's readings [[./thread#^c24686007|hexbinencoded]]. The sensor critique itself is also an unverified commenter claim.
- This unit does not establish effects on illness, cognition, or sleep, or a measured health benefit from filtration. hexbinencoded [[./thread#^c24686007|hexbinencoded]] reports particle concentrations rather than health outcomes; Tade0 [[./thread#^c24685807|Tade0]] reports diminished sunlight, and guenthert [[./thread#^c24690062|guenthert]] reports odor. Those sensory accounts should not be converted into quantified medical effects.
- Opening windows is not tested here. Under hexbinencoded's reported 57 µg/m³ outdoor versus 5 µg/m³ indoor PM2.5 conditions [[./thread#^c24686007|hexbinencoded]], importing outdoor air could plausibly worsen indoor particle exposure, but that is an inference rather than a reported experiment. There is no clean-outdoor-air comparison showing when opening windows helps, no NO2 or CO2 measurements, and no personal cooking or traffic comparison. Filter costs, energy use, noise, and ventilation rates are also unreported, preventing a fuller comparison of practical tradeoffs.
