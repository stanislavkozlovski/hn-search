# [How a dangerous stew of air pollution is choking the United States](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=33897379)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants discuss worsening smoke and particulate pollution, especially from wildfires, alongside the distinction between local air quality and climate change. They compare wildfire smoke with urban vehicle, industrial, agricultural, and international pollution, and challenge claims about the causes of particular air-quality patterns using weather, wind, geography, and emissions. A substantial thread debates forest management: prescribed and cultural burns, fuel reduction, fire suppression, staffing and funding, ecological effects, urban development near forests, and the risks of escaped burns. Commenters also describe personal experiences with severe smoke and practical responses such as indoor filters, masks, and filtered ventilation, while disputing whether national pollution trends reflect conditions in wildfire-prone regions.
Tags: air pollution, wildfire smoke, forest management, prescribed burns, climate change, air filtration, public policy, regional air quality

## Relevance

Comments report severe firsthand wildfire-smoke exposure and describe indoor filtration, mask use, and the tension between keeping smoke out and allowing CO2 to escape. The discussion also considers windows, filtered ventilation, changing outdoor conditions, and the contribution of traffic and other sources. ## Arguments

- Recirculating filtration is presented as a practical defense against outdoor smoke. Syonyk [[./thread#^c33898159|Syonyk]] reports using a fan with furnace filters at home and a HEPA filter in a shed office, with indoor particle levels usually remaining acceptable despite bad outdoor conditions. These are qualitative operating reports, without numerical before-and-after measurements.
- Closing windows may help when outdoor pollution dominates. Eumenes [[./thread#^c33898954|Eumenes]] recalls somebody else's Raspberry Pi monitoring project reporting worse air with windows open and improvement with them closed. Replying to a challenge, bombcar [[./thread#^c33901581|bombcar]] proposes particle settling and recirculation through any available filters as explanations. Neither supplies the original readings or identifies the measured pollutant.
- Filtered outdoor-air ventilation is proposed as a way to address particles and accumulating CO2 together. hedora [[./thread#^c33902485|hedora]] recommends a filtered air exchanger that transfers heat between incoming and outgoing air. Syonyk [[./thread#^c33901997|Syonyk]] considers a filtered inlet that slightly pressurizes the office, explicitly saying it has not been built.
- hedgehog [[./thread#^c33898860|hedgehog]] proposes displacement ventilation conditionally: contaminants warmer or more humid than room air might rise and be exhausted near the ceiling. The comment acknowledges disadvantages without specifying them; it provides no installation results or evidence that this would address every contaminant in Syonyk's smoke-exposed office.
- Syonyk [[./thread#^c33898159|Syonyk]] recommends P100-equipped half-face respirators for outdoor work during smoke episodes, reporting adoption within a personal social circle. Newer white-and-pink filters reportedly breathe more easily than older pink ones. Running in them is a possibility the commenter speculates about, rather than an exercise result.
- ryukafalz [[./thread#^c33898871|ryukafalz]] argues that inhaled vehicle pollution deserves attention independently of atmospheric CO2, distinguishing exhaust from tire particles. In the ensuing transport discussion, ryukafalz [[./thread#^c33900265|ryukafalz]] favors transit and cycling where urban density makes them viable and concentrates exposure to vehicle pollution.
- Reducing wildfire fuel is proposed as a longer-term exposure intervention. godshatter [[./thread#^c33898260|godshatter]] advocates thinning, undergrowth removal, and protection around developed areas; culi [[./thread#^c33898546|culi]] advocates Indigenous cultural burning as an alternative to treating tree removal as the only option. These comments argue for prevention but provide no measured comparison of cumulative smoke exposure under different management strategies.

## Counterarguments

- Closing a building creates a competing ventilation problem. Syonyk [[./thread#^c33898159|Syonyk]] reports that restricting ventilation during smoke episodes keeps particles more manageable while CO2 climbs. The same commenter acknowledges winter kerosene-lantern use, so the account also contains an indoor source that complicates any assumption that excluding outdoor air makes every aspect of indoor air safe.
- olddustytrail [[./thread#^c33899807|olddustytrail]] challenges the secondhand closed-window account by asking why trapping polluted air would improve it. After bombcar's settling-and-filtration explanation, olddustytrail [[./thread#^c33925925|olddustytrail]] invokes continuing dirty-air infiltration and particles entering lungs. Neither objection supplies measurements that establish or disprove the reported improvement.
- hammock [[./thread#^c33898713|hammock]] attributes unusually clear Thanksgiving views over Los Angeles to reduced traffic, asserting that weather stayed constant. LA resident agp2572 [[./thread#^c33899149|agp2572]] instead reports strong Santa Ana winds and discusses humidity; jedberg [[./thread#^c33899313|jedberg]] also attributes the clearing to those winds. cptskippy [[./thread#^c33909877|cptskippy]] cites weather reports supporting strong winds. The visible clearing is an observation; its attribution to traffic is contested.
- bin\_bash [[./thread#^c33900546|bin\_bash]] directly challenges hammock's [[./thread#^c33898713|hammock]] interpretation of the national heat map as agricultural and industrial pollution, identifying it instead as satellite-measured smoke plumes. The reply also questions the proposed geographic correspondence using Nevada, Montana, and the Imperial Valley. The supplied discussion does not independently verify the map.
- sparrish [[./thread#^c33897664|sparrish]] quotes EPA summaries of improving national pollutant concentrations since 1980 and a 74\% decline in air-toxic emissions from 1990 to 2017. idiotsecant [[./thread#^c33897715|idiotsecant]] counters that national averages do not describe prolonged, locally dangerous PM2.5 episodes in the Pacific Northwest. wizofaus [[./thread#^c33897731|wizofaus]] says the article itself acknowledges historical improvements while discussing their recent erosion by wildfire particulates.
- pirate787 [[./thread#^c33899671|pirate787]] argues that heavier EVs generate more tire wear and compares cars unfavorably with mass transit. boringg [[./thread#^c33899982|boringg]] counters that eliminating urban exhaust represents a major improvement despite remaining tire pollution. Their comparisons use different alternatives—mass transit versus combustion vehicles—and neither quantifies resulting personal exposure.
- latchkey [[./thread#^c33897733|latchkey]] predicts that extensive previous burning and utility repairs might reduce future fires, based on a drive through eastern California forests. crumpled [[./thread#^c33898206|crumpled]] reports reaching the opposite conclusion after similar travel; lazide [[./thread#^c33898371|lazide]] identifies roadside sampling bias; Syonyk [[./thread#^c33898238|Syonyk]] cites much larger remaining forest acreage. Roadside devastation therefore does not establish that future smoke will soon decline.

## Assumptions

- The numerical AQI reports are not interchangeable pollutant measurements. daVe23hu [[./thread#^c33898006|daVe23hu]] reports approximately 800 in winter in Changzhou without naming the index scale; Tade0 [[./thread#^c33898207|Tade0]] explicitly reports the US AQI scale above 200 without naming the location; rootusrootus [[./thread#^c33898663|rootusrootus]] describes readings above a claimed maximum. None identifies the instrument, data provider, calibration, or underlying pollutant concentration.
- Syonyk's [[./thread#^c33898159|Syonyk]] descriptions of low or acceptable indoor PM and rising CO2 lack sensor models, concentrations, placement, calibration, and sampling periods. Eumenes's [[./thread#^c33898954|Eumenes]] Raspberry Pi account is secondhand and does not identify the sensor or pollutant. These comments support accounts of perceived monitoring results, but cannot establish sensor accuracy or a numerical filtration effect.
- belorn [[./thread#^c33902928|belorn]] offers a simplified source breakdown involving nitrogen oxides, sulfur oxides, ozone, wood smoke, and other particles, including a claim that vehicles produce about half of nitrogen oxides. The commenter acknowledges environmental variation. These are source-apportionment claims, not supplied NO2 measurements or a demonstrated breakdown of an individual's PM2.5 exposure.
- culi [[./thread#^c33897803|culi]] quotes an article passage saying exceptional wildfire and dust events can be excluded from regulatory consideration. That does not, by itself, establish that those observations were excluded from the particular national monitoring trends quoted by sparrish [[./thread#^c33897664|sparrish]]. Regulatory treatment, recorded concentrations, and actual inhaled exposure must remain distinct.
- eYrKEC2 [[./thread#^c33898822|eYrKEC2]] explicitly labels the pilot's account of firefighting changes as hearsay and separately relays an Alaska smoke jumper's views about increased caution. These accounts do not establish that staffing, interagency conflict, or safety practices caused the commenter's later reported smoke exposure.
- Broad health claims are not clinical findings from this discussion. idiotsecant [[./thread#^c33897715|idiotsecant]] describes wildfire PM2.5 as dangerous for all groups, while clairity [[./thread#^c33899916|clairity]] asserts that pollution kills millions annually. Neither supplies an exposure-response analysis or health study results within the comment; those claims should not be converted into measured outcomes for the participants.

## Conditions

- Local geography and weather complicate exposure decisions. thorncorona [[./thread#^c33899473|thorncorona]] attributes LA smog retention to basin geography and inversions; agp2572 [[./thread#^c33899149|agp2572]] discusses wind and humidity; cptskippy [[./thread#^c33909877|cptskippy]] additionally claims desert and Salton Sea dust can reach LA. The last claim is not accompanied by local chemical measurements. Wind-related visibility changes therefore do not establish which pollutants changed.
- Neither national borders nor national averages define a local airshed. kompatible [[./thread#^c33898449|kompatible]] emphasizes cross-border air movement. clsec [[./thread#^c33900944|clsec]] cites an external paper for a claim that 29\% of San Francisco pollution comes from Asia and suspects shipping contributions, but the comment does not specify the percentage's pollutant, period, or methodological limits.
- Seasonal heating and local sources may change the mixture. MetaverseClub [[./thread#^c33898376|MetaverseClub]] notes that Changzhou lies south of the Yangtze public-heating divide and suggests northern coal-heated cities could be worse; no north–south measurements are supplied. latchkey [[./thread#^c33898285|latchkey]] attributes Vietnamese pollution to trash burning, industry, motorbikes, and diesel trucks, distinguishing it experientially from California wildfire smoke.
- Filtration burden varies substantially between accounts. rootusrootus [[./thread#^c33898663|rootusrootus]] reports replacing furnace filters daily during severe 2020 smoke, whereas Syonyk [[./thread#^c33898159|Syonyk]] describes a fan-filter arrangement as worthwhile over years. Filter types, loading, airflow, and replacement criteria are unspecified, so these cannot become a universal maintenance schedule.
- Equipment costs constrain proposed improvements, but no actual budgets are given. Syonyk [[./thread#^c33898159|Syonyk]] describes interest in a powered respirator arrangement cheaper than certified options. bombcar [[./thread#^c33901553|bombcar]] wonders about stored compressed air or oxygen; Syonyk [[./thread#^c33901997|Syonyk]] sees no benefit over a simpler filtered inlet. hedora's [[./thread#^c33902485|hedora]] heat-recovery proposal addresses incoming-air temperature, but supplies no installation cost or energy measurements.
- Wildfire prevention faces scale and funding constraints. buildsjets [[./thread#^c33898688|buildsjets]] emphasizes extensive, remote forest acreage and labor costs; cowmoo728 [[./thread#^c33898512|cowmoo728]] adds expanding settlements, escaped-burn risks, agency coordination, and air-quality restrictions. godshatter [[./thread#^c33899023|godshatter]] argues for paying more upfront to reduce later losses while acknowledging that bad luck can defeat preventive work.
- Forest recovery claims have different geographic limits. Syonyk [[./thread#^c33898238|Syonyk]] invokes regrowth across a broad western forest area, whereas jeffbee [[./thread#^c33898700|jeffbee]] claims conifer forests south of Tahoe cannot reestablish under current conditions. Neither supplies a local smoke forecast, and the different spatial scopes prevent treating these as directly comparable predictions.

## Lived experiences

- Syonyk [[./thread#^c33898159|Syonyk]], in southwest Idaho's Treasure Valley, describes smoke becoming a distinct season over the preceding seven or eight years. Outdoor conditions reportedly remained vile from June into October in 2021, while 2022 was substantially milder. Burning eyes are an explicit physical complaint; the account does not report cognitive or sleep changes.
- rootusrootus [[./thread#^c33898663|rootusrootus]] recalls a level-two evacuation warning near Portland during the 2020 fires, smoke readings described as off-scale, continuous furnace operation, and daily filter replacement. A 2022 smoke event was unpleasant but much less severe. No measured indoor concentration or health improvement from the furnace filtration is supplied.
- Eugene resident joshmarinacci [[./thread#^c33899538|joshmarinacci]] reports ash falling from a red sky for two days in September 2020. Separately, eYrKEC2 [[./thread#^c33901911|eYrKEC2]] says wildfire smoke made outdoor exercise unsafe in two of the preceding three years and delayed children's fall soccer by a month until mountain snow ended the fires. These document disruption and perceived danger without exposure logs.
- Interior BC resident moistly [[./thread#^c33904362|moistly]] reports two recent occasions when smoke obscured the house across the street and describes a citywide evacuation notice. This directly challenges kderbyma's [[./thread#^c33898141|kderbyma]] broad claim that Canada was doing well, but does not establish conditions throughout Canada or a precise annual trend.
- daVe23hu [[./thread#^c33898006|daVe23hu]] reports working intermittently in China during 2005–2017 and encountering winter AQI around 800 in Changzhou. In a later reply, daVe23hu [[./thread#^c33903500|daVe23hu]] describes pollution as a recurring concern across otherwise positive work and travel experiences. The numerical AQI observation remains undocumented beyond the comment.
- latchkey [[./thread#^c33898285|latchkey]] reports routinely changing clothes and showering after returning home in Vietnam because of exhaust and pollution odors. Concern about that exposure is a reason for reluctance to return after leaving shortly before COVID. This is a firsthand account of odor, inconvenience, and avoidance, rather than a documented diagnosis or chemical analysis.
- Tade0 [[./thread#^c33898207|Tade0]] describes a direct vehicle-ventilation tradeoff during conditions above 200 on the US AQI scale: recirculation caused window fogging, while outside-air mode produced an unpleasant aftertaste despite filtering. The location is unspecified. The report that locals called it fog is firsthand narration; the proposed social taboo around smog is the commenter's interpretation.
- vondur [[./thread#^c33899588|vondur]] remembers childhood LA County smog alerts in the 1980s that advised limiting outdoor exercise. bombcar [[./thread#^c33901482|bombcar]] similarly remembers much worse historical LA smog. These support individual recollections of severe past conditions, without quantifying current exposure or demonstrating which regulations produced improvement.
- Visual accounts show striking but unmeasured changes: RangerScience [[./thread#^c33900415|RangerScience]] recalls clearer air after rain and early COVID restrictions; alamortsubite [[./thread#^c33900601|alamortsubite]] describes LA visibility improving after rain and smog rebuilding over subsequent days. TechBro8615 [[./thread#^c33901690|TechBro8615]] recalls seeing NYC smog once from Connecticut. clsec [[./thread#^c33900944|clsec]] instead noticed increased SF smog while ships were anchored during the pandemic; differing locations and periods prevent treating these as a single pandemic experiment.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The requested health endpoints remain mostly unanswered. Syonyk [[./thread#^c33898159|Syonyk]] reports eye irritation and accumulating CO2, while Tade0 [[./thread#^c33898207|Tade0]] reports unpleasant taste and fogging. This part contains no cognitive testing, sleep measurements, diagnosed outcomes, or numerical CO2 thresholds linking exposure to symptoms. These gaps do not establish that such effects are absent.
- NO2 and cooking receive little usable coverage. belorn [[./thread#^c33902928|belorn]] discusses nitrogen oxides generally, and Syonyk [[./thread#^c33898159|Syonyk]] mentions kerosene lanterns, but neither provides a NO2 reading. No supplied account compares cooking methods, kitchen exhaust, or cooking-related pollutant peaks, so this unit cannot answer those portions of the approved question.
- When to open windows remains conditional and unquantified. Eumenes's [[./thread#^c33898954|Eumenes]] secondhand account favors closing them in a polluted city, while Syonyk's [[./thread#^c33898159|Syonyk]] experience exposes the competing CO2 cost of restricted ventilation. No simultaneous indoor/outdoor PM2.5 and CO2 series establishes a switching threshold, and no sensor-validation evidence resolves confidence in the reported improvements.
- The proposed ventilation alternatives lack demonstrated comparative performance. hedora [[./thread#^c33902485|hedora]] recommends filtered heat exchange, hedgehog [[./thread#^c33898860|hedgehog]] suggests displacement ventilation, and Syonyk [[./thread#^c33901997|Syonyk]] considers a filtered inlet. Syonyk's [[./thread#^c33898159|Syonyk]] algae-based CO2 absorber is also speculative. The discussion supplies no trial showing which option best balances particles, CO2, comfort, energy, and cost.
- Traffic's contribution remains disputed at several levels. hammock's [[./thread#^c33898713|hammock]] Thanksgiving attribution conflicts with agp2572's [[./thread#^c33899149|agp2572]] wind account; pirate787 [[./thread#^c33899671|pirate787]] and boringg [[./thread#^c33899982|boringg]] disagree over EV improvement while using different comparison modes. No local source-apportionment or personal exposure measurements settle either dispute.
- The causes of worsening smoke seasons are unresolved within this evidence. ars [[./thread#^c33898434|ars]] attributes a change in wildfire statistics to management policy, nightfly [[./thread#^c33898643|nightfly]] emphasizes heat, drought, and reduced snowfall, and Fricken [[./thread#^c33900755|Fricken]] points to increases in boreal areas with little management. joshmarinacci [[./thread#^c33899538|joshmarinacci]] suspects mixed causes. The discussion does not isolate their relative contributions to residents' exposure.
- Immediate smoke relief and longer-term fuel reduction remain in tension. eYrKEC2 [[./thread#^c33901930|eYrKEC2]] wants management that avoids a month of unsafe air, while rendang [[./thread#^c33899843|rendang]] and opportune [[./thread#^c33902562|opportune]] warn that suppressing fires accumulates fuel. culi [[./thread#^c33900217|culi]] expects prescribed burning to provide a net benefit; justinwp [[./thread#^c33899992|justinwp]] cites an escaped New Mexico burn as a practical counterexample. No cumulative smoke-dose comparison resolves the tradeoff.
- Participants also dispute whether cultural and agency prescribed burns differ substantially in goals and execution. culi [[./thread#^c33899897|culi]] and culi [[./thread#^c33900564|culi]] emphasize ecological knowledge and smaller, cooler burns. Drawing on claimed years working for USFS/NPS, justinwp [[./thread#^c33900054|justinwp]] says agency plans already pursue multiple ecological goals, and justinwp [[./thread#^c33901587|justinwp]] emphasizes conditions, objectives, and risk constraints. Neither position demonstrates which approach minimizes nearby residents' air exposure.
