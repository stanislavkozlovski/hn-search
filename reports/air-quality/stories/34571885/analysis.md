# [AirGradient Open Source Air Quality Monitor for CO2 and PM2.5 Measurements](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=34571885)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion reviews an open-source monitor for carbon dioxide and particulate matter, with attention to its design, sensors, assembly, firmware, dashboard, and hardware revisions. Participants compare low-cost particle sensors and CO2 technologies, calibration and accuracy, consumer monitors, and independent evaluation programs. Practical questions address kit instructions, power supplies, connectivity, Home Assistant and other integrations, local versus cloud data, privacy, and routes for sharing measurements through open networks. The thread also covers additional pollutants such as VOCs, NO2, and CO; sensor limitations; alternative DIY projects; commercial products; and the costs and feasibility of adding features such as PoE or battery operation.
Tags: air quality monitor, open source hardware, CO2 sensors, PM2.5 sensors, sensor accuracy, DIY electronics, Home Assistant, cloud services, data privacy, air quality standards, VOC sensors, citizen science

## Relevance

The discussion focuses on monitors for CO2 and PM2.5, sensor accuracy, calibration, and low-cost sensor limitations. It also includes firsthand reports of CO2 increases from gas cooking, PM changes from indoor sources and construction, and how ventilation or opening windows affects readings. ## Arguments

- Monitoring can provide useful feedback on ventilation. revicon [[./thread#^c34574977|revicon]] reports seeing CO2 readings change quickly after opening doors in response to alerts; Tempest1981 [[./thread#^c34572176|Tempest1981]] reports that opening windows lowered TVOC readings accumulated over several days. These are observations of instrument responses, not demonstrations of improved health.
- The accounts support a conditional approach to opening windows: airing helped Tempest1981 [[./thread#^c34572176|Tempest1981]] reduce indoor TVOC readings, while 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c34576560|5e92cb50239222b]] reports maintaining much lower PM2.5 indoors through filtration and isolation from polluted surroundings. The latter observed pollution entering through an interior door; extending that result to open windows is a reasonable inference, not a directly reported window experiment.
- Indoor monitoring can reveal nearby pollution events that a regional reading may miss. harrydehal [[./thread#^c34576140|harrydehal]] reports detecting neighbors' yardwork and a nearby fire indoors. In response to a construction-site question, ahaucnx [[./thread#^c34573875|ahaucnx]] predicts that a PM sensor would detect construction dust and changes with wind direction; that prediction is separate from the firsthand account.
- Several participants argue that useful consumer measurements are achievable with appropriate sensors. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c34573632|ahaucnx]] recommends good sensors and independent RESET AIR certification for PM, CO2, temperature and humidity. pppone [[./thread#^c34573044|pppone]] recommends AQ-SPEC evaluations and considers both Sensirion SPS30 and Plantower PMS5003 viable, while explaining their project's preference for Sensirion documentation and packaging.
- CO2 monitoring is advocated partly for cognition and as an indicator of other indoor problems. M3L0NM4N [[./thread#^c34572906|M3L0NM4N]] cites a Tom Scott video for reduced brain function at higher CO2, and reeckoh [[./thread#^c34573263|reeckoh]] suggests CO2 can be a canary for mold accumulation. Neither supplies personal outcome measurements, study details or a validated diagnostic relationship.
- Relative TVOC monitoring may help identify activities worth investigating even when absolute concentrations are unreliable. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c34572289|ahaucnx]] recommends looking for spikes associated with cleaning chemicals; ted\_dunning [[./thread#^c34572699|ted\_dunning]], replying to a question about 3D printing, considers relative measurements potentially useful. These arguments support detecting changes, not establishing that a particular exposure is safe.
- Accessible histories make monitoring more actionable. blueblimp [[./thread#^c34574698|blueblimp]] values Qingping's on-device and app graphs, parker\_mountain [[./thread#^c34572265|parker\_mountain]] recommends consistent sampling over time, and sunsetMurk [[./thread#^c34575372|sunsetMurk]] reports obtaining a LEVOIT purifier's PM2.5 readings through Home Assistant. None reports a controlled comparison showing that integration itself improved exposure.

## Counterarguments

- A monitor's air-quality label does not establish coverage of every relevant pollutant. jve [[./thread#^c34579432|jve]] confirms that Aranet measures CO2, temperature and humidity only; belter [[./thread#^c34584659|belter]] notes that AtmotubePro lacks CO2 measurement; blueblimp [[./thread#^c34574698|blueblimp]] could not find a device that measures allergens. These replies limit the original request for one trustworthy box covering gases, dust and allergens.
- Specific product claims are corrected within their reply chains. vijaykodam [[./thread#^c34578742|vijaykodam]] says Netatmo measures CO2 and PM2.5, but daydream [[./thread#^c34579280|daydream]] disputes the PM2.5 capability. ycombinatornews [[./thread#^c34575779|ycombinatornews]] clarifies that Amazon's monitor measures CO rather than CO2. Separately, revicon [[./thread#^c34574977|revicon]] describes uHoo as measuring nitrogen monoxide; that statement must not be silently converted into evidence of NO2 measurement.
- Positive assessments of Plantower do not establish uniform performance across models or batches. dheera [[./thread#^c34572764|dheera]] reports poor low-pollution sensitivity with PMSA003I, and ahaucnx [[./thread#^c34572881|ahaucnx]] reports accuracy problems and early failures with an earlier A003. pppone [[./thread#^c34573044|pppone]] flags a reported recent PMS5003 batch issue, while chubbnix [[./thread#^c34574414|chubbnix]] worries that changed internals retain the same exterior markings.
- Environmental and installation effects can undermine apparently precise readings. rkagerer [[./thread#^c34576445|rkagerer]] connects daily Dylos fluctuations with a reported humidity sensitivity in laboratory testing, although only tentatively. jve [[./thread#^c34576955|jve]] suspects cooking grease caused an IKEA sensor to remain at maximum. aidenn0 [[./thread#^c34575159|aidenn0]] also challenges the idea that temperature measurement is easy when nearby electronics generate heat.
- TVOC numbers are not interchangeable with measurements of toxicological risk. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c34572289|ahaucnx]] says different chemicals trigger the same sensor, temperature and humidity influence results, and absolute concentrations are dependable only under specified laboratory conditions. An index avoids presenting a misleading absolute concentration but does not identify the chemicals responsible.
- A reassuring display can conceal a permissive threshold. leokennis [[./thread#^c34578086|leokennis]], responding to interest in a forthcoming IKEA monitor, warns that its predecessor stays green up to 35 µg/m³, comparing this with WHO and US air-quality benchmarks. The comment does not specify averaging periods, and its criticism concerns the predecessor rather than a tested result for the forthcoming model.
- Price alone is not a demonstrated test of whether CO2 measurement is genuine. tomaskafka [[./thread#^c34573737|tomaskafka]] claims that products below \$60 lack a real CO2 sensor; s0rce [[./thread#^c34574090|s0rce]] counters that an SCD41-based device can be built below that price. The reply concerns DIY construction, so it does not independently validate very cheap finished retail monitors.
- Automatic calibration is not necessarily maintenance-free. daydream [[./thread#^c34579280|daydream]] says Netatmo needs exposure to essentially outdoor air because it uses the weekly minimum as a reference. summm [[./thread#^c34577597|summm]] argues that automatic calibration is impractical for serious indoor monitoring, contrasting with rogers18445 [[./thread#^c34576108|rogers18445]], who presents periodic outdoor exposure as a manageable requirement.
- The claim that IQAir requires cloud access is corrected by an owner. erinnh [[./thread#^c34572487|erinnh]] objects to apparent cloud-only integration, but dgacmu [[./thread#^c34572651|dgacmu]] reports retrieving local CSV logs over SMB and later identifies a local JSON file containing current measurements [[./thread#^c34574721|dgacmu]]. The local interface is less convenient than a conventional API, but the original categorical limitation does not survive the reply.

## Assumptions

- Automatic baseline correction assumes that the lowest encountered concentration represents an appropriate outdoor reference. rogers18445 [[./thread#^c34576108|rogers18445]] describes a 400 ppm minimum assumption, while daydream [[./thread#^c34579280|daydream]] describes Netatmo's weekly minimum. Persistently occupied or insufficiently ventilated spaces may violate that assumption; the comments do not establish that every device handles this identically.
- Taking a device outdoors is not automatically an independent calibration check. crazygringo [[./thread#^c34572767|crazygringo]] reports outdoor readings varying around 400–600 ppm and asks for a reference map. Those readings could reflect environmental variation, measurement error or both; the supplied account does not separate them.
- Repeatability, responsiveness and agreement between consumer devices are weaker claims than reference accuracy. 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c34576560|5e92cb50239222b]] reports repeatable responses to very small PM changes, and jve [[./thread#^c34579432|jve]] reports matching readings from old and new Aranets. Neither supplies an independently calibrated reference establishing the absolute accuracy of those readings.
- Source attribution generally rests on timing rather than controlled experiments. karmelapple [[./thread#^c34575323|karmelapple]] associates CO2 spikes with turning on a gas stove, and harrydehal [[./thread#^c34576140|harrydehal]] associates indoor sensor changes with yardwork and a nearby fire. These observations support investigating those sources but do not quantify their complete pollutant mixtures or resulting health effects.
- An intervention's success for one measured quantity cannot establish success for all air-quality concerns. The purifier account from 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c34576560|5e92cb50239222b]] measures PM2.5, while revicon [[./thread#^c34574977|revicon]] follows CO2 after opening doors. Neither account establishes simultaneous control of PM2.5, CO2, NO2 and VOCs.
- A single reading is assumed to represent the relevant place and time, an assumption questioned by parker\_mountain [[./thread#^c34572265|parker\_mountain]], who describes uneven VOC distribution and recommends repeated sampling. Similarly, mastax [[./thread#^c34573726|mastax]] reports temperature readings affected by the assembled monitor, limiting interpretation as room conditions.
- External evaluations and manufacturer experience remain attributed evidence here. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c34572326|ahaucnx]], speaking for the project, reports testing thousands of PMS5003 sensors and cites favorable studies; pppone [[./thread#^c34573044|pppone]] points to AQ-SPEC. The prompt contains their descriptions and links, not the underlying datasets or a demonstration that every delivered unit performs equivalently.

## Conditions

- The reported budget depends heavily on pollutant coverage and willingness to assemble hardware. aedocw [[./thread#^c34572827|aedocw]] describes an approximately \$30 IKEA/ESP8266 setup measuring PM2.5, temperature and humidity, explicitly excluding CO2 and allowing lower accuracy. s0rce [[./thread#^c34574090|s0rce]] describes DIY CO2 measurement below \$60, while hrez [[./thread#^c34575454|hrez]] identifies a roughly \$60 ready-to-use Sensirion CO2 Gadget. These are prices reported in this 2023 discussion.
- Better documentation and humidity correction can change the component-cost tradeoff. chubbnix [[./thread#^c34574414|chubbnix]] estimates an SPS30 at about \$50, roughly five times a Plantower sensor. pppone [[./thread#^c34578844|pppone]] reports approximately \$30 SPS30 pricing at volume and cheaper SEN5X variants, with higher-priced variants adding temperature/humidity measurements useful for humidity correction.
- Broader gas coverage and calibrated deployment across many rooms can be expensive. kayson [[./thread#^c34572261|kayson]] reports NO2 and hydrogen-sulfide sensor quotes of \$90 or more each, while considering cheaper SPEC Sensors based on datasheets. kayson [[./thread#^c34576327|kayson]] interprets AQ-SPEC summaries as showing weak gas-sensor performance, with the best costing thousands. marcel\_hecko [[./thread#^c34572351|marcel\_hecko]] emphasizes the cumulative cost of calibrated CO2 probes in schools, offices or homes.
- Ownership costs and data access affect practical usefulness. XorNot [[./thread#^c34575398|XorNot]] reports rising uHoo prices and dislikes cloud dependence. mikkelam [[./thread#^c34577376|mikkelam]] suspects AirGradient ONE requires subscription software, whereas ahaucnx [[./thread#^c34573834|ahaucnx]] says DIY users had never been charged for the dashboard, while leaving future charges possible. Those statements concern different offerings and should not be collapsed into one pricing claim.
- Outdoor pollution conditions differ sharply between accounts. In an unnamed highly polluted area, 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c34576560|5e92cb50239222b]] reports spending much of their time in one room with a purifier at full output. Separately, pigtailgirl [[./thread#^c34572363|pigtailgirl]] describes Seoul's air as sometimes unbreathable and uses an air-quality API for smart-home alerts. These are particular circumstances, not representative estimates for all homes or cities.
- Humidity, weather and elevation complicate interpretation. rkagerer [[./thread#^c34576445|rkagerer]] lives in a humid climate and suspects humidity contributes to changing particle readings. crazygringo [[./thread#^c34572767|crazygringo]] contrasts windy winter conditions with still, muggy summer weather. quinncom [[./thread#^c34585462|quinncom]] reports calibration trouble after moving a CO2 Gadget from Mexico City at approximately 2,050 m to San Francisco at approximately 50 m.
- Continuous particle monitoring has different power requirements from long-lived CO2-only devices. comboy [[./thread#^c34572761|comboy]] estimates that a continuously running 30 mA particle-sensor fan would require 262,800 mAh over a year at its operating voltage, suggesting intermittent sampling instead. karmelapple [[./thread#^c34573757|karmelapple]] reports nearly two years without recharging an Aranet; this is not evidence that a fan-driven PM monitor can achieve the same endurance.

## Lived experiences

- 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c34576560|5e92cb50239222b]] reports using three PMS5003 sensors for years and keeping a purifier running at full output in one room. Reported indoor PM2.5 is 0–1 µg/m³ when outdoors is 100 µg/m³, and 2–2.5 µg/m³ when outdoors reaches 250 µg/m³. Briefly opening an interior door reportedly produces a detectable increase of about 0.5 µg/m³. These are sensor readings without independent reference validation.
- karmelapple [[./thread#^c34575323|karmelapple]] reports Aranet CO2 readings rising to 1,200 or more when turning on a gas stove. Combined with recently encountered claims about children's health, this motivates a planned switch to induction. The comment reports neither measured NO2 nor an induction installation and subsequent improvement.
- revicon [[./thread#^c34574977|revicon]] reports that uHoo alerts reveal CO2 buildup and that opening doors produces quickly visible changes in its charts. The account describes satisfaction with observable ventilation effects but gives no concentrations, ventilation rates or symptom changes.
- Tempest1981 [[./thread#^c34572176|Tempest1981]] reports that a \$65 TVOC meter needs 5–10 minutes to warm up and then fluctuates by nearly a factor of two despite displaying four digits. Nevertheless, readings rise over two or three days indoors and fall after windows are opened. No absolute concentrations or health outcomes are supplied.
- harrydehal [[./thread#^c34576140|harrydehal]], replying to a question about construction-site exposure, reports detecting neighbors' yardwork and a fire down the street with an indoor sensor. Investigating a possible association between passing trains and emissions is a future interest, not an already observed correlation.
- crazygringo [[./thread#^c34572767|crazygringo]] reports outdoor CO2 readings around 400–600 ppm, with higher readings on muggy, still summer days than expected on windy winter days. The lack of a trustworthy local reference is the reason for asking about an outdoor CO2 map.
- quinncom [[./thread#^c34585462|quinncom]] reports that a CO2 Gadget calibrated in Mexico City subsequently read 800 ppm outdoors in San Francisco. Leaving it continuously powered outdoors for four days did not recalibrate it before departure. They report contacting a Sensirion engineer about adding manual calibration.
- dheera [[./thread#^c34572764|dheera]] reports a homemade PMSA003I monitor displaying zero AQI whenever the actual AQI was below approximately 100, without specifying the reference establishing that actual value. This is an AQI account, not a report that PM2.5 below 100 µg/m³ was undetectable.
- rkagerer [[./thread#^c34576445|rkagerer]] bought a Dylos DC1700-PM for around \$400 and observes large daily reading changes. After seeing a laboratory report of variable performance at humidity of 65\% or higher, they suspect a connection with their humid climate and ask for a reliable, low-maintenance meter below \$3,000. That suspected explanation is not personally verified.
- jve [[./thread#^c34576955|jve]] built the inexpensive IKEA modification to follow changes during baking and humidifier use rather than obtain precise concentrations. It later became stuck at a red maximum reading; kitchen grease contamination is their hypothesis, not a confirmed diagnosis.
- jve [[./thread#^c34579432|jve]] reports an office Aranet operating for more than two years without a battery change, matching a newly purchased unit, and showing roughly 600–700 ppm with people present. Their suggestion that this office air might be fresh enough for recalibration is explicitly uncertain.
- Several favorable ownership reports acknowledge limited validation. blueblimp [[./thread#^c34574698|blueblimp]] finds Qingping readings reasonable but has not tested rigorously; shafyy [[./thread#^c34579071|shafyy]] reports roughly three years of satisfactory Awair use without comparison testing. Forge36 [[./thread#^c34573720|Forge36]] considers \$20 and \$30 monitors close enough, observes VOC readings mainly in a garage, and attributes a possible 100 ppm CO2 error to reviews rather than personal calibration.
- Kit experiences show that dependable data also requires working hardware and configuration. mastax [[./thread#^c34573726|mastax]] reports unreliable sensor connections and temperature readings 5–10°F high; ahaucnx [[./thread#^c34573834|ahaucnx]] says a newer revision addresses most reported issues. sc00ty [[./thread#^c34573652|sc00ty]] reports two kits failing to create Wi-Fi hotspots, and ahaucnx [[./thread#^c34573716|ahaucnx]] offers replacements. Separately, runjake [[./thread#^c34586792|runjake]] reports successful operation after choosing the appropriate firmware.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Health, cognition, sleep and comfort outcomes remain largely unmeasured in this source unit. M3L0NM4N [[./thread#^c34572906|M3L0NM4N]] relies on a video for the cognition claim; karmelapple [[./thread#^c34575323|karmelapple]] mentions childhood-health concerns without study details; pigtailgirl [[./thread#^c34572363|pigtailgirl]] describes subjective discomfort from Seoul's air. None supplies a measured cognitive or sleep change, or a clinical outcome following an intervention.
- NO2 is a conspicuous evidence gap. kayson [[./thread#^c34572261|kayson]] discusses sensor availability and datasheets rather than measured household NO2. slawwwc [[./thread#^c34577606|slawwwc]] suggests Air-Q but explicitly does not know its quality. revicon [[./thread#^c34574977|revicon]] names nitrogen monoxide, and jph [[./thread#^c34572266|jph]] expresses interest in a NOx module. These comments do not establish NO2 concentrations, validated NO2 performance or the effect of changing cooking practices.
- Traffic and wildfire exposure are not directly characterized. harrydehal [[./thread#^c34576140|harrydehal]] reports a nearby fire without identifying it as a wildfire and proposes investigating train emissions. alwayslikethis [[./thread#^c34577063|alwayslikethis]] discusses highways and combustion sources as possible causes of locally elevated outdoor CO2, but supplies no measurements. There is no quantified traffic-exposure comparison or wildfire-specific filtration trial here.
- The optimal balance between airing and filtration remains unresolved. Tempest1981 [[./thread#^c34572176|Tempest1981]] observes lower TVOC readings after opening windows, revicon [[./thread#^c34574977|revicon]] observes CO2 responses to opening doors, and 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c34576560|5e92cb50239222b]] observes PM ingress into a filtered room. These different settings do not establish a universal window-opening rule, simultaneous pollutant tradeoffs or an exposure-minimizing ventilation schedule.
- Claims about rapid automatic recalibration conflict with a travel experience. rogers18445 [[./thread#^c34576108|rogers18445]] says outdoor exposure can correct drift within minutes; quinncom [[./thread#^c34585462|quinncom]] says the Gadget requires seven continuously powered days and reports failure to recalibrate during four days outdoors. rogers18445 [[./thread#^c34587675|rogers18445]] proposes a secondary below-400-ppm mechanism and a possible sequence of recalibrations, but that proposed resolution is not tested in the thread.
- The proposed dual-NDIR remedy is disputed rather than established. summm [[./thread#^c34588018|summm]] describes a sealed reference chamber sharing sensor components and cites SCD30. rogers18445 [[./thread#^c34583275|rogers18445]] questions how a separate reference chamber corrects the measurement channel, then notes that SCD30 also uses outdoor-air automatic calibration [[./thread#^c34588071|rogers18445]]. No comparative validation resolves the disagreement.
- Outdoor CO2 variability and suitable reference conditions remain uncertain. crazygringo [[./thread#^c34572767|crazygringo]] reports substantial variation, whereas alwayslikethis [[./thread#^c34577063|alwayslikethis]] expects relatively little variation away from sources and little immediate health effect from local elevations. keenanj [[./thread#^c34574467|keenanj]] acknowledges poor accuracy in uncalibrated inexpensive sensors and says proposed network-based correction still needs validation. breput [[./thread#^c34574208|breput]] suggests a zero-gas reference, but supplies no completed calibration experiment.
- Independent validation of specific monitors remains incomplete. thatcat [[./thread#^c34574462|thatcat]] questions apparently zero correlation values in a uHoo evaluation while acknowledging possible missing evaluation data. kayson [[./thread#^c34573099|kayson]] asks whether AirGradient's reported chamber tests are published; the supplied replies do not provide them. These open questions prevent owner satisfaction or manufacturer assurances from settling absolute accuracy.
- How much accuracy is sufficient for a particular household decision remains unsettled. dotancohen [[./thread#^c34593354|dotancohen]] proposes checking a \$15 monitor outdoors against approximately 420 ppm before interpreting a hypothetical 900 ppm bedroom reading. Forge36 [[./thread#^c34573720|Forge36]] accepts approximate readings for their purposes. The thread does not establish that one outdoor check validates indoor accuracy across concentrations or identifies a health-danger threshold.
- Relative TVOC monitoring during 3D printing is suggested, but exposure assurance remains unanswered. drakenot [[./thread#^c34572545|drakenot]] asks whether sensors can ensure VOC levels stay down; ahaucnx [[./thread#^c34572841|ahaucnx]] thinks they should be suitable. That qualified endorsement must be read alongside ahaucnx's own warning about chemical selectivity and unreliable absolute concentrations [[./thread#^c34572289|ahaucnx]]. No printing trial or health-based acceptance criterion is supplied.
