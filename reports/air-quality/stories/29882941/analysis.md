# [Windy.com: See the wind, temperature, rain and air quality around you](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=29882941)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Users discuss Windy’s maps and forecasts for outdoor activities, sailing, aviation, and local air-quality projects, while comparing it with other weather services and visualization tools. A central thread cautions that detailed maps can imply more local accuracy than model grids and station coverage support; others explain that Windy presents forecasts from external models and lets users compare them. Commenters also describe building community pollution-monitoring dashboards and the cost and capabilities of environmental sensors.
Tags: weather-forecasting, Windy, forecast-models, data-visualization, air-quality-monitoring, local-sensors

Comments discuss air-quality maps and local pollutant monitoring, including a community project with PM and gas sensors and plans to relate readings to traffic, industry, and weather. ## Arguments

- Community monitoring could make local pollution more visible. kingsloi [[./thread#^c29884595|kingsloi]] reports deploying an AQMesh pod in Gary, Indiana; kingsloi [[./thread#^c29885141|kingsloi]] lists PM2.5 and NO2 among its channels. Clarifying the project after a weather-station comparison, kingsloi [[./thread#^c29885612|kingsloi]] describes combining air sensors with traffic, train, aircraft, and weather information to investigate local sources. Deployment is reported, but pollution attribution and forecasting remain ambitions.
- Maps and wind forecasts can support practical decisions around pollution. notatoad [[./thread#^c29885198|notatoad]] found IQAir helpful during summer wildfire smoke. cozzyd [[./thread#^c29886593|cozzyd]] describes using Windy to anticipate north winds at Greenland's Summit Station, when snowmobile riding and certain other activities were restricted to reduce emissions reaching an air-monitoring lab. These establish reported usefulness, not measured improvements in personal exposure or health.
- Industrial odors may warrant investigating specific pollutants. Responding to plumeria's question about an abattoir's burning residues and oxidation lagoons [[./thread#^c29886235|plumeria]], kingsloi [[./thread#^c29886558|kingsloi]] quotes a study reporting hazard quotients above one for H2S, SO2, and NH3 and suggesting adverse health risks for nearby residents. This is a claim from a study excerpt found through a quick search, not a firsthand health account or evidence about local PM2.5 or NO2.

## Counterarguments

- A detailed map can imply more accuracy than users have established. fshbbdssbbgdd [[./thread#^c29885508|fshbbdssbbgdd]] reports a mountain-temperature discrepancy, while cconcepts [[./thread#^c29885715|cconcepts]] says visual specificity encouraged excessive trust during decisions in a remote area. cconcepts also qualifies the criticism: Windy might still be the best forecasting app. These complaints concern weather forecasts; they do not demonstrate an error in its air-quality layer.
- The proposed explanation for those discrepancies is contested. Replying to fshbbdssbbgdd, nkurz [[./thread#^c29886669|nkurz]] says Windy displays forecasts interpolated from model grids, rather than directly interpolating current station temperatures. Answering aardvark179's description of forecast production [[./thread#^c29885034|aardvark179]], nkurz [[./thread#^c29886903|nkurz]] says Windy presents third-party forecasts; a\_square\_peg [[./thread#^c29889091|a\_square\_peg]] agrees. This corrects the claimed data pipeline without establishing that a particular local forecast is accurate.
- The abattoir recommendation exceeds what the supplied equipment description validates. kingsloi [[./thread#^c29886558|kingsloi]] says the pod appears suitable after a quick search, but kingsloi [[./thread#^c29885141|kingsloi]] lists H2S as a future addition requiring replacement of CO or SO2, and does not list an NH3 channel. The cited study therefore does not establish that the currently described configuration measures every implicated gas, burning-related pollutant, or the perceived stench.

## Assumptions

- The proposed resident-reporting and alert system assumes that reported bad-air episodes can be matched to measured conditions well enough to predict future episodes. kingsloi [[./thread#^c29885141|kingsloi]] presents this as a plan, alongside deploying more pods. Similarly, kingsloi's proposed investigation of traffic, industry, and weather [[./thread#^c29885612|kingsloi]] assumes the combined information will help distinguish sources; no demonstrated predictive or attribution results appear here.
- nkurz [[./thread#^c29886669|nkurz]] treats agreement among weather models as a reason for greater confidence and disagreement as a warning of uncertainty. This is a suggested interpretation strategy, not a validated uncertainty estimate in this discussion. It also assumes users distinguish model forecasts from local measurements; the supplied comments do not establish how the air-quality layer is generated.

## Conditions

- The community-monitoring example is geographically specific: kingsloi [[./thread#^c29884595|kingsloi]] is building a dashboard for the Gary/Miller Beach community, and kingsloi [[./thread#^c29885612|kingsloi]] wants to understand local traffic, industry, and weather influences. No pollutant concentrations or source contributions are supplied, so the project cannot establish how those influences compare with another neighborhood or an indoor environment.
- Cost is a substantial constraint in this example. kingsloi [[./thread#^c29886558|kingsloi]] reports a three-month rental costing a few thousand dollars despite a special deal, estimates purchase at roughly \$10,000 or more, and mentions a \$1,000 annual fee. The purchase figure is explicitly uncertain. Favorable service is a firsthand assessment, but neither price nor service establishes measurement accuracy.
- Weather-model usefulness varies by terrain and location. nkurz [[./thread#^c29886669|nkurz]] suggests choosing a finer model where available; nkurz [[./thread#^c29886903|nkurz]] lists models with approximately 5–22 km grids at their US location. lqueenan [[./thread#^c29884966|lqueenan]] favors NAM and HRRR around Vancouver's mountainous coast and inlets, whereas cozzyd [[./thread#^c29886593|cozzyd]] found ECMWF best at Summit Station. These weather comparisons do not identify the spatial resolution or accuracy of pollution estimates.

## Lived experiences

- fshbbdssbbgdd [[./thread#^c29885508|fshbbdssbbgdd]] describes seeing temperatures about 15 degrees cooler in nearby mountains on personal-weather-station websites while Windy showed temperatures matching the flatlands. The temperature unit, station details, and contemporaneous records are absent. This is a firsthand comparison of displayed information, not a personal pollutant measurement.
- cconcepts [[./thread#^c29885715|cconcepts]] reports relying on Windy in a remote area for reasonably important decisions and mistaking detailed presentation for accuracy. The commenter later acknowledges following the forecast too blindly. The account supplies no location, error magnitude, specific consequence, or air-pollution measurement.
- notatoad [[./thread#^c29885198|notatoad]] reports that IQAir was very helpful during summer wildfire smoke. There are no accompanying PM2.5 values, sensor checks, symptoms, indoor/outdoor comparisons, or descriptions of changes to windows, filtration, or activities.
- cozzyd [[./thread#^c29886593|cozzyd]] reports that Summit Station wind predictions became noticeably worse when the NOAA weather station went down, and that ECMWF performed best among available models. This supports a reported relationship between local observing infrastructure and forecast usefulness, but supplies no quantified comparison or verification of the precise data dependency. The associated emissions restrictions protected monitoring conditions; no personal health benefit was measured.
