# [How bad is my gas stove?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=29935939)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

The discussion considers whether gas stoves and other gas appliances raise indoor nitrogen dioxide and other pollution, and how much can be inferred from a household’s measurements without controls for outdoor air, cooking emissions, furnaces, or ventilation. Participants ask about monitor accuracy, range hoods, direct-vent appliances, and practical ways to track air quality. A large thread compares induction, gas, and conventional electric cooking: speed, heat control, pan compatibility, wok use, noise, controls, reliability, and the cost of upgrading cookware or wiring. Some favor switching for indoor air quality and electrification; others emphasize cooking preferences, cost, outages, or uncertainty in the health evidence, including a dispute about electromagnetic exposure. The thread also debates subsidies, gas restrictions, personal choice, and broader transitions to electric heating and hot water.
Tags: indoor air quality, gas stoves, nitrogen dioxide, induction cooking, electric appliances, ventilation, air quality monitors, public health, energy transition, consumer costs

## Relevance

The discussion directly addresses indoor NO2 and other pollution associated with gas stoves and furnaces. It includes firsthand reports and questions about sensor readings, possible outdoor and neighboring sources, and whether cooking itself contributes particles. Participants weigh ventilation hoods, outside-air makeup, open windows, air filtration, and switching to induction, while noting uncertainty about hood effectiveness, sensor accuracy, exposure levels, and cooking preferences and conversion costs. It also raises reported respiratory and comfort concerns, but the discussion does not establish individual health effects conclusively. ## Arguments

- Removing indoor combustion targets a specific pollution source even if cooking still generates particles. ClumsyPilot [[./thread#^c29946807|ClumsyPilot]] distinguishes combustion-related NO2 from the particle increases their purifier detects during frying; garaetjjte [[./thread#^c29940338|garaetjjte]] explains NOx as forming from atmospheric air during combustion. This supports evaluating burner emissions separately from food and oil emissions, rather than treating every air-quality spike as interchangeable.
- Health concerns motivate switching, but the claimed benefits vary in evidential strength. faeriechangling [[./thread#^c29946439|faeriechangling]] invokes an approximately one-quarter increase in childhood asthma associated with gas, without supplying the underlying evidence in that comment. dismalpedigree [[./thread#^c29945901|dismalpedigree]] describes health benefits as a bonus after switching, but reports improved cooking experience rather than measured health changes.
- Heating and water-heating equipment also deserve attention. crmd [[./thread#^c29938839|crmd]] recommends sealed-combustion, direct-vent appliances; BXLE\_1-1-BitIs1 [[./thread#^c29938715|BXLE\_1-1-BitIs1]] identifies possible exposure pathways through unsuitable chimney arrangements, heat-exchanger leaks, and leakage before a draft develops. Their argument is that addressing the stove alone may leave other combustion sources connected to indoor air.
- Exhaust ventilation remains relevant with any cooking fuel. fennecfoxen [[./thread#^c29946994|fennecfoxen]] emphasizes emissions from high-temperature cooking with oil, while xenadu02 [[./thread#^c29940548|xenadu02]] recommends using the hood for both stovetop and oven cooking to prevent accumulated grease. wintermutestwin [[./thread#^c29946896|wintermutestwin]] argues that recirculating hoods are a central problem because they do not exhaust cooking pollution outside.
- Opening windows is proposed as a relatively inexpensive intervention. Spooky23 [[./thread#^c29946198|Spooky23]] recommends slightly opening a couple of windows, and vehemenz [[./thread#^c29947224|vehemenz]] favors a good vent fan plus a cracked window over replacing multiple appliances. c\_o\_n\_v\_e\_x [[./thread#^c29943009|c\_o\_n\_v\_e\_x]] supplies the proposed mechanism: outdoor exhaust draws replacement air into the building, potentially diluting indoor NOx. These are conditional ventilation arguments, not measured NO2 reductions from window opening.
- Partial substitution offers an alternative to immediately replacing an entire kitchen. raffraffraff [[./thread#^c29946742|raffraffraff]] describes using an electric kettle and other appliances to reduce reliance on gas burners; diiaann [[./thread#^c29944702|diiaann]] proposes induction for routine tasks with occasional gas use for particular cookware or techniques. This could reduce combustion use, although neither comment quantifies the resulting exposure reduction.
- Some users find that avoiding gas also improves comfort and everyday usability. gambiting [[./thread#^c29945237|gambiting]] reports less unwanted heat in the kitchen after switching to induction, and samwillis [[./thread#^c29945774|samwillis]] describes satisfactory performance with compatible cookware. These accounts establish that the change can be attractive on practical grounds; they do not establish a universal cooking preference or a measured health benefit.
- Ventilation improvements need to address actual use. homero [[./thread#^c29947094|homero]] says they were never taught to use the hood and barely use it; rsanheim [[./thread#^c29944280|rsanheim]] recognizes routinely omitting it during boiling and prolonged oven use. xenadu02 [[./thread#^c29940548|xenadu02]] proposes automatic activation, and leoedin [[./thread#^c29944395|leoedin]] reports that communicating stove-and-hood systems exist, albeit at a higher price.

## Counterarguments

- Induction does not make cooking pollution disappear. mc32 [[./thread#^c29946073|mc32]] reports indoor pollution spikes while frying on induction, and fasteddie31003 [[./thread#^c29946776|fasteddie31003]] reports substantial PM2.5 during frying. This challenges broad claims of completely clean cooking, while leaving intact ClumsyPilot’s narrower distinction between particles and combustion-related NO2 [[./thread#^c29946807|ClumsyPilot]].
- The household observations described in the discussion do not isolate gas from the cooking process. gibbonsrcool [[./thread#^c29946308|gibbonsrcool]] initially treats food emissions as a controlled variable, but topaz0 [[./thread#^c29947483|topaz0]] explains that the author observed their existing appliances and meals rather than comparing matched meals across stove types. jtbayly [[./thread#^c29961795|jtbayly]] reiterates that the contribution of cooking itself was not established. Their illustrative 5\%–95\% range is an expression of uncertainty, not a measured result.
- Outdoor pollution could confound attribution to indoor appliances. ericbarrett [[./thread#^c29944294|ericbarrett]] points to traffic-related NO2 and changing regional concentrations, then requests an outdoor control away from household exhausts [[./thread#^c29946279|ericbarrett]]. nickkell [[./thread#^c29945174|nickkell]] counters that nighttime spikes and lower readings on non-cooking days still need explanation. The exchange does not establish that either outdoor pollution or indoor combustion explains all the variation.
- The evidence cited about hoods does not establish that a correctly installed, operating exhaust hood cannot reduce NO2. rsanheim [[./thread#^c29944280|rsanheim]] reports a study finding no statistically significant reduction after hood installation, while acknowledging installation and non-use as possible explanations. throwawaysea [[./thread#^c29948634|throwawaysea]] specifically challenges generalizing from installation to effectiveness when actual operation was uncertain.
- A conflicting household account comes from throwawaysea [[./thread#^c29944624|throwawaysea]]: their gas stove and high-efficiency furnace vent outside, and cooking does not trigger their air-quality monitors. They interpret this as evidence that concern is exaggerated. However, the comment does not identify the monitored pollutants, sensor accuracy, alarm thresholds, or exposure duration, so it cannot establish an absence of NO2 exposure.
- Replacing everything is not the only proposed response. antishatter [[./thread#^c29946283|antishatter]] argues for repairing the suspected leak and installing a good hood instead of the expensive conversion described by mrblampo. adrianN [[./thread#^c29946548|adrianN]] argues that lower-cost appliances or assistance with electrical and plumbing work could make substitution affordable. No supplied comparison establishes which option delivers the greatest verified exposure reduction per dollar.
- Cooking objections cannot all be dismissed as unfamiliarity or confusion with resistive electric stoves. Aaargh20318 [[./thread#^c29947645|Aaargh20318]] explicitly confirms experience with induction and describes frustrating controls and cookware limitations. justinlloyd [[./thread#^c29952814|justinlloyd]] reports professional experience with inexpensive and expensive equipment but still prefers gas for several techniques. These are practical objections to an intervention, not evidence that gas emissions are harmless.
- Induction introduces other comfort concerns for some users. na85 [[./thread#^c29946834|na85]] reports hearing objectionable coil whine even from expensive equipment, and carterschonwald [[./thread#^c29962813|carterschonwald]] describes painful whine from cheaper portable units. Their accounts limit claims that buying a premium induction appliance necessarily resolves every usability problem.
- A separate objection concerns electromagnetic exposure rather than air quality. lumb63 [[./thread#^c29946067|lumb63]] presents a paper as showing induction exposure up to 16 times a safe limit. bluejellybean [[./thread#^c29948892|bluejellybean]] quotes distance-dependent, modeled exposure results and argues that the claim needs substantial qualification. ospzfmbbzr [[./thread#^c29945814|ospzfmbbzr]] also reports a concerning EMF-meter reading close to a stove, without numerical or frequency details. These comments do not establish clinical harm or a quantitative equivalence between EMF exposure and inhaled combustion pollution.

## Assumptions

- The evidence unit is story 29935939, part 0. The supplied story has no article body, so descriptions of the author’s experiment, equipment, and linked research remain commenters’ reports. For example, topaz0 [[./thread#^c29947483|topaz0]] describes household NO2 monitoring, while garrettgrimsley [[./thread#^c29948218|garrettgrimsley]] says the author identified a uHoo monitor through correspondence. Neither constitutes an independently inspected dataset here.
- Pollutant identity matters. ClumsyPilot [[./thread#^c29946807|ClumsyPilot]] distinguishes a particle-responsive purifier from NO2 measurement, and ysleepy [[./thread#^c29957740|ysleepy]] explicitly clarifies that their recommended sensor measures CO2 only. A generic pollution warning, particle reading, or CO2 measurement cannot simply be treated as a measurement of NO2 or of the toxicity of the entire mixture.
- Several claims of improvement assume that perceived air quality tracks the relevant pollutant. jsharpe [[./thread#^c29939069|jsharpe]] explicitly separates a change in perceived gas smell from an unknown effect on NO2. Likewise, mrblampo [[./thread#^c29944866|mrblampo]] describes a suspected leak and probable cooking-related NO2 spikes, not measured before-and-after concentrations. Odor observations and suspected exposure should retain those qualifications.
- Absolute NO2 readings depend on sensor performance that is not demonstrated in the supplied material. aulin [[./thread#^c29948544|aulin]] argues that inexpensive sensors may be useful mainly for trends and spikes, then identifies calibration, temperature, humidity, aging, and cross-sensitivity as issues requiring documentation [[./thread#^c29952907|aulin]]. This is an expert-style critique within the discussion, not a supplied validation experiment proving that every consumer instrument fails.
- Product specifications and owner satisfaction are different from accuracy evidence. thurn [[./thread#^c29951496|thurn]] cites uHoo’s claimed accuracy of ±20 ppb or ±10\%; aulin [[./thread#^c29952907|aulin]] requests reference-instrument comparisons and agreement tests between units. doitLP [[./thread#^c29946302|doitLP]] being happy with a uHoo for two years does not resolve those questions.
- The amount of gas burned does not by itself establish the amount reaching occupants. asdfasgasdgasdg [[./thread#^c29946398|asdfasgasdgasdg]] prioritizes heating equipment partly because it uses more gas than cooking, whereas BXLE\_1-1-BitIs1 [[./thread#^c29938715|BXLE\_1-1-BitIs1]] describes how air supply, venting, and leakage govern possible indoor exposure. Ranking appliances by fuel consumption alone assumes comparable containment and exposure pathways.
- Practical comparisons depend on appliance identity, power supply, cookware, and control behavior. peregrine [[./thread#^c29946372|peregrine]] correctly identifies one described stove as resistive rather than induction, but duped [[./thread#^c29949672|duped]] explicitly confirms that their problematic appliance is induction. samwillis’s claim that all disappointing induction performance comes from pans [[./thread#^c29945774|samwillis]] therefore should not be adopted as a settled explanation.

## Conditions

- The financial figures are historical comments from 2022. In Cambridge, Massachusetts, mrblampo [[./thread#^c29944866|mrblampo]] reports approximately \$1,800 for a 40-amp circuit, \$300 for plumbing, and \$3,000 for an induction range—about \$5,100 altogether. They retained gas for the water heater and installed a valve allowing the unit’s gas supply to be restored later. This was a particular retrofit, not a universal minimum cost.
- Cheaper entry points exist, with different capabilities. giaour [[./thread#^c29947266|giaour]] reports using a \$50 induction plate for tasks requiring precision; nightfly [[./thread#^c29944933|nightfly]] does about 90\% of their cooking on a roughly \$100 plate, although their previous stove was electric. gibspaulding [[./thread#^c29946997|gibspaulding]] finds a \$200 two-burner unit usable but coarse. These experiences support trying partial substitution without assuming inexpensive units perform identically.
- Electrical and kitchen layouts complicate international price comparisons. maccard [[./thread#^c29946366|maccard]] describes inexpensive UK plug-in options, but asdfasgasdgasdg [[./thread#^c29946453|asdfasgasdgasdg]] points out the shared power limit of ordinary US 120-volt, 15-amp outlets. Their follow-up distinguishes converting a gas-equipped kitchen from replacing an existing electric range and identifies cabinet changes and dedicated wiring as additional costs [[./thread#^c29947811|asdfasgasdgasdg]].
- Even within the US, wiring costs are disputed and site-dependent. darksaints [[./thread#^c29948966|darksaints]] suggests a typical installation cost of \$200–\$300, while asdfasgasdgasdg [[./thread#^c29949320|asdfasgasdgasdg]] stresses cable routing and local labor costs. rootusrootus [[./thread#^c29947260|rootusrootus]] expects close to \$2,000 because their kitchen requires a difficult route from the panel. A low appliance price therefore does not establish a low total conversion price.
- Cookware replacement is a possible cost rather than an automatic requirement. samwillis [[./thread#^c29945774|samwillis]] advises budgeting heavily for compatible pans, provoking cooperadymas’s objection to replacing existing cookware [[./thread#^c29947549|cooperadymas]]. gcheong [[./thread#^c29951686|gcheong]] instead recommends checking magnetic compatibility, and Gibbon1 [[./thread#^c29951735|Gibbon1]] reports that five of six existing pots and pans worked in a borrowed-burner trial.
- Housing stock and tenancy affect available interventions. marcianx [[./thread#^c29944950|marcianx]] reports kitchens without hoods in older NYC studio apartments; nightfly [[./thread#^c29944939|nightfly]] has encountered both absent hoods and recirculating ones. wintermutestwin [[./thread#^c29946896|wintermutestwin]] says recirculation is permitted in California. These accounts qualify claims that modern code requirements guarantee effective exhaust in occupied homes.
- Hood capacity depends on replacement air as well as advertised CFM. briHass [[./thread#^c29946680|briHass]] reports improved visible capture only after opening a window, while jacobolus [[./thread#^c29944372|jacobolus]] estimates that dedicated makeup air can add substantially to installation costs. jacobolus also proposes a remotely located fan and silencer for quieter operation. These are design suggestions and estimates, not measured NO2-removal efficiencies.
- Window opening depends on outdoor conditions. Combining c\_o\_n\_v\_e\_x’s dilution argument [[./thread#^c29943009|c\_o\_n\_v\_e\_x]] with ericbarrett’s concern about variable ambient NO2 [[./thread#^c29946279|ericbarrett]] yields a conditional inference: cleaner incoming air may help, whereas more polluted incoming air may increase exposure. Schiendelman [[./thread#^c29939367|Schiendelman]] also emphasizes that exhausted NO2 remains relevant outdoors in dense cities. No supplied account directly measures a window-induced increase in NO2 or PM2.5.
- Energy prices, outages, and climate affect the feasibility of eliminating combustion appliances. vehemenz [[./thread#^c29947224|vehemenz]] describes a Midwestern household with several gas appliances and anticipates substantial conversion and backup-power costs; cpsns [[./thread#^c29946217|cpsns]] says delivered propane remains cheaper locally. In contrast, b112 [[./thread#^c29944886|b112]] describes all-electric heating through very cold weather and attributes its affordability to Quebec electricity prices [[./thread#^c29954065|b112]]. These are local conditions, not transferable operating-cost estimates.
- Cold-weather electric heating has conflicting descriptions. oxymoran [[./thread#^c29946333|oxymoran]] argues that heat pumps require furnaces in cold climates, but dashundchen [[./thread#^c29946911|dashundchen]] reports using a heat pump in a drafty Great Lakes house with resistance backup needed only a few days annually. keeganjw [[./thread#^c29946299|keeganjw]] reports adequate heat at a Vermont office. These accounts concern comfort and feasibility; none measures the NO2 benefit of replacing a furnace.
- Accessibility can change which replacement is practical. gerikson [[./thread#^c29945122|gerikson]] reports paying more for an induction stove with knobs because their partner is blind. reaperducer [[./thread#^c29945766|reaperducer]] cautions that knobs can still operate discrete power steps. Consequently, the cheapest available induction unit may not meet a household’s access or control requirements.
- Repair costs and service consequences are also geographically specific. selimthegrim [[./thread#^c29948501|selimthegrim]] reports that reporting a gas leak in New Orleans can result in meter removal, weeks without service, and substantial reconnection costs. This is an unverified local account, but it complicates the suggestion that repairing a leak is always administratively simple or inexpensive.

## Lived experiences

- fasteddie31003 [[./thread#^c29946776|fasteddie31003]] reports a PM2.5 reading reaching 400 while frying and argues that proper outdoor exhaust matters more than the gas-versus-induction distinction. The comment supplies no units, averaging interval, instrument model, calibration, or matched comparison between fuels. It is a reported household measurement, not a fully characterized exposure estimate.
- mc32 [[./thread#^c29946073|mc32]] reports repeated pollution spikes while frying on an induction stove, without identifying the pollutant or magnitude. operatingthetan [[./thread#^c29944657|operatingthetan]] separately reports monitor reactions associated with smoke from an electric stove. Both observations support cooking itself as a source of monitor changes, without establishing NO2 generation or comparative health risk.
- bluejellybean [[./thread#^c29948892|bluejellybean]] reports that their rental has no fume hood and that their partner develops nausea and headaches in another room when the gas burners operate. This is a firsthand household symptom account with a reported temporal association. It does not identify the responsible pollutant, measure exposure, or establish a medical diagnosis.
- jsharpe [[./thread#^c29939069|jsharpe]] began using the range hood whenever operating the gas stove or oven and noticed a difference in perceived gas smell, while explicitly remaining unsure about NO2. zejn [[./thread#^c29945603|zejn]] reports that turning on the fan significantly helps PM2.5 and PM10, but supplies no numerical results and expressly does not know about NO2 or VOCs.
- fma [[./thread#^c29946545|fma]] reports switching to a higher-capacity hood and running it before cooking and afterward. They later say cooking smells previously reached upstairs but no longer do, while speculating that a drafty chimney supplies replacement air [[./thread#^c29949745|fma]]. This is an odor-based before-and-after account; actual airflow and NO2 removal were not measured.
- briHass [[./thread#^c29946680|briHass]] describes a roughly 400-CFM hood whose visible capture improves when a window across the kitchen is cracked: steam then clearly moves into the hood. This directly illustrates a window helping airflow in one installation, but visible steam capture does not quantify capture of NO2 or fine particles.
- throwawaysea [[./thread#^c29944624|throwawaysea]] reports a contrasting setup with outside-vented gas appliances, air-quality monitors, and filters, where cooking does not trigger the monitors. The coexisting ventilation and filtration prevent attributing the result to either intervention separately, and the unspecified sensors prevent comparison with NO2-specific claims.
- crmd [[./thread#^c29938839|crmd]] upgraded the furnace and water heater to direct-vent models because both appliances occupied essentially the same space as their bed in a converted industrial loft. The account explains an exposure concern and a completed intervention, but reports no pollutant measurements, symptom changes, or sleep outcomes.
- c\_o\_n\_v\_e\_x [[./thread#^c29944664|c\_o\_n\_v\_e\_x]], describing earlier work as an HVAC technician, recounts a restaurant with inadequate cooling despite functioning air conditioning. The owner had omitted makeup air, allowing the kitchen exhaust to remove conditioned restaurant air. This is a concrete comfort and energy tradeoff from ventilation, not an indoor-pollutant measurement.
- bigfudge [[./thread#^c29946592|bigfudge]] reports that a curved induction wok burner performs better than their former gas wok ring and produces less smell. They attribute the difference to the absence of gas flowing around the pan and carrying vapors, but provide no measurement establishing that mechanism. gambiting [[./thread#^c29945237|gambiting]] separately reports a more comfortable kitchen with less unwanted heat after adopting induction.
- Actual use of mixed kitchens differs sharply. silverlake [[./thread#^c29947006|silverlake]] uses induction for approximately 95\% of cooking and questions whether keeping gas is worthwhile; throwawaysea [[./thread#^c29948634|throwawaysea]] has both but largely reserves induction for boiling water. askvictor [[./thread#^c29945283|askvictor]] usually takes high-temperature cooking to an outdoor gas barbecue. These are behavioral accounts, with no measured exposure comparison indoors or at the outdoor cooking position.
- Temperature-control problems can themselves affect cooking smoke and adoption. duped [[./thread#^c29947946|duped]] reports alternating excessive heat, oil passing its smoke point, and insufficient heat, and later confirms the appliance is a tabletop induction unit [[./thread#^c29949672|duped]]. leoedin [[./thread#^c29944382|leoedin]] contrasts smooth control on their own induction stove with longer, noticeable cycles on their parents’ model. reaperducer [[./thread#^c29945750|reaperducer]] reports cooking at home less after six months struggling with an apartment’s induction stove.
- ysleepy [[./thread#^c29945388|ysleepy]] reports successfully using an MH-Z19 CO2 sensor with an ESP board and ESPHome, then clarifies that it does not measure NO2 [[./thread#^c29957740|ysleepy]]. doitLP [[./thread#^c29946311|doitLP]] reports two years of satisfaction with a uHoo. These establish ownership and reported usability, not measured concentrations or independent accuracy.
- mikewarot [[./thread#^c29939917|mikewarot]] describes running two gas burners with water overnight during a power outage, with outdoor temperature around 15°F and indoor temperature around 49°F in a poorly insulated house. After teirce [[./thread#^c29944571|teirce]] warns about fire and carbon-monoxide poisoning, mikewarot [[./thread#^c29945801|mikewarot]] acknowledges the practice was a bad idea and explains that they had CO detectors, stayed awake, and feared frozen pipes. This was an emergency tradeoff, not evidence of safe routine heating or of low NO2.
- Humidity-related comfort differs across locations. antisthenes [[./thread#^c29944557|antisthenes]] reports indoor humidity of 17\%; maxerickson [[./thread#^c29946235|maxerickson]] says a house-sized humidifier cannot maintain more than 20\% in colder weather. lostlogin [[./thread#^c29952803|lostlogin]], responding from New Zealand, describes both summers and winters as humid within a much narrower temperature range. These accounts limit any general claim that additional cooking moisture is either welcome or undesirable.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The absolute NO2 exposure remains uncertain. garrettgrimsley [[./thread#^c29948216|garrettgrimsley]] identifies the purported monitor and points to external testing tables; thurn [[./thread#^c29951517|thurn]] interprets those tables as favoring expensive scientific equipment, and garrettgrimsley [[./thread#^c29952049|garrettgrimsley]] agrees. The testing results themselves are not supplied. aulin’s requested calibration and reference comparisons [[./thread#^c29952907|aulin]] remain missing, so neither safety-threshold exceedances nor precise intervention benefits can be verified here.
- Source attribution is unresolved among stove combustion, furnace operation, cooking emissions, and outdoor infiltration. wlecometo [[./thread#^c29978127|wlecometo]] quotes an apparent correspondence between overnight readings and furnace operation; ericbarrett [[./thread#^c29946279|ericbarrett]] calls for outdoor controls; antattack [[./thread#^c29948652|antattack]] proposes neighboring sources. These are competing interpretations or hypotheses, not demonstrated source contributions.
- The health-risk magnitude is disputed without a resolving comparison. faeriechangling [[./thread#^c29946439|faeriechangling]] invokes increased childhood asthma, while nemo44x [[./thread#^c29946053|nemo44x]] raises urban residence as a possible confounder and throwawaysea [[./thread#^c29948748|throwawaysea]] asserts that meaningful effects mainly concern unusually sensitive people. None establishes the applicable risk for a particular vented household. lr4444lr [[./thread#^c29946544|lr4444lr]] and drobert [[./thread#^c29948708|drobert]] ask for food-service-worker evidence, but no such analysis is supplied.
- How much properly operated ventilation reduces NO2 remains open. rsanheim’s reported installation-study result [[./thread#^c29944280|rsanheim]] and throwawaysea’s criticism of uncertain use [[./thread#^c29948634|throwawaysea]] do not resolve performance under controlled conditions. andrewberls [[./thread#^c29939572|andrewberls]] asks directly about higher-capacity hoods, purifiers, and windows, but the supplied firsthand reports largely concern particles, smell, or visible steam rather than NO2 capture.
- Filtration effectiveness for gases is not demonstrated. londons\_explore [[./thread#^c29945490|londons\_explore]] claims that a recirculating hood with a carbon filter, replaced annually, can remove smells and NOx; no supporting measurements or capacity information accompany that claim. ClumsyPilot’s particle-responsive purifier [[./thread#^c29946807|ClumsyPilot]] and throwawaysea’s combined filters-and-ventilation setup [[./thread#^c29944624|throwawaysea]] do not establish standalone NO2 removal or an appropriate filter-replacement interval.
- A city reading does not settle household safety. sputr [[./thread#^c29945733|sputr]] cites an official NO2 value of 48 against a purported safe level of 200, with units written as 'um/m3', and asks why their stove is a problem. The comment gives no averaging period, indoor reading, cooking peak, or exposure duration. The relationship between that ambient figure and personal stove exposure therefore remains unanswered.
- Acceptable tradeoffs remain partly a disagreement about values. JasonFruit [[./thread#^c29947931|JasonFruit]] emphasizes family rituals, wood heat, and enjoyment over maximizing longevity. dionidium [[./thread#^c29948262|dionidium]] responds that the possible cost includes prolonged illness and children’s asthma, rather than only fewer years of otherwise healthy life.
