# [My best estimate is gas stoves decrease life expectancy by 53 days on average](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=30275953)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

A discussion of claims that gas stove pollution affects life expectancy, with participants weighing the evidence, ventilation, and possible confounders. Much of the thread compares gas, resistance electric, and induction cooking in terms of health, safety, speed, temperature control, cookware, wok use, cost, and household preferences.
Tags: gas-stoves, induction-cooking, indoor-air-quality, health-evidence, ventilation, cooking-equipment

## Relevance

The discussion focuses on gas-stove pollution, including NO2, whether range hoods reduce exposure, and the limits of HEPA filtration for gases. It includes firsthand kitchen air-quality measurements and conflicting views on ventilation, induction cooking, and the strength of the estimated health effect. ## Arguments

- com2kid [[./thread#^c30276476|com2kid]] advocates avoiding gas combustion because of household NO2 exposure and claimed developmental effects in children. stinkytaco [[./thread#^c30276160|stinkytaco]] and shade [[./thread#^c30277650|shade]] also cite health or air quality among their reasons for preferring induction. These are stated motivations and health claims, not measured improvements in health.
- colechristensen [[./thread#^c30276456|colechristensen]] argues that high-temperature cooking with fats produces substantial pollution independently of the stove’s fuel, so switching away from gas does not eliminate the need for extraction or particle filtration. Their later recommendation emphasizes measuring and mitigating fine particles [[./thread#^c30276571|colechristensen]].
- Responding directly to the suggestion to get HEPA filtration instead of replacing gas, com2kid [[./thread#^c30276706|com2kid]] distinguishes particle removal from NO2 removal; Riseed [[./thread#^c30278466|Riseed]] makes the same objection in another branch. com2kid additionally claims meaningful gas adsorption requires pounds of activated charcoal rather than the thin sheets in typical filters, without supplying a performance test.
- Ventilation is presented as a practical intervention whose effectiveness depends on the building. PragmaticPulp [[./thread#^c30276286|PragmaticPulp]] supports externally exhausting hoods, while com2kid [[./thread#^c30276333|com2kid]] says airtight homes may need an open window or mechanical makeup air to supply replacement air while extraction runs.

## Counterarguments

- throwaway0a5e [[./thread#^c30276411|throwaway0a5e]] questions whether the estimated 53-day lifespan difference could reflect residual confounding despite demographic and location controls. lucisferre [[./thread#^c30276342|lucisferre]] argues that an effect below 0.2\% of average lifespan cannot be measured definitively. These are methodological objections; the supplied comments do not establish either objection through a reanalysis.
- PragmaticPulp [[./thread#^c30276286|PragmaticPulp]] challenges the dismissal of ordinary externally vented hoods, arguing that incomplete mitigation can still matter. hammock [[./thread#^c30276234|hammock]] reports that ordinary hoods work well in their experience, and mmaunder [[./thread#^c30276382|mmaunder]] emphasizes large differences between hood designs. None supplies measured residual NO2 concentrations demonstrating complete protection.
- HardwareLust [[./thread#^c30276054|HardwareLust]] considers cooking frustration a larger personal cost than the headline lifespan estimate. JoeAltmaier [[./thread#^c30276116|JoeAltmaier]] suggests better food could offset the alleged harm, but michaelmior [[./thread#^c30276304|michaelmior]] challenges both gas’s supposed food-quality advantage and its proposed connection to longevity. These are preferences and unquantified health hypotheses.
- giantg2 [[./thread#^c30276341|giantg2]] raises a separate concern about induction’s electromagnetic exposure, citing a study and acknowledging uncertainty about exposure limits. arcticbull [[./thread#^c30276324|arcticbull]] disputes whether the cited findings establish health harm. This disagreement affects perceived replacement tradeoffs but supplies no comparative air-quality measurements.

## Assumptions

- jimbob45 [[./thread#^c30276127|jimbob45]] quotes the estimate as lifelong gas use instead of electric use with everything else unchanged, then questions what amount of use it assumes: their own cooking is approximately monthly and under ten minutes. Applying the headline to an individual therefore requires exposure assumptions that this unit does not resolve.
- Pollution proxies must remain distinct. colechristensen [[./thread#^c30276456|colechristensen]] describes unspecified air-quality readings; mmaunder [[./thread#^c30276382|mmaunder]] describes an overhead hood producing no visible smoke during searing. Neither observation establishes a measured NO2 concentration, and the former cannot be labeled a numerical PM2.5 measurement from the information supplied.
- Claims about hood protection assume the hood is used, exhausts outdoors, captures emissions, and receives sufficient replacement air. asdff [[./thread#^c30276224|asdff]] questions actual operation and outside connections, while com2kid [[./thread#^c30276582|com2kid]] emphasizes capture geometry and makeup air. asdff’s suggestion that most users neglect their hoods is not supported by usage data here.
- Cooking-performance comparisons require correctly identifying the equipment and considering cookware and burner configuration. heydonovan [[./thread#^c30276555|heydonovan]] explicitly retracts their assumption that glowing resistance coils were induction. Separately, pandaman [[./thread#^c30281369|pandaman]] questions whether demonstration videos favor induction through pot-to-burner sizing; that critique is itself an interpretation, not a reproduced experiment.

## Conditions

- Conversion costs can extend beyond buying a stove. Arainach [[./thread#^c30276254|Arainach]] describes needing a new 220V outlet and breaker-box expansion and consequently replacing gas with gas. asdff [[./thread#^c30276334|asdff]] considers replacing an already functional stove unjustified by modest convenience improvements. Neither provides a complete installation quote.
- Partial substitution may be cheaper but requires space. colechristensen [[./thread#^c30276379|colechristensen]] recommends approximately \$100, 1.3 kW portable induction burners and estimates they could replace roughly half of cooktop use. sokoloff [[./thread#^c30277193|sokoloff]] owns one and likes it for slow cooking but lacks countertop space for daily use.
- Wok compatibility changes the practical options. com2kid [[./thread#^c30276435|com2kid]] describes a roughly \$500 rounded induction wok burner requiring its own 220V connection and reports difficulty finding an integrated version in the US. stinkytaco [[./thread#^c30276412|stinkytaco]] finds their wide-bottom induction wok usable but lacking side heating. vosper [[./thread#^c30276245|vosper]] proposes a hybrid induction-and-gas appliance, which retains some gas cooking.
- Effective extraction may require installation work rather than simply a premium appliance. From shopping and research, com2kid [[./thread#^c30276582|com2kid]] recommends a deep capture cavity, substantial cooktop coverage, makeup air, and an exterior fan for noise reduction. They criticize both \$200 and \$2,000 retail options and claim a custom installation can cost less than premium prebuilt equipment; these are not documented comparative test results.

## Lived experiences

- colechristensen [[./thread#^c30276456|colechristensen]] provides the clearest firsthand monitoring account: cooking caused readings they describe as worse than the worst city globally, lasting hours without filtration versus minutes with it. No numerical concentrations, instrument details, or comparable city dataset accompany that description, so its magnitude and comparison cannot be checked.
- com2kid [[./thread#^c30276706|com2kid]] reports running two HEPA filters and finding that they do not help with spicy food. This is an experienced limitation, but the comment does not identify the relevant airborne substance, quantify symptoms, or demonstrate NO2 removal or persistence through measurement.
- shade [[./thread#^c30277650|shade]] reports rapid heating and an immediate transition from boiling to simmering after replacing resistance coils with induction. stinkytaco [[./thread#^c30276618|stinkytaco]] estimates their induction boils water at least 50\% faster than gas appliances they have used, while also reporting cookware buzzing and wok limitations [[./thread#^c30276160|stinkytaco]]. These support usability claims, not measured reductions in exposure.
- Experiences with alternatives conflict: HardwareLust [[./thread#^c30287892|HardwareLust]] says they have used gas, resistance electric, and induction and strongly prefer gas. quasarj [[./thread#^c30276556|quasarj]] reports that induction does not work with 85\% of their pans, whereas abletonlive [[./thread#^c30276660|abletonlive]] says they have never owned incompatible cookware. smallerfish [[./thread#^c30276240|smallerfish]] finds their induction controls cumbersome.
- mmaunder [[./thread#^c30276382|mmaunder]] reports unexpectedly liking a cheap resistance-electric stove because it boils water faster than their gas equipment, despite poorer precision. They also describe an overhead hood preventing visible smoke during searing. The account offers no before-and-after pollutant readings.
- During the Texas freeze, heydonovan [[./thread#^c30276380|heydonovan]] manually lit a gas range to cook and obtain some warmth while keeping a window open. This illustrates the value they placed on outage resilience, but includes no exposure measurements establishing safety. Their accompanying complaint about induction heating slowly was subsequently identified as confusion with resistance electric [[./thread#^c30276555|heydonovan]].
- jimbob45 [[./thread#^c30276127|jimbob45]] reports stress when gas fails to ignite immediately. gjvc [[./thread#^c30276217|gjvc]] and asdff [[./thread#^c30276440|asdff]] suggest cleaning and reseating burner components. This is a firsthand comfort concern with proposed maintenance responses, not evidence of a measured pollutant-related health effect.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The magnitude and causality of the lifespan estimate remain unresolved. jimbob45 [[./thread#^c30276127|jimbob45]] supplies its quoted lifetime-use framing, while throwaway0a5e [[./thread#^c30276411|throwaway0a5e]] disputes its resistance to confounding. com2kid’s child-development claim [[./thread#^c30276476|com2kid]] is not accompanied by a firsthand clinical account. No supplied account connects measured exposure changes to cognition, sleep, or diagnosed illness.
- Whether hood mitigation is sufficient remains disputed. com2kid [[./thread#^c30277088|com2kid]] claims approximately 50\% NO2 reduction for mediocre hoods and 70\% for the best, regarding residual exposure as unacceptable. giantg2 [[./thread#^c30278151|giantg2]] considers 70\% sufficient using a secondhand 225 ppb example and quoted standards of 100 ppb for a specified one-hour statistical measure and 53 ppb annually. A 70\% reduction would leave 67.5 ppb in that example, but no exposure time series establishes how it compares with either averaging framework.
- Sensor reliability and pollutant specificity cannot be assessed. colechristensen [[./thread#^c30276456|colechristensen]] supplies no sensor model, calibration information, sampling placement, raw readings, or named measurement channel. Their recommendation to measure fine particles [[./thread#^c30276571|colechristensen]] therefore does not supply numerical PM2.5 evidence, and this unit contains no CO2 measurements.
- Opening windows is proposed to support exhaust in airtight homes by com2kid [[./thread#^c30276333|com2kid]], but there are no paired indoor/outdoor readings showing the resulting exposure. The unit does not establish when incoming traffic pollution or wildfire smoke would outweigh that benefit. Naga [[./thread#^c30276136|Naga]] asks whether outdoor gas barbecues carry similar risks; giantg2’s speculative reply about charred food [[./thread#^c30276181|giantg2]] does not answer the inhalation question.
- Idle-stove leakage is another unquantified issue. jnwatson [[./thread#^c30276195|jnwatson]] argues that gas stoves leak when not cooking, so cooking-time ventilation would miss some pollution. asdff [[./thread#^c30276405|asdff]] asks how much methane actually leaks. No household leak measurements or associated personal health-risk estimates are supplied, and this allegation should not be conflated with measured combustion-generated NO2.
