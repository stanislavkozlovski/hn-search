# [Experts are sounding the alarm about the hidden dangers of gas stoves](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=25332332)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

The discussion examines reports linking gas stoves to indoor air pollution and respiratory risks, with participants questioning whether the evidence establishes causation and whether it accounts for ventilation, housing, climate, income, and other sources of pollution. Many compare gas with induction and conventional electric cooking, sharing views on performance, safety, cost, cookware, wok and open-flame techniques, and the learning curve of newer appliances. A major thread concerns whether effective exterior-vented range hoods, fresh-air exchange, and building standards could reduce exposure without restricting gas appliances; commenters describe how common recirculating or ineffective hoods can be. Participants also discuss CO alarms and gas appliance safety, heat pumps and heating costs, ventilation systems, air filters and monitors, and broader disputes about regulation, climate policy, and trust in expert messaging.
Tags: gas stoves, indoor air pollution, asthma evidence, range hoods, ventilation, induction cooking, appliance safety, regulation

## Relevance

The discussion covers reported NO2 and other combustion pollutants from gas stoves, possible respiratory effects, and the effectiveness and limitations of range hoods, ventilation and air filtration. It includes firsthand experiences with air-quality monitors, household filtration and moving away from traffic, as well as disagreement about whether observed health associations establish causation. ## Arguments

- This source unit, story 25332332 part 0, supports distinguishing combustion emissions, pollutants generated by cooking food, and inadequate ventilation. wskinner explicitly separates those three problems [[./thread#^c25334067|wskinner]]. sandworm101 argues that replacing combustion equipment does not itself provide ventilation or address dust and mold [[./thread#^c25332633|sandworm101]]. Together, these comments support evaluating source reduction, exhaust, and filtration separately.
- nslav argues, based on their reading rather than personal NO2 measurements, that NO2 is a byproduct of normally operating gas combustion, not merely defective appliances. They shifted stovetop cooking to two portable induction units [[./thread#^c25332948|nslav]]. This presents eliminating an indoor combustion source as an alternative to continually managing its emissions.
- jcoq counters the latitude-confounding objection by describing a meta-analysis containing 41 studies, including many single-city studies, and decades of research [[./thread#^c25334214|jcoq]]. soupson argues that cold weather and gas use could both contribute to asthma rather than one necessarily explaining away the other [[./thread#^c25333692|soupson]]. These are competing interpretations of research cited by commenters; the research itself is not supplied.
- musicale quotes an estimate that, during a typical winter week without venting range hoods, 1.7 million Californians could experience CO concentrations exceeding ambient-air standards and 12 million could experience excessive NO2 [[./thread#^c25340832|musicale]]. This is a reported population exposure estimate under specified conditions, not a firsthand measurement or a demonstrated count of resulting illnesses.
- The discussion contains qualified support for effective external exhaust. After initially suggesting NO2 was difficult to capture, nslav revises their position upon finding a paper they describe as reporting 80–95\% pollutant reduction at 108 L/s airflow [[./thread#^c25336106|nslav]]. com2kid summarizes the same linked paper as showing NO2 removal ranging from roughly 25\% for some microwave exhausts to 90\% for a good hood [[./thread#^c25335290|com2kid]]. Neither summary establishes one universal removal rate.
- sjg007 recommends a hood exhausting outdoors, with window extraction, opening a window, or bathroom exhaust as alternatives or supplements; they also identify the need for replacement air [[./thread#^c25332751|sjg007]]. musicale recommends operating the hood before ignition, throughout cooking, and afterward [[./thread#^c25340832|musicale]]. These are practical recommendations, without household measurements validating a particular operating duration.
- Retric explains heat recovery as a way to exchange indoor and outdoor air while reducing the heating penalty, while retaining a need for filtration and humidity management [[./thread#^c25332882|Retric]]. kens explains why countercurrent exchange can recover more heat than simply averaging the two air temperatures [[./thread#^c25334259|kens]]. kaitai notes that standalone HRV/ERV installations need not depend on central heating or air conditioning [[./thread#^c25334518|kaitai]].
- Monitoring can reveal when and where problems occur even without establishing laboratory accuracy. pbronez notes that central systems may process rooms unevenly [[./thread#^c25332817|pbronez]], while Raphmedia describes an intermittent workplace problem detected during actual AC operation after an initial professional assessment with the AC off missed it [[./thread#^c25338526|Raphmedia]]. This supports representative placement and operating conditions as well as attention to sensor specifications.

## Counterarguments

- ruste argues that latitude and colder winters could confound an association between gas appliances and asthma, and disputes whether the cited meta-analysis measures stove use or indoor exposure described as NO [[./thread#^c25333115|ruste]] [[./thread#^c25334361|ruste]]. sokoloff raises income, urban location, housing age, ventilation, and using stoves for heating as additional possible confounders [[./thread#^c25332580|sokoloff]]. These objections identify questions about attribution; they do not themselves demonstrate that gas contributes no risk.
- refurb argues that properly functioning gas stoves burn cleanly and challenges references to unspecified legal limits and vaguely higher NO2 concentrations [[./thread#^c25332719|refurb]]. throwawaysea similarly assumes a hood on maximum should minimize the impact [[./thread#^c25335266|throwawaysea]]. These positions preserve disagreement about the magnitude of ordinary-use risk, but neither commenter supplies NO2 measurements demonstrating safety under those conditions.
- A purifier reacting during cooking does not isolate gas combustion as the cause. mikestew reports the same cooking and shower-related responses in a home without a gas connection [[./thread#^c25333891|mikestew]]. outworlder observes sensitivity to shower humidity and cautions that increased purifier activity does not identify what is airborne or establish that it is harmful [[./thread#^c25337316|outworlder]].
- Particle filtration is not equivalent to removing combustion gases. DoingIsLearning questions whether particle-monitoring purifiers address the article's NO2 concern [[./thread#^c25333323|DoingIsLearning]]; CarVac states that HEPA does not filter molecules [[./thread#^c25333642|CarVac]]. BoorishBears and strangegecko discuss activated carbon for gases, emphasizing substantial media quantities and replacement requirements [[./thread#^c25336115|BoorishBears]] [[./thread#^c25335145|strangegecko]]. These comments do not establish removal performance for every gas of concern.
- More exhaust power can introduce other hazards. macNchz warns that strong exhaust in a tight house can backdraft combustion appliances, especially alongside a dryer, unless makeup air is considered [[./thread#^c25334078|macNchz]]. yetihehe describes their closed-chamber water heater with separate intake and exhaust through a concentric pipe [[./thread#^c25335102|yetihehe]], showing that the relevance of this concern depends on appliance design.
- Outdoor air is not invariably the cleaner supply. cbhl reports an SF high-rise AC system importing wildfire smoke [[./thread#^c25334459|cbhl]]. Izkata reports clearer thinking after keeping windows closed and identifies outdoor allergy triggers and insects as reasons to avoid opening them [[./thread#^c25336903|Izkata]]. These accounts qualify blanket recommendations to open windows or increase outdoor-air intake.
- AndyMcConachie's investigation of consumer monitors ended after repeatedly encountering reviews alleging factory miscalibration or calibration drift; this was a review-based assessment, not their own sensor testing [[./thread#^c25333243|AndyMcConachie]]. jschwartzi describes professional experience with oxygen sensors and raises airflow, pressure, and fouling concerns, while explicitly lacking contaminant-sensor experience [[./thread#^c25335350|jschwartzi]]. Their analogy warrants caution without validating or invalidating every consumer air monitor.
- The houseplant recommendation receives a substantive correction. csnover cites a VOC-removal review and an estimate of about ten plants per square foot for a meaningful effect [[./thread#^c25334110|csnover]]. jacquesm adds concerns about fungi, pests, upkeep, and cost [[./thread#^c25334209|jacquesm]]. samstave acknowledges the correction and reframes their preference around liking plants [[./thread#^c25334208|samstave]] [[./thread#^c25334418|samstave]]. The quoted removal estimate concerns VOCs, not demonstrated PM2.5, NO2, or CO2 control.
- The claim that airing a room saves heating energy by reducing humidity is contested. awild advances that argument [[./thread#^c25333787|awild]], while throwaway0a5e says the linked heat-capacity difference is only a few percent and insufficient to offset replacing warm air with cold air [[./thread#^c25334254|throwaway0a5e]]. DubiousPusher also says outside air increases humidity where they live [[./thread#^c25334090|DubiousPusher]]. The discussion does not establish a universal humidity or energy benefit from opening windows.

## Assumptions

- Attributing improved health to a particular pollutant requires more than a before-and-after account. pbronez reports improvement after both adding HEPA units and moving away from a major road [[./thread#^c25332605|pbronez]]; Nacdor reports improvement after leaving a stuffy Philadelphia high-rise for the suburbs [[./thread#^c25334366|Nacdor]]. These changes could alter several exposures and housing conditions simultaneously; neither account isolates PM2.5, NO2, or another cause.
- The different pollutants and instruments must remain distinct. exhilaration reports kitchen CO2 increases on an Awair [[./thread#^c25333751|exhilaration]], treeman79 describes a CO alarm and a reported natural-gas leak [[./thread#^c25333264|treeman79]], and binarycodedhex reports very low particle concentrations [[./thread#^c25340625|binarycodedhex]]. None of those observations, by itself, measures the other pollutants or establishes comprehensive indoor-air safety.
- Claims that an exchanger necessarily improves air quality assume a suitable outdoor supply and functioning air paths. sjg007 says their exchanger improves AQI [[./thread#^c25333211|sjg007]], while tempestn recommends continuous furnace-fan operation, particularly with a fresh-air intake [[./thread#^c25338352|tempestn]]. cbhl's smoke-import experience shows why the outdoor supply matters [[./thread#^c25334459|cbhl]]; fan operation alone does not establish how much clean outdoor air reaches an occupied room.
- Stove ownership and cooking frequency cannot be inferred reliably from income here. kaitai explicitly offers an impression that poorer Americans are more likely to use gas [[./thread#^c25334639|kaitai]], whereas criley2 and phil21 describe cheap electric ranges and gas in higher-end or older housing [[./thread#^c25335279|criley2]] [[./thread#^c25335112|phil21]]. ummonk's suggestion that electric-stove owners cook less is also a hypothesis without supplied usage data [[./thread#^c25338184|ummonk]] [[./thread#^c25338462|ummonk]].
- Gas heating equipment should not automatically be treated as exposing occupants in the same way as an open stove burner. outworlder says furnace exhaust should remain separate from indoor air [[./thread#^c25337421|outworlder]], and intrasight reports externally vented furnace and water-heater exhaust [[./thread#^c25334288|intrasight]]. However, throwaway201103 supplies an example of an unvented gas heater [[./thread#^c25333057|throwaway201103]]. Appliance configuration and condition are essential missing variables.
- Comfort explanations are sometimes asserted without measurements. bcheung attributes office lethargy to oxygen depletion and noxious gases but supplies no oxygen or pollutant readings [[./thread#^c25337904|bcheung]]. Likewise, 0xbadcafebee's suggestion that traditional housing avoided mold and CO problems is unsupported historical generalization in this record [[./thread#^c25342537|0xbadcafebee]]. Neither should be promoted into an established mechanism.
- Filter maintenance frequency and particle-removal performance are separate questions. beerandt recommends frequent replacement, even with inexpensive fiberglass filters [[./thread#^c25333997|beerandt]], and jacquesm endorses that approach while discussing contamination and energy costs [[./thread#^c25334169|jacquesm]]. No comparative PM2.5 measurements are supplied to establish that this strategy matches or exceeds appropriately maintained higher-efficiency filtration.
- Broader health claims also require evidence beyond these comments. kaitai links neighborhood air quality, socioeconomic status, and COVID mortality without supplying results [[./thread#^c25334639|kaitai]]. floatingatoll suggests sufficiently good ventilation could have avoided restrictions associated with indoor smoke and COVID exposure [[./thread#^c25335235|floatingatoll]]. These are arguments about interconnected risks and possible solutions, not demonstrated protection in the described buildings.

## Conditions

- Filtration has continuing costs and availability constraints. pbronez reports buying several standalone units for approximately \$100–200 each and replacing different filters monthly and quarterly, which became expensive [[./thread#^c25336447|pbronez]]. Raphmedia warns about proprietary replacements and discontinued stock [[./thread#^c25338416|Raphmedia]]. dralley suggests a filter attached to a \$20 box fan [[./thread#^c25333466|dralley]], but supplies no comparative performance results; jcims describes a more expensive Coway as quiet at low speed and low-maintenance [[./thread#^c25334244|jcims]].
- Renters and older buildings may make source removal or external exhaust difficult. nslav's recirculating hood is inconvenient to duct outdoors [[./thread#^c25336106|nslav]], and zaphoyd uses a portable induction unit over a rental's gas stove because replacing the full appliance is difficult [[./thread#^c25335663|zaphoyd]]. drobert identifies dedicated wiring and potentially a new fuse box as major UK conversion costs [[./thread#^c25339137|drobert]]; OldHand2018 faces a cooktop-circuit upgrade [[./thread#^c25336682|OldHand2018]].
- Cooking technique and equipment affect the acceptability of induction. nsm values direct flame for rotis [[./thread#^c25335678|nsm]], and SECProto identifies limitations for tilting and heating the sides of a wok [[./thread#^c25339531|SECProto]]. raspyberr says tilting a pan interrupts induction [[./thread#^c25332744|raspyberr]], while killion reports successfully searing and basting within their unit's height limits [[./thread#^c25335552|killion]]. These differences suggest model- and technique-dependent tradeoffs rather than one universal cooking experience.
- Fuel availability and local tariffs can reverse the financial comparison. Bedon292 finds induction cheaper because their alternative is propane rather than piped natural gas [[./thread#^c25339952|Bedon292]]. RocketOne says rural gas-line trenching is prohibitive and their stove, furnace, and dryer use propane [[./thread#^c25338353|RocketOne]]. mschuster91 reports much cheaper gas than electricity and lower combined household bills than electrically heated neighbors in Germany [[./thread#^c25337498|mschuster91]]. Those are local 2020 circumstances, not current universal prices or cooking-only cost comparisons.
- Replacing combustion heating is also climate- and equipment-dependent. mikestew describes poor cold-weather heat-pump experiences, partly from Indiana roughly 25 years earlier [[./thread#^c25333829|mikestew]], whereas crazygringo reports a newer NYC unit operating at 0°F and cites specifications favoring it over resistance heat down to about −10°F [[./thread#^c25333720|crazygringo]]. Massachusetts cost claims also differ: gwbas1c reports favorable economics [[./thread#^c25335172|gwbas1c]], while sokoloff questions whether delivery charges were included [[./thread#^c25335475|sokoloff]].
- Building age, jurisdiction, and implementation limit code-based assurances. exhilaration's Pennsylvania home built in 2015 lacks an HRV/ERV [[./thread#^c25333609|exhilaration]] [[./thread#^c25334155|exhilaration]]; lostapathy says they are rare even around \$650,000 in Kansas City [[./thread#^c25335810|lostapathy]]; bluGill recalls Minnesota requirements permitting ventilation alternatives [[./thread#^c25336520|bluGill]]. rsync's broad assertion about required range exhaust [[./thread#^c25333104|rsync]] is challenged by crazygringo's NYC apartments [[./thread#^c25333551|crazygringo]] and 52-6F-62's newer condos with cosmetic, non-exhausting hood enclosures [[./thread#^c25333408|52-6F-62]].
- Implementing a solution depends on competent design and service, not just buying equipment. beerandt warns that changing vents can affect pressure, cycling, condensation, and system performance [[./thread#^c25333997|beerandt]]. clusterhacks reports difficulty finding knowledgeable technicians [[./thread#^c25335514|clusterhacks]]; mackey describes an oversized boiler that cannot reach its advertised operating efficiency [[./thread#^c25336189|mackey]]; ssully recounts expensive, unsuccessful troubleshooting during Chicago's severe cold [[./thread#^c25337660|ssully]]. These experiences qualify the practical usefulness of simply advising someone to call a professional.
- Window ventilation depends on weather, outdoor pollution, and insects. ummonk describes routinely opening windows while cooking in SF [[./thread#^c25338462|ummonk]], but cbhl reports wildfire smoke entering an SF high-rise through mechanical intake [[./thread#^c25334459|cbhl]]. vram22 describes long periods of open windows in India, with exceptions for cold, pollution, and mosquitoes [[./thread#^c25333974|vram22]]; valarauko identifies window mesh as an insect-management option [[./thread#^c25336465|valarauko]]. None establishes that local outdoor air is always suitable.
- Habits and building design also constrain airing. rsync describes daily winter ventilation in Minneapolis [[./thread#^c25333045|rsync]], while lifelong Twin Cities resident coldpie has never encountered the practice [[./thread#^c25333505|coldpie]]. NikolaNovak describes non-opening windows, reliance on HVAC, and cultural resistance to drafts [[./thread#^c25333206|NikolaNovak]]. Mediterraneo10 reports Romanian families combining daily airing with strong objections to drafts afterward [[./thread#^c25333941|Mediterraneo10]]. These accounts show variation even within regions rather than a population-wide custom.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The supplied discussion does not settle the causal contribution or absolute asthma risk attributable to ordinary gas cooking. ruste emphasizes confounding and questions the interpretation of a claimed 40\% increase [[./thread#^c25334361|ruste]], while jcoq invokes the broader research record [[./thread#^c25334214|jcoq]]. scythe discusses a 24\% increase [[./thread#^c25332743|scythe]]. These percentages cannot be treated as interchangeable without the underlying populations, exposure definitions, outcomes, and controls.
- Quantitative exposure evidence is limited. johnboiles describes PM2.5 spikes without numbers [[./thread#^c25404434|johnboiles]], binarycodedhex reports approximate near-zero particle concentrations without validation details [[./thread#^c25340625|binarycodedhex]], and exhilaration and hinkley report CO2 increases without ppm values [[./thread#^c25333751|exhilaration]] [[./thread#^c25333603|hinkley]]. This unit contains no firsthand numerical NO2 concentrations or paired indoor/outdoor measurements sufficient to rank the described homes or interventions.
- Ordinary CO2 effects on cognition and sleep remain unestablished here. tempestn describes an overnight closed-bedroom difference observable with a CO2 monitor but gives neither readings nor measured sleep outcomes [[./thread#^c25338352|tempestn]]. arp242's headaches [[./thread#^c25333990|arp242]] and Izkata's clearer thinking with closed windows [[./thread#^c25336903|Izkata]] do not identify CO2 as their cause. treeman79's difficult-waking account concerns reported CO and gas exposure, not evidence about routine bedroom CO2 [[./thread#^c25335454|treeman79]].
- How much protection a properly installed and consistently used hood provides remains contested. drewcon requests a vented-versus-unvented comparison [[./thread#^c25332546|drewcon]], and coffeefirst asks that appliance age be distinguished [[./thread#^c25332760|coffeefirst]]. modoc infers nearly complete gas capture from a hood drawing visible smoke from ten feet away [[./thread#^c25337138|modoc]], whereas johnboiles still observes cooking-related deterioration after external ducting [[./thread#^c25404434|johnboiles]]. Smoke movement, particle reduction, and NO2 exposure are not interchangeable measurements.
- The failed CO alarm is not explained conclusively. jly suggests that standard alarms may miss lower sustained concentrations and mentions monitors alerting at 5 ppm [[./thread#^c25333714|jly]], but treeman79 reports a discrepancy even when a gas-company instrument showed extreme readings [[./thread#^c25333822|treeman79]]. mint2 speculates about interference from background gases [[./thread#^c25340930|mint2]]. gist suggests multiple manufacturers' detectors [[./thread#^c25335594|gist]], but no failure analysis or reliability comparison is provided.
- Evidence for alternative gas removal and low-maintenance cleaning is incomplete. soupson links a manufacturer's claim that an Austin Air purifier removes NO2 [[./thread#^c25332945|soupson]], without household test results. inquirerofsorts asserts that a water fountain is an efficient cleaner [[./thread#^c25333871|inquirerofsorts]], but guest\_guest's request for evidence and strangegecko's airflow objection receive no substantive answer here [[./thread#^c25334207|guest\_guest]] [[./thread#^c25335120|strangegecko]]. turtlebits recommends washable-disc airwashers while explicitly acknowledging uncertainty about cleaning effectiveness [[./thread#^c25337560|turtlebits]].
- Several exposure questions remain unanswered or only indirectly addressed: RocketOne asks whether propane's refinement changes the risk [[./thread#^c25338353|RocketOne]]; voisin and RyanShook ask about gas fireplaces [[./thread#^c25335676|voisin]] [[./thread#^c25344584|RyanShook]]; mattgreenrocks asks about furnaces, gas dryers, and what monitors detect their problems [[./thread#^c25336362|mattgreenrocks]]. ampdepolymerase mentions radon without measurements or a supporting geographic comparison [[./thread#^c25334187|ampdepolymerase]]. This unit cannot establish comparative risks for those sources.
