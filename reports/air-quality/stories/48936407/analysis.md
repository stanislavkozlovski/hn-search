# [800 Canadian wildfires burning as air quality alerts extend to US](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48936407)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion follows the effects of widespread Canadian wildfires and smoke reaching communities in Canada and the United States, with reports of poor visibility and unusual sky colors. Participants debate how much recent fires reflect climate change, natural fire cycles, forest management, logging, beetle outbreaks, and human ignition. They challenge claims about historical fire patterns and discuss the scale and practicality of controlled burns and firebreaks. A separate heated thread attributes coordinated fires to geopolitical sabotage, prompting disputes over evidence and arson classifications.
Tags: wildfires, wildfire smoke, air quality, climate change, forest management, boreal forest, fire history, arson claims

## Relevance

People describe wildfire smoke affecting visibility and local air quality across regions, including very poor visibility and unusual sky color. The thread also debates wildfire causes, but the firsthand smoke observations directly concern outdoor exposure. ## Arguments

- Wildfire smoke is described as an exposure that extends far beyond the fire zone. segmondy [[./thread#^c48939780|segmondy]] reports severe air quality 700 miles away, while longitudinal93 [[./thread#^c48942069|longitudinal93]] describes smoke crossing the border in the opposite direction, from Washington and Oregon into Vancouver. These accounts support regional concern without establishing pollutant concentrations or individual doses.
- aurelius\_v [[./thread#^c48943203|aurelius\_v]] argues that smoky skies have become a recurring feature of urban summers in Toronto, Vancouver, Montreal, and New York compared with earlier decades. This is a broad historical claim about everyday exposure, not a measured city-by-city trend or a representative survey.
- edawg88 [[./thread#^c48939180|edawg88]] suggests managed burns could prevent the worst fires; remarkEon [[./thread#^c48940041|remarkEon]] proposes controlled burns, vegetation removal, and firebreaks even in remote forests. These are proposals to reduce smoke at its source. Neither supplies evidence comparing personal exposure before and after treatment or quantifying smoke generated by the treatments themselves.
- tencentshill [[./thread#^c48948667|tencentshill]] predicts that smoke will eventually diminish if burned trees cannot regrow. This is a conditional argument about fuel exhaustion, not an observed improvement in air quality.

## Counterarguments

- Responding to nubinetwork’s self-deprecating comment about insufficient firefighters [[./thread#^c48938145|nubinetwork]], bathtub365 [[./thread#^c48942752|bathtub365]] argues that remoteness, geographic scale, and fires becoming uncontrollable make this more than a staffing problem. defrost [[./thread#^c48965014|defrost]] likewise challenges the feasibility of treating vast, exceptionally dry forests with clearing and firebreaks.
- gwerbin [[./thread#^c48939707|gwerbin]] questions whether explanations based on historical fire suppression in populated or agricultural US regions transfer to remote Canadian forests. Teever [[./thread#^c48953476|Teever]] asks whether Canada ever conducted prevention at the assumed scale, and foxyv [[./thread#^c48940286|foxyv]] stresses that the ecological role of fire differs between biomes. These replies challenge the premises behind a universal prevention prescription.
- pvaldes [[./thread#^c48956596|pvaldes]] disputes the assumption that removing brush or dead wood necessarily reduces fire risk, arguing that decomposition supports water retention and that grazing can damage forest regeneration. These are ecological claims offered against simplified clearing proposals, without measured effects on residents’ smoke exposure.
- In response to everdrive’s question about why repeated annual fires do not exhaust the available fuel [[./thread#^c48950607|everdrive]], rjrjrjrj [[./thread#^c48952298|rjrjrjrj]] challenges the premise that the same areas are repeatedly burning. Canada’s enormous forest area permits fires in widely separated places; annual national fire reports alone do not establish repeated combustion of the same fuel.
- alexk307 [[./thread#^c48947893|alexk307]] disputes aurelius\_v’s claim of an unprecedented millennium-scale fire season, invoking heavy-tailed fire sizes and a distinction between fire counts and burned area while acknowledging some climate contribution. pvaldes [[./thread#^c48956484|pvaldes]] counters the suggestion that historical evidence is unavailable by pointing to incomplete but detailed tree-ring records.

## Assumptions

- The descriptions from AngryData [[./thread#^c48938037|AngryData]], bathtub365 [[./thread#^c48942772|bathtub365]], and longitudinal93 [[./thread#^c48942069|longitudinal93]] use smell, sky colour, dimmed sunlight, and perceived breathability as indicators of smoke severity. These observations do not identify PM2.5, NO2, or CO2 concentrations and cannot be converted into pollutant doses from the supplied information.
- The meaning of a yellow map is unresolved. busymom0 [[./thread#^c48938856|busymom0]] does not identify the weather app or its scale; mikestew [[./thread#^c48939511|mikestew]] explicitly questions the colour’s meaning and calls yellow “healthy” while linking a US guide. The supplied discussion does not establish a shared colour convention, so that label should not be treated as a verified interpretation of busymom0’s display.
- mikestew [[./thread#^c48939511|mikestew]] infers that British Columbia must have had worse air than Washington because BC fires produced Washington’s severe readings, but supplies no BC reading. Similarly, segmondy’s description of having the worst air quality in the US [[./thread#^c48939780|segmondy]] lacks a stated index, comparison source, or measurement method.

## Conditions

- Regional experience differs within Canada. mikestew [[./thread#^c48939511|mikestew]] asks whether busymom0 [[./thread#^c48938856|busymom0]] is in eastern Canada, but that location is never confirmed. Their first encounter with a yellow weather-app map therefore cannot establish that severe smoke was new throughout Canada, particularly given bathtub365’s account of earlier extreme conditions in BC [[./thread#^c48942772|bathtub365]].
- Geographic scale matters both for recurring smoke sources and proposed prevention. rjrjrjrj [[./thread#^c48952298|rjrjrjrj]] places Lytton roughly 700 km west of Jasper and Jasper roughly 2,000 km west of Thunder Bay. defrost [[./thread#^c48965014|defrost]] argues that the size and dryness of remote forests constrain preventive treatment; neither comment provides a costed alternative.
- Not every US smoke episode in this discussion is attributed solely to Canada. heelix [[./thread#^c48941398|heelix]] identifies fires in the Boundary Waters Canoe Area as well as Canadian fires north of Ely, placing Minneapolis approximately 190 nautical miles south of the BWCA fires. Their account therefore involves multiple regional smoke sources.

## Lived experiences

- segmondy [[./thread#^c48939780|segmondy]] reports being 700 miles from the fires while experiencing what they describe as the worst air quality in the US. This is a personal report of distant impact, but the location, pollutant, instrument, and numerical reading are unspecified.
- busymom0 [[./thread#^c48938856|busymom0]], identifying as Canadian, describes an unusually coloured sky and says this was their first week seeing a yellow air-quality map in their weather app. This combines a visual observation with an app display, without a numerical concentration.
- mikestew [[./thread#^c48939511|mikestew]] reports that BC wildfires previously pushed Washington maps into the “very unhealthy” AQI range of 201–300. This is a reported map reading, not a documented personal sensor measurement; the underlying pollutant, station, date, and averaging period are not supplied.
- bathtub365 [[./thread#^c48942772|bathtub365]] describes BC smoke at its worst as a dark yellow sky, a dark blood-red sun, and daylight resembling a heavily overcast Seattle day with everything tinted yellow. This is a detailed sensory account, not a pollutant measurement.
- longitudinal93 [[./thread#^c48942069|longitudinal93]], replying to the account of Canadian smoke affecting Washington, recalls Washington and Oregon fires making Vancouver’s air “unbreathable” three years earlier. The wording conveys perceived severity; it does not document a diagnosed respiratory condition or measured physiological effect.
- AngryData [[./thread#^c48938037|AngryData]] describes Michigan smoke as a heavy, smelly fog persisting day and night, strongly dimming the sun and smelling like numerous nearby brush fires. This reports sustained sensory discomfort, but does not establish indoor exposure or a sleep effect.
- heelix [[./thread#^c48941398|heelix]] reports smoke reducing visibility at the local KFCM airfield to one mile that morning, producing IFR conditions. They worry about travel to the following week’s Oshkosh airshow because their intended aircraft is unsuitable for that visibility. This is a quantitative visibility report and a concrete mobility concern, not an air-pollutant concentration or a confirmed cancellation.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The historical significance and likely recurrence of severe smoke-producing fire seasons remain disputed. aurelius\_v [[./thread#^c48943203|aurelius\_v]] cites more than 15 million hectares and approximately 4\% of Canada’s forest burning in 2023; alexk307 [[./thread#^c48994552|alexk307]] and aurelius\_v [[./thread#^c49051935|aurelius\_v]] subsequently disagree over what linked historical research supports. The papers’ contents are not supplied, and this exchange does not establish a future smoke-exposure trajectory for any particular city.
- Pollutant levels and sensor reliability remain unassessed. The AQI range reported by mikestew [[./thread#^c48939511|mikestew]] and visibility reported by heelix [[./thread#^c48941398|heelix]] are quantitative, but this unit contains no explicit PM2.5, NO2, or CO2 concentrations, sensor comparisons, calibration checks, or indoor–outdoor measurements. It cannot establish pollutant composition or monitor accuracy.
- Health, cognition, and sleep effects are not measured here. AngryData’s day-and-night smoke account [[./thread#^c48938037|AngryData]] and longitudinal93’s description of unbreathable air [[./thread#^c48942069|longitudinal93]] document perceived conditions, but provide no clinical outcomes, cognitive testing, sleep observations, or exposure–response evidence. Persistent nighttime smoke should not be recast as demonstrated sleep impairment.
- Household mitigation and window-opening tradeoffs are unanswered. The outdoor accounts from AngryData [[./thread#^c48938037|AngryData]] and bathtub365 [[./thread#^c48942772|bathtub365]] contain no comparisons of open versus closed windows, ventilation, filtration, or indoor CO2. There are also no relevant cooking or traffic exposure experiments in this unit. It therefore supplies no tested household improvement or evidence for deciding when opening windows would help or worsen total exposure.
