# [Each day a cruise ship emits as much particulate matter as a million cars](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=19016428)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

The thread challenges the headline comparison between cruise-ship particulate emissions and car emissions, asking what pollutants, operating periods, locations, and exposure levels the figures represent. Participants distinguish sulfur and nitrogen pollution from carbon dioxide and compare ships with modern vehicles, including how diesel particulate filters work and whether pollution shifts during regeneration. Discussion considers harbor impacts, ship fuel and regulation, cargo shipping, and possible alternatives such as cleaner fuels or nuclear propulsion. A separate branch considers worker exposure, cruise travel, and how to compare its emissions with other forms of travel.
Tags: cruise ships, particulate matter, shipping emissions, diesel filters, air pollution, emissions comparisons, fuel policy

## Relevance

The thread questions how ship particulate measurements relate to where passengers and workers actually spend time, and discusses direct exposure in port cities. It includes a firsthand account from someone who worked for a tourist-ship company and was surprised by its emissions, while noting that the headline comparison is poorly specified. ## Arguments

- Ship exhaust is presented as a potentially important pollution source. raxxorrax [[./thread#^c19016772|raxxorrax]] attributes this partly to heavy-oil use offshore; masonic [[./thread#^c19033681|masonic]] emphasizes that bunker fuel is dirtier than road diesel. These are explanations offered by commenters, without measurements establishing passengers’ or nearby residents’ exposure.
- Vehicle emissions controls are offered as evidence that source reduction can improve air quality. Responding to growlist’s frustration with restrictions on motorists [[./thread#^c19019232|growlist]], skywhopper [[./thread#^c19019731|skywhopper]] argues that cleaner cars partly explain the enormous ship-to-car emissions ratio. Nasrudith [[./thread#^c19017813|Nasrudith]] similarly emphasizes strict automotive particulate standards. Neither supplies measured trends in this export.
- Potential improvements concern emissions at their source. sdoering [[./thread#^c19025939|sdoering]] describes cleaner-fuel switching near shore, ship particulate filtration, and the absence of electricity connections at many harbors, which leaves ships running engines for onboard power. growlist [[./thread#^c19019232|growlist]] proposes LPG. The discussion provides no measured comparison of the resulting personal exposure reductions.
- Health concerns are asserted rather than demonstrated. makerofspoons [[./thread#^c19017525|makerofspoons]] emphasizes health consequences of sulfur dioxide. raxxorrax [[./thread#^c19016772|raxxorrax]] vaguely recalls a story about widespread poisoning symptoms after a ship failed to switch fuel before entering a harbor. That recollection is secondhand, with no identified incident, concentrations, diagnoses, or supporting study in the supplied material.

## Counterarguments

- Total emissions do not establish what people breathe. skywhopper [[./thread#^c19019692|skywhopper]] says the article compares particulate readings near ship funnels with Piccadilly Circus but omits exposure duration and readings from balconies, pools, and staff quarters. Consequently, the reported sampling cannot establish typical passenger or worker exposure, even if the measurements themselves were collected fairly.
- The separate cargo-ship comparison is challenged on both pollutant and fleet selection. agentofoblivion [[./thread#^c19017221|agentofoblivion]] says 16 ships equal all cars in pollution; rkul [[./thread#^c19017472|rkul]] says the comparison concerns sulfur, while TheCoelacanth [[./thread#^c19027851|TheCoelacanth]] identifies NOx and SOx. sailingparrot [[./thread#^c19018756|sailingparrot]] specifies the 16 largest ships, not representative vessels from the claimed fleet of over 100,000. Nasrudith [[./thread#^c19017813|Nasrudith]] also notes that CO2 is not particulate matter. These comments do not establish a PM2.5 or NO2-specific comparison.
- The claim that diesel particulate filters merely relocate soot is explicitly corrected and withdrawn. supermatt [[./thread#^c19024666|supermatt]] initially says regeneration releases trapped particles on highways. sdoering [[./thread#^c19025909|sdoering]] replies that regeneration burns most collected material, while some particles escape filtration or remain as residue; their approximately 5\% figure and filter lifetime below 200,000 km are commenter claims, not supplied test results. supermatt [[./thread#^c19043575|supermatt]] acknowledges being misinformed.
- Distance from cities does not resolve all exposure concerns. archi42 [[./thread#^c19018407|archi42]] contrasts offshore shipping with traffic emissions directly affecting urban residents, and dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c19024986|dzhiurgis]] questions whether distant maritime pollution matters. Replying specifically to archi42, sdoering [[./thread#^c19025939|sdoering]] points to direct exposure in harbor cities when docked ships keep engines running. skywhopper’s passenger-and-staff questions [[./thread#^c19019692|skywhopper]] also concern people aboard the emitting vessel.

## Assumptions

- Cleaner air elsewhere aboard is a hypothesis, not a finding. skywhopper [[./thread#^c19019692|skywhopper]] explicitly labels as a guess the suggestion that most ship locations would have substantially better particulate levels than Piccadilly Circus. The missing spatial measurements support neither that reassuring hypothesis nor an assumption of uniformly high exposure.
- The million-car comparison leaves its operating-time assumptions unclear. skywhopper [[./thread#^c19019692|skywhopper]] asks whether it means cars operating continuously or following ordinary daily driving routines. The comment also notes that ships provide accommodation and electricity. Those questions complicate emissions comparisons, but neither occupancy nor equivalent services determines an individual’s inhaled exposure.
- Job-specific health predictions rest on unsupported assumptions. In response to a nine-month employment question, jstarfish [[./thread#^c19029228|jstarfish]] distinguishes above-deck and below-deck work, alleges substantial carcinogen and solvent exposure, and makes a deterministic cancer prediction before qualifying whether nine months would suffice. No exposure assessment or epidemiological evidence supports that prediction, and RealDinosaur’s later clarification identifies the prospective job as bassist [[./thread#^c19036009|RealDinosaur]].

## Conditions

- Geography and regulation matter. archi42 [[./thread#^c19018407|archi42]] describes German restrictions affecting older diesels, usually Euro 5 and below, in particular cities or streets. This is local regulatory context, not a measured exposure comparison or a universal rule. The discussion dates to 2019, and skywhopper [[./thread#^c19019692|skywhopper]] identifies the underlying article as dating to 2017.
- Fuel switching and port infrastructure constrain potential improvements. According to sdoering [[./thread#^c19025939|sdoering]], ships may use cleaner fuel near shore yet still pollute while generating power at berth, and many harbors lack supporting electricity connections. The comment supplies no before-and-after concentrations, installation costs, or evidence that fuel switching eliminates local exposure.
- A financial explanation is offered but remains unverified: b0bby\_tabl3s [[./thread#^c19020410|b0bby\_tabl3s]], in a flagged comment qualified by recollection, says cargo ships use the cheapest, lowest fuel grade. This supplies a proposed incentive for dirty fuel use, not documented prices or an economic comparison of cleaner fuels and filtration.

## Lived experiences

- sdoering [[./thread#^c19025939|sdoering]] provides the clearest firsthand account: during brief employment at a tourist-ship company, they learned that a ship under construction was considered clean because it had a prototype particulate filter. They report initial problems associated with changing salt levels in the surrounding water and surprise at the industry’s pollution. This is an account of equipment implementation and reliability, without personal air measurements or health outcomes; it does not establish air-sensor accuracy.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Typical onboard concentrations and cumulative exposure remain unknown. skywhopper [[./thread#^c19019692|skywhopper]] identifies missing measurements across occupied spaces and missing information about time spent near exhaust. Ancalagon [[./thread#^c19020365|Ancalagon]] agrees with the substantive concerns but argues that a thorough study or report should have answered most of them; the reply adds no data.
- The employment-health question remains unanswered. RealDinosaur [[./thread#^c19019739|RealDinosaur]] asks whether a nine-month cruise-ship season would reduce life expectancy and later clarifies the musician role [[./thread#^c19036009|RealDinosaur]]. This is a prospective concern, not a report of completed employment or illness. jstarfish’s speculative reply [[./thread#^c19029228|jstarfish]] provides no defensible risk estimate for that role.
- The explanation for the extreme ship-to-car ratio remains disputed. skywhopper [[./thread#^c19019731|skywhopper]] emphasizes successful automotive pollution controls; masonic [[./thread#^c19033681|masonic]] replies that this overlooks dirty bunker fuel. Both factors could contribute, but the comments do not quantify their respective effects or settle growlist’s concern about the relative attention given to maritime and road emissions [[./thread#^c19019232|growlist]].
- Comfort accounts conflict without establishing an air-quality cause. dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c19024976|dzhiurgis]] characterizes cruise work and travel as grueling and mentions smells, without explicitly identifying firsthand experience or the odor source. Keverw [[./thread#^c19066108|Keverw]] challenges the passenger generalization using travel vlogs and expectations about different ships, not personal exposure measurements. Neither account connects odors to PM2.5, NO2, or health effects.
- This source unit cannot resolve most indoor-air questions. skywhopper [[./thread#^c19019692|skywhopper]] discusses inadequately contextualized particulate measurements, but the export reproduces no numerical concentrations, particle-size specification, instrument details, or calibration checks. Nasrudith’s CO2 distinction [[./thread#^c19017813|Nasrudith]] and TheCoelacanth’s NOx/SOx comparison [[./thread#^c19027851|TheCoelacanth]] provide no indoor CO2 or NO2 readings. There are no supplied measurements or firsthand trials addressing cognition, sleep, household ventilation, cooking, wildfire smoke, or whether opening windows improves or worsens exposure.
