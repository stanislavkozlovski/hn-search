# [How much does air pollution cost the U.S.?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=21194518)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants discuss the economic and health costs of air pollution and how to interpret estimates of those costs. The thread ranges across electric vehicles, lifecycle emissions, traffic particulates, vehicle weight and brake wear, air quality in different regions, data limitations, policy choices, and the role of technology and individual behavior.
Tags: air-pollution, public-health, electric-vehicles, emissions, environmental-policy, research-methods

## Relevance

The discussion connects vehicle emissions and non-exhaust PM2.5 with exposure and lung health in cities, and distinguishes those pollutants from combustion-related nitrogen and sulfur oxides. Much of the thread shifts to climate emissions, but the air-exposure discussion is directly relevant. ## Arguments

- d\_burfoot [[./thread#^c21196004|d\_burfoot]] argues that eliminating exhaust where people walk could improve urban lung health even when electricity generation causes pollution elsewhere. seanwilson [[./thread#^c21198807|seanwilson]] adds that centralized electricity production might make pollution easier to control. These are arguments about exposure location and pollution control, not measured health comparisons.
- wffurr [[./thread#^c21196057|wffurr]] argues that electrification leaves substantial traffic particulate pollution, quoting estimates that EV PM2.5 emissions are only 1–3\% below modern combustion vehicles and that non-exhaust sources account for over 90\% of traffic PM10 and 85\% of traffic PM2.5. These are disputed estimates from a cited paper, not the commenter's own measurements or percentages of all ambient pollution.
- nvarsj [[./thread#^c21200962|nvarsj]] argues that additional cars contribute to congestion and pollution while discouraging walking and cycling. megaremote [[./thread#^c21198669|megaremote]] emphasizes continuing tire pollution and advocates avoiding car ownership. These support reducing traffic alongside changing propulsion, but neither supplies measured exposure reductions.
- aaron695 [[./thread#^c21198564|aaron695]] recommends normalizing indoor air cleaning, using filters for PM2.5 and PM10, and encouraging ordinary people to measure PM2.5. The comment explicitly recognizes forest fires and windblown dust as sources alongside human activity; it reports no purifier installation, readings, or health outcome.
- The health harms discussed are principally respiratory discomfort and premature mortality. SmirkingRevenge [[./thread#^c21199878|SmirkingRevenge]] invokes blackened traffic air, coughing, and lung pain as an illustration of immediately perceptible harm, not an explicit personal symptom history. snthd [[./thread#^c21195707|snthd]] quotes reporting that estimated air-pollution deaths in Africa exceed deaths from unsafe water or childhood malnutrition; this is a claim about research, not firsthand clinical evidence.
- dcolkitt [[./thread#^c21196388|dcolkitt]] proposes replacing older, more polluting cars as a more cost-effective air-quality intervention than shifting new-car production toward EVs. The comment supplies no pollutant-specific reduction, replacement cost, or supporting calculation.

## Counterarguments

- gwbas1c [[./thread#^c21196223|gwbas1c]] and \_ea1k [[./thread#^c21196554|\_ea1k]] challenge the claim that EV weight necessarily increases brake wear, pointing to regenerative braking; \_ea1k nevertheless accepts increased tire wear. m463 [[./thread#^c21198633|m463]] offers an anecdotal Tesla brake-pad lifespan of 100,000–200,000 miles, with low regeneration settings and track driving as exceptions. This is maintenance-related evidence, not a measurement of airborne PM2.5.
- jly [[./thread#^c21196273|jly]] counters a particulate-only comparison by identifying nitrogen and sulfur oxides from combustion as additional traffic pollutants. goalieca [[./thread#^c21196911|goalieca]] replies that PM2.5 and PM10 remain harmful. Together, these comments dispute treating either tailpipe gases or particles alone as a complete account of respiratory risk; neither provides NO2-specific measurements.
- mturmon [[./thread#^c21197263|mturmon]] challenges the non-exhaust paper's limited modeling and quotes a correction removing an Edinburgh affiliation and disclosing nonfinancial support from Innas. Amygaz [[./thread#^c21197126|Amygaz]] additionally alleges that no research or peer review occurred, but those stronger allegations are not established by the correction excerpt supplied here. The disclosure concerns warrant scrutiny without themselves determining the numerical result.
- dcolkitt [[./thread#^c21200927|dcolkitt]], replying to Robotbeat [[./thread#^c21197102|Robotbeat]], points out that carbon-emissions comparisons do not answer the preceding air-pollution cost-effectiveness question. The extensive vehicle CO2 accounting in this branch supplies neither local PM2.5/NO2 exposure estimates nor indoor CO2 concentrations.

## Assumptions

- The disputed EV particulate comparison depends heavily on modeling choices. polyomino [[./thread#^c21196872|polyomino]] describes treating heavier EVs like light trucks, attributing 67\% more brake emissions but only 2\% more tire emissions. mturmon [[./thread#^c21197263|mturmon]] describes weight-scaled emissions with road-dust resuspension dominating; rconti [[./thread#^c21200146|rconti]] questions using weight as a proxy for poorer aerodynamics. These are commenters' readings of assumptions, not independently verified emission factors.
- aaron695 [[./thread#^c21198564|aaron695]] tentatively suggests mother-in-law's tongue for VOC removal and changing CO2/oxygen levels, linking a NASA study. Applying that suggestion to an occupied home assumes useful effects at realistic plant quantities and ventilation rates, none of which the comment establishes. The commenter also warns of toxicity to children and animals.
- kiliantics [[./thread#^c21197451|kiliantics]] suspects inadequate vehicle-emission regulation or enforcement explains visible East African road pollution. ido [[./thread#^c21200967|ido]] attributes historically yellow urban air in India to highly polluting vehicles or fuel despite lower car ownership. Their observations support accounts of poor perceived air quality; these causal explanations remain inferences without emissions inventories or measurements.
- Mathnerd314 [[./thread#^c21195684|Mathnerd314]] considers relative sector and time comparisons more credible than absolute monetary damages, quoting that approximately 60\% of modeled damages could be assigned to industrial sectors. Their suggested 2–10\% of GDP range is speculation, not a reported confidence interval. zaroth [[./thread#^c21200082|zaroth]] interprets avoided mortality and other damages as economic value recoverable through cleaner air; these monetary interpretations are distinct from measured personal exposure or household savings.

## Conditions

- The proposed benefit of moving combustion away from streets depends on geography and generation sources. d\_burfoot [[./thread#^c21196004|d\_burfoot]] explicitly imagines distant power generation, while nwah1 [[./thread#^c21196291|nwah1]] conditions a claimed overall health advantage on the local power plant being something other than coal. Neither quantifies exposure near the plant or establishes a universal coal-versus-noncoal health threshold.
- Vehicle replacement economics depend on usage and local vehicle lifetimes. mikepurvis [[./thread#^c21197540|mikepurvis]] describes driving under 10,000 km annually in Ontario and considers a new Tesla or Prius unjustified for that household; they also contrast Ontario's rust-limited fleet with older vehicles surviving in the southwest. Dylan16807 [[./thread#^c21200578|Dylan16807]] identifies road salting as an explanation. These conditions limit blanket claims about replacing older vehicles.
- Regional PM2.5 estimates require caution where monitoring is sparse. snthd [[./thread#^c21195707|snthd]] quotes a warning about limited African ground measurements and a review assigning 17\% to traffic, 10\% to industry, 34\% to domestic fuel burning, 17\% to unspecified human sources, and 22\% to natural sources such as dust and sea salt. These limited-study estimates do not establish uniform conditions across Africa or exposure inside particular homes.
- Cooking and heating enter through regional source attribution: sxates [[./thread#^c21195571|sxates]] asks whether burning fuels for those purposes explains the map, and snthd [[./thread#^c21195707|snthd]] supplies the domestic-fuel estimate. This supports considering household combustion as a source, but does not identify stove types, kitchen ventilation, indoor concentrations, or an individual cooking intervention.

## Lived experiences

- kiliantics [[./thread#^c21197451|kiliantics]] reports recently visiting East Africa and finding road air barely breathable, with vehicles visibly emitting large black clouds. This is firsthand sensory evidence from the roads they encountered, without named monitoring sites, pollutant concentrations, or diagnosed health effects.
- ido [[./thread#^c21200967|ido]], replying to the East Africa account, recalls yellow air in Indian cities of roughly one million people during a visit approximately 15 years before the 2019 discussion. The account is historical and visual; it does not establish present conditions, specific pollutants, or equivalent conditions throughout India.
- mikepurvis [[./thread#^c21197540|mikepurvis]] describes year-round bicycle commuting, a partner relying largely on walking and transit, and limited household driving, previously in a 2003 Volvo and subsequently a 2013 Mazda 5. Their practical response is to minimize kilometers rather than purchase a more efficient new car. They report no resulting personal air-exposure or health measurements.
- avocado4 [[./thread#^c21195129|avocado4]] reports finding the WHO air-pollution map useful when traveling abroad. This is firsthand use of an information resource, not firsthand monitoring or validation of the map's local accuracy.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Whether the cited EV study adequately accounted for regenerative braking remains explicitly disputed. polyomino [[./thread#^c21196872|polyomino]] and Robotbeat [[./thread#^c21196959|Robotbeat]] say it ignored regeneration, whereas rconti [[./thread#^c21200146|rconti]], after briefly examining the full text, says it modeled zero EV brake wear. mikepurvis [[./thread#^c21197460|mikepurvis]] requests actual brake-use or service-record evidence. The supplied discussion does not reconcile these readings or provide those records.
- Road-dust resuspension is contested both as an accounting category and as a driver of the estimated result. jesuschroist [[./thread#^c21199286|jesuschroist]] questions counting it as an emission and says it distorts the comparison. wffurr [[./thread#^c21207473|wffurr]] replies that resuspended material matters for people breathing near roads regardless. Its actual magnitude and sensitivity to vehicle characteristics remain unmeasured in these comments.
- The net personal health benefit of electrification cannot be calculated from this unit. d\_burfoot [[./thread#^c21196004|d\_burfoot]] emphasizes removing nearby exhaust, wffurr [[./thread#^c21196057|wffurr]] emphasizes residual particulate emissions, and jly [[./thread#^c21196273|jly]] emphasizes combustion gases. No common dataset connects these competing considerations to roadside concentrations, individual dose, or health outcomes.
- The claimed economic advantage of replacing older polluting cars remains unsupported within the exchange. tankerdude [[./thread#^c21196583|tankerdude]] requests data and a comparison with new combustion vehicles; dcolkitt [[./thread#^c21200927|dcolkitt]] subsequently rejects a carbon-based rebuttal. No agreed cost per reduction in harmful air exposure emerges.
- Sensor accuracy and exposure thresholds cannot be assessed here. aaron695 [[./thread#^c21198564|aaron695]] advocates PM2.5 measurement without naming instruments, calibration procedures, or readings, while snthd [[./thread#^c21195707|snthd]] highlights sparse ground monitoring. This unit supplies no firsthand PM2.5, NO2, or indoor CO2 concentration series, and no measured cognition or sleep outcomes; kiliantics [[./thread#^c21197451|kiliantics]] provides sensory discomfort rather than instrumented or clinical assessment.
- Indoor intervention effectiveness and the window-opening tradeoff remain unanswered. aaron695 [[./thread#^c21198564|aaron695]] proposes filtration and mentions forest fires only as a general particulate source; snthd [[./thread#^c21195707|snthd]] supplies regional domestic-combustion attribution. Neither provides indoor/outdoor comparisons, ventilation trials, cooking-related readings, or firsthand wildfire-smoke management. This unit cannot establish when opening windows would improve indoor air or increase exposure.
- Some geographic concerns remain questions or unsupported allegations. ip26 [[./thread#^c21195910|ip26]] asks about Coyhaique, Chile, and links a report without supplying its explanation or measurements. top-news-talk [[./thread#^c21196646|top-news-talk]] alleges that China conceals worse pollution figures but supplies no figures or corroboration. Neither comment establishes the local sources, concentrations, or personal exposure consequences.
