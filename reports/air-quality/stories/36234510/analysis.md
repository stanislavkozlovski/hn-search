# [Air Purifier vs. Positive Pressure Fresh Air System – An Unfair Battle?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=36234510)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion of a comparison between room air purifiers and positive-pressure fresh-air systems. Participants weigh particulate removal against fresh-air exchange and indoor CO2, and consider filtration quality, house tightness, pressure control, energy and temperature losses, heat recovery, humidity and mold, radon and other pollutants, installation constraints, monitoring, and DIY alternatives.
Tags: positive-pressure-ventilation, air-purifiers, indoor-air-quality, PM2.5, CO2, HVAC, heat-recovery

## Relevance

The thread compares positive-pressure filtered fresh air with recirculating purifiers, including firsthand PM2.5 results during wildfire smoke, CO2 concerns, and tradeoffs involving energy, temperature, humidity, and mold. It also discusses sensor-based control and disagreement about what the measurements show. ## Arguments

- Filtered outdoor-air supply addresses two different problems: incoming particles and insufficient ventilation. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36235774|ahaucnx]] advocates HEPA-filtered positive pressure with PM and CO2 monitoring, and emphasizes that supply air must be cleaned in one pass [[./thread#^c36237277|ahaucnx]]. ilyt [[./thread#^c36235309|ilyt]] clarifies that pressurization itself requires continually bringing in outside air; it is not an alternative to fresh-air exchange.
- For recirculating purifiers, sufficient clean-air throughput and distribution matter alongside filtration efficiency. LTL\_FTC [[./thread#^c36236774|LTL\_FTC]] favors several inexpensive units spread across rooms for more circulation and less noise, later clarifying that these are complete HEPA purifiers [[./thread#^c36252999|LTL\_FTC]]. rocqua [[./thread#^c36238434|rocqua]] contrasts repeated filtration with a positive-pressure supply's single-pass requirement; this is an engineering argument, not a controlled comparison in the supplied comments.
- Ventilation and recirculating filtration can complement each other. turtlebits [[./thread#^c36236802|turtlebits]] actually runs a positively balanced ERV alongside a purifier on low. stinos [[./thread#^c36237735|stinos]] explains that a sealed home's ventilation may still admit fine particles through inadequate inlet filters, leaving useful work for an indoor purifier.
- Demand control could reduce unnecessary air exchange and energy use. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36235899|ahaucnx]] reports adjusting supply-fan speed from indoor monitoring and using CO2 to infer occupancy. Former HVAC-controls worker c\_o\_n\_v\_e\_x [[./thread#^c36236673|c\_o\_n\_v\_e\_x]] proposes a distinct approach: directly measure differential pressure and maintain only a small positive margin. These approaches control related but different quantities.
- Heat recovery is proposed as a way to make filtered ventilation more comfortable and affordable in demanding weather. ttul [[./thread#^c36238617|ttul]] advocates HRV plus a heat pump; MichaelZuo [[./thread#^c36250059|MichaelZuo]] claims some enthalpy-recovery systems recover over 75\% of otherwise wasted energy. Neither supplies measured household energy savings in this discussion.
- Airtightness combined with deliberate ventilation offers control over incoming pollution and moisture. Bluestrike2 [[./thread#^c36240969|Bluestrike2]] argues that accidental leakage also brings heat loss and contaminants, while stressing that ventilation cannot repair water intrusion through a defective building envelope. davemp [[./thread#^c36239915|davemp]] emphasizes insulation and dew-point placement; valenterry [[./thread#^c36240297|valenterry]] adds that humid rooms still need moisture removal.
- Opening windows is presented as conditional on the relative quality of indoor and outdoor air. kshahkshah [[./thread#^c36235015|kshahkshah]] suggests it can help a building with a recirculating range hood and accumulated indoor pollution. Georgelemental [[./thread#^c36235216|Georgelemental]] objects when outdoor air contains wildfire smoke, and valenterry [[./thread#^c36240336|valenterry]] favors filtered mechanical ventilation when humidity removal would otherwise draw polluted air indoors.
- Low-cost recirculating filtration has both reported results and proposed improvements. bb88 [[./thread#^c36236809|bb88]] reports a box fan with a MERV 13 filter reaching a PM2.5 reading of zero in an office. lamontcg [[./thread#^c36237901|lamontcg]] proposes four filter walls around a fan to increase filter area and airflow; that proposed improvement is not accompanied by comparative measurements.

## Counterarguments

- Positive pressure is not established as necessary for very low indoor PM2.5. sbirch [[./thread#^c36235095|sbirch]] reports near-zero readings with standalone purifiers during wildfire smoke in a well-sealed house. In direct reply, ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36235129|ahaucnx]] accepts that purifiers can achieve this in modern houses and shifts the argument toward ventilation, stuffiness, and CO2.
- A sealed modern house does not necessarily lack fresh-air exchange. stinos [[./thread#^c36237735|stinos]] directly challenges that premise in ahaucnx's reply, explaining that properly designed airtight housing should also have ventilation. The comparison therefore depends on the home's existing ventilation, not merely whether its windows and envelope are tight.
- A low particle reading does not establish that all relevant pollutants are controlled. LTL\_FTC [[./thread#^c36236774|LTL\_FTC]] distinguishes particulate filtration from VOC treatment and claims substantial charcoal mass is needed for the latter. domoritz [[./thread#^c36241583|domoritz]] worries that continuous outdoor-air supply could import industrial gases, while stephen\_g [[./thread#^c36240401|stephen\_g]] asks about NOx, SOx, and charcoal. No gas-removal results resolve those concerns.
- Ordinary ventilation filters cannot be assumed to deliver the article author's HEPA results. Australian HVAC engineer tjmc [[./thread#^c36235913|tjmc]] asks for a cheaper, lower-resistance F5 baseline; ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36237277|ahaucnx]] says only true HEPA was tested. tjmc [[./thread#^c36238109|tjmc]] says F5 is unsuitable for smoke and describes recirculation as an option for some critical facilities, while arguing it is inadequate for persistent outdoor pollution.
- More supply airflow has costs beyond the fan itself. \_Nat\_ [[./thread#^c36235782|\_Nat\_]] points to heating and cooling losses, and c\_o\_n\_v\_e\_x [[./thread#^c36236673|c\_o\_n\_v\_e\_x]] adds excess fan energy and possible difficulty operating exterior doors at excessive pressure. amluto [[./thread#^c36236051|amluto]] notes tension between positive pressure and heat recovery, so ventilation cannot simply be assumed thermally free.
- Positive pressure is not presented consistently as protection against mold. regularfry [[./thread#^c36238766|regularfry]] was offered airtightening plus positive pressure as damp prevention, whereas 2rsf [[./thread#^c36242032|2rsf]] reports Swedish concern that positive pressure can drive indoor moisture into walls. These are different building and climate contexts, and neither supplies a comparative mold experiment.
- The mold-spore discussion contains a specific correction. rocqua [[./thread#^c36238415|rocqua]], answering DANmode's question about preventing spores from reaching surfaces, says particles that penetrate the supply filter could linger indefinitely. FredPret [[./thread#^c36238695|FredPret]] replies that continuing supply airflow also pushes indoor air and particles out. No spore-deposition measurements establish superiority over recirculating filtration.
- Success with a box-fan purifier does not establish suitability for supplying air through a restrictive filter into a pressurized house. bb88 [[./thread#^c36236809|bb88]] describes indoor recirculation, whereas ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36235774|ahaucnx]] recommends a duct fan for the pressure required by a DIY supply system. These accounts concern different fan duties.

## Assumptions

- Reported zero or near-zero PM2.5 values should be treated as instrument readings, not proof of literally particle-free air. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36235066|ahaucnx]], usbinsider [[./thread#^c36237302|usbinsider]], and bb88 [[./thread#^c36236809|bb88]] report such results, but their comments do not establish detection limits, reference calibration, placement effects, or uncertainty at the low end.
- The household comparisons are not controlled trials. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36235066|ahaucnx]] changed both airtightness and ventilation; sbirch [[./thread#^c36235095|sbirch]] identifies a well-sealed house; jdavis703 [[./thread#^c36235510|jdavis703]] describes a different room and wildfire exposure. usbinsider [[./thread#^c36237302|usbinsider]] explicitly raises filter specification, airflow, mixing, and delivered throughput as possible explanations for different outcomes.
- CO2 is assumed useful for occupancy inference and ventilation control, but proposed targets are not demonstrated health thresholds or achieved results. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36235899|ahaucnx]] uses it in fan control. hedora [[./thread#^c36236777|hedora]] is considering a controller targeting CO2 below roughly 600–800 ppm and PM2.5 below 10 µg/m³; this is a planned setup, not a reported measurement series.
- The central advocate has relevant experience and a commercial connection. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36235066|ahaucnx]] identifies as the article author and AirGradient founder, and recommends the company's kits as using industry-grade sensors [[./thread#^c36235774|ahaucnx]]. That disclosure provides context but neither validates nor invalidates the measurements; the supplied comments contain no independent accuracy assessment of those kits.
- Product specifications and recommendations are a different evidence class from household measurements. bradknowles [[./thread#^c36238337|bradknowles]] cites an IQAir filtration rating and hospital use, while nerdbaggy [[./thread#^c36236184|nerdbaggy]] cites a manufacturer's 12–15-month filter-life claim. These are not independently demonstrated comparisons of exposure reduction, durability, or health outcomes in this source unit.
- The article's graph cannot be independently assessed from this export. mhb [[./thread#^c36235539|mhb]] requests labels, and sampo [[./thread#^c36239838|sampo]] explains that the black curve represents outdoor PM2.5 and the colored bars represent indoor PM2.5. Their comments clarify interpretation but do not supply the underlying data, protocol, or uncertainty.

## Conditions

- Pollution context varies substantially. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36235066|ahaucnx]] describes recurring severe wildfire pollution in Northern Thailand. chuckledog [[./thread#^c36237270|chuckledog]] specifies a mild California climate for a DIY system. 2rsf [[./thread#^c36242032|2rsf]] regards outdoor pollution as largely unimportant locally in Sweden apart from some Stockholm streets; that is the commenter's regional assessment, not evidence that Swedish outdoor air is universally clean.
- Large temperature or humidity differences increase the importance of conditioning incoming air. frognumber [[./thread#^c36235541|frognumber]] describes widely varying weather and reluctance to pump hot or freezing air indoors. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36235792|ahaucnx]] recommends heat recovery under large differentials, but does not quantify the additional load requested for WhatIsDukkha's tight 700-square-foot space [[./thread#^c36235375|WhatIsDukkha]].
- Heat recovery depends on where exhaust air goes. \_Nat\_ [[./thread#^c36251763|\_Nat\_]] distinguishes air escaping through numerous leaks from exhaust routed through a defined channel that could support recovery. This makes envelope sealing and exhaust design relevant to both energy costs and the practicality of a positive-pressure retrofit.
- Installation options depend on the existing air paths. Shank [[./thread#^c36235776|Shank]] has an existing 4–6-inch intake opening in a modern apartment. Too [[./thread#^c36267525|Too]] points out that kitchen and bathroom exhaust may already determine airflow. amluto [[./thread#^c36236036|amluto]] suggests commercial through-wall systems but explicitly leaves uncertainty about whether particular models can provide the desired positive-pressure operation.
- Reversibility and regional availability constrain adoption. prvc [[./thread#^c36239074|prvc]] needs a non-destructive installation; btbuildem [[./thread#^c36241258|btbuildem]] suggests adapting window-AC sealing arrangements. rapjr9 [[./thread#^c36237133|rapjr9]] reports being unable to find a suitable inexpensive window unit and is designing one. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36235214|ahaucnx]] says their own supplier serves Thailand and China only.
- Upfront price and distributed coverage shape purifier choices. LTL\_FTC [[./thread#^c36236774|LTL\_FTC]] preferred seven approximately \$100 units to one \$700 unit, while virtuallynathan [[./thread#^c36235522|virtuallynathan]] emphasizes clean-air delivery per dollar. nerdbaggy [[./thread#^c36235567|nerdbaggy]] cites an approximately \$400 Alen model and its advertised coverage. ninacomputer [[./thread#^c36235595|ninacomputer]] reports that fire-related demand made more elaborate options unaffordable.
- Filter costs and maintenance intervals are model- and exposure-dependent in these accounts. bb88 [[./thread#^c36236809|bb88]] reports 3–4 months for a commodity filter; nerdbaggy [[./thread#^c36236184|nerdbaggy]] cites 12–15 months for an Alen filter. 29athrowaway [[./thread#^c36236487|29athrowaway]] advises vacuuming the discussed Levoit every two weeks and replacing its filter every six months, and separately identifies pets as a factor shortening filter life [[./thread#^c36236841|29athrowaway]]. No common loading test reconciles these intervals.
- Space, noise, appearance, and servicing affect whether filtration remains usable. bredren [[./thread#^c36238411|bredren]] finds DIY boxes inexpensive but bulky, awkward, and sometimes loud enough to mask wanted sounds, preferring compact commercial units in living areas. 29athrowaway [[./thread#^c36241883|29athrowaway]] adds replacement difficulty; bb88 [[./thread#^c36236925|bb88]] favors commodity filters and suggests thicker filters for longer life.
- Energy-saving equipment adds capital cost and retrofit constraints. MichaelZuo [[./thread#^c36250059|MichaelZuo]] recommends a cost-benefit calculation for recovery systems, many aimed at larger customers. In response to 2rsf's ground-source suggestion [[./thread#^c36239843|2rsf]], sagarm [[./thread#^c36244848|sagarm]] argues air-source heat pumps are easier to retrofit; 2rsf [[./thread#^c36245237|2rsf]] agrees that the extra benefit of ground source can be small, especially in milder countries.

## Lived experiences

- ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36235066|ahaucnx]] reports spending several years trying different purifiers in Northern Thailand without consistent success during severe smoke, then reaching reported zero PM after airtightening the house and installing positive-pressure ventilation. Because both the envelope and ventilation changed, the account does not isolate the contribution of pressure alone.
- sbirch [[./thread#^c36235095|sbirch]] reports very low PM2.5 with several standalone purifiers during bad wildfire events, checked using two air-quality monitors, in a modern, well-sealed house. They stress sizing for room volume and drafts. The supplied exchange does not identify those monitors or purifier models; thebigman433 [[./thread#^c36235524|thebigman433]] asks about the monitors without a supplied answer.
- The wildfire accounts conflict substantially. jdavis703 [[./thread#^c36235510|jdavis703]] reports that three purifiers in a closed master bedroom reduced outdoor purple conditions only to indoor orange during West Coast fires. crazygringo [[./thread#^c36236060|crazygringo]] reports a single approximately \$100 Levoit keeping a NYC bedroom in the green, measured by an Awair, while outdoor AQI exceeded 400. These are different settings, not a direct equipment comparison.
- atombeef [[./thread#^c36237912|atombeef]], also in Thailand, reports Smartair purifiers keeping PM2.5 near zero even during severe annual pollution, while a tightly sealed house without ventilation develops high CO2; no CO2 concentration is given. erkkie [[./thread#^c36260839|erkkie]] describes using Smartair and IKEA Starkwind and wanting integrated ventilation, cooling, and dehumidification, but supplies no measured VOC-removal result.
- usbinsider [[./thread#^c36237302|usbinsider]] reports a new HVAC setup with MERV 13 plus MERV 16 filtration reaching PM2.5 zero at roughly two air changes per hour and quickly removing deliberately introduced bad air. They explicitly qualify that the upgraded system has not yet faced comparably strong wildfire smoke.
- bb88 [[./thread#^c36236809|bb88]] reports an office reaching PM2.5 zero after a couple of hours with a 20-inch box fan and bungee-attached MERV 13 filter. nerdbaggy [[./thread#^c36235417|nerdbaggy]] separately reports zero PM2.5 using an Alen 75i. Neither comment specifies the measuring instrument or provides a time series.
- bmitc [[./thread#^c36235921|bmitc]] explicitly lacks independent air-quality monitoring for their Vornado purifiers. Their positive impression includes seeing extinguished-candle smoke travel toward the unit. That is an observation of airflow and visible smoke capture, not a quantified PM2.5 measurement or demonstration of complete removal.
- chuckledog [[./thread#^c36237270|chuckledog]] reports about a year of operation from three DIY supply systems using salvaged Winix filter housings, low-speed duct fans, and ducts delivering air into bedrooms. They describe the filters as HEPA-ish, state a \$50/year intake-filter cost, and specify mild-climate California. They provide no PM, CO2, pressure, or energy measurements and describe readiness for the next severe smoke episode.
- ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36237299|ahaucnx]], answering \_Nat\_'s localized-supply question [[./thread#^c36235920|\_Nat\_]], says filtered fresh air enters peripheral rooms, mainly bedrooms, and then pressurizes the rest of the home. turtlebits [[./thread#^c36236802|turtlebits]] reports a different combined arrangement: positive-pressure ERV operation plus continuous low-speed purification. Neither account reports sleep outcomes.
- bredren [[./thread#^c36238411|bredren]] has built three DIY filtration variants and finds them cheap and effective, while reporting practical downsides involving bulk, appearance, portability, and noise. They use Coway units in common living spaces. Their comment supports usability comparisons but supplies no quantitative head-to-head filtration results.
- domoritz [[./thread#^c36241583|domoritz]] lives near industrial pollution, including coal-related sources, and reports problems they associate with an unidentified chemical. Their concern is that a continuous fresh-air supply could increase exposure to gases despite improving particles and CO2. The account gives neither a chemical identification nor a specific symptom or concentration.
- regularfry [[./thread#^c36238766|regularfry]] describes dealing with serious basement mold and being offered progressively more elaborate treatments: sealing plaster, a stand-off lining, and airtightening plus positive pressure. Each successive option reportedly added a zero to the price. The comment does not identify the selected treatment or demonstrate its eventual mold outcome.
- kshahkshah [[./thread#^c36235015|kshahkshah]] reports that bathroom fans in a moderately leaky 1985 house create negative pressure and draw air from beneath the slab, raising a radon concern without a stated radon measurement. In the reply chain, pivo [[./thread#^c36235167|pivo]] recalls opening windows to tolerate overpowering radiators and enjoying the warm/cold air mixture, while explicitly questioning how clean that outdoor air was.
- geerlingguy [[./thread#^c36237075|geerlingguy]] reports owning four AirGradient monitors, receiving two for evaluation and buying additional units, and reflashing them with ESPHome for Home Assistant integration. This is firsthand evidence about adoption and integration, with the evaluation relationship disclosed; it is not an accuracy or calibration study.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Why some purifiers achieve near-zero readings while others leave substantially polluted indoor air remains unresolved. crazygringo [[./thread#^c36236060|crazygringo]] suggests maintenance or airflow problems in jdavis703's account [[./thread#^c36235510|jdavis703]], but supplies no diagnosis of that room. \_Nat\_ [[./thread#^c36235782|\_Nat\_]] proposes a controlled airflow sweep using a 280 m³/h purifier and initially 220 m³/h supply; no completed sweep is reported.
- The source unit does not establish effects on cognition, sleep, or clinical health. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36235129|ahaucnx]] asserts health impacts from high CO2 without presenting outcome data. andai [[./thread#^c36238775|andai]] asks whether filtered indoor air could be healthier than outdoor air; dsab [[./thread#^c36238868|dsab]] responds with a claimed Kraków pollution/life-expectancy correlation, which does not isolate personal exposure or establish causation.
- NO2 specifically is not measured, and the broader gas-exposure question remains open. domoritz [[./thread#^c36241583|domoritz]] asks about VOCs, NOx, and sulfur pollution entering from outside. stephen\_g [[./thread#^c36240401|stephen\_g]] asks whether the monitors measure NOx/SOx and whether charcoal would help; the question itself does not establish sensor capability. LTL\_FTC's charcoal recommendation [[./thread#^c36236774|LTL\_FTC]] supplies no gas-specific performance or saturation measurements.
- The net benefit of opening windows is not quantified for cooking or traffic exposure. kshahkshah [[./thread#^c36235015|kshahkshah]] discusses recirculating range hoods, while valenterry [[./thread#^c36240336|valenterry]] discusses cooking-related humidity and polluted replacement air. Neither supplies cooking PM2.5 or NO2 measurements. 2rsf's mention of polluted Stockholm streets [[./thread#^c36242032|2rsf]] also supplies no traffic-specific readings.
- The joint PM2.5–CO2–energy optimum remains unspecified. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c36235899|ahaucnx]] reports successful automatic adjustment but gives no numerical CO2 history, measured pressure, or energy savings. hedora's 600–800 ppm CO2 and under-10 µg/m³ PM2.5 targets [[./thread#^c36236777|hedora]] are proposed setpoints. It therefore remains unclear how little filtered outdoor airflow would maintain both targets in particular buildings.
- Sensor reliability and affordable pressure measurement remain open. bb88 [[./thread#^c36237137|bb88]] suggests common Plantower modules and possible calibration, and LTL\_FTC [[./thread#^c36236849|LTL\_FTC]] recommends monitor brands without validation data. c\_o\_n\_v\_e\_x [[./thread#^c36237604|c\_o\_n\_v\_e\_x]] recommends differential-pressure sensing but knows no DIY-priced option; rocqua's proposal to subtract two calibrated pressure sensors [[./thread#^c36238457|rocqua]] is untested in the supplied discussion.
- Heat-recovery performance is not demonstrated for the proposed DIY systems. rapjr9 [[./thread#^c36237133|rapjr9]] proposes paired CPU coolers; rocqua [[./thread#^c36238390|rocqua]] questions their suitability at small temperature differences. rapjr9's estimated 50\% winter and 25\% summer recovery [[./thread#^c36239363|rapjr9]] remains speculation, and the later heatsink-design discussion [[./thread#^c36274891|rapjr9]] still contains uncertainty rather than measurements.
- Mold control remains dependent on unresolved envelope and climate details. Cocktail [[./thread#^c36238351|Cocktail]] asks about actual mold after the Thai house was sealed, without a supplied firsthand answer from its owner. valenterry [[./thread#^c36239379|valenterry]] predicts little problem in Thailand, while Bluestrike2 [[./thread#^c36240969|Bluestrike2]] warns that humid infiltration can support mold and 2rsf [[./thread#^c36242032|2rsf]] raises moisture driven into walls by positive pressure. No humidity or wall-moisture data adjudicate these accounts.
- Radon concerns are not supported by measurements here. kshahkshah [[./thread#^c36235015|kshahkshah]] identifies a possible under-slab pathway; stephen\_g [[./thread#^c36239708|stephen\_g]] proposes pressure, intake placement, and charcoal-related measures. 29athrowaway [[./thread#^c36237569|29athrowaway]] makes a claim about radon released when trees burn, and ricardobayes [[./thread#^c36238915|ricardobayes]] makes broad regional prevalence claims. The supplied material verifies neither those claims nor mitigation effectiveness.
- DIY fan-filter fire safety is disputed using unevaluated external evidence. hollerith [[./thread#^c36237755|hollerith]] cites an overheating-fire report and later provides its link [[./thread#^c36242845|hollerith]], rather than describing a personal incident. bredren [[./thread#^c36238380|bredren]] cites a study as finding no added hazard, while btbuildem [[./thread#^c36241191|btbuildem]] argues a four-filter design addresses airflow concerns. The export contains neither underlying investigation nor study methods, so it does not settle safety across fan models and constructions.
