# [Leaving Pittsburgh because of industrial air pollution](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=21997412)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion responds to a personal account of leaving Pittsburgh over industrial air pollution, with residents comparing neighborhood-level experiences, smells, health concerns, and air-quality data. Participants debate the role of Clairton Coke Works and regional emissions, the limits of AQI and consumer monitors, water infrastructure concerns, and whether leaving, reporting, or pursuing government action is the appropriate response.
Tags: Pittsburgh, industrial pollution, air quality, public health, environmental monitoring, local government

## Relevance

Contains conflicting firsthand accounts of industrial air pollution in Pittsburgh, including odors, respiratory symptoms, and neighborhood variation, alongside AQI comparisons and discussion of pollutants that aggregate indices may miss. It illustrates why smell, broad monitoring data, and local exposure measurements can tell different stories. ## Arguments

- Exposure may differ substantially within one city. eyegor [[./thread#^c21998152|eyegor]] describes Pittsburgh pollution as highly neighborhood-dependent; gethoht [[./thread#^c21998378|gethoht]] proposes that hills concentrate pollutants unevenly. These accounts support investigating particular locations and times rather than assigning every resident the same exposure.
- Absence of odor does not establish clean air. tempsy [[./thread#^c21997703|tempsy]] explicitly challenges that inference, while gkop [[./thread#^c21998747|gkop]] reports winter bronchitis without noticing the disputed smell. The latter is an anecdote illustrating the distinction, not proof that pollution caused the illness.
- AQI may not answer every odor complaint. darksaints [[./thread#^c21997595|darksaints]] says it includes sulfur dioxide and nitrogen dioxide but excludes hydrogen sulfide, and argues that geographically aggregated readings can miss near-source peaks. Their identification of hydrogen sulfide as the likely odor is conjecture, not a measurement.
- The complaint reportedly had corroboration beyond one person's nose. Responding to dismissal of the author's experience, johndubchak [[./thread#^c21997886|johndubchak]] says the article described county-official confirmation of industrial pollution and other residents' app reports. This is a commenter's account of supporting evidence; the underlying observations and measurements are not reproduced.
- Residents reportedly used monitoring and indoor filtration to manage outdoor pollution. Game\_Ender [[./thread#^c21997589|Game\_Ender]] describes acquaintances buying a Purple Air monitor and indoor purifiers and staying inside on particularly bad days. This is secondhand evidence of a practical response, with no reported concentration reductions or health outcomes.

## Counterarguments

- Several residents dispute the apparent ubiquity of the problem. mtbomb [[./thread#^c21997694|mtbomb]] reports neither personal odor problems nor coworker complaints and suggests another neighborhood might suffice. Their reply [[./thread#^c21997730|mtbomb]] treats this as a counterexample to the smell account, but observations at different places and times cannot directly disprove another resident's experience.
- Stronger odor does not necessarily mean greater health risk than in another city. ChikkaChiChi [[./thread#^c22002945|ChikkaChiChi]] emphasizes odorless contaminants when challenging Pittsburgh comparisons. This limits what can be concluded from fiblye's [[./thread#^c21997597|fiblye]] observation that clear Pittsburgh days could smell worse than visibly smoggy Beijing.
- The numerical comparisons do not uniformly support a simple city ranking. et2o [[./thread#^c21997553|et2o]] reports a relatively favorable Pittsburgh AQI snapshot; Game\_Ender [[./thread#^c21997589|Game\_Ender]] cites an unfavorable ranking that nevertheless places Pittsburgh ahead of San Francisco. Neither supplies enough underlying data to reconcile the comparisons or establish an individual's exposure.
- shas3 [[./thread#^c21998039|shas3]] challenges pollution-related cognitive claims by pointing to successful universities in polluted cities. acollins1331 [[./thread#^c21998140|acollins1331]] responds that partial cognitive harm could coexist with exceptional ability and that institutions locate where people live. Neither supplies a cognitive measurement or study result.
- Air concerns coexist with positive assessments of the city. cfallin [[./thread#^c21998058|cfallin]] personally preferred Pittsburgh's affordability, walkability, and parks despite occasional sulfur odors. Conversely, duxup [[./thread#^c21997951|duxup]], answering kccqzy's [[./thread#^c21997580|kccqzy]] question about livability rankings, argues that composite rankings can omit relevant factors. Neither pleasant amenities nor a favorable ranking establishes clean air.

## Assumptions

- Several source identifications rest on interpretation of smells. joeblau [[./thread#^c21997681|joeblau]] initially attributed pungent mornings near Google to the adjacent train; cfallin [[./thread#^c21998058|cfallin]] understood the problem as Clairton emissions interacting with weather; darksaints [[./thread#^c21997595|darksaints]] guessed hydrogen sulfide from a refinery comparison. These are not chemical identifications.
- Attributing symptoms to air pollution assumes a causal connection that the accounts cannot isolate. gkop [[./thread#^c21998747|gkop]] associates different residences with changes in bronchitis, and PascLeRasc [[./thread#^c21998927|PascLeRasc]] associates travel with easier breathing. Neither reports exposure measurements or controls for other changes.
- Informal respiratory-health observations are not prevalence estimates. eyegor's [[./thread#^c21998152|eyegor]] suggestion that one of four locals will have asthma supplies no sampling method; PascLeRasc's [[./thread#^c21998927|PascLeRasc]] account of friends diagnosed with adult asthma is secondhand and does not establish why they developed it.
- The household mitigation strategy assumes that remaining indoors and using the selected purifier reduces the relevant exposure. Game\_Ender [[./thread#^c21997589|Game\_Ender]] provides no indoor/outdoor comparison or pollutant-specific performance. mesozoic's [[./thread#^c21997870|mesozoic]] suggestion that Google install large scrubbers likewise supplies no implementation or effectiveness evidence.
- The argument that leaving is the only realistic protection assumes both an accessible healthier destination and little prospect of timely local improvement. lotsofpulp [[./thread#^c21998024|lotsofpulp]] makes the short-term-remedy assumption and later explicitly conditions relocation on income security and the ability to move [[./thread#^c22002810|lotsofpulp]].

## Conditions

- Neighborhood, terrain, and weather matter to the reported experience. zorkus [[./thread#^c22001805|zorkus]] describes strong, intermittent early-morning odors in the East End and their absence after moving south of Pittsburgh, attributing the difference to river-valley weather. gethoht [[./thread#^c21998378|gethoht]] offers topography as an explanation, without measurements testing it.
- Wildfire conditions can reverse a favorable regional assessment. thundergolfer [[./thread#^c21997569|thundergolfer]], living in New South Wales during the fires, reports excellent or terrible air depending on wind. gkop [[./thread#^c21998747|gkop]] praises Bay Area air specifically when fires are not burning. Neither provides particulate concentrations.
- Seasonal pollutants complicate relocation comparisons. jakebol [[./thread#^c21997652|jakebol]] describes the Salt Lake area as having winter particulate problems and summer ozone or smoke. This is a qualitative local assessment, not a PM2.5 time series or an account of indoor exposure.
- Affordability and amenities affect willingness to tolerate perceived exposure. cfallin [[./thread#^c21998058|cfallin]] weighs occasional odor against lower rent stress, walkability, and access to parks; exogeny [[./thread#^c21997620|exogeny]] remembers Pittsburgh as affordable, safe, and culturally rich. These are personal quality-of-life judgments rather than health-risk calculations.
- Relocation options differ financially and occupationally. mrsaint [[./thread#^c21999616|mrsaint]] highlights the author's ability to move without finding another job, while munk-a [[./thread#^c21997578|munk-a]] contrasts that mobility with residents who are stuck. PascLeRasc [[./thread#^c21998927|PascLeRasc]] reports trying unsuccessfully for two years to find work elsewhere.
- Income alone does not capture relocation costs. rytill [[./thread#^c22002380|rytill]] points to family, property, cultural ties, and local political involvement as reasons to improve or accept conditions rather than leave. lotsofpulp [[./thread#^c22002810|lotsofpulp]] acknowledges that family-related tradeoffs may limit the applicability of their health-first relocation preference.

## Lived experiences

- et2o [[./thread#^c21997553|et2o]] reports looking up AQI values of 34 for Pittsburgh, 32 for Palo Alto, and 50 for the New York region, explicitly calling the comparison unscientific. These are retrieved index values, not personal sensor measurements or PM2.5, NO2, or CO2 concentrations.
- fiblye [[./thread#^c21997597|fiblye]] reports waking with a metallic taste after leaving windows open and frequently smelling burnt tires or coins outdoors, including in parks before morning traffic. They also describe Pittsburgh as smelling worse than Beijing despite clearer skies. This directly concerns discomfort and possible outdoor-air ingress, but identifies neither pollutant nor dose.
- m0llusk [[./thread#^c21998457|m0llusk]] recalls unbearable nighttime air in Squirrel Hill during 1986–1991, including summers, and attributes it to coke plants. cfallin [[./thread#^c21998058|cfallin]], recalling 2009–2013 and 2015–2019 in that neighborhood, describes the smell as intermittent and not especially strong. Their accounts concern different periods and cannot establish a trend.
- gkop [[./thread#^c21998747|gkop]] reports severe winter bronchitis while living in Central and South Oakland during 2004–2008, improvement after moving to East Liberty during 2008–2010, and no notable odor in either location. They recall still worse bronchitis in inner western London. These are firsthand symptom comparisons without measured exposures.
- PascLeRasc [[./thread#^c21998927|PascLeRasc]], a lifelong Pittsburgh resident, reports noticeably easier breathing by the second day of a Seattle visit and explicitly acknowledges having no data. Reports about friends developing adult asthma are secondhand. In a later reply, PascLeRasc [[./thread#^c21999059|PascLeRasc]] also describes a Pittsburgh-specific metallic outdoor taste or smell.
- joeblau [[./thread#^c21997681|joeblau]] recalls extremely pungent mornings while living less than half a mile from Google. zorkus [[./thread#^c22001805|zorkus]] independently describes intermittent strong odors during approximately nine years in the East End, usually beginning very early in the morning, followed by a different experience south of the city.
- Contrasting firsthand accounts include ApolloFortyNine's [[./thread#^c21998413|ApolloFortyNine]] fifteen years without noticing unusual conditions and mtm7's [[./thread#^c21998093|mtm7]] ten years across four neighborhoods, including time at Bakery Square, without the reported problems. mysterydip [[./thread#^c21997571|mysterydip]] similarly noticed no such smell during visits, while acknowledging intermittent exposure might explain that absence. These are sensory accounts, not negative pollutant tests.
- danielecook [[./thread#^c21997581|danielecook]] reports multiple petroleum-like smells while cycling in London and being able to taste emissions around idling vehicles; the chemical identities were guesses. Symbiote [[./thread#^c22005157|Symbiote]] reports choosing backstreets for about 95\% of cycling to avoid pollution and perceiving improvement after more electric and hybrid vehicles appeared. No route measurements or quantified before-and-after comparison are supplied.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The actual pollutant burden remains unquantified in this export. et2o [[./thread#^c21997553|et2o]] supplies AQI snapshots, and darksaints [[./thread#^c21997595|darksaints]] discusses NO2 and other gases, but no commenter supplies PM2.5, NO2, or CO2 concentrations, personal exposure monitoring, or cooking-related measurements. The discussion therefore cannot answer the approved question equally well for all pollutants and indoor sources.
- Consumer-sensor reliability is disputed without validation evidence. vzaliva [[./thread#^c21998325|vzaliva]] argues that buying an air-quality meter would make the complaint scientific; hatmatrix repeats the objection that such devices are insufficiently accurate [[./thread#^c21998401|hatmatrix]] [[./thread#^c21998385|hatmatrix]]. No model-specific testing, calibration comparison, or error estimate is provided. Game\_Ender's [[./thread#^c21997589|Game\_Ender]] mention of Purple Air use does not resolve that dispute.
- The relative contributions of local industry and transported pollution remain unresolved. rwem [[./thread#^c21997615|rwem]] initially attributes the problem to pollution arriving from the west, then acknowledges relying on reading about Ohio Valley sources rather than expertise [[./thread#^c21998155|rwem]]. dgacmu [[./thread#^c22000789|dgacmu]] instead identifies Clairton as the main source. harshaw's [[./thread#^c21997769|harshaw]] suggestion of newly operating cracker plants also receives a specific correction: mysterydip [[./thread#^c21997804|mysterydip]] says the large Beaver County plant was still under construction.
- Opening windows has one directly reported adverse experience: fiblye [[./thread#^c21997597|fiblye]] associates it with waking metallic taste. There is no supplied case measuring a ventilation benefit, including CO2 reduction, or comparing open and closed windows across outdoor conditions. Game\_Ender's [[./thread#^c21997589|Game\_Ender]] indoor-purifier account also lacks measurements, leaving the balance between ventilation, outdoor pollution ingress, and filtration unresolved.
- Health causality, cognitive effects, and sleep effects remain uncertain. gkop [[./thread#^c21998747|gkop]] supplies symptom changes rather than causal evidence; the exchange between shas3 [[./thread#^c21998039|shas3]] and acollins1331 [[./thread#^c21998140|acollins1331]] supplies competing reasoning rather than study results. fiblye's [[./thread#^c21997597|fiblye]] waking metallic taste and m0llusk's [[./thread#^c21998457|m0llusk]] nighttime discomfort do not establish impaired sleep quality.
- Participants disagree about leaving versus pursuing local improvement. munk-a [[./thread#^c21997578|munk-a]] criticizes discouraging newcomers without sufficient advocacy; bobthepanda [[./thread#^c21997704|bobthepanda]] responds by quoting the author's promotion of SmellPGH and environmental complaints, which gethoht [[./thread#^c21998607|gethoht]] dismisses as barely trying. lotsofpulp [[./thread#^c21998024|lotsofpulp]] prioritizes leaving a perceived hazard, while rytill [[./thread#^c22002380|rytill]] rejects a universal answer detached from people's circumstances. No evidence here establishes which response would deliver the greatest practical exposure reduction.
