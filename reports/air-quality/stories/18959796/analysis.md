# [On whether changes in bedroom CO2 levels affect sleep quality](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=18959796)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion responds to an unfinished self-experiment about whether bedroom CO2 levels affect sleep, cognition, and daytime alertness. Participants share experiences with stuffy rooms and ventilation, question the experiment’s controls and conclusions, and debate whether elevated CO2 effects can be separated from oxygen levels, temperature, sleep disorders, and other factors. The thread also covers CO2 sensor accuracy and calibration, indoor ventilation options such as open windows and heat-recovery systems, plants as air improvers, and the implications of rising outdoor CO2.
Tags: CO2, sleep, indoor-air-quality, ventilation, cognition, self-experiment, research-methods, CO2-sensors, building-science, climate-change

## Relevance

The discussion directly examines bedroom and workplace CO2 readings, reported effects on alertness, sleep, and comfort, ventilation options, and sensor accuracy. It preserves uncertainty: personal reports and claims about cognition are challenged, and commenters dispute whether CO2 effects can be separated from oxygen levels or other factors. ## Arguments

- The central practical argument is that occupied rooms can accumulate CO2 quickly enough to justify monitoring ventilation. DocG [[./thread#^c18962727|DocG]] describes measurements in offices, classrooms, and a closed bedroom, alongside perceived fatigue and dizziness. JauntyHatAngle [[./thread#^c18964092|JauntyHatAngle]] adds a secondhand account of a professor demonstrating a rise from a recalled below-400 ppm starting point to thousands of ppm during a crowded lecture; that account is neither firsthand nor precisely documented.
- Some commenters invoke research supporting cognitive effects at ordinary indoor concentrations. Shank [[./thread#^c18962437|Shank]] says studies suggest effects around 1000 ppm, and nwah1 [[./thread#^c18971998|nwah1]] links the same paper as evidence of impaired test performance. simulate [[./thread#^c18963451|simulate]] describes an external literature review and another person's improvements in headaches, fatigue, and sleep. These are reports about external evidence; the supplied comments do not establish its methods, effect sizes, or applicability to bedrooms.
- Opening a door or window is the most repeatedly reported intervention that lowered measured CO2. simulate [[./thread#^c18963451|simulate]], graeme [[./thread#^c18964119|graeme]], and maxk42 [[./thread#^c18975009|maxk42]] give before-and-after readings. gnat [[./thread#^c18963225|gnat]] also reports improved alertness after opening an outside door. Together, these accounts support trying and measuring ventilation changes in the particular room, while leaving the cause of subjective improvements uncertain.
- Several replies argue that airtight construction and fresh air are compatible when ventilation is deliberately provided. dangravell [[./thread#^c18967267|dangravell]] distinguishes controlled ventilation from leakage, criticizing leakage as pressure-dependent, potentially damaging through condensation, and wasteful. throw0101a [[./thread#^c18971122|throw0101a]] adds that controlled ventilation can recover heat and filter incoming air. nkurz [[./thread#^c18965703|nkurz]] distinguishes HRVs, which exchange heat, from ERVs, which also exchange moisture, with suitability depending on house and climate.
- Air circulation can solve a different problem from outdoor-air replacement. Kadin [[./thread#^c18966115|Kadin]] explains that many residential forced-air systems recirculate indoor air and that return-air paths matter when bedroom doors close. Klathmon [[./thread#^c18962891|Klathmon]] reports that scheduled circulation equalized bedroom and central-house temperatures. These replies support checking how an existing system actually moves air before assuming its fan provides fresh outdoor air.
- Filtration is proposed for exposures that ventilation alone may worsen. millettjon [[./thread#^c18964769|millettjon]] recommends blower filters in cities; twothamendment [[./thread#^c18965861|twothamendment]] extends the concern to seasonal forest smoke. clairity [[./thread#^c18969987|clairity]] reports fewer allergy/asthma symptoms after adding an air purifier. graeme [[./thread#^c18963434|graeme]] explicitly distinguishes cleaning air from lowering CO2: the discussion does not establish that an ordinary purifier replaces ventilation.
- Moisture control and ventilation need to be designed together. throw0101a [[./thread#^c18968272|throw0101a]] emphasizes humidity and condensation control; chrismeller [[./thread#^c18968366|chrismeller]] objects that a small, closed shower room still needs ventilation, which throw0101a [[./thread#^c18970188|throw0101a]] then acknowledges. dangravell [[./thread#^c18968439|dangravell]] further distinguishes water-vapor permeability from air leakage, making material choice and installation relevant to successful sealing.
- Several contributors argue that useful feedback need not require laboratory-grade precision. DocG [[./thread#^c18963688|DocG]], 05 [[./thread#^c18963585|05]], and benjaminjackman [[./thread#^c18963311|benjaminjackman]] suggest that inexpensive sensors or relative changes can reveal when ventilation needs attention. marijnz [[./thread#^c18964097|marijnz]] describes a former employer's CO2 dashboards and alerts, showing that continuous monitoring was actually deployed, although no health or productivity evaluation is supplied.
- Whole-house ducting is not presented as the only ventilation option. aresant [[./thread#^c18965770|aresant]] mentions both whole-house and single-room exchangers, while kilotaras [[./thread#^c18966484|kilotaras]] describes through-wall units for older renovated buildings. general8bitso [[./thread#^c18989210|general8bitso]] mentions a window-style design with optional HEPA filtration and a small Panasonic ERV. These are suggested configurations, without measured performance results in the supplied discussion.

## Counterarguments

- The perceived benefits of opening windows are not specific evidence of a CO2 effect. Replying to gnat's rapid improvement, John\_KZ [[./thread#^c18964269|John\_KZ]] identifies colder air as an uncontrolled alternative explanation; jodrellblank [[./thread#^c18965589|jodrellblank]] challenges the suggestion that improvement could not occur quickly. Symmetry [[./thread#^c18963975|Symmetry]] separately suggests that cooler temperatures help sleep. The exchange does not resolve which change produced the reported benefit.
- Fatigue, headaches, poor concentration, and waking breathless do not identify an environmental CO2 cause. toufiqbarhamov [[./thread#^c18963428|toufiqbarhamov]] raises sleep apnea as an alternative explanation for persistent sleepiness, snoring, and morning headaches. aicioara [[./thread#^c18967132|aicioara]] reports that apparently similar symptoms around a partner were associated with an allergy to cosmetics. Neither account establishes the explanation for other commenters' symptoms.
- The oxygen-depletion explanation is explicitly contested. greenleaf2 [[./thread#^c18963220|greenleaf2]] and swixmix [[./thread#^c18966214|swixmix]] argue that falling O2 could explain effects attributed to rising CO2. hannasanarion [[./thread#^c18963667|hannasanarion]] counters that the CO2 concentrations under discussion imply a very small change relative to the much larger oxygen concentration. These are competing physiological arguments, without paired bedroom O2 measurements.
- The oxygen debate contains corrections that should not be flattened into agreement. tasty\_freeze [[./thread#^c18966740|tasty\_freeze]] corrects Symmetry's [[./thread#^c18964063|Symmetry]] oxygen concentration from 21000 to 210000 ppm. wnoise [[./thread#^c18972997|wnoise]] retracts an earlier claim of exact molar matching between oxygen consumption and CO2 production, while rejecting greenleaf2's [[./thread#^c18964278|greenleaf2]] explanation that most produced CO2 remains in the body. Neither correction supplies evidence that ordinary occupied bedrooms become hypoxic.
- Strong claims of bodily damage are not substantiated at the bedroom concentrations discussed. JauntyHatAngle [[./thread#^c18966268|JauntyHatAngle]] characterizes CO2-induced drowsiness as impaired function and assumes poorer sleep; fao\_ [[./thread#^c18968425|fao\_]] invokes hypoxia. krageon [[./thread#^c18966690|krageon]] asks for direct sources and later distinguishes low oxygen from high CO2 [[./thread#^c18987196|krageon]]. fao\_'s subsequent hypercapnia reference [[./thread#^c18988205|fao\_]] does not settle the exposure-specific question in the supplied text.
- Newer or tighter houses are not shown to be inherently worse. dangravell [[./thread#^c18967267|dangravell]] argues for airtightness plus engineered ventilation, and rb666 [[./thread#^c18965267|rb666]] describes modern homes locally using heat-exchanging central ventilation. gregmac [[./thread#^c18966757|gregmac]] reports Canadian examples, including a house built around 1985, and clarifies that the recent-house claim concerns construction dates [[./thread#^c18968075|gregmac]]. Conversely, clairity [[./thread#^c18962367|clairity]] still measured substantial nighttime accumulation in a drafty prewar building.
- The claim that older heating systems supplied 100\% outside air is directly challenged. userbinator [[./thread#^c18965671|userbinator]] attributes newer problems to replacing that arrangement with recirculation. Kadin [[./thread#^c18966163|Kadin]] recalls a grandparents' coal furnace that recirculated indoor air while exhausting combustion air through a chimney; leakage supplied some replacement air. The distinction is between incidental and deliberate air exchange, not a simple historical switch from all-fresh to all-recirculated air.
- The initial assertion that cheap CO2 devices are almost all VOC proxies does not survive unchanged. bizzleDawg [[./thread#^c18962244|bizzleDawg]] initially makes that argument, then identifies the discussed Netatmo device as infrared-based and shifts the criticism to calibration [[./thread#^c18962282|bizzleDawg]]. dschuler [[./thread#^c18962278|dschuler]] reports buying a \$20 NDIR sensor, gsich [[./thread#^c18966474|gsich]] describes approximately €25 optical modules, and bizzleDawg [[./thread#^c18963309|bizzleDawg]] acknowledges that the market has moved on. Low price alone therefore does not establish proxy sensing or inaccuracy.
- A few houseplants are challenged as an adequate CO2 intervention. graeme [[./thread#^c18963022|graeme]] argues that slow plant growth cannot keep pace with human emissions, while thisisweirdok [[./thread#^c18963035|thisisweirdok]] suggests hundreds would be needed for complete compensation. naner [[./thread#^c18965868|naner]] doubts even the larger illuminated growing setup would produce a noticeable difference. These objections coexist with proposed benefits for comfort or other pollutants; they do not establish a single reliable plant requirement.
- Neither overnight accumulation nor obvious dizziness is universal in these accounts. z3t4 [[./thread#^c18963164|z3t4]] measured a bedroom peak of about 1000 ppm before sleep followed by a decline during sleep. Tharkun [[./thread#^c18967423|Tharkun]] describes poorly ventilated offices and a worse bedroom without having experienced office dizziness. These counterexamples limit extrapolation from the more dramatic accounts, but do not establish safe concentrations or absence of subtler effects.
- Several commenters challenge the evidentiary strength of the discussion itself. fromthestart [[./thread#^c18964286|fromthestart]] emphasizes subjective outcomes and plausible but unverified explanations. diminoten [[./thread#^c18963061|diminoten]] criticizes confounding and later argues that inadequate environmental description prevents reproduction [[./thread#^c18970804|diminoten]]. Their objections concern inference and methodology; they are not experimental demonstrations that ventilation or CO2 has no effect.

## Assumptions

- Using a displayed CO2 value assumes the device measures CO2 rather than estimating it from other gases. bizzleDawg [[./thread#^c18962244|bizzleDawg]] describes VOC-derived estimates that assume human emissions rather than sources such as paint. However, clairity's [[./thread#^c18962367|clairity]] correlated CO2 and TVOC readings do not by themselves establish that one channel is derived from the other; shared occupancy and ventilation changes could also produce correlation.
- Outdoor calibration assumes an appropriate reference concentration. 05 [[./thread#^c18963585|05]] proposes approximately 400 ppm outdoors, but graeme [[./thread#^c18964119|graeme]] reports outdoor city readings of 450 ppm or more, and Robotbeat [[./thread#^c18963466|Robotbeat]] cites approximately 413 ppm ambient at the time. wcoenen [[./thread#^c18970129|wcoenen]] questions the recalled below-400 ppm lecture starting point on historical grounds. These comments do not justify treating every outdoor location as an exact calibration standard.
- A room reading is assumed to represent the sleeper's exposure. Someone [[./thread#^c18962722|Someone]] proposes measuring near breathing height and speculates about higher CO2 near the floor; martinpw [[./thread#^c18963286|martinpw]] asks whether the gradient could make bed height consequential. No supplied measurements establish that gradient or show how strongly placement affected the reported bedroom readings.
- Attributing a before-and-after improvement specifically to CO2 assumes other relevant changes are controlled. John\_KZ [[./thread#^c18964269|John\_KZ]] identifies temperature; clairity [[./thread#^c18962367|clairity]] observes concurrent VOC variation; tvladeck [[./thread#^c18963413|tvladeck]] changes ventilation, time outdoors, and working location together. Those interventions can be useful personally without isolating one causal exposure.
- A fan reducing a bedroom concentration assumes it can mix that room with lower-concentration air; sustained removal from the dwelling additionally depends on air exchange. Kadin [[./thread#^c18966115|Kadin]] describes systems that mostly recirculate, whereas tylerl [[./thread#^c18964695|tylerl]] describes an actual outside intake and timed exhaust. jere's [[./thread#^c18962160|jere]] fan experience therefore cannot be generalized to every HVAC system or interpreted automatically as whole-house CO2 removal.
- The discussion sometimes treats 'stale' or 'stuffy' air as synonymous with high CO2. hawski [[./thread#^c18967614|hawski]] explicitly makes that interpretation, but hnick [[./thread#^c18966396|hnick]] describes simultaneous warmth, equipment, and difficulty focusing, and clairity [[./thread#^c18962367|clairity]] measures both CO2 and VOC changes. The sensory description does not identify a single pollutant.
- Botanical CO2 removal proposals depend on growth, illumination, plant type, and timing. danieltillett [[./thread#^c18964803|danieltillett]] distinguishes ordinary nighttime respiration from CAM plants' nighttime uptake. nkingsy [[./thread#^c18963429|nkingsy]] deliberately runs growing lights at night, so m-i-l's [[./thread#^c18967977|m-i-l]] account of plants respiring overnight without photosynthesis cannot simply be transferred to that illuminated setup. None of these comments measures its net respiratory-gas balance.
- Reducing furnace use is sometimes assumed to reduce occupants' CO2 exposure. nkingsy [[./thread#^c18965188|nkingsy]] gives that as a reason for the growing-light arrangement, but supplies no combustion-exhaust or bedroom CO2 measurements. Kadin [[./thread#^c18966163|Kadin]] distinguishes heating-air circulation from combustion intake and exhaust. paradoxparalax's [[./thread#^c18966523|paradoxparalax]] separate warning concerns CO, not CO2, and does not establish a CO problem in any reported home.
- The argument about rising outdoor CO2 assumes it raises the indoor baseline or reduces dilution toward a fixed target under otherwise comparable conditions. graeme [[./thread#^c18964977|graeme]] illustrates a smaller indoor–outdoor concentration difference if outdoor air rises from 400 to 500 ppm, and Retric [[./thread#^c18962638|Retric]] emphasizes that indoor accumulation already matters. This personal-exposure argument does not require accepting the thread's disputed climate forecasts.

## Conditions

- Cold weather creates an immediate comfort and heating-cost constraint. DocG [[./thread#^c18962727|DocG]] says open vents or windows are uncommon for half the year locally; tux1968 [[./thread#^c18964150|tux1968]] objects to opening windows at −20 degrees. graeme [[./thread#^c18964461|graeme]] favors a tiny opening even in similarly cold weather, while KozmoNau7 [[./thread#^c18973118|KozmoNau7]] suggests brief, wide-open airing to limit cooling of walls and furniture. No comparative heat-loss measurements settle those approaches.
- Outdoor pollution can make the apparent fresh-air solution unacceptable, and relocation may be unaffordable. pojzon [[./thread#^c18966312|pojzon]] describes opening windows as exchanging stale indoor air for pollution. After paradoxparalax [[./thread#^c18966494|paradoxparalax]] assumes Beijing and advises moving, pojzon [[./thread#^c18977092|pojzon]] specifies Poland and insufficient money to relocate. The location correction is essential; this is an exposure concern, not a reported cancer or asthma diagnosis.
- Outdoor conditions are not simply 'city bad, countryside clean.' millettjon [[./thread#^c18964769|millettjon]] emphasizes urban filtration, while twothamendment [[./thread#^c18965861|twothamendment]] describes forest air turning smoky each August. jodrellblank [[./thread#^c18965617|jodrellblank]] asks about buildings beside car parks; kijin [[./thread#^c18965841|kijin]] distinguishes parked, switched-off cars from active highway traffic. These are contextual concerns and hypotheses, without paired indoor/outdoor measurements.
- Ventilation habits and comfort differ with climate. mikorym [[./thread#^c18967539|mikorym]] describes South African experience with limited insulation, comparatively brief cold periods in warmer areas, and grandparents arranging cross-drafts. sinuhe69 [[./thread#^c18966377|sinuhe69]] prefers open-window sleeping in a tropical country but finds noise disruptive. xeromal's [[./thread#^c18964686|xeromal]] recalled 68°F ideal is challenged by paradoxparalax's later clarification that preferred sleeping temperature varies between people [[./thread#^c18966252|paradoxparalax]].
- Sensor access spans very different budgets in this 2019 discussion. dschuler [[./thread#^c18962278|dschuler]] reports a \$20 NDIR purchase; gsich [[./thread#^c18966474|gsich]] reports approximately €25 optical modules; Ductapemaster [[./thread#^c18966549|Ductapemaster]] recommends a K-30 costing a couple hundred dollars. DocG [[./thread#^c18963688|DocG]] considers the university's Evikon instruments too expensive for personal use, and simpsond [[./thread#^c18964537|simpsond]] describes expensive Vaisala probes requiring an adapter. These historical prices are not an accuracy ranking or current purchasing advice.
- The economics of mechanical ventilation remain disputed. dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c18965403|dzhiurgis]] calls HRVs cheap, while Tharkun [[./thread#^c18967456|Tharkun]] questions equipment and complicated installation costs. kilotaras [[./thread#^c18966484|kilotaras]] identifies through-wall alternatives, and general8bitso [[./thread#^c18989210|general8bitso]] quotes about \$400 for a Panasonic bathroom ERV. extrapickles [[./thread#^c18967196|extrapickles]] emphasizes maintenance. These comments give options and objections, not comparable installed-cost or operating-cost estimates.
- Recurring maintenance costs can change actual ventilation behavior. manmal [[./thread#^c18963115|manmal]] reports €20 filters, perceived ventilation deterioration after two weeks, and replacement only every one to two months; the workaround is weakening the window seal. sam\_goody [[./thread#^c18967139|sam\_goody]] describes a frigid house, a resealing quote framed as a choice between open windows and mold, and concern about a relative's insect-infested vent. Both accounts show barriers beyond buying equipment.
- Construction practices and requirements vary geographically. tylerl [[./thread#^c18964695|tylerl]] describes a Washington house with timed exhaust and an outside intake, reportedly designed for approximately one whole-house air replacement per day under an older code. freetime2 [[./thread#^c18964093|freetime2]] describes Japanese room vents; rikkus [[./thread#^c18967488|rikkus]] reports trickle vents in new UK windows; gregmac [[./thread#^c18966757|gregmac]] describes Canadian HRV installations. These are commenters' accounts, not independently checked statements of current codes.
- Heating choices depend on existing buildings and local costs. SketchySeaBeast [[./thread#^c18964141|SketchySeaBeast]] reports much cheaper natural gas than electricity in Alberta and later emphasizes limits to retrofitting ordinary homes as passive houses [[./thread#^c18970676|SketchySeaBeast]]. freetime2 [[./thread#^c18964046|freetime2]] reports successful winter heating with mini-splits in another snowy setting. The relevance to air exposure is the affordability of maintaining warmth while ventilating, not a universal conclusion about one heating system.
- Opening bedroom doors and windows also encounters safety, security, and housing constraints. skuhl [[./thread#^c18962547|skuhl]] cites advice to close bedroom doors for fire safety; wolco [[./thread#^c18963383|wolco]] raises burglary; \_ea1k [[./thread#^c18963708|\_ea1k]] raises HOA and resale concerns about window bars; abootstrapper [[./thread#^c18966077|abootstrapper]] raises escape risks from those bars. darkpuma [[./thread#^c18970501|darkpuma]] proposes restricted-opening mechanisms and explicitly makes the balance neighborhood-dependent. Someone [[./thread#^c18962722|Someone]] also notes that a larger bedroom is easier to obtain with money.

## Lived experiences

- DocG [[./thread#^c18962727|DocG]] reports using five university CO2 instruments across offices, classrooms with 20–30 occupants, and a bedroom. Ventilation was associated with less tiredness and perceived higher productivity; sleeping alone behind a closed bedroom door produced substantial overnight accumulation. DocG [[./thread#^c18963563|DocG]] recalls noticing effects above roughly 2000 ppm, but does not answer how productivity was measured. The school paper mentioned later by DocG [[./thread#^c18963688|DocG]] is separate, secondhand evidence.
- gnat [[./thread#^c18963225|gnat]] reports that testing a CO2 sensor at an IoT startup revealed a correlation with early-afternoon sleepiness. Opening an outside door lowered the reading, and occupants felt more awake within about five minutes. The experience changed their willingness to tolerate colder air and wear more clothing. No numerical concentrations or controlled temperature comparison are supplied.
- graeme [[./thread#^c18964119|graeme]] reports readings no higher than approximately 1300 ppm with windows closed and approximately 700–1000 ppm with a window cracked, plus outdoor city readings of 450 ppm or more. leonroy [[./thread#^c18962918|leonroy]] reports using an IQAir AirVisual Pro, aiming for 400–500 ppm, and observing rapid increases with even one person in a medium-sized room. The latter figures describe a target, not a demonstrated universal requirement.
- clairity [[./thread#^c18962367|clairity]] reports Airthings Wave Plus readings of approximately 1500–1600 ppm CO2 at night with doors and windows closed, falling to approximately 500 ppm with them open during the day. TVOC readings reportedly moved from approximately 600 to 20 ppb over the same cycle. This occurred in a drafty prewar building. The recalled TVOC safety threshold is not established by the measurements themselves.
- jere [[./thread#^c18962160|jere]] reports an Awair 2.0 bedroom rise from approximately 1000 to 2000 ppm within a couple of sleeping hours unless the thermostat fan was on. Earlier nighttime breathlessness coincided with mild weather when heating and cooling rarely ran; the bedroom door stayed closed to exclude a cat. jere [[./thread#^c18968792|jere]] later acknowledges having no independent CO2 sensor comparison. The symptoms and readings are associated observations, not proof of oxygen deprivation.
- simulate [[./thread#^c18963451|simulate]] reports a Netatmo bedroom peak around 1500 ppm at 6 a.m. with the door closed and readings below 500 ppm with either the door or a window open. This commenter does not report a personal measured sleep improvement. The headache, fatigue, and sleep improvements discussed in the same comment are attributed to Scott Alexander's external account.
- maxk42 [[./thread#^c18975009|maxk42]] reports more than 1100 ppm despite a cracked window, followed by approximately 550 ppm after leaving a sliding door open all day, with unusually strong subjective alertness and activity. elektor [[./thread#^c18995150|elektor]] asks which meter was used, but the supplied thread gives no answer, leaving the instrument and its calibration unknown.
- tvladeck [[./thread#^c18963413|tvladeck]] reports approximately 1000–1500 ppm on an Awair in a new NYC apartment whose windows and doors were usually closed for air conditioning. Ventilating, going outside more, and working more in a large, lightly occupied office coincided with feeling more awake and healthier. The commenter explicitly says it is impossible to tell, preserving uncertainty about which changes helped.
- lmilcin [[./thread#^c18964402|lmilcin]] describes sleeping in a newly occupied, tightly sealed 10 m² office and struggling with work after a job and city change. Keeping the door open at night reportedly helped immediately after about three months. There are no gas measurements, and the original context also included unusual hours, moving, and perceived overwork.
- manmal [[./thread#^c18963115|manmal]] reports poorer rest and little dream recall with the window sealed in a small bedroom shared by four people and usually two cats. Slightly weakening the window seal reportedly restored dream recall and improved next-day rest. Built-in ventilation and filter costs are part of the account, but no CO2 readings or objective sleep measurements are supplied.
- z3t4 [[./thread#^c18963164|z3t4]] reports the opposite overnight trajectory from several other users: approximately 1000 ppm before sleep, then decreasing during sleep. Tharkun [[./thread#^c18967423|Tharkun]] reports poor ventilation without office dizziness. These are useful contrasting experiences, although ventilation rates, instruments, and individual susceptibility are not established.
- KozmoNau7 [[./thread#^c18968112|KozmoNau7]] reports better sleep after airing immediately before bed and less shower moisture after morning airing in a home whose bathroom lacks a window or forced ventilation. efficax [[./thread#^c18968717|efficax]] reports an improved working-from-home experience after adopting periodic window opening, including plans to continue in subzero weather. Both are subjective accounts without gas readings.
- Klathmon [[./thread#^c18962891|Klathmon]] reports running circulation for 15 minutes each hour to reduce a warm-bedroom/cool-house imbalance, later using it all day. Klathmon [[./thread#^c18963430|Klathmon]] reports no noticeable electricity-bill change. zrail [[./thread#^c18962794|zrail]] describes intermittent low-speed circulation, and harshulpandav [[./thread#^c18965105|harshulpandav]] reports useful airflow with a bedroom door open, a window open three inches, and HVAC running. None supplies measured CO2 removal for these arrangements.
- AngryData [[./thread#^c18964126|AngryData]], drawing on approximately 25 years of house-building and repair work, reports seeing poorly planned sealing cause ventilation and mold problems. segmondy [[./thread#^c18965553|segmondy]] describes greater personal comfort in a drafty, century-old radiator-heated house than in tightly sealed forced-air homes. These are construction experience and comfort judgments, not controlled comparisons proving leakage preferable to engineered ventilation.
- Unmetered workplace accounts vary. codetrotter [[./thread#^c18964851|codetrotter]] remembers an employer's building as unusually comfortable and much less tiring; gamma-male [[./thread#^c18964333|gamma-male]] recalls unexplained dizziness in a previous office; hnick [[./thread#^c18966396|hnick]] reports afternoon focusing trouble in a warm, stuffy two-person office with computers and a printer. Their descriptions do not distinguish CO2, temperature, VOCs, or other causes.
- clairity [[./thread#^c18969987|clairity]] reports that six purchased plants, one of which died, made the home feel more lush and comfortable, but suspects placebo in any health improvement and had no monitor beforehand. A subsequent Blueair 211+ purifier was associated with a clearer decrease in sneezing, coughing, and occasional headaches described as allergy/asthma symptoms. No CO2 reduction from the purifier or plants is reported.
- nkingsy [[./thread#^c18963429|nkingsy]] describes growing vegetables in bedroom tents under 800 watts of nighttime lighting, reporting added warmth, humidity, and food in a dry, high-altitude setting. nkingsy [[./thread#^c18965188|nkingsy]] identifies poor Salt Lake City air, reports about \$38 extra monthly electricity cost, and says the tents vent toward the beds. The commenter explicitly has no CO2 sensors, so perceived benefits do not demonstrate CO2 removal or added oxygen.
- MikeTV [[./thread#^c19036329|MikeTV]] reports experimenting with 18 liters of Spirulina in a home office and perceiving some effect, far short of fully offsetting a person. No concentration readings, instrument, or comparison period are supplied. The claimed 18-liter Chlorella requirement comes from the commenter's reading of other experiments, not a demonstrated result of this setup.
- benmccann [[./thread#^c18966064|benmccann]] reports that a newly purchased Laser Egg 2+ spikes during lunch or dinner cooked on a gas stove. The comment does not identify the channel or give numerical readings. drited [[./thread#^c18969179|drited]] subsequently asks whether that model even monitors CO2. This is a firsthand cooking-related sensor response, not an established CO2, NO2, or quantified PM2.5 exposure.
- gsich [[./thread#^c18966474|gsich]] reports owning several inexpensive MH-Z14/Z19 optical sensors and calibrating them outdoors every six months, judging them accurate enough. Ductapemaster [[./thread#^c18966549|Ductapemaster]] reports long-term workplace use of K-30 sensors with good results, but provides no error statistics. tossandturn [[./thread#^c18964020|tossandturn]] describes professional experience with infrared ethylene instruments and interference concerns; that experience is relevant to calibration caution but is not a CO2-device validation.
- co2\_throwaway [[./thread#^c18965145|co2\_throwaway]] describes grogginess, dizziness, and brain fog after prolonged close contact with a partner, offering CO2 and hormones as possibilities. jdpigeon [[./thread#^c18965341|jdpigeon]] reports similar feelings while emphasizing physiological complexity; aicioara [[./thread#^c18967132|aicioara]] identifies an allergy to a partner's cosmetics in their own case. Separately, gamma-male's [[./thread#^c18964316|gamma-male]] sensation of bedroom 'dew' and speculation about lead include no exposure measurement or diagnosis.

## Unresolved disagreements

- This source unit—story 18959796, part 0—does not provide a completed result from the featured sleep experiment. gwern [[./thread#^c18962124|gwern]] explicitly says it is unfinished; privong [[./thread#^c18962185|privong]] points to the in-progress status. The missing conclusion cannot be interpreted as either a demonstrated benefit or a null result. The commenters' personal experiments are separate evidence.
- The direct effect of bedroom CO2 on sleep quality remains unresolved here. vagab0nd [[./thread#^c18965992|vagab0nd]] asks whether induced sleepiness could help sleep; justinator [[./thread#^c18966003|justinator]] objects to waking sleepy and dizzy. Accounts such as manmal's [[./thread#^c18963115|manmal]] concern rest and dream recall, while the research claims cited by Shank [[./thread#^c18962437|Shank]] concern cognition. The supplied material does not establish a sleep threshold, dose-response relationship, lasting harm, or which individuals benefit.
- The proposed oxygen mechanism is not experimentally resolved. greenleaf2 [[./thread#^c18964137|greenleaf2]] suggests combined high CO2 and low O2; hannasanarion [[./thread#^c18963667|hannasanarion]] disputes the quantitative plausibility at ordinary indoor concentrations; cordonbleu [[./thread#^c18973155|cordonbleu]] introduces a biological transport argument. graeme [[./thread#^c18963469|graeme]] invokes reportedly absent effects in submarines, but provides no study or exposure details. No supplied firsthand account measures both room O2 and CO2.
- Consumer-sensor reliability remains insufficiently characterized. bizzleDawg [[./thread#^c18962295|bizzleDawg]] proposes a \$20-versus-\$250 comparison without reporting one, and aurbano [[./thread#^c18977476|aurbano]] requests comparisons after buying highly reviewed inexpensive devices. leonroy [[./thread#^c18964684|leonroy]] recalls a favorable evaluation but cannot locate it. benmccann [[./thread#^c18966064|benmccann]] cites an agency comparison, yet the later CO2-capability question from drited [[./thread#^c18969179|drited]] prevents transferring that endorsement confidently to CO2 measurement.
- Evidence for PM2.5 and NO2 is especially limited in this unit. Matrixik [[./thread#^c18967128|Matrixik]] supplies a particle-counter comparison link without results in the comment; benmccann [[./thread#^c18966064|benmccann]] supplies an unspecified cooking spike. There are no numerical PM2.5 or NO2 exposure readings in the supplied comments. twothamendment's [[./thread#^c18965861|twothamendment]] seasonal forest-smoke remark and jodrellblank's [[./thread#^c18965617|jodrellblank]] traffic question do not establish doses, health outcomes, or filtration effectiveness.
- The best time to open windows cannot be determined from these accounts alone. graeme [[./thread#^c18963434|graeme]] explicitly recognizes trading lower CO2 for outdoor pollutants, and pojzon [[./thread#^c18966312|pojzon]] experiences that as a practical dilemma. throw0101a [[./thread#^c18971122|throw0101a]] proposes filtered, controlled ventilation. No supplied account simultaneously measures outdoor pollution, indoor CO2, filtration performance, and sleep or cognition well enough to identify a generally preferable schedule or configuration.
- Plant-based remedies have inconsistent scales and little measured support. simulate [[./thread#^c18963451|simulate]] relays advice for about ten succulents; graeme [[./thread#^c18963434|graeme]] suggests at least 30–40; thisisweirdok [[./thread#^c18970472|thisisweirdok]] cites roughly 400 houseplants for respiratory compensation. These may involve different targets and conditions and are not directly comparable. thatcat [[./thread#^c18972089|thatcat]] proposes an engineered root-zone/carbon system but describes very low throughput; nanomonkey's [[./thread#^c18972757|nanomonkey]] algae estimate lacks its recalled citation, and MikeTV's [[./thread#^c19036329|MikeTV]] trial supplies no quantitative validation.
- Heat recovery's practical value remains contested. extrapickles [[./thread#^c18967196|extrapickles]] claims roughly half the heat loss with maintenance, while AstralStorm [[./thread#^c18970502|AstralStorm]] argues that recovery is optional and fan pressure losses can make many systems unattractive. Tharkun [[./thread#^c18967456|Tharkun]] questions installation economics. The discussion supplies no comparable recovery efficiencies, airflows, fan power, installed costs, or measured bedroom outcomes to settle these claims.
- Sensor placement and bedroom mixing remain open questions. Someone [[./thread#^c18962722|Someone]] initially recommends moving the sensor into the bedroom; larrik [[./thread#^c18962997|larrik]] points out that the article already describes doing so, which Someone [[./thread#^c18963100|Someone]] acknowledges. That correction removes one criticism but does not answer martinpw's [[./thread#^c18963286|martinpw]] question about exposure differences between a high bed and a mattress near the floor.
- The value of an unfinished, uncontrolled self-experiment is itself disputed. diminoten [[./thread#^c18963783|diminoten]] argues that an uncontrolled room makes the work misleading; your-nanny [[./thread#^c18964321|your-nanny]] argues that usefulness to the individual can suffice, and tgb [[./thread#^c18963369|tgb]] cites defenses of one-person trials. colechristensen [[./thread#^c18965847|colechristensen]] distinguishes incompleteness from lack of rigor, while diminoten [[./thread#^c18970804|diminoten]] maintains that both are problems. privong [[./thread#^c18962139|privong]] favors publishing unclear findings. None of these methodological positions supplies the missing causal result.
