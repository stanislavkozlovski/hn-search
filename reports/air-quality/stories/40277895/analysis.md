# [Gas and propane stoves linked to 50k cases of childhood asthma, study finds](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40277895)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion examines claims that gas and propane stoves are associated with childhood asthma, focusing on combustion byproducts, the quality of ventilation, and whether range hoods capture pollutants effectively. Participants compare prevention through electric cooking with ventilation and air purification, noting that cooking on electric appliances can also raise particle readings. Other topics include hood design and noise, tightly sealed homes and heat recovery ventilation, and skepticism about the framing of the article.
Tags: gas stoves, childhood asthma, indoor air pollution, range hoods, ventilation

## Relevance

The discussion covers gas-stove pollutants and childhood asthma, ventilation and range-hood performance, and a study reporting different NO2 reductions from stove replacement, filtration, and hood installation. A firsthand account reports a sharp PM2.5 spike while cooking with an electric oven and describes opening windows; replies discuss ventilation and heat-recovery systems. ## Arguments

- xkqd [[./thread#^c40278347|xkqd]] argues that proper ventilation can manage gas-combustion byproducts, while tighter building envelopes may reduce the incidental ventilation older, leakier homes provided. Their position favors informed gas-stove use with adequate exhaust; it is an argument, not a measured demonstration.
- hadlock [[./thread#^c40278613|hadlock]] argues that cooking emissions deserve attention even with electric appliances: their reported particle spike while baking pizza motivates recommending a cracked window during cooking. This broadens the discussion beyond pollution produced by burning gas.
- throw0101c [[./thread#^c40279914|throw0101c]] favors preventing pollution at its source. They summarize a 2014 study involving 78 gas-stove homes, conducted by Baltimore researchers: replacing gas with electric reportedly reduced NO2 by 50\%, carbon-filter air purifiers by 22\%, and installing range hoods produced no statistically significant difference. These are a commenter's accounts of linked research, not firsthand measurements or independently checked findings.

## Counterarguments

- Against confidence that adequate ventilation resolves gas exposure, throw0101c [[./thread#^c40279914|throw0101c]] cites research reporting hood capture efficiencies around 55\% and 30\%, with better capture over back burners than front burners. They also cite pollution released by some gas stoves while off, questioning whether intermittent hood use addresses every exposure pathway. The off-state pollution is not identified here as NO2.
- mjhay [[./thread#^c40278462|mjhay]], replying to the ventilation argument, criticizes microwave/hood combinations for restricted exhaust paths and clogging. SoftTalker [[./thread#^c40278565|SoftTalker]] adds that many recirculate air through mesh and possibly carbon filters instead of exhausting outdoors. These identify distinct limitations: inadequate capture and lack of outdoor discharge.
- hadlock [[./thread#^c40278613|hadlock]]'s electric-oven particle episode qualifies any interpretation that electrification eliminates cooking pollution. It does not contradict the NO2 reductions reported by throw0101c [[./thread#^c40279914|throw0101c]]: the accounts concern different pollutants and sources, and neither establishes that switching appliances removes every exposure.
- xkqd [[./thread#^c40281055|xkqd]], responding directly to throw0101c, accepts that source prevention is easier but maintains that informed adults should choose their appliances. The remaining disagreement concerns acceptable residual risk and personal choice, rather than a denial that pollution can occur.

## Assumptions

- xkqd [[./thread#^c40278347|xkqd]] assumes that sufficiently effective ventilation is a practical option and speculates that builders commonly underprovide it and most Americans do not understand the risks or ventilation shortcomings. No survey, housing sample, or definition of adequate ventilation supports those prevalence claims in this unit.

Neither comment supplies that other discussion's evidence, so it cannot establish additional findings or broader HN agreement.

## Conditions

- hadlock [[./thread#^c40278613|hadlock]] places their experience in a 1,200-square-foot Bay Area unit and reports typical outdoor PM2.5-related figures of 5–35, noticeable distant haze around 75, and 300-plus during fireworks festivals in unspecified international cities. The outdoor figures lack explicit units and measurement provenance; they are contextual comparisons, not a simultaneous indoor/outdoor record.
- hadlock [[./thread#^c40278613|hadlock]] recommends opening a window except during extreme heat, illustrated as 115°F, or driving rain. The advice assumes outdoor air is suitable for dilution. Opening could instead introduce outdoor pollution, especially during smoke events or near traffic; that is a conditional implication, not a measured result here. throw0101c [[./thread#^c40279914|throw0101c]] attributes remaining NO2 after stove replacement to cars and other outdoor sources only tentatively.
- In the reply chain about recirculating hoods, dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c40279070|dzhiurgis]] claims Lithuanian A+++ building requirements restrict exhausting warm air and replacing it with cold air, while expressing uncertainty about whether HRV can compensate. After throwthrowuknow [[./thread#^c40279120|throwthrowuknow]] mocks the resulting stale-air prospect, dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c40279957|dzhiurgis]] says they think HRV is mandatory and also retrofitted into older homes. These are unverified descriptions of local requirements and practices; the joke adds no exposure evidence.
- rokkitmensch [[./thread#^c40281852|rokkitmensch]] dismisses the story as a familiar problem affecting poor people with poorly ventilated kitchens and objects to its child-focused framing. This introduces housing resources as a possible exposure condition, but supplies neither income-stratified measurements nor appliance, ventilation, or filtration costs. It does not empirically rebut the asthma claim.

## Lived experiences

- hadlock [[./thread#^c40278613|hadlock]] reports buying an indoor air-quality monitor because of California wildfires and subsequently seeing an electric oven baking a frozen pizza produce a roughly 15-minute reading described as PM2.5 AQI 400. This is the clearest firsthand measurement in the unit. They report no associated symptoms or measured wildfire exposure.
- dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c40279097|dzhiurgis]], replying to hadlock's cooking account, says they turn off their HRV to avoid spreading fumes through the house and advocates opening a window to provide extractor makeup air. No before-and-after pollutant readings establish the effectiveness or whole-house consequences of this practice.
- throw0101c [[./thread#^c40279938|throw0101c]], replying to SoftTalker's statement that these appliances often recirculate, reports that a relative has an externally vented example. This establishes an equipment exception, not its capture efficiency, and does not contradict the qualified claim that many units recirculate.
- voisin [[./thread#^c40278523|voisin]] wants a residential hood with its motor outside the kitchen, describing available choices as powerful but 'so loud you can’t think' or quiet but ineffective. This is a perceived comfort and usability tradeoff, without named equipment, airflow measurements, or a tested remote-motor solution.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The reliability and interpretation of hadlock [[./thread#^c40278613|hadlock]]'s readings remain uncertain: no monitor model, calibration, independent comparison, averaging method, or repeated observations are supplied. The indoor 400 is described as AQI and should not be relabeled as 400 µg/m³. The episode also does not isolate emissions from the food, oven, or accumulated cooking residue.
- Why the cited hood intervention showed no significant NO2 reduction while carbon filtration did remains unexplained. xkqd [[./thread#^c40281055|xkqd]] finds this counterintuitive and proposes extraction closer to the burners. throw0101c [[./thread#^c40279914|throw0101c]]'s summary lacks sufficient installation, operation, and statistical detail to resolve the result; it does not establish that every hood is ineffective.
- Filtration comparisons remain limited. throw0101c [[./thread#^c40279914|throw0101c]] reports a NO2 reduction from carbon-filter purifiers, while SoftTalker [[./thread#^c40278565|SoftTalker]] describes hoods with mesh and sometimes carbon. Filter specifications, maintenance, airflow, and operating conditions are absent, so these devices cannot be treated as equivalent. No firsthand PM2.5 filtration results or CO2-removal measurements are supplied.
- There is no paired test establishing when opening windows improves or worsens exposure during cooking, traffic pollution, or wildfire smoke. hadlock [[./thread#^c40278613|hadlock]]'s wildfire-motivated monitor purchase does not validate their near-universal window-opening recommendation under smoky conditions, and throw0101c [[./thread#^c40279914|throw0101c]]'s proposed outdoor NO2 contribution remains unmeasured in this account.
- The headline's estimate of 50,000 childhood-asthma cases cannot be evaluated from these comments. throw0101c [[./thread#^c40279914|throw0101c]] discusses pollutant reductions rather than asthma outcomes or attribution methods. The unit supplies no personal asthma outcomes, CO2 readings, sleep accounts, or cognitive testing; voisin [[./thread#^c40278523|voisin]]'s difficulty thinking concerns hood noise, not a demonstrated effect of air pollution.
