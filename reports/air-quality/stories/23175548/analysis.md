# [Studies show Alzheimer’s occurs more often in people exposed to polluted air](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=23175548)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants discuss studies associating polluted air with Alzheimer's disease and other cognitive risks, questioning causal interpretation, exposure histories, diagnoses, and confounding variables. The thread also turns to pollution reduction, household filtration and ventilation, transportation policy, public transit, urban design, and how the pandemic changed perceptions of air quality.
Tags: air-pollution, Alzheimers, dementia, health-research, correlation-and-causation, air-filtration, transportation-policy

## Relevance

The thread discusses PM2.5 health claims and uncertainty about causation, alongside firsthand observations of clearer skies and traffic-related air exposure. Comments also compare ventilation, indoor filtration, and the drawbacks of relying on windows when outdoor air is polluted. ## Arguments

- vosper [[./thread#^c23176704|vosper]] argues that clearer air during pandemic restrictions made previously normalized pollution more noticeable. aaron695 [[./thread#^c23177624|aaron695]] considers the general harm from PM2.5 sufficient reason to improve indoor and outdoor air without waiting for another Alzheimer's finding. These are arguments for action, not quantified exposure or health results.
- 2sk21 [[./thread#^c23177103|2sk21]] quotes a proposed mechanism connecting chronic polluted-air exposure with excessive inflammatory cytokines and nerve-cell death, then draws a parallel with COVID-19 deterioration. The comment supplies a mechanistic claim from the article, not measurements establishing that pathway or its contribution to Alzheimer's risk.
- radu\_floricica [[./thread#^c23177057|radu\_floricica]] distinguishes several indoor-air functions: ventilation to lower CO2 and VOCs, filtration for PM2.5 and other particles, temperature control, heat recovery, and humidification. Their central argument is that recirculating air conditioning or a standalone purifier does not provide all these functions.
- WalterBright [[./thread#^c23176833|WalterBright]] recommends adding HEPA filtration to a dwelling's HVAC, and WalterBright [[./thread#^c23177144|WalterBright]] proposes a box fan with a furnace filter described as HEPA as an alternative. kube-system [[./thread#^c23181151|kube-system]] says common North American box fans accommodate a 20x20 filter held against the inlet by suction. These are practical suggestions without verified filter ratings, airflow measurements, or measured exposure reductions in the supplied discussion.
- synaesthesisx [[./thread#^c23176778|synaesthesisx]] advocates replacing combustion cars with electric vehicles, supported by vehicle and charging rebates. kilroy123 [[./thread#^c23177511|kilroy123]] emphasizes replacing visibly polluting older buses, while tonyedgecombe [[./thread#^c23177794|tonyedgecombe]] suggests trolley buses. Their proposed air-quality benefit concerns removing combustion emissions from streets; none reports a measured before-and-after intervention.
- arijun [[./thread#^c23177750|arijun]] proposes excluding private cars from many city centers to reduce air and noise pollution. spodek [[./thread#^c23178292|spodek]] suggests avoiding unnecessary trips, cycling, and carpooling. These proposals reduce traffic activity rather than only changing vehicle propulsion, but the comments contain no resulting pollutant concentrations.
- mensetmanusman [[./thread#^c23178388|mensetmanusman]] proposes incorporating PM2.5-related health costs into taxes on contributing products or processes and returning the proceeds to taxpayers. This is a proposal to change pollution-producing behavior, not evidence of its effectiveness or distributional consequences.

## Counterarguments

- thedudeabides5 [[./thread#^c23179546|thedudeabides5]] questions whether pollution–Alzheimer's associations reflect intervening variables rather than causation. KKKKkkkk1 [[./thread#^c23180799|KKKKkkkk1]] invokes a general criticism of finding correlations in large datasets, while Nasrudith [[./thread#^c23180700|Nasrudith]] suggests industrialization could correlate with better access to diagnosis. These identify possible methodological problems, but none demonstrates that the particular studies failed to address them.
- nnain [[./thread#^c23178406|nnain]] challenges the association because Alzheimer's does not seem prominent to them despite severe pollution around Delhi. JordanFarmer [[./thread#^c23178079|JordanFarmer]] invokes a mentally sharp elderly relative who smoked and asserts that moderate long-term smokers have no increased risk. The regional impression and individual example do not establish population incidence or support the broad smoking-risk assertion.
- s5300 [[./thread#^c23177272|s5300]] asks whether clearer-looking skies establish substantially cleaner air, and checker659 [[./thread#^c23177834|checker659]] suggests seasonality could explain the appearance. Responding to the visibility question, kaybe [[./thread#^c23177491|kaybe]] points to monitoring plots and reported satellite-observed NO2 reductions, while cautioning that weather and wind strongly influence individual readings. The prompt contains their description of this evidence, not the underlying measurements.
- marliechiller [[./thread#^c23176810|marliechiller]] and stormdennis [[./thread#^c23177500|stormdennis]] raise reported tire and brake particle emissions that electrification would not entirely eliminate. labster [[./thread#^c23176828|labster]] goes further, claiming EVs do not reduce PM and that heavier vehicles increase tire-derived microplastics. lazyjones [[./thread#^c23177163|lazyjones]] disputes that conclusion, citing eliminated exhaust and reduced friction-brake use; pg-gadfly [[./thread#^c23176844|pg-gadfly]] identifies regenerative braking as the mechanism. stuaxo [[./thread#^c23177127|stuaxo]] argues that an incomplete solution can still be worthwhile. Neither the size of the remaining problem nor the net reduction is established here.
- xenonite [[./thread#^c23177084|xenonite]] objects that electric vehicles also involve power-plant pollution. pcr0 [[./thread#^c23177562|pcr0]] responds that installing pollution controls at thousands of plants is easier than at millions of vehicles. This distinguishes street-level exhaust reduction from pollution elsewhere, but neither comment quantifies personal exposure under the alternatives.

## Assumptions

- Interpreting geographic Alzheimer's differences requires assumptions about age, survival, exposure duration, and historical pollutants. adrianN [[./thread#^c23178461|adrianN]] asks about Delhi residents' life expectancy and lifetime exposure; the\_dripper [[./thread#^c23178438|the\_dripper]] speculates that increases might not appear for 30 years; Zenst [[./thread#^c23182147|Zenst]] raises past lead exposure from vehicle emissions. The proposed 30-year interval is conjecture, and the discussion supplies no individual exposure histories.
- xiphias2 [[./thread#^c23177414|xiphias2]] infers that tire and brake materials are probably much less harmful than diesel exhaust because they have not noticed comparable sickness from them. That inference assumes immediate subjective sensitivity tracks longer-term toxicity and identifies the responsible pollutant, neither of which their account establishes.
- Treating visible filter dirt as proof of a particular PM2.5 reduction assumes that the captured material represents the relevant particle sizes and a meaningful decrease in inhaled dose. asah [[./thread#^c23177867|asah]] reports a DIY filter turning gray, and Cactus2018 [[./thread#^c23179355|Cactus2018]] reports substantial material on a purifier's pre-filter; neither provides particle-size measurements or room concentrations.
- A claim that a purifier solves indoor air quality generally would assume it addresses the pollutants of concern. radu\_floricica [[./thread#^c23177057|radu\_floricica]] explicitly separates ventilation for CO2/VOCs from particle filtration, whereas WalterBright [[./thread#^c23176833|WalterBright]] recommends HEPA filtration. The supplied accounts do not establish that these proposed particle filters lower CO2 or NO2.
- clarry [[./thread#^c23177429|clarry]] prioritizes cities partly on the assumption that rural residents are neither major causes nor major victims of pollution. koonsolo [[./thread#^c23177373|koonsolo]] instead emphasizes rural dependence on cars. No rural exposure measurements support a general conclusion that rural air is safe or that the same transport remedy fits both settings.

## Conditions

- Window opening involves a conditional tradeoff. radu\_floricica [[./thread#^c23177057|radu\_floricica]] wants outside air to dilute CO2/VOCs but warns that opening a window can reintroduce particles into filtered rooms; lazyjones [[./thread#^c23177181|lazyjones]] nevertheless prefers opening windows more often. p1mrx [[./thread#^c23177302|p1mrx]] identifies an additional benefit when outside air is cooler. Together these suggest considering outdoor pollution and temperature, but they do not establish that every opening erases filtration gains or identify a measured operating rule.
- Housing infrastructure limits the available improvements. radu\_floricica [[./thread#^c23177057|radu\_floricica]] reports difficulty obtaining an integrated system for an apartment, especially retrofitting heat-recovery ventilation. They distrust some air-conditioner HEPA claims and say advertised ventilation can mean recirculation. They would pay extra for a humidifier without frequent refilling. These are their purchasing and installation constraints, not proof that such systems are unavailable in all apartments.
- Filtration must also accommodate airflow and maintenance. mensetmanusman [[./thread#^c23178416|mensetmanusman]] recounts installers recommending a very porous filter to avoid harming the HVAC system, then interprets the concern as excessive pressure drop when more effective filters become dirty. They mention pressure-drop monitoring as a replacement guide. The account supports examining maintenance and compatibility, but contains no engineering assessment validating a particular filter upgrade.
- The practicality of reducing car use depends heavily on the transport network. Responding to rimliu's concern about transporting children [[./thread#^c23177810|rimliu]], DoingIsLearning [[./thread#^c23178351|DoingIsLearning]] contrasts places requiring a car or hourly buses plus long walks with a city where their family rarely needs one. dasil003 [[./thread#^c23179490|dasil003]] reports managing in London with a daughter aged two to six without a car or stroller. These replies qualify a blanket family-transport objection without establishing universal feasibility.
- Affordability constrains electrification. In this 2020 discussion, polishdude20 [[./thread#^c23181091|polishdude20]] would switch only when a reliable used EV costs about \$2,000, comparable to their older Corolla, rather than requiring \$15,000 or more. strstr [[./thread#^c23176812|strstr]] says agreement changes when a deadline is specified, and synaesthesisx [[./thread#^c23176778|synaesthesisx]] proposes rebates for new and used vehicles and charging infrastructure. The prices are one commenter's historical purchasing constraint.
- Traffic-reduction proposals must account for necessary travel and unequal burdens. koonsolo [[./thread#^c23177373|koonsolo]] asks about rural residents, nicbou [[./thread#^c23177768|nicbou]] notes that goods still require delivery, and globular-toast [[./thread#^c23180992|globular-toast]] proposes road-use charges with discounts for greater needs, including disability. These comments qualify how an urban pollution intervention could be implemented.
- The outdoor observations occurred during pandemic restrictions. open-source-ux [[./thread#^c23177010|open-source-ux]] anticipates competing effects: avoidance of crowded transit could increase driving, while working from home could reduce it. kaybe [[./thread#^c23177491|kaybe]] also emphasizes weather and historical comparisons. Temporary clearer skies therefore do not establish a durable improvement or isolate its cause.

## Lived experiences

- christophilus [[./thread#^c23177128|christophilus]] reports unusually blue, less hazy skies and substantially less stress while walking on blocks without cars, particularly with children. Responding to the seasonality objection, christophilus [[./thread#^c23190625|christophilus]] locates the observation between Atlanta and Charlotte and says the normally visible five or more jet contrails had disappeared. These are visual and emotional observations, not measurements of PM2.5, NO2, or a pollutant-specific health effect.
- xiphias2 [[./thread#^c23177414|xiphias2]] describes extreme sensitivity, daily suffering attributed to air pollution, and deep regret about previously living beside a main road. They advise another roadside resident to move. The account conveys a serious personal burden but supplies no measured exposure, diagnosis, or comparison after relocation.
- kilroy123 [[./thread#^c23177511|kilroy123]] reports several years living in Mexico City and describes severe pollution and old buses visibly emitting large amounts of exhaust. fiftyfifty [[./thread#^c23181355|fiftyfifty]] reports that most local diesel buses were replaced by natural-gas buses, which look cleaner, and guesses that they cost less to operate. Both observations are firsthand; neither is a measured pollutant comparison, and the operating-cost claim is explicitly a guess.
- giu [[./thread#^c23178400|giu]] reports that a Philips AC2887/10 HEPA purifier, purchased two months earlier for an apartment, noticeably improved quality of life at home. They do not identify particular symptoms, pollutant changes, cognitive benefits, or sleep outcomes.
- asah [[./thread#^c23177867|asah]] says they tried the proposed box-fan filter and observed it turning gray. Cactus2018 [[./thread#^c23179355|Cactus2018]] reports substantial accumulated material on a Winix purifier's permanent pre-filter and rinsing it every two weeks. These accounts demonstrate observed collection and maintenance work, without measuring fine-particle exposure reduction.
- p1mrx [[./thread#^c23177302|p1mrx]] reports taping a window shut for a portable air conditioner and finding it wasteful to run cooling at night when outside air is colder. They describe short cooling runs under those conditions and regard ventilation as a potentially free alternative. This is an operational and comfort tradeoff, not a measured air-quality or sleep experiment.
- cknight [[./thread#^c23177753|cknight]] says being a light sleeper makes streets with late-night or early-morning bus routes unsuitable when looking for housing. Their enthusiasm for electric buses concerns noise. This is the thread's explicit sleep-related account, and it should not be recast as evidence that air pollution caused their sleep difficulty.
- goblin89 [[./thread#^c23180114|goblin89]] reports using AirVisual and repeatedly seeing Glasgow display a US AQI around 10 or below after adding several cities for comparison. This is firsthand observation of an app's environmental data, not a personal monitor deployed in Glasgow. The comment does not provide the underlying pollutant concentration, station, averaging period, or calibration.
- nnain [[./thread#^c23191344|nnain]] reports grandparents living beyond 80 with little medication and argues that poverty lowers average longevity without making long-lived Indian residents unavailable for study. JordanFarmer [[./thread#^c23178079|JordanFarmer]] reports a mentally sharp 96-year-old grandmother who smoked for 42 years. These individual histories explain their skepticism but cannot estimate Alzheimer's risk under comparable exposure.
- eloff [[./thread#^c23179599|eloff]] reports four car-free years in Vancouver, feeling healthier and happier while walking and cycling, and avoiding parking and roughly \$3,000 annually in insurance. In contrast, Red\_Leaves\_Flyy [[./thread#^c23181827|Red\_Leaves\_Flyy]] reports transit journeys of 90–120 minutes versus 5–15 minutes by car, with car ownership costing more but saving roughly 700 hours yearly. These contrasting experiences show how local infrastructure changes the personal costs of traffic reduction; neither measures an air-pollution health effect.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The pollution–Alzheimer's relationship remains unresolved within the supplied evidence. 2sk21 [[./thread#^c23177103|2sk21]] offers an inflammatory mechanism, while thedudeabides5 [[./thread#^c23179546|thedudeabides5]] raises confounding. king\_panic [[./thread#^c23181759|king\_panic]] invokes an earlier herpes association, and adrianN [[./thread#^c23188964|adrianN]] responds that neurodegeneration may have multiple causes and that Alzheimer's may not describe a single disease. The comments supply no study methods, effect sizes, exposure-response estimates, or adjusted results capable of resolving these disagreements; a competing association alone does not exclude pollution's contribution.
- The magnitude of EV-related particulate improvement is disputed. After labster supplies a study link [[./thread#^c23183486|labster]], lazyjones [[./thread#^c23184034|lazyjones]] interprets it as showing little reduction in non-tailpipe emissions while preserving the benefit of eliminating tailpipe emissions. xenonite [[./thread#^c23177123|xenonite]] additionally argues that engine braking and gentler speed changes can reduce wear in combustion vehicles. The export contains no comparative particle measurements establishing the balance among exhaust, tires, friction brakes, vehicle weight, and driving behavior.
- The public-transport comparison uses different reference cases. sjwright [[./thread#^c23177847|sjwright]] argues that transit emits less per passenger; lazyjones [[./thread#^c23178008|lazyjones]] counters with an electric car versus diesel buses. sjwright [[./thread#^c23179267|sjwright]] then points to electric public transport, and lazyjones [[./thread#^c23179456|lazyjones]] reiterates that electrifying urban buses was the original point. No shared fleet, occupancy, pollutant, or exposure comparison resolves the disagreement.
- No measured window-opening strategy emerges. radu\_floricica [[./thread#^c23177057|radu\_floricica]] emphasizes particle ingress into filtered rooms, while lazyjones [[./thread#^c23177181|lazyjones]] favors more frequent opening. Neither reports paired indoor/outdoor PM2.5, NO2, or CO2 readings, ventilation rates, or health outcomes. The discussion consequently cannot identify an exposure threshold, duration, or schedule that balances ventilation against incoming pollution.
- Sensor reliability cannot be assessed from this unit. goblin89 [[./thread#^c23180114|goblin89]] supplies an app-based AQI observation, and kaybe [[./thread#^c23177491|kaybe]] describes monitoring and satellite evidence with weather caveats. There are no personal sensor calibration tests, reference comparisons, or raw concentration series. radu\_floricica's distrust of advertised HEPA features [[./thread#^c23177057|radu\_floricica]] concerns filtration claims rather than demonstrated sensor accuracy.
- Story 23175548, part 0 leaves several approved topics unaddressed: it contains no cooking-emission or wildfire-smoke accounts, numerical indoor CO2 or NO2 measurements, or measured acute cognitive effects. radu\_floricica [[./thread#^c23177057|radu\_floricica]] mentions CO2 as a ventilation target; 2sk21 [[./thread#^c23177103|2sk21]] discusses a proposed chronic neurodegenerative mechanism; cknight [[./thread#^c23177753|cknight]] discusses sleep vulnerability to traffic noise. These comments do not establish effects of those gases on cognition or sleep, or tested advice for cooking and wildfire conditions.
