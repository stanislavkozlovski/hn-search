# [Gas utilities used tobacco tactics to avoid gas stove regulations](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=38771185)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

The article’s account of gas utilities’ efforts to resist stove regulation leads into disagreement about indoor emissions, ventilation, and the strength and interpretation of the health evidence. Participants compare gas, induction, and other electric stoves on efficiency, cost, environmental impact, safety, cookware compatibility, and cooking technique, including whether induction works well for specialized methods. The discussion also considers carbon pricing, energy sources and infrastructure, consumer choice, building ventilation standards, and the influence of industry lobbying. Some commenters challenge the journalism or the research; others argue that industry tactics have obscured risks and that ordinary ventilation may not reliably protect households.
Tags: gas stoves, indoor air quality, induction cooking, public health, energy policy, industry lobbying, ventilation

## Relevance

The discussion directly addresses NO2 and other pollutants from gas cooking, including a reported kitchen reading above an hourly guideline and conflicting consumer-monitor experiences. It compares ventilation setups and induction, while noting that ventilation quality varies and that the health evidence and practical effectiveness remain disputed. ## Arguments

- The clearest NO2 number is secondhand: perihelions [[./thread#^c38771871|perihelions]] quotes a photo caption reporting 0.159 ppm, or 159 ppb, in epidemiologist Josiah Kephart’s kitchen, compared with a cited WHO hourly guideline of 106 ppb. The caption also says Kephart subsequently switched to electric. This is a reported measurement reproduced from the article, not the commenter’s own experiment or a documented exposure time series.
- Removing indoor gas combustion is advocated as a way to reduce exposure, especially around children. Responding to an argument for unrestricted appliance choice, dangus [[./thread#^c38771397|dangus]] raises childhood asthma and informed consent. Professional cook chefandy [[./thread#^c38771765|chefandy]] recommends induction particularly for homes with children or without outside-venting hoods, while acknowledging gas’s culinary advantages. These comments provide reasons for precaution, rather than quantified health benefits from switching.
- Ventilation is proposed for every use of gas burners and ovens, including apparently clean cooking: peteradio [[./thread#^c38772462|peteradio]] emphasizes that combustion occurs whenever these appliances operate. dangus [[./thread#^c38773140|dangus]] proposes exhaust that starts automatically with a burner. saalweachter [[./thread#^c38771837|saalweachter]] separately stresses the distinction between an externally exhausting hood and a recirculating microwave hood, describing the latter as barely filtration.
- Moving cooking outdoors is proposed as another way to keep cooking emissions out of living spaces. TheAceOfHearts [[./thread#^c38771576|TheAceOfHearts]] suggests outdoor kitchens, and bob1029 [[./thread#^c38771625|bob1029]] says they are considering one because substantial cooking can leave persistent indoor odors despite a powerful hood. Neither supplies measurements demonstrating an exposure reduction.
- Trustworthy health information is itself a practical requirement. pinkmuffinere [[./thread#^c38771782|pinkmuffinere]] distinguishes whether stove NO2 is dangerous from how a household could know, expressing concern that industry influence may obstruct informed decisions while explicitly saying the available information does not let them judge their own health risk.

## Counterarguments

- Some commenters argue that adequate extraction makes gas acceptable: fullspectrumdev [[./thread#^c38771369|fullspectrumdev]] says a proper extractor mitigates the problem entirely, nemo44x [[./thread#^c38771388|nemo44x]] calls vented gas perfectly safe, and perihelions [[./thread#^c38771871|perihelions]] treats the photographed installation as unrepresentative of normal, code-compliant stoves. These are strong safety conclusions without accompanying NO2 measurements under the proposed ventilation conditions.
- Other commenters dispute that ordinary extraction is sufficient. pinkmuffinere [[./thread#^c38771658|pinkmuffinere]] and ReflectedImage [[./thread#^c38771555|ReflectedImage]] attribute that position to the article. Gigachad [[./thread#^c38776345|Gigachad]] says they have never encountered adequate gas-stove ventilation and proposes something resembling an enclosed laboratory fume hood. The supplied comments establish disagreement, but do not demonstrate either universal adequacy or universal failure.
- Changing the heat source does not address every cooking emission. bob1029 [[./thread#^c38771625|bob1029]] argues that heating oil and food for prolonged periods produces air-quality problems regardless of the stove; nemo44x [[./thread#^c38771499|nemo44x]] likewise says all indoor cooking needs ventilation. This qualifies reactordev’s [[./thread#^c38771432|reactordev]] claim that induction means they are no longer breathing harmful chemicals. The thread does not quantify the relative contributions of fuel combustion and food preparation.
- Exposure reduction has cooking tradeoffs for some participants. chefandy [[./thread#^c38771765|chefandy]] values induction for pastry precision and consistency but prefers flame feedback, heating while lifting a pan, and gas’s heat distribution for other work. fullspectrumdev [[./thread#^c38772176|fullspectrumdev]] describes easier pepper charring and temperature control with gas and prefers a hybrid kitchen. Their preferences explain resistance to switching without establishing that gas exposure is harmless.

## Assumptions

- Arguments that ventilation solves the problem assume that a hood’s presence, apparent suction, or code compliance establishes effective pollutant capture. perihelions [[./thread#^c38771871|perihelions]] questions the photographed stove’s legality, while nephy [[./thread#^c38771688|nephy]] describes a powerful five-speed fan exhausting directly through an exterior wall. Neither provides a relevant code provision, measured airflow, capture efficiency, or NO2 result.
- The consumer-monitor arguments assume their readings address the pollutant under dispute. Mountain\_Skies [[./thread#^c38772184|Mountain\_Skies]] does not identify what their air-quality meter measures; koheripbal [[./thread#^c38771589|koheripbal]] specifically discusses VOC readings. The supplied descriptions do not establish that either observation measures NO2, PM2.5, or every relevant cooking pollutant.
- Comparing the photographed 159 ppb reading with an hourly guideline assumes the sampling duration is appropriate. perihelions [[./thread#^c38771871|perihelions]] supplies no hourly average, sampling position, calibration information, or repeated measurements. Numerically, the reported reading is 50\% above the quoted 106 ppb guideline; calling that barely above does not establish its health significance.
- Smell, familiarity, and subjective reassurance are treated as exposure indicators in parts of the discussion. kergonath [[./thread#^c38771897|kergonath]] argues that inconspicuous pollution and decades of habitual use encourage underestimation. Conversely, the cooking odors described by bob1029 [[./thread#^c38771625|bob1029]] establish a comfort complaint, but do not identify a pollutant or quantify a harmful dose.
- Ventilation-based protection assumes consistent operation and maintenance. dangus [[./thread#^c38773140|dangus]] reports that family members needed encouragement to run the fan for tasks such as boiling water. fullspectrumdev [[./thread#^c38773008|fullspectrumdev]] describes overdue extractor servicing prompted by impending rental inspections. Having equipment installed therefore does not establish how it performs during everyday use.

## Conditions

- Reported operating costs vary substantially by household and location. nephy [[./thread#^c38771453|nephy]] reports about \$15 monthly for several gas appliances, identifies South Carolina [[./thread#^c38771575|nephy]], and says the county supplies the gas [[./thread#^c38772899|nephy]]. thelastgallon [[./thread#^c38772156|thelastgallon]] reports \$50–\$255 monthly in Texas despite little cooking. smileysteve [[./thread#^c38771685|smileysteve]] reports roughly \$33 monthly in service fees with low gas consumption much of the year. Different appliance loads and fixed charges prevent a direct comparison of cooking costs.
- Existing equipment changes the economics of reducing combustion exposure. nephy [[./thread#^c38771544|nephy]] says local builders commonly install gas and that replacing their current setup is not worthwhile, although induction might have appealed in a new build. dangus [[./thread#^c38771473|dangus]] also distinguishes retrofitting an entire house from choosing equipment during construction. lupusreal [[./thread#^c38772102|lupusreal]] predicts that households pressured into early replacement may select cheaper resistance electric rather than induction; this is a prediction, not a measured purchasing pattern.
- Tenancy and enforcement constrain ventilation improvements. Mountain\_Skies [[./thread#^c38772184|Mountain\_Skies]] has not investigated their oven’s air-quality effects because the property is rented. fullspectrumdev [[./thread#^c38773008|fullspectrumdev]] says extraction is required in Irish rentals but historically under-enforced, and reports their landlord arranging servicing when council inspections began. Neither account establishes conditions in rentals elsewhere.
- Outdoor cooking depends on weather, space, and energy availability. TheAceOfHearts [[./thread#^c38771576|TheAceOfHearts]] explicitly identifies poor weather and unreliable electrical supplies as constraints. the\_third\_wave [[./thread#^c38771676|the\_third\_wave]] describes a rural Swedish farm with ready access to wood, plus occasional outdoor induction use during drought-related fire restrictions. That setting cannot be assumed available to an apartment resident, and the fire restrictions are not an account of measured wildfire-smoke exposure.
- Switching costs depend partly on existing cookware and appliance durability. fragmede [[./thread#^c38771972|fragmede]] needed no new pots; sliken [[./thread#^c38773329|sliken]] found only one incompatible item among a couple dozen. pandaman [[./thread#^c38776025|pandaman]] challenges the suggestion that recent purchase alone guarantees compatibility, pointing to material differences. rolobio [[./thread#^c38771634|rolobio]] reports a shattered glass cooktop that was excluded from warranty coverage. These are household-specific costs, rather than evidence that every conversion requires replacement cookware.
- Electrical capacity can constrain the induction equipment available. bob1029 [[./thread#^c38771574|bob1029]] says the strongest option they found was 5 kW and required a 50 A circuit. isoprophlex [[./thread#^c38772579|isoprophlex]] describes 7.2 kW cooktops as common locally and believes they require multiple phases. The exchange does not resolve whether the quoted ratings refer to one element or the whole cooktop, so it cannot establish a uniform performance comparison.

## Lived experiences

- Mountain\_Skies [[./thread#^c38772184|Mountain\_Skies]] reports using a consumer-grade air-quality meter: their natural-gas stovetop did not produce a concerning result even during substantial cooking, whereas the oven affected air quality considerably after a couple of hours. Extended oven use was mostly for turkey. They provide no pollutant identity, concentrations, or sensor specifications and explicitly do not know whether the hood would help the oven.
- koheripbal [[./thread#^c38771589|koheripbal]], replying to the claim that extraction is insufficient, presents a measurement-based rebuttal: Airthings or Awair VOC monitoring reportedly shows a fan keeping readings at zero, or an open window bringing readings almost to zero quickly after cooking. They invoke their own data, but supply no device model, numerical trace, elapsed time, outdoor comparison, or NO2 measurement.
- CharlieDigital [[./thread#^c38771546|CharlieDigital]] describes a daughter with longstanding mild coughing, negative allergy testing, and a history of premature birth. Her coughing declined around a move at age five from a home with an internally recirculating microwave hood to one exhausting outdoors. When zdragnar [[./thread#^c38771937|zdragnar]] suggests hidden mold as an alternative explanation, CharlieDigital [[./thread#^c38772396|CharlieDigital]] clarifies that the new house had mold problems and underwent basement and bathroom-ventilation work. The account is suggestive but cannot isolate the hood change from moving, maturation, or other interventions; it is not a confirmed asthma or NO2 diagnosis.
- Clamchop [[./thread#^c38775565|Clamchop]] reports three apartments without genuine kitchen exhaust, including two gas-equipped new builds. After TheCleric [[./thread#^c38777670|TheCleric]] describes installations that vented into a wall, Clamchop [[./thread#^c38800565|Clamchop]] clarifies the physical observations: one hood lacked its intended duct connection, and two microwaves discharged through their fronts. The reply supports an installation-specific account rather than merely assuming every hood recirculates.
- Rental experiences conflict substantially. dangus [[./thread#^c38771400|dangus]] says they have never seen a rental with a proper extractor and later notes their rentals were at least 50 years old [[./thread#^c38771443|dangus]]. tomrod [[./thread#^c38771430|tomrod]] reports eight ordinary rentals with extraction and confirms checking external venting in buildings dating from 1890 to 2005 [[./thread#^c38771629|tomrod]]. rascul [[./thread#^c38771545|rascul]] finds outside-venting fans in most rentals they repair, specifically detached single-family houses. These differing samples do not establish rental-wide prevalence.
- liveoneggs [[./thread#^c38771585|liveoneggs]] reports gas appliances with little ventilation beyond building leakage and bathroom fans. They describe their gas oven as slow and smelly and try to open a window during prolonged use. This documents discomfort and a coping practice, but no measured benefit from opening the window.
- lucidguppy [[./thread#^c38771424|lucidguppy]] reports that switching to radiant electric eliminated the large amount of heat their gas stove had put into the kitchen and avoided scorching saucepan sides, while sacrificing some pan manipulation. chefandy [[./thread#^c38771765|chefandy]] also values avoiding the heat of numerous large gas burners in a confined professional kitchen. These are comfort and working-condition observations, not pollutant measurements.
- dangus [[./thread#^c38773140|dangus]] reports having to establish a family habit of using the hood because tasks that seem clean, such as heating water for macaroni and cheese, did not motivate its use. This firsthand behavioral limitation underlies their suggestion of automatic extraction.
- Jailbird [[./thread#^c38771454|Jailbird]] reports no notable utility-bill change after replacing gas with induction. Before committing, they successfully cooked many meals on a plug-in 1,800 W unit with 1\% power increments; their installed cooktop instead had unevenly spaced power levels. Their favorable experience shows that control and affordability depend on the equipment, rather than supporting one universal induction experience.
- the\_third\_wave’s account appears twice [[./thread#^c38771676|the\_third\_wave]] [[./thread#^c38772167|the\_third\_wave]] and constitutes one firsthand account: residential induction setups disappointed them for wok cooking, whereas their wood-fired stove worked well for that purpose. They also report repairing two induction units, including one capacitor failure that emitted substantial black smoke. That failure is relevant to an exceptional exposure incident, but supplies no comparison of normal operating emissions.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The magnitude and causality of the health risk remain unresolved within this export. pinkmuffinere [[./thread#^c38771782|pinkmuffinere]] explicitly cannot determine the personal health implications; dangus [[./thread#^c38771397|dangus]] invokes childhood asthma; CharlieDigital [[./thread#^c38771546|CharlieDigital]] supplies a respiratory anecdote without exposure measurements or a controlled comparison. The material does not establish a dose-response relationship, an individual diagnosis caused by a stove, or the health benefit of a particular intervention.
- There is no common measurement basis for deciding how much extraction is enough. fullspectrumdev [[./thread#^c38771369|fullspectrumdev]] claims complete mitigation, pinkmuffinere [[./thread#^c38771658|pinkmuffinere]] reports the article’s contrary position, and koheripbal [[./thread#^c38771589|koheripbal]] offers VOC observations. No matched NO2 measurements, hood capture tests, calibration checks, or controlled gas-versus-electric cooking comparisons settle the dispute. Mountain\_Skies’ [[./thread#^c38772184|Mountain\_Skies]] stove/oven contrast also remains unexplained.
- Claims about the research cannot be adjudicated from the supplied discussion. dfgfek [[./thread#^c38771339|dfgfek]] calls studies contradictory, but firtoz’s [[./thread#^c38771356|firtoz]] request for examples receives no supplied answer. In separate branches, RcouF1uZ4gsC [[./thread#^c38771857|RcouF1uZ4gsC]] criticizes judging researchers by their conclusions rather than methods, while JoBrad [[./thread#^c38773373|JoBrad]] argues that the article links supporting health research. Neither methodological distrust nor confidence in those links substitutes for study details absent from this export.
- Outdoor consequences of vented combustion receive only an unmeasured exchange. koheripbal [[./thread#^c38771573|koheripbal]] points to furnace chimneys as the response to concerns about burning things near homes; k\_\_ [[./thread#^c38771639|k\_\_]] maintains that neighborhood-wide combustion can remain harmful. The comments do not quantify outdoor concentrations, identify a specific pollutant, or establish subsequent indoor exposure.
- Opening windows has a reported VOC benefit in koheripbal’s [[./thread#^c38771589|koheripbal]] account and is a habitual response to prolonged oven use for liveoneggs [[./thread#^c38771585|liveoneggs]]. Neither supplies simultaneous outdoor readings or conditions under which the intervention failed. This source unit therefore cannot determine when opening windows would worsen exposure, including during traffic pollution or wildfire smoke.
- Story 38771185, part 0 provides a quoted NO2 reading through perihelions [[./thread#^c38771871|perihelions]], qualitative consumer-meter observations through Mountain\_Skies [[./thread#^c38772184|Mountain\_Skies]], and VOC observations through koheripbal [[./thread#^c38771589|koheripbal]]. It supplies no PM2.5 or indoor CO2 concentration measurements, no measured effects on cognition or sleep, and no firsthand traffic- or wildfire-smoke exposure account. Those portions of the approved question remain unanswered by this unit.
