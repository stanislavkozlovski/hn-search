# [China's dramatic drop in air pollution may have prevented suicides in the 2010s](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41159163)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

The discussion considers whether China's air-pollution reductions may have helped prevent suicides, alongside accounts of improved air quality and changes in energy and vehicle use. Participants reflect on how policy coordination may enable rapid change, while a later moderation exchange warns against turning country-specific emissions discussion into inflammatory debate.
Tags: China, air pollution, public health, energy transition, climate policy

## Relevance

A commenter provides a firsthand account of changed outdoor air quality and comfort in Shenzhen across visits, while the thread also discusses reported pollution reductions and their possible health connection. ## Arguments

- nabla9 [[./thread#^c41159507|nabla9]] presents declining coal dependence and growing EV adoption as explanations for improving Chinese urban air quality. They claim coal supplied 81\% of electricity in 2007, 75\% in 2013, and roughly 55\% by their August 2024 comment, with EVs accounting for 37\% of new-car sales. These are contextual assertions, not measurements of personal exposure.
- aurareturn [[./thread#^c41159383|aurareturn]] connects widespread EV use with more pleasant roadside walking: no perceived gasoline smell or engine heat. This supports a reported comfort benefit, without establishing pollutant-specific or medical benefits.

## Counterarguments


## Assumptions

- Interpreting aurareturn [[./thread#^c41159383|aurareturn]]'s blue skies and fresh-feeling air as evidence of lower PM2.5 or NO2 assumes those sensory impressions track pollutant concentrations. The account contains no instrument readings.
- Attributing Shenzhen's improvement to the trends cited by nabla9 [[./thread#^c41159507|nabla9]] assumes national electricity and new-car-sales figures explain local exposure changes. Neither those figures nor aurareturn [[./thread#^c41159383|aurareturn]]'s observation that most passing cars were EVs isolates the contribution of particular pollution sources.

## Conditions

- The direct comparison concerns Shenzhen visits in 2017 and late 2023, with pleasant breezes specifically reported during the latter visit by aurareturn [[./thread#^c41159383|aurareturn]]. These are geographically and temporally limited observations; comparable weather, visit duration, and locations are unspecified.
- Replying to that Shenzhen account, xedrac [[./thread#^c41162485|xedrac]] describes late 2019 as still highly polluted. This qualifies the improvement's timeline rather than contradicting the late-2023 observation: xedrac explicitly welcomes the reported cleanup.

## Lived experiences

- aurareturn [[./thread#^c41159383|aurareturn]] explicitly reports visiting Shenzhen in 2017 and again in late 2023, finding a large improvement: blue skies, fresh air, nice breezes, people enjoying the outdoors, and pleasant walking beside roads dominated by EVs. This is firsthand qualitative evidence of perceived outdoor conditions and comfort.
- xedrac [[./thread#^c41162485|xedrac]] says conditions in late 2019 were 'still horrible.' The reply supplies a contrasting earlier assessment, but does not explicitly establish a personal visit, describe symptoms, or provide measurements.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The magnitude and causes of pollutant reductions remain uncertain. aurareturn [[./thread#^c41159383|aurareturn]] supplies sensory observations and nabla9 [[./thread#^c41159507|nabla9]] offers broader explanations, but neither supplies PM2.5 or NO2 concentrations, monitoring methods, or evidence about sensor reliability.
- The headline's suggested suicide benefit is not evaluated in the substantive comments from nabla9 [[./thread#^c41159507|nabla9]], aurareturn [[./thread#^c41159383|aurareturn]], or xedrac [[./thread#^c41162485|xedrac]]. They provide no study analysis or reported health, cognition, or sleep outcomes; perceived comfort cannot establish those effects.
- Indoor exposure, CO2, ventilation, filtration, cooking, wildfire smoke, costs, and window-opening tradeoffs are unaddressed by the substantive accounts from aurareturn [[./thread#^c41159383|aurareturn]], xedrac [[./thread#^c41162485|xedrac]], and nabla9 [[./thread#^c41159507|nabla9]]. These are evidence gaps, not expressed disagreements or a basis for recommendations.
