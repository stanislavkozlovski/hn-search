# [Experiments on a \$50 DIY air purifier (2020)](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=28394752)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion evaluates inexpensive do-it-yourself air purifiers made from box fans and furnace filters, including reported performance during wildfire smoke and ways to improve airflow with thicker or multiple filters. Participants compare homemade setups with commercial purifiers and HVAC filtration on cost, clean-air delivery, noise, energy use, appearance, maintenance, and filtration of particles, odors, and VOCs. Safety concerns include restricted airflow, overheating, and fire risk. Related exchanges cover how to measure indoor air quality, sensor accuracy and setup, ventilation during smoke events, cooking emissions, filter availability, and the tradeoffs between emergency solutions and equipment intended for continuous use.
Tags: DIY air purifier, box fan filters, wildfire smoke, air filtration, MERV filters, HEPA filters, indoor air quality, air quality sensors, fire safety, HVAC, consumer products

## Relevance

The thread reports measured PM2.5 changes from DIY and commercial filters, including wildfire smoke and cooking-related spikes. Participants compare filtration setups, sensor limits, airflow, noise, filter life, and fire risk, and discuss when ventilation helps or brings polluted air indoors. ## Arguments

- Repeated room-air circulation is the central argument for inexpensive filtration: ncmncm [[./thread#^c28400211|ncmncm]] argued that five passes capturing 75\% each can approach 99.9\% cumulative capture. falcolas [[./thread#^c28395304|falcolas]] likewise argued that recirculating air need not receive HEPA-level filtration on every pass. This reasoning supports evaluating airflow and capture together, with a different requirement for air passing through a window intake only once.
- A self-described filter-manufacturer product engineer, BrickandPorter [[./thread#^c28398534|BrickandPorter]], reported a clean air delivery rate, or CADR, around 150 cfm for tested box fans with MERV 13 filters at maximum speed, versus 158 for the company's roughly \$200 purifier. They reported diminishing returns beyond MERV 12–13, including slightly worse results with MERV 14. These are reported tests of particular configurations, supporting a performance-per-dollar argument rather than equivalence between all DIY and commercial units.
- Increasing pleated filter area was proposed as a way to preserve airflow. DeRock [[./thread#^c28396217|DeRock]] explained that a four-inch-deep pleated filter spreads airflow over more material; Buttons840 [[./thread#^c28396447|Buttons840]] clarified that the material itself is not necessarily thicker. DeRock [[./thread#^c28395232|DeRock]] also used an intake-side coarse prefilter to protect the finer filter and keep the fan clean. sokoloff [[./thread#^c28395633|sokoloff]] added the practical limitation that suction alone does not retain a filter when the fan stops.
- Cooking observations support removing pollution at its source. Syonyk [[./thread#^c28395191|Syonyk]] reported markedly higher PM2.5 and PM10 when cooking without the stove vent, including PM2.5 above 100 µg/m³ after browning meat. quercusa [[./thread#^c28396433|quercusa]] reported substantially less smoke, steam, and grease after converting a recirculating microwave hood to outdoor exhaust. The evidence concerns these kitchens and cooking events, not every stove or cooking method.
- Ventilation addresses a need distinct from recirculating particle filtration. s0rce [[./thread#^c28399493|s0rce]] used commercial room purifiers alongside a furnace-filter-and-fan window intake, reporting that keeping the house closed otherwise allowed CO2 to build up and the air to feel stuffy. Their account supports considering fresh-air supply alongside particle removal, although it supplies no CO2 readings.
- Commenters distinguished particle capture from gaseous-pollutant removal. fighterpilot [[./thread#^c28395086|fighterpilot]] corrected the claim that MERV 13 automatically provides HEPA filtration and VOC removal, identifying activated carbon as a separate component. Someone1234 [[./thread#^c28395655|Someone1234]] said this distinction motivated switching from a box-fan filter to a commercial purifier containing carbon. That is a rationale for considering gases separately, not measured evidence of the product's gas-removal performance.
- Household measurements can reveal both sources and intervention effects that appearance alone misses. DeRock [[./thread#^c28395232|DeRock]] placed a sensor across the house from the DIY filter; Syonyk [[./thread#^c28395191|Syonyk]] compared room air, purifier output, and cooking episodes; 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c28396068|5e92cb50239222b]] described several sensors and years of recorded readings. These accounts provide more specific evidence than simply noticing a dirty filter, while leaving calibration questions open.
- Ease of continuous use can matter as much as purchase price. Arainach [[./thread#^c28395327|Arainach]] reported that smaller, quieter commercial units with automatic speed control were substantially easier to leave running around the clock. afandian [[./thread#^c28395755|afandian]] preferred an available IKEA purifier over a DIY project they had postponed for years, despite reviews questioning its power. Both arguments concern practical use rather than demonstrated superiority under matched conditions.
- dynm [[./thread#^c28395876|dynm]] argued that reducing even moderately elevated PM2.5 could be worthwhile, estimating roughly one disability-adjusted life year lost from lifelong exposure to 33 µg/m³ rather than zero. They explicitly characterized this as a highly uncertain order-of-magnitude estimate. dynm [[./thread#^c28396470|dynm]] then made a precautionary argument: particles were unlikely to be beneficial, and using a purifier seemed easier than other potentially beneficial lifestyle changes.

## Counterarguments

- The value of filtering relatively modest pollution was disputed. 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c28395511|5e92cb50239222b]], writing from a much more polluted setting, considered DeRock's starting air relatively clean and said they might not bother. fighterpilot [[./thread#^c28395956|fighterpilot]] questioned whether density and traffic-related stress confounded the health estimate, and dynm [[./thread#^c28396470|dynm]] acknowledged that possibility. stjohnswarts [[./thread#^c28405749|stjohnswarts]] separately questioned excessive purification outside allergy or smoky conditions. These positions do not establish a harmless threshold.
- Noise complicates the claim that purification has only benefits. emn13 [[./thread#^c28404279|emn13]] argued that purifier noise could itself be unhealthy, while explicitly not knowing how its effects compare with particle exposure. Conversely, coding123 [[./thread#^c28395251|coding123]] valued a loud fan for masking a neighbor's barking dog. The discussion therefore contains conflicting comfort preferences, without measurements that quantify the health tradeoff.
- Restrictive filters can create equipment problems in particular DIY configurations. mynameisash [[./thread#^c28395074|mynameisash]] reported a box-fan-and-HEPA setup whose cord connection melted and whose fan intermittently stopped. BrickandPorter [[./thread#^c28398534|BrickandPorter]] attributed overheating risks to restricted motor-cooling airflow. The failure is firsthand; the exact electrical cause and the risk across other fan/filter combinations were not established.
- An airtight seal was not universally considered beneficial. amluto [[./thread#^c28396065|amluto]] argued that substantial bypass around restrictive HEPA filters wastes energy and money. function\_seven [[./thread#^c28396994|function\_seven]] countered that the described experiment still worked and that bypass might preserve motor cooling. BrickandPorter [[./thread#^c28398534|BrickandPorter]] reported little efficiency difference between fully taped filters and loosely attached ones in their tests. These claims concern different arrangements and do not establish a universal sealing rule.
- A proposed improvement was explicitly withdrawn: ncmncm [[./thread#^c28396464|ncmncm]] suggested misting filters to make them stickier, but amluto [[./thread#^c28397318|amluto]] warned that moisture might impair electrostatic filtration, while acknowledging not having tested it. ncmncm [[./thread#^c28397777|ncmncm]] accepted the objection and raised possible permanent degradation. This ancestry makes the misting suggestion a corrected speculation, not an endorsed finding.
- Filter labels and marketing claims do not establish interchangeable capabilities. hourislate [[./thread#^c28395043|hourislate]] described a MERV 13 arrangement as providing HEPA and VOC removal; fighterpilot [[./thread#^c28395086|fighterpilot]] rejected both identifications. When DoingIsLearning [[./thread#^c28401225|DoingIsLearning]] questioned an IKEA filter's advertised performance, kleyu [[./thread#^c28402083|kleyu]] quoted its classification as EPA12 rather than HEPA. The thread itself contains corrections to broad claims about what a filter rating means.
- The choice was not necessarily between an unattractive DIY unit and unhealthy air. chickenpotpie [[./thread#^c28396175|chickenpotpie]] explicitly objected to that framing because commercial alternatives exist. pushswap [[./thread#^c28395408|pushswap]], after using three DIY units for more than a year, found them louder, more power-hungry, and more expensive overall than several commercial alternatives in a relatively clean indoor environment, while still finding them useful in a garage.
- Claims that DIY universally beats commercial purification were challenged by differing reported comparisons. ddlatham [[./thread#^c28395969|ddlatham]] described Wirecutter results favoring its best commercial units; jly [[./thread#^c28397140|jly]] suggested that differing filter specifications could explain the discrepancy. Johnny555 [[./thread#^c28397279|Johnny555]] also corrected cf100clunk's characterization of a television test: the DIY unit reportedly beat some products, including an expensive Dyson, rather than receiving the highest marks overall.

## Assumptions

- AQI values, particulate mass concentrations, and averaging periods must remain distinct. DeRock [[./thread#^c28395232|DeRock]] described approximately 30 µg/m³ as approximately AQI 100; Bertio [[./thread#^c28399428|Bertio]] explicitly reported US PM2.5 AQI above 400; 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c28401115|5e92cb50239222b]] distinguished winter daily averages of 300–500 µg/m³ from much higher evening concentrations. Treating these numbers as the same scale or exposure duration would distort the comparison.
- Reliable relative readings cannot simply be assumed from an inexpensive sensor. bawolff [[./thread#^c28405008|bawolff]] challenged the quoted article's assumption that an unidentified monitor could give useful relative results despite untrusted absolute readings. s0rce [[./thread#^c28398898|s0rce]] questioned PurpleAir/Plantower accuracy at extreme concentrations. Multiple sensors and long records, such as those reported by 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c28396068|5e92cb50239222b]], provide additional observations but do not by themselves establish independent calibration.
- Applying repeated-pass capture calculations to a whole room assumes that contaminated air actually reaches the filter repeatedly and that capture efficiency remains applicable. ncmncm's explanation [[./thread#^c28400053|ncmncm]] does not itself establish room mixing, infiltration, or source strength. BrickandPorter [[./thread#^c28398534|BrickandPorter]] emphasized room volume when estimating CADR, reinforcing that a filter's per-pass efficiency alone is insufficient to predict occupied-room exposure.
- A falling particle reading must be distinguished from removal attributable specifically to the filter. BrickandPorter [[./thread#^c28398534|BrickandPorter]] argued that natural particle decay could contribute substantially at the article's low starting concentrations and offered only rough CADR estimates because room dimensions were uncertain. JoshTko [[./thread#^c28399787|JoshTko]] asked whether fan-driven deposition without filtration might explain some improvement. The supplied discussion does not establish the controls needed to resolve that question.
- Odor reduction, filter discoloration, and perceived freshness are different observations from calibrated pollutant removal or health improvement. justinzollars [[./thread#^c28396465|justinzollars]] inferred effectiveness from no longer smelling smoke while seeking an actual measurement; leoedin [[./thread#^c28396015|leoedin]] inferred capture from a browning filter; SilverRed [[./thread#^c28399526|SilverRed]] explicitly acknowledged other changes between allergy seasons. These reports should retain their respective evidentiary limits.
- Proposals must not be counted as implemented systems. everdrive [[./thread#^c28395369|everdrive]] described an actual timer-controlled fan but proposed sensor-based automation; their reply [[./thread#^c28396418|everdrive]] clarified that they were not currently using a sensor. Similarly, newman314 [[./thread#^c28396576|newman314]] was considering building Corsi–Rosenthal boxes, whereas Wistar [[./thread#^c28399286|Wistar]] reported having built a classroom unit.
- Low noise and low wattage are not automatically evidence of sufficient cleaning at that setting. asdff [[./thread#^c28395395|asdff]] described a quiet \$30 unit and satisfactory bedroom coverage without particle measurements; amanaplanacanal [[./thread#^c28395552|amanaplanacanal]] specifically asked whether its effectiveness had been measured. leoedin [[./thread#^c28396015|leoedin]] reported an approximately 1 W silent IKEA setting, but supplied no corresponding CADR or indoor concentration.

## Conditions

- The most extreme reported chronic pollution occurred in an undisclosed location with thousands of coal-heated houses, winter temperatures around −30 to −40 °C, and very little wind, according to 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c28396022|5e92cb50239222b]]. The commenter declined to identify the location [[./thread#^c28396096|5e92cb50239222b]]. These circumstances differ materially from the California wildfire readings used elsewhere in the discussion; the country should not be inferred.
- DIY feasibility depended on local supply chains. amluto [[./thread#^c28396205|amluto]] and DeRock [[./thread#^c28395700|DeRock]] linked furnace-filter availability to the prevalence of forced-air heating. avtolik [[./thread#^c28401700|avtolik]] reported difficulty finding box fans and rectangular HEPA filters in their European country. Even the Home Depot stocking claim was local: ncmncm [[./thread#^c28396464|ncmncm]] said four-inch filters were unavailable there, while albrewer [[./thread#^c28407615|albrewer]] reported buying one the previous day.
- Wildfire demand could eliminate either commercial or DIY options. Eric\_WVGG [[./thread#^c28395332|Eric\_WVGG]] remembered commercial purifiers selling out while fans and furnace filters remained available. rootusrootus [[./thread#^c28396236|rootusrootus]] described crowds buying DIY components immediately when smoke arrived, and Wistar [[./thread#^c28399619|Wistar]] reported widespread filter shortages in Washington State in September 2020. X6S1x6Okd1st [[./thread#^c28395482|X6S1x6Okd1st]] used DIY as a stopgap while waiting for a commercial purifier.
- Lifecycle cost was contested rather than reducible to a small purchase-price premium. hinkley [[./thread#^c28396868|hinkley]] warned about proprietary replacement filters and discontinued models. sooheon [[./thread#^c28400529|sooheon]], however, reported choosing a used, established-brand purifier with plentiful aftermarket filters. Johnny555 [[./thread#^c28397382|Johnny555]] reported approximately \$100 annual filters for their particular purifier. These are product-specific ownership experiences and contemporaneous prices.
- Electricity could change the financial comparison. mrtnmcc [[./thread#^c28395880|mrtnmcc]] reported approximately 60 W for DIY versus 6 W for a commercial unit and estimated a \$30 annual difference at California rates. Their clarification [[./thread#^c28400709|mrtnmcc]] said the comparison was at roughly the same cleaning rate. The discussion does not give enough operating-hours or tariff detail to generalize that annual figure.
- Housing tenure and existing HVAC equipment shaped available improvements. s0rce [[./thread#^c28399493|s0rce]] wanted HEPA-filtered energy-recovery ventilation but would not install it in an Oakland rental for a few smoky weeks each year. Johnny555 [[./thread#^c28397382|Johnny555]] could upgrade a furnace filter box instead. bialpio [[./thread#^c28397578|bialpio]] described an older house with unchanged wiring and uncertainty about independent blower operation, illustrating that an existing furnace is not automatically an immediately usable whole-house purification solution.
- Ventilation for thermal comfort depends on local outdoor conditions. jcims [[./thread#^c28399951|jcims]] recommended exhausting indoor air through one window to draw cooler evening air through others, but supplied no pollutant measurements for that practice. In Colorado, stevenhubertron [[./thread#^c28405453|stevenhubertron]] reported that Coway units restored satisfactory indoor air after running a swamp cooler in the morning or evening. These accounts expose a cooling-versus-air-quality tradeoff without quantifying it.
- Monitoring had its own cost and skill requirements. thamer [[./thread#^c28400024|thamer]] reported a \$40 PMS5003, a \$10 Raspberry Pi Zero W, free-tier cloud logging, and a separate CO2 station. DeRock [[./thread#^c28395636|DeRock]] reported approximately 20 minutes to assemble their monitoring setup, while jve [[./thread#^c28396723|jve]] described roughly a working day for a broader setup that also included external access. Neither duration is a universal installation estimate.
- Emergency pricing was discussed as an access issue. ncmncm [[./thread#^c28396464|ncmncm]] praised stable Home Depot prices during the Caldor fire; Johnny555 [[./thread#^c28397095|Johnny555]] replied that disaster price-gouging laws constrained the retailer. Their subsequent exchange concerned legal coverage of fans and filters, including Johnny555 [[./thread#^c28397934|Johnny555]]. It supplies context about affordability and availability, not evidence resolving the wider economic debate about price controls.

## Lived experiences

- DeRock [[./thread#^c28395232|DeRock]] reported using a 20-inch box fan, a 20×20×4-inch MERV 12 filter, and a coarse one-inch intake prefilter, with the sensor across a 900-square-foot house. The supplied text identifies a peak near 30 µg/m³ and links a trace after activation, but does not reproduce a numerical endpoint. DeRock [[./thread#^c28395381|DeRock]] identified the sensor as an HM3301 connected through ESPHome/Home Assistant, and separately reported headaches beginning around AQI 100 [[./thread#^c28395700|DeRock]].
- Syonyk [[./thread#^c28395191|Syonyk]] reported testing a four-filter box, recalled as approximately MERV 13, with Temtop meters. At room PM2.5 around 5 µg/m³, purifier output measured below 1 µg/m³, and room concentrations reportedly declined quickly. Their cooking observations included PM2.5 spikes above 100 µg/m³ after browning meat without adequate venting—worse than the outdoor smoke they were comparing against, not a universal wildfire maximum.
- 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c28401115|5e92cb50239222b]] reported winter daily averages around 300–500 µg/m³ depending on location, evening readings above 1,000, and some above 1,500. They said official state monitoring supported the measurements and clarified that the highest values were not continuous. The export supplies their account, not the underlying official records or a calibration report.
- In a roughly 25 m² room, 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c28395743|5e92cb50239222b]] reported that a Xiaomi purifier could maintain indoor concentrations around 20 µg/m³ while outdoor concentrations exceeded 800, but only with intolerable noise requiring earplugs and making sleep difficult. After admitting heavily polluted outdoor air, closing the window, and running maximum power, they reported approximately 20–25 minutes to clean the room. They used multiple PMS5003 sensors.
- Bertio [[./thread#^c28399428|Bertio]] reported doubling up DIY fans using Filtrete 1900 filters in a roughly 300-square-foot Canadian home office during conditions above US PM2.5 AQI 400. Their Dyson handled light or moderate smoke but did not keep up with heavy smoke. Bertio [[./thread#^c28399466|Bertio]] described roughly six weeks with AQI above 300 almost every day. No post-filtration office concentration was supplied.
- Johnny555 [[./thread#^c28397382|Johnny555]] reported a commercial Whirlpool purifier reducing bedroom AQI from approximately 150 to below 10 in about 15 minutes on high, with low speed quiet enough for sleep. After installing an AprilAire furnace filter box with MERV 13 filtration, they reported the whole house remaining below AQI 10 with outdoor AQI up to 250. The meter, AQI calculation, and concentrations in µg/m³ were not specified.
- Some wildfire improvements were sensory rather than instrumented. ncmncm [[./thread#^c28396464|ncmncm]] said a single one-inch '\#9' filter rapidly cleared smoke from their whole house during the Caldor fire. kyleblarson [[./thread#^c28395486|kyleblarson]] reported no hint of indoor smoke after an HVAC filtration upgrade during two weeks of outdoor AQI above 500. Neither account supplies an indoor particulate measurement.
- SilverRed [[./thread#^c28398826|SilverRed]] associated getting a Dyson purifier with substantially less hay fever, including easier short periods outdoors. Their follow-up [[./thread#^c28399526|SilverRed]] explained that pollen entered through windows and explicitly acknowledged that several other factors had changed since the previous season. This is a cautiously presented symptom improvement, not a demonstrated causal effect of filtration.
- Settled-dust experiences conflicted sharply. malfist [[./thread#^c28395973|malfist]] reported dusting falling from weekly to approximately every other month and described a washable prefilter that became very dirty [[./thread#^c28396117|malfist]]. By contrast, 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c28396068|5e92cb50239222b]] reported little dust benefit despite sensor readings near zero in summer and 20–40 µg/m³ in winter. s0rce [[./thread#^c28399474|s0rce]] still had substantial dust with two Coway units in an approximately 800-square-foot apartment, while also having a dog and frequently opening windows outside smoke periods.
- oblib [[./thread#^c28396211|oblib]] reported running box-fan filters continuously in a rural home office for more than 15 years, buying two fans during that period, and noticing differences when filtration stopped or filters were overdue for replacement. The setting included a gravel road, forest pollen, and many cats. oblib [[./thread#^c28525855|oblib]] clarified that the filters were disposable paper, not washable; visible discoloration after a week did not establish a weekly replacement schedule.
- Noise comparisons varied with products and speeds. duffyt [[./thread#^c28396691|duffyt]] switched from DIY units to Blue Air and Levoit purifiers because the latter were subjectively quieter, without sound measurements. falcolas [[./thread#^c28395325|falcolas]] found a box-fan filter and small Honeywell similarly loud on low, but the box fan louder and moving much more air on high. These reports support considering the operating setting rather than treating either category as uniformly quiet.
- Individual modifications produced narrower observations than a general design verdict. sixothree [[./thread#^c28397783|sixothree]] could run a fan slower after changing from a one-inch to a four-inch filter, but said the filters differed and supplied no measurements. dylan604 [[./thread#^c28398705|dylan604]] liked a Lasko filter-slot fan with a '\#10' filter for baking soda and mica particles, without wildfire testing. snivelus [[./thread#^c28425764|snivelus]] moved a filter from exhaust to intake after unpleasant startup smells, hypothesizing—but not measuring—dust re-release.
- DIY adoption was real, although construction alone does not establish exposure reduction. Wistar [[./thread#^c28399286|Wistar]] built a four-filter cube with a cardboard exhaust shroud for a spouse's classroom. exhilaration [[./thread#^c28395147|exhilaration]] used a box-fan-and-furnace-filter arrangement to control dust in an unfinished-basement gym. Neither comment provides concentration changes or health outcomes.
- Outdoor personal-protection comfort appeared in lobocinza's cycling account [[./thread#^c28404128|lobocinza]]: an N99 required more breathing pressure, but heat and humid air felt more troublesome than breathing effort, especially during hard riding; valved masks felt better to them. This is an individual comfort report, without ambient exposure measurements or a controlled comparison of protection.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Extreme PM2.5 measurements remained contested. DeRock [[./thread#^c28395947|DeRock]] categorically rejected concentrations above 1,000 µg/m³ and cited a California PurpleAir reading of 368 [[./thread#^c28396420|DeRock]]. 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c28401115|5e92cb50239222b]] responded with recurrent coal-heating peaks and claimed official corroboration; s0rce [[./thread#^c28398898|s0rce]] questioned optical-sensor accuracy at extremes. A California observation cannot establish a worldwide maximum, but the supplied material also cannot independently validate the disputed local readings.
- Whether to exhaust cooking air during wildfire smoke was unresolved. Johnny555 [[./thread#^c28397225|Johnny555]] warned that outdoor exhaust pulls replacement outdoor air into the house. codemac [[./thread#^c28399218|codemac]] replied that cooking pollution could be worse and buildings already leak. s0rce [[./thread#^c28399493|s0rce]] found filtered window intake useful except on the worst smoky days, described as AQI above 200 with strong smoke odor. No paired indoor/outdoor experiment establishes a general switching threshold.
- The optimal geometry and airflow relationship were not settled. DeRock [[./thread#^c28396217|DeRock]] argued that more pleated area reduces resistance, while aronpye [[./thread#^c28399573|aronpye]] argued that increased surface area increases frictional losses. BrickandPorter [[./thread#^c28398534|BrickandPorter]] reported different fan-speed reductions for intake and exhaust placement. The replies do not reconcile the quantities or geometries being compared, and repeated requests for quantitative airflow-versus-area measurements remained incompletely answered.
- Filter lifetime and the economics of multi-filter cubes remained disputed. BrickandPorter [[./thread#^c28398534|BrickandPorter]] claimed five filters cost five times as much, improve performance about 50\%, and last about as long as one. KVFinn [[./thread#^c28441269|KVFinn]] challenged the lifetime claim because loading should be distributed. joveian [[./thread#^c28410443|joveian]], explicitly relying on imperfect recollection, added that electrostatic and other media may age differently and that smoke odor can force replacement before particle-loading limits.
- Performance for smaller particles and gases was substantially less documented than PM2.5 response. mholm [[./thread#^c28395102|mholm]] requested smaller-particle comparisons, and kujaomega [[./thread#^c28406699|kujaomega]] requested PM1.0, TVOC, and formaldehyde tests. For DIY carbon, malfist [[./thread#^c28396081|malfist]] questioned whether aquarium media was appropriate; sparker72678 [[./thread#^c28398324|sparker72678]] acknowledged not having calculated or tested the proposed arrangement. No supplied gas-concentration results resolve these questions.
- This unit does not establish effects of NO2 or CO2 on cognition or sleep. thamer [[./thread#^c28400024|thamer]] described owning an SCD-41 CO2 station but gave no concentrations; s0rce [[./thread#^c28399493|s0rce]] described CO2 buildup and stuffiness qualitatively. Syonyk's cooking measurements [[./thread#^c28395191|Syonyk]] concern particles and do not identify fuel or measure NO2. fighterpilot [[./thread#^c28395956|fighterpilot]] raised traffic-related stress as a possible confound, not measured traffic exposure. Sleep evidence here chiefly concerns purifier noise, as in 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c28395743|5e92cb50239222b]].
- The magnitude and causality of health benefits remain uncertain. dynm [[./thread#^c28395876|dynm]] offered an explicitly rough lifetime-risk model, DeRock [[./thread#^c28395700|DeRock]] reported headaches at a personal AQI threshold, and SilverRed [[./thread#^c28399526|SilverRed]] acknowledged confounding in an allergy improvement. These are different evidence types, with no controlled health intervention, cognitive testing, or sleep tracking supplied. emn13's noise-versus-pollution health tradeoff [[./thread#^c28404279|emn13]] was likewise left unquantified.
- Why filtration substantially reduced settled dust for some households but not others remained open. malfist [[./thread#^c28396117|malfist]] suggested prefilter design, airflow, and placement; 5e92cb50239222b [[./thread#^c28396068|5e92cb50239222b]] suggested coarse particles may settle before reaching the purifier; s0rce [[./thread#^c28399474|s0rce]] identified pets and open windows as additional complications. The accounts cannot isolate these factors or turn a clean PM2.5 reading into a prediction about dusting frequency.
- Long-term safety and filter emissions were not established across products. mynameisash [[./thread#^c28395074|mynameisash]] documented a melting connection in one DIY setup, while oblib [[./thread#^c28396211|oblib]] described years of continuous use. staunch [[./thread#^c28395075|staunch]] raised off-gassing and failure reports from product reviews; vkou [[./thread#^c28397429|vkou]] attributed many negative reviews they inspected to users leaving filters shrink-wrapped. Those review interpretations do not resolve the firsthand failure or provide comparable failure rates.
- The adjacent infectious-aerosol discussion did not establish household protection or a universal ranking of masks and room filters. bluGill [[./thread#^c28407748|bluGill]] argued that reducing inhaled viral dose should improve the odds, while stjohnswarts [[./thread#^c28405824|stjohnswarts]] doubted filtration could overcome close household exposure. OJFord [[./thread#^c28397928|OJFord]] compared poorly fitting cloth masks with repeated room filtration, whereas JohnBooty [[./thread#^c28399546|JohnBooty]] emphasized protection near another person before air reaches a room filter. No pathogen measurements or intervention outcomes in this unit settle those context-dependent comparisons.
