# [Ask HN: Which air quality monitor to buy?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=34867872)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants recommend and compare indoor air quality monitors and air purifiers around the stated budget. They discuss AirGradient, Airthings, Awair, and Mila; sensor reliability, seasonal variation, radon, and device integrations; and whether a purifier with built-in monitoring can meet the need. The original poster prefers a ready-made device and considers the risk of a vendor failing.
Tags: air quality monitor, product recommendations, indoor air, Home Assistant, air purifier

## Relevance

The thread compares household air monitors, mentions using measurements to control fans, notes changes in readings over seasons and rooms, and distinguishes particulate monitoring from CO2 measurement. It also raises concerns about low-cost sensor reliability. ## Arguments

- Combining monitoring and treatment may suit this buyer’s budget: gfd recommends a purifier with built-in monitoring [[./thread#^c34869926|gfd]], while mrks\_ recommends Mila’s automatic adjustment to air quality [[./thread#^c34871160|mrks\_]]. Neither supplies comparative performance measurements.
- Room comparisons need temporal context. Drawing on Airthings use, retino cautions that seasons, heating, and outdoor temperature change results, recommends multiple monitors for comparing rooms, and notes that interventions may take time to show effects [[./thread#^c34868294|retino]].
- Monitoring might reveal otherwise unrecognized exposure: mittermayr recounts a story in which an Awair reportedly revealed substantial VOC exposure associated with health problems in DHH’s family. This is a secondhand account recalled uncertainly, without exposure readings, symptoms, or evidence establishing causation in the supplied discussion [[./thread#^c34867908|mittermayr]].

## Counterarguments

- A purifier’s monitor may cover only part of the exposure question. gfd says these devices usually detect particulate matter and that purifiers do not really help with CO2; the recommendation therefore does not establish adequate CO2 monitoring or treatment, or any NO2 capability [[./thread#^c34869926|gfd]].
- DIY suitability depends on the buyer’s constraints. runjake’s favorable AirGradient account includes a bumpy setup process [[./thread#^c34869758|runjake]], whereas thisisjason explicitly excludes DIY [[./thread#^c34868001|thisisjason]]. Satisfaction with an assembled device does not remove that usability objection.

## Assumptions

- Price is treated as a proxy for reliability: mittermayr claims cheaper devices use inexpensive sensors to estimate some measurements [[./thread#^c34867908|mittermayr]], and thisisjason explicitly calls lower reliability an assumption [[./thread#^c34868001|thisisjason]]. Neither provides sensor specifications, calibration results, or comparisons establishing that relationship.
- Monitoring and automation are implicitly expected to improve exposure, but the reported implementations do not establish the outcome: mattivc describes fan control [[./thread#^c34869865|mattivc]], and mrks\_ describes an automatically adjusting purifier [[./thread#^c34871160|mrks\_]], without before-and-after pollutant readings or health results.

## Conditions

- The purchasing discussion centers on roughly £300, with some flexibility. gfd and mrks\_ propose combined purifier-monitor options in that budget context [[./thread#^c34869926|gfd]] [[./thread#^c34871160|mrks\_]]. mittermayr mentions a recent \$100 Awair coupon, without confirming continuing availability [[./thread#^c34867908|mittermayr]]. The currencies do not establish the commenters’ locations or outdoor exposure conditions.
- Vendor continuity is a financial consideration. mittermayr tentatively reports trouble associated with an Awair crypto investment and explicitly says the current situation is unclear [[./thread#^c34867908|mittermayr]]. In reply, thisisjason plans to investigate before buying [[./thread#^c34868001|thisisjason]]; this expresses concern, not independent confirmation of impending company failure.

## Lived experiences

- runjake reports easy AirGradient assembly, difficult initial setup, reliable subsequent operation, and identification of unspecified household issues; they would immediately replace it if it failed. No pollutant readings or corrective outcomes are supplied [[./thread#^c34869758|runjake]].
- retino used Airthings for similar household investigation and identifies radon as the issue for which changes took time to become apparent. No concentrations, intervention details, or elapsed times are given [[./thread#^c34868294|retino]].
- mrks\_ owns three Mila purifiers and strongly recommends them, but reports no measured filtration performance or personal health effect [[./thread#^c34871160|mrks\_]].
- mattivc owns two Airthings View Plus monitors, is happy with them, and uses Home Assistant to switch fans according to air quality. The monitored pollutants, thresholds, fan arrangements, and resulting exposure changes are unspecified [[./thread#^c34869865|mattivc]].
- mittermayr reports loving the Awair and preferring its appearance. This firsthand satisfaction is separate from their secondhand VOC-health story and does not validate measurement accuracy [[./thread#^c34867908|mittermayr]].

## Unresolved disagreements

- No objective comparison resolves which monitor is most accurate or suitable. runjake’s AirGradient endorsement [[./thread#^c34869758|runjake]], mattivc’s Airthings endorsement [[./thread#^c34869865|mattivc]], and mittermayr’s Awair endorsement [[./thread#^c34867908|mittermayr]] provide satisfaction reports without numerical readings or reference-instrument checks. There are no supplied PM2.5 or NO2 measurements, and gfd’s CO2 caveat is qualitative [[./thread#^c34869926|gfd]].
- Health effects remain unestablished in this source unit: mittermayr supplies only the secondhand VOC account, with no firsthand evidence about cognition, sleep, or comfort [[./thread#^c34867908|mittermayr]]. The favorable ownership reports do not settle those questions.
- The discussion cannot resolve when opening windows helps or worsens exposure. retino mentions outdoor temperature as a measurement influence [[./thread#^c34868294|retino]], and mattivc reports fan automation without specifying whether it brings in outdoor air [[./thread#^c34869865|mattivc]]. Neither provides indoor–outdoor comparisons or experiences involving cooking, traffic, wildfire smoke, or window opening.
