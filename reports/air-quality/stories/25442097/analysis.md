# [Experiments on a \$50 DIY air purifier that takes 30s to assemble](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=25442097)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion evaluates inexpensive DIY air purifiers made from fans and filters, including how well they reduce particles, how to test them, and how filter resistance, surface area, sealing, airflow guides, and fan type affect performance. People compare DIY builds with commercial purifiers on cost, noise, appearance, power use, and filter availability, and share adaptations for wildfire smoke, woodworking dust, and pet odors. Related questions cover particle and CO₂ sensors, calibration, ventilation, HVAC compatibility, filter replacement, and whether particulate filters can address smells, gases, or infectious aerosols.
Tags: DIY air purifiers, Corsi-Rosenthal boxes, air filtration, filter performance, fans and airflow, air-quality sensors, wildfire smoke, HVAC, CO₂ and ventilation, product costs

## Relevance

The discussion directly compares DIY and commercial particle filtration, including firsthand PM measurements and wildfire-smoke results, airflow and noise tradeoffs, and filter choices. It also covers indoor CO2 accumulation, sensor calibration and accuracy, ventilation, and attempts to remove NO2 and odors. ## Arguments

- The positive case for simple fan-and-filter purifiers is that several participants report useful particle reduction with inexpensive equipment. hundchenkatze [[./thread#^c25458131|hundchenkatze]] quotes the article as claiming faster removal of measurable particles than a commercial purifier costing twice as much. Scene\_Cast2 [[./thread#^c25456786|Scene\_Cast2]] supplies a separate firsthand comparison using two monitors during California fires. These support investigating particular DIY configurations; the supplied comments do not establish that every DIY purifier outperforms commercial products.
- Filter efficiency must be considered together with airflow. nkurz [[./thread#^c25445942|nkurz]] argues that their product determines useful cleaning and explains that deeper pleats increase filter surface area. rootusrootus [[./thread#^c25463071|rootusrootus]] distinguishes thicker pleated filters or filters arranged in parallel from stacking filters in series, which adds resistance. These arguments favor matching filter area and resistance to the fan rather than selecting solely by HEPA or MERV labels.
- Particle filtration and fresh-air ventilation address different problems. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c25453948|ahaucnx]] says rooms with purifiers can still have insufficient ventilation and CO2 often above 1,500 ppm, associates elevated CO2 with headaches and reduced cognitive performance, and reports unfavorable classroom measurements. The comment provides neither the classroom readings nor cognitive or symptom measurements, but clearly argues for monitoring CO2 alongside PM2.5.
- The odor discussion distinguishes particles from gases. rckoepke [[./thread#^c25450849|rckoepke]] explains that HEPA can capture particles smaller than its commonly discussed 0.3 µm test size while many odors are gases or vapors requiring different treatment. cyounkins [[./thread#^c25451763|cyounkins]] accepts those filtration distinctions while maintaining a broader physics usage of the word particles. Activated carbon is proposed for odors, while ddeck [[./thread#^c25454094|ddeck]] identifies permanganate-impregnated activated alumina as a treatment for NO2 and other acidic gases.
- Operating schedules and replaceable coarse filters can matter alongside maximum filtration ratings. EricE [[./thread#^c25460631|EricE]] argues that continuous operation on low speed with cut-to-fit furnace filters can outperform intermittent operation with original cartridges, although no measurements accompany that comparison. EricE [[./thread#^c25460675|EricE]] also suggests quilt batting as a prefilter, while Rebelgecko [[./thread#^c25458454|Rebelgecko]] describes a separate coarse prefilter in their commercial unit and expects it to extend the other filters' lives.
- Small virus size alone is not presented as a reason HEPA cannot help with airborne exposure. In reply to jansan's concern about approximately 130 nm virions [[./thread#^c25454019|jansan]], mkskm [[./thread#^c25454485|mkskm]] points to larger respiratory aerosols, and ddeck [[./thread#^c25454185|ddeck]] discusses filtration efficiency around the most penetrating particle size. ddeck also stresses that a room purifier processes only air passing through it and needs time to circulate room air. These are technical explanations and linked claims, not reported infection-outcome studies.

## Counterarguments

- Several configurations delivered little useful airflow. Drawing on clean-room equipment experience, chopin [[./thread#^c25453911|chopin]] questions whether an axial fan can overcome HEPA resistance. yborg [[./thread#^c25458578|yborg]] reports near-zero through-filter airflow and front-side recirculation with a cheap box fan and MERV 12 filter. neilv [[./thread#^c25457776|neilv]] describes similar backflow with an outlet-mounted filter. These accounts directly limit broad claims that attaching almost any filter to almost any fan will work well.
- mleonhard's assertion that repeated circulation makes MERV 8 just as good as HEPA [[./thread#^c25454778|mleonhard]] exceeds the evidence presented with it: their supporting example is perceived wildfire-smoke clearance without reported instrument readings. Scene\_Cast2 [[./thread#^c25456786|Scene\_Cast2]] instead observed different steady-state PM2.5 results among filter configurations. The discussion supports evaluating the complete system, but does not demonstrate equivalence between filter grades.
- DIY is not necessarily cheaper when the comparison includes sensors, controls, and operating costs. Rebelgecko [[./thread#^c25452892|Rebelgecko]] prices the author's two-filter, fan, and sensor combination at \$150 versus \$153 for their commercial purifier with HEPA, charcoal, and an integrated sensor, and estimates approximately \$50 annually in electricity savings from automatic operation. thomas [[./thread#^c25455948|thomas]] adds claims about commercial noise, testing, and energy certification. These are participant comparisons, not a standardized ownership-cost analysis.
- Automatic operation is only as useful as the pollutant the control sensor detects. Replying to Rebelgecko's integrated-sensor argument, riverdroid [[./thread#^c25460000|riverdroid]] claims many purifier sensors respond to VOCs rather than particles and therefore may react to odors without responding appropriately to dust or smoke. The comment does not establish which sensor Rebelgecko's Coway uses, so it is a limitation to investigate rather than a demonstrated defect in that particular purifier.
- The experimental evidence attracts methodological objections. Rebelgecko [[./thread#^c25452892|Rebelgecko]] asks for repeated trials because results are noisy and smoke generation is hard to reproduce; leafmeal [[./thread#^c25459786|leafmeal]] requests a fan-only control. causality0 [[./thread#^c25456251|causality0]] interprets an initial particle rise as dust disturbed by the fans, but nkurz [[./thread#^c25457703|nkurz]] replies that incense was deliberately burned at the start. That ancestry matters: the resuspension explanation is challenged, while the usefulness of additional controls remains.
- The claim that effective NO2 removal requires industrial equipment is disputed but not experimentally resolved. xiphias2 [[./thread#^c25453883|xiphias2]] says extensive trials of commercial purifiers with large carbon filters failed. ddeck [[./thread#^c25454094|ddeck]] responds that residential units with specialized gas media exist. xiphias2 [[./thread#^c25454757|xiphias2]] then clarifies that IQAir and multiple filters, possibly including permanganate media, still did not significantly lower room NO2. Product availability and successful reduction in this person's room are different claims.

## Assumptions

- Automatic CO2 baseline calibration assumes periodic exposure to genuinely fresh air. throwaway9d0291 [[./thread#^c25454616|throwaway9d0291]] says the SenseAir S8 expects that exposure at least once every eight days and can otherwise mistake an indoor level around 500 ppm for its fresh-air baseline. Their expectation that unoccupied commercial buildings receive fresh-air purges is a contextual assumption, not evidence about every building. Closed winter homes with people or pets may violate the calibration requirement.
- Low price and a manufacturer's accuracy claim do not establish measurement validity. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c25453948|ahaucnx]] describes extensive testing and high accuracy, but supplies no calibration results in the comment. jsingleton [[./thread#^c25454392|jsingleton]] reports disagreement among particle-monitoring devices. batushka3 [[./thread#^c25457898|batushka3]] supplies detector readings without a device model or calibration information; especially, an alcohol spray registering as formaldehyde does not independently establish that the reported substance and concentration were correctly identified.
- Visible dust, filter discoloration, smell, and laser visibility are qualitative indicators rather than calibrated PM2.5 measurements. dbsmith83 [[./thread#^c25457427|dbsmith83]] and heavyset\_go [[./thread#^c25454475|heavyset\_go]] observe darkening filters, while chopin [[./thread#^c25453911|chopin]] uses laser-beam visibility as a check. rootusrootus [[./thread#^c25457538|rootusrootus]] notes that laser color affects visibility, complicating that proxy. WalterBright's childhood smoke-through-water experiment [[./thread#^c25453872|WalterBright]] demonstrates visible capture of material, but supplies no room-cleaning rate or exposure measurement.
- Cleaner-looking air is sometimes assumed to mean demonstrated health improvement. spthorn60 [[./thread#^c25455483|spthorn60]] feels sure their lungs are cleaner after improving workshop dust control, but reports no medical assessment. kylebenzle [[./thread#^c25453862|kylebenzle]] makes the much stronger claim that adding a purifier is the single most important health intervention amid rising COVID rates, without presenting comparative evidence. Neither statement establishes a measured health benefit or its magnitude.
- Universal filter-life and prefilter requirements are unsupported. textech [[./thread#^c25456161|textech]] asserts that HEPA becomes useless within weeks without an activated-carbon outer filter, yet Rebelgecko [[./thread#^c25458454|Rebelgecko]] describes a separate coarse prefilter and EricE [[./thread#^c25460675|EricE]] proposes quilt batting. These comments do not establish that carbon is necessary for collecting large particles or provide comparable loading tests from which to infer a fixed replacement interval.
- Maintenance neglect should not be inferred merely from the presence of central heating. dsr\_ [[./thread#^c25454824|dsr\_]] guesses that NietTim's mother's filter has gone unchanged for years. NietTim [[./thread#^c25458412|NietTim]] replies that the system issues cleaning reminders, that they perform the cleaning, and that replacement apparently occurs through a service arrangement. The reply narrows an unsupported assumption about this particular household.

## Conditions

- The DIY sensor price depends on what is included and who performs assembly. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c25453948|ahaucnx]] advertises a build below \$20, while cinntaile [[./thread#^c25454167|cinntaile]] says the bill of materials already exceeds \$40 and suggests approximately \$80 assembled. azmodeus [[./thread#^c25457435|azmodeus]] remains willing to pay \$80. headmelted [[./thread#^c25461528|headmelted]] emphasizes equipment purchases and assembly time, and dmitriid [[./thread#^c25454285|dmitriid]] points to low-volume production costs. The conflicting component totals are not reconciled.
- Access to an inexpensive design does not necessarily mean access to a convenient finished monitor. shared\_frog [[./thread#^c25454382|shared\_frog]] objects to unclear prices and the absence of a PCB purchase button; Semaphor [[./thread#^c25454468|Semaphor]] says users can submit the downloadable Gerber files to a fabricator. gh02t [[./thread#^c25455775|gh02t]] reports repeated positive experience ordering five boards for \$2 plus shipping. Speculation that the business serves only enterprise customers is challenged by input\_sh [[./thread#^c25456428|input\_sh]], who notes its explicit targeting of homes.
- US sensor sourcing problems were supplier-specific in this exchange. JJMalina [[./thread#^c25458240|JJMalina]] finds that the linked PMS5003 listing will not ship to the US, but phaedrus441 [[./thread#^c25459471|phaedrus441]] reports ordering from another listing for \$15.81 including shipping. jeremywho [[./thread#^c25458736|jeremywho]] finds an alternative from Adafruit at roughly four times the AliExpress price. Those are contemporaneous purchasing reports, not current availability or price guarantees.
- The cheap-parts proposition varies geographically. jsingleton [[./thread#^c25454413|jsingleton]] finds inexpensive HEPA filters difficult to obtain in the UK; abyssin [[./thread#^c25456443|abyssin]] cannot match the advertised build cost in Belgium; boredpudding [[./thread#^c25456685|boredpudding]] reports limited suitable filter choices in the Netherlands. Conversely, infofarmer [[./thread#^c25454333|infofarmer]] reports a Smart Air DIY model at \$35 shipped within mainland China and says they own and like the company's filters, while missing competitors' smart features.
- Emergency availability can outweigh compactness or engineering refinement. UncleOxidant [[./thread#^c25457033|UncleOxidant]] describes people in the Pacific Northwest urgently using familiar box fans during wildfire smoke rather than waiting for automotive blowers and constructing enclosures. bluGill [[./thread#^c25460443|bluGill]] notes that an automotive blower would also need a 12 V supply. In Thailand, lurkertech [[./thread#^c25460866|lurkertech]] reports adapting round desk fans because box fans were unavailable, with an air-guide design that subjectively improved performance.
- Consumables and procurement affect sustained protection. rootusrootus [[./thread#^c25457701|rootusrootus]] reports local filters selling out within hours of wildfire smoke arriving and says frequent fresh replacements helped. peteretep [[./thread#^c25463152|peteretep]] recalls planning approximately three-month replacements for a different purifier running continuously. These are different conditions, not interchangeable schedules. neilv [[./thread#^c25457776|neilv]] additionally worries about counterfeit replacement cartridges and lacks a way to verify their effectiveness; actual counterfeit purchases are not established.
- Noise, space, and appearance can justify paying more or choosing a different configuration. Mister\_Snuggles [[./thread#^c25457183|Mister\_Snuggles]] finds larger filters easier to buy locally but lacks room for a two-filter arrangement. nkurz [[./thread#^c25445942|nkurz]] tolerates low-speed DIY noise from another room and reserves higher speeds for when away. totalZero [[./thread#^c25453879|totalZero]] values an unobtrusive appearance, while chopin [[./thread#^c25453940|chopin]] notes that radial fans capable of higher pressure can be larger at the same throughput.

## Lived experiences

- Scene\_Cast2 [[./thread#^c25456786|Scene\_Cast2]] reports testing filter configurations in a medium-sized apartment using an Awair and a Plume Flow v2, assessing PM2.5 removal speed and the level eventually reached. A Nordic Pure MERV 15 filter with carbon performed worst because of restricted airflow; a thin Filtrete MERV 14 worked acceptably; a thicker Nordic Pure worked well; and a five-inch Lennox MERV 16 produced the lowest steady-state readings during California fires. No numerical PM2.5 results, repeated-trial statistics, or calibration comparisons are supplied.
- Over two years of detector use, batushka3 [[./thread#^c25457898|batushka3]] reports frying with a little smoke and aerosol sprays sending PM2.5/PM10 readings into the hundreds, without specifying the PM units. Paraffin candles reportedly produced formaldehyde readings above 1 mg/m³, and alcohol-based surface sanitizer also registered high formaldehyde. They observed readings rise in another room within minutes of spraying, without fans. These are firsthand instrument observations; the chemical identification and absolute accuracy remain unverified.
- throwaway9d0291 [[./thread#^c25454616|throwaway9d0291]] says they personally measured misleading automatic CO2 baseline behavior in residential conditions. In response to cleorama's request for a suitable home monitor [[./thread#^c25462040|cleorama]], they later describe using an SCD30 with an ESP32 and ESPHome and recommend the ability to disable automatic calibration [[./thread#^c25493669|throwaway9d0291]]. Separately, jsingleton [[./thread#^c25454392|jsingleton]] reports using a PMS5003 with a Raspberry Pi and SDS011-based builds: useful-looking relative responses, but readings that disagree with a Plume Flow 2 and no established absolute calibration.
- mleonhard [[./thread#^c25454778|mleonhard]] describes using a 12-by-12-inch MERV 8 filter taped to a small fan during wildfire smoke. They periodically opened windows to replace stuffy indoor air with air they explicitly recognized as smoky, then reported that the filter removed the indoor smoke in about 30 minutes after closing the windows. There are no PM2.5 or CO2 readings, so this is a perceived clearance and comfort account, not a quantified demonstration of safe exposure.
- konjin [[./thread#^c25454781|konjin]] reports that a roughly 50 cm fan with MERV 14 filters made a 10-by-8-metre room pleasant within ten minutes during forest fires. Their proposed improvement was a lower-rated intake filter to catch larger particles stirred up by the fan. igor47 [[./thread#^c25458194|igor47]] also reports using several homemade units through a fire season, while dbsmith83 [[./thread#^c25457427|dbsmith83]] observed a wildfire-use filter become grey/yellow within days. None of these comments gives numerical exposure reductions.
- The failed-build accounts differ in construction and observed failure. yborg [[./thread#^c25458578|yborg]] saw airflow recirculate around a \$12 fan rather than pass through its intake filter; neilv [[./thread#^c25457776|neilv]] encountered backflow with an outlet-mounted filter and subsequently preferred two quieter Levoit units. acvny [[./thread#^c25457692|acvny]] found an apparently clean filter after months while dust accumulated on the fan. yoyman [[./thread#^c25455593|yoyman]] reports a small fan dying within a week with restrictive HEPA media, without establishing why it failed.
- Other successful-looking builds still lacked quantitative exposure checks. nkurz [[./thread#^c25445942|nkurz]] built sealed units with deep MERV 15 filters and a front cardboard cowling but explicitly states that no air-quality measurements were taken. bluGill [[./thread#^c25460380|bluGill]] reports that an unsealed four-inch filter quickly became black. JohnJamesRambo [[./thread#^c25454598|JohnJamesRambo]] uses a furnace-filter-and-fan arrangement in a grow house. These support practical feasibility in particular settings, not a common measured performance level.
- Mister\_Snuggles [[./thread#^c25455580|Mister\_Snuggles]] has used a box fan and inexpensive furnace filter for years to control relatively large cat-litter dust. To manage noise, they run it for ten minutes after litter-box use, triggered by a motion sensor, and describe compromises involving cat acceptance, litter availability, cost, dust, and filter life. In reply, batter [[./thread#^c25456001|batter]] reports that a Vornado with two MERV 11 filters arranged as a triangle is quiet on low speed and cheaper to run than their commercial cleaner. DIY noise therefore varies with the build and operating speed.
- spthorn60 [[./thread#^c25449180|spthorn60]] reports apparent workshop-air clearance within 10–15 minutes after sawing or sanding, including MDF. When StavrosK [[./thread#^c25455182|StavrosK]] asks whether sawdust would settle anyway, spthorn60 [[./thread#^c25455483|spthorn60]] distinguishes rapidly settling heavy dust from finer material and explains that they also use a cyclone extractor and a mask. The perceived improvement cannot isolate the fan filter's contribution, quantify fine-particle exposure, or demonstrate a medical outcome.
- ocdtrekkie [[./thread#^c25456861|ocdtrekkie]] reports over a year of perceived ferret-odor improvement with a 20-by-20-by-1-inch filter attached to a box fan. The outlet-mounted filter repeatedly became loose when tape failed, allowing bypass. rootusrootus [[./thread#^c25457017|rootusrootus]] suggests intake mounting to help retain the filter and keep the fan clean, while turtlebits [[./thread#^c25457471|turtlebits]] reports inexpensive purpose-made retaining bands. The odor account includes no gas measurements or clear specification of the filter medium.
- riverdroid [[./thread#^c25460000|riverdroid]] reports normally running Winix 5300 and 5500 purifiers at setting three: approximately 12 W and quiet, versus about 70 W and noisy at setting four, with airflow they perceive as not greatly different. Rebelgecko [[./thread#^c25452892|Rebelgecko]] describes their purifier reaching approximately 60 W when cooking or something is burning. These are useful operating accounts, but no airflow-meter results or corresponding pollutant reductions accompany the wattage comparisons.
- xiphias2 [[./thread#^c25454757|xiphias2]] reports being unable to significantly lower room NO2 using an IQAir purifier and multiple filters, and likewise being unable to get normal/high exposure down to low levels using 3M masks. They are uncertain whether the filters contained potassium permanganate and have not yet tried an integrated filtration system. No monitor, concentration values, exposure source, fit details, or test protocol are identified.
- metiscus [[./thread#^c25455780|metiscus]] tentatively associates newer drywall construction with more dust than well-maintained plaster and lath, explicitly limiting the observation to personal experience. They later contrast frequently cleaned tile floors at home with their parents' less frequently vacuumed carpet, which concealed dirt and seemed dustier [[./thread#^c25458727|metiscus]]. This is an observation about surfaces, maintenance, and visible dust, not a measured comparison of airborne PM2.5.
- EricE [[./thread#^c25460631|EricE]] reports years of cutting furnace filters to fit discontinued Sharp units and newer Levoits. neilv [[./thread#^c25461572|neilv]] replies that this would fit their larger rectangular-filter unit but not their smaller cylindrical one, and prefers original filters for now because the additional layers and local filtration needs are uncertain. The exchange illustrates a maintenance workaround and a geometry constraint without establishing equivalent filtration performance.
- culopatin [[./thread#^c25456206|culopatin]], replying to ozone treatment for smoky car interiors, reports steering-wheel material turning gel-like and developing a worse smell after ozone use. This is firsthand material damage and odor experience, not a controlled assessment of pollutant removal or a health outcome.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Comparative DIY versus commercial effectiveness remains unresolved. hundchenkatze [[./thread#^c25458131|hundchenkatze]] quotes the article's favorable DIY result, whereas kingnothing [[./thread#^c25458407|kingnothing]] summarizes a separate Wirecutter comparison as 99\% removal for a good HEPA purifier versus 87\% for its box-fan setup. The comments do not establish common particles, room conditions, durations, airflow, or measurement methods. Together with Rebelgecko's request for repetitions [[./thread#^c25452892|Rebelgecko]], this prevents treating either result as a universal ranking.
- The best sealing and airflow arrangement is unsettled. spthorn60 [[./thread#^c25449180|spthorn60]] favors an unsealed setup for circulation; nkurz [[./thread#^c25445942|nkurz]] seals and adds a cowling; lurkertech [[./thread#^c25460866|lurkertech]] attributes improvement to an air guide; and cyounkins [[./thread#^c25450277|cyounkins]] predicts increasing bypass as filters load. These may describe genuinely different operating conditions. No supplied comparison isolates sealing, guide geometry, filter loading, and delivered clean airflow.
- Restriction-related electrical and fire risks are disputed. westurner [[./thread#^c25454934|westurner]] quotes a claim that restricted airflow increases motor load and electricity consumption, while ruste [[./thread#^c25458521|ruste]] argues that restricting intake can reduce load. westurner's follow-up [[./thread#^c25460665|westurner]] proposes pressure-related motor failure without measurements. yoyman's failed fan [[./thread#^c25455593|yoyman]] does not establish the mechanism. Separately, nkurz [[./thread#^c25454918|nkurz]] warns that overly restrictive central-heating filters can reduce airflow and overheat heater components; that is a system-specific concern, not proof of the box-fan motor explanation.
- Odor treatment discussion mixes ongoing air cleaning with remediation of contaminated interiors. xnyan [[./thread#^c25452679|xnyan]] challenges Havoc's suggestion that odor removal calls for ozone; Havoc [[./thread#^c25480426|Havoc]] responds with the example of professional treatment after hotel-room smoking. Traubenfuchs [[./thread#^c25454581|Traubenfuchs]] advocates carbon for VOCs and warns that ozone concentrations used to destroy odors are unhealthy. No controlled comparison establishes effectiveness for a specified odor source, and the hotel example does not settle routine occupied-room filtration.
- NO2 remains the clearest unresolved gas-removal problem in this unit. ddeck [[./thread#^c25454094|ddeck]] identifies residential equipment with relevant sorbents, but xiphias2 [[./thread#^c25454757|xiphias2]] reports unsuccessful real-world attempts. Missing concentrations, monitor validation, airflow, media details, and source information prevent deciding whether the limitation was the technology, implementation, or continuing exposure. Because xiphias2 does not identify the NO2 source, this account cannot establish a traffic-specific exposure pattern.
- Opening windows presents a documented tradeoff but no quantified decision rule. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c25453948|ahaucnx]] raises inadequate fresh air and high CO2; mleonhard [[./thread#^c25454778|mleonhard]] deliberately admits smoky outdoor air to relieve stuffiness before filtering again. These accounts suggest ventilation can help a poorly ventilated room while worsening particle exposure during wildfire smoke. No paired indoor/outdoor PM2.5 and CO2 series establishes when, how widely, or how long windows should be opened, or whether the reported 30-minute clearance sufficiently reduced exposure.
- Health, cognition, and sleep conclusions are substantially weaker than the practical filtration accounts. ahaucnx [[./thread#^c25453948|ahaucnx]] asserts headache and cognitive effects of elevated CO2, but supplies no participant-level outcomes. konjin [[./thread#^c25454781|konjin]] reports improved comfort, and Mister\_Snuggles [[./thread#^c25455580|Mister\_Snuggles]] reports noise-management needs, neither of which demonstrates better sleep or cognition. This source unit provides no firsthand sleep measurements, controlled cognitive results, or clinical before-and-after evidence; kylebenzle's sweeping health claim [[./thread#^c25453862|kylebenzle]] therefore remains unsupported here.
