# [Air pollution causes ‘huge’ reduction in intelligence: study](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=17867085)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants assess research connecting air pollution with cognitive test performance, focusing on causation, study design, confounding factors, and whether the reported effect is temporary or lasting. A lengthy exchange challenges the comparison to a year of education and explores the meanings, measurement, and stability of intelligence and IQ. Other discussion considers pollution exposure in different regions, its implications for a family choosing between schools, practical steps such as filtration and masks, and policy options including cleaner transport and emissions controls.
Tags: air pollution, cognitive health, IQ, research methods, education, air filtration, public policy

## Relevance

The discussion considers reported cognitive effects of air pollution, with comments on PM2.5 exposure, NO2 and SO2 measures, study uncertainty, and whether effects may be temporary or cumulative. It also includes practical exposure reduction through filtration and masks, plus observations about traffic pollution and indoor CO2. ## Arguments

- emiliobumachar [[./thread#^c17867412|emiliobumachar]] quotes the newspaper describing language and arithmetic tests on 20,000 people in China between 2010 and 2014, compared with nitrogen dioxide and sulphur dioxide records. This identifies NO2-related observational evidence, not firsthand exposure measurements. The reported loss equivalent to a year of education describes test-score differences, not an observed year of schooling lost or a directly measured IQ decline.
- black\_puppydog [[./thread#^c17867213|black\_puppydog]] considers following the same individuals as pollution changes stronger than a simple comparison between cities, because stable individual differences are partly accounted for. majjam [[./thread#^c17867592|majjam]] additionally reports that poverty and household income were controlled, but obtains this information through a Reddit discussion rather than presenting the analysis.
- yorwba [[./thread#^c17868074|yorwba]] supplies the most detailed study excerpt: a one-standard-deviation increase in seven-day average pollution index was associated with a 0.278-point verbal-score reduction, versus 1.132 points for a one-standard-deviation increase in the three-year average. These corresponded to 0.026 and 0.108 standard deviations of scores. Associations were stronger for verbal than arithmetic performance; one-day and seven-day arithmetic results were exceptions to the generally significant negative findings.
- nopinsight [[./thread#^c17867616|nopinsight]] quotes a projection that reducing China's annual mean PM10 concentration to 50 μg/m³ would move median verbal and arithmetic performance to the 63rd and 58th percentiles respectively. This is a reported model-based projection involving PM10, not a measured PM2.5 intervention or a demonstrated benefit from buying a purifier.
- geomark [[./thread#^c17867263|geomark]] reports another study claiming approximately 6\% lower lung capacity by age eight among children living within 100 metres of a busy street. Brakenshire [[./thread#^c17867794|Brakenshire]] describes impaired lung development and increased asthma risk as established pollution effects, while emodendroket [[./thread#^c17868286|emodendroket]] argues that uncertainty about particular cognitive claims need not overturn the broader health concern. None reports personally measuring these health outcomes.
- 0xcde4c3db [[./thread#^c17867797|0xcde4c3db]] points to studies suggesting that particulate exposure reduces brain-derived neurotrophic factor activity, offering a possible biological pathway rather than demonstrating it in commenters. snarfybarfy [[./thread#^c17875598|snarfybarfy]] invokes studies attributing a global average IQ reduction to leaded petrol; this is a historical analogy involving a different exposure, not evidence for the magnitude of the present study's effect.
- nopinsight [[./thread#^c17867616|nopinsight]] proposes HEPA filtration in bedrooms and offices to reduce exposure and potentially improve respiratory comfort and productivity. mike\_h [[./thread#^c17868303|mike\_h]], replying to a parent considering Bangkok schools, suggests combining home filtration with masks on bad days, school filters, and restrictions on outdoor athletics. These are proposed protections, without reported before-and-after results.
- Silixon [[./thread#^c17867737|Silixon]] and dmschulman [[./thread#^c17867600|dmschulman]] argue for reducing exposure despite uncertainty about cognition: cleaner air may have other benefits, so action need not depend on accepting the strongest headline claim.

## Counterarguments

- bobbyadamson [[./thread#^c17867348|bobbyadamson]] questions whether repeated observations adequately separate pollution from other urban conditions. Jgoure [[./thread#^c17868194|Jgoure]] raises traffic-related stress and memory effects as an alternative explanation, citing unspecified studies. These challenge causal attribution rather than demonstrating harmlessness. natch [[./thread#^c17869141|natch]] also cautions that a claim of the most likely explanation is different from the logical proof bobbyadamson requests.
- liftbigweights [[./thread#^c17867588|liftbigweights]] argues that historical increases in both pollution and IQ contradict the headline; nnain [[./thread#^c17873474|nnain]] similarly invokes continued scientific achievement in China and India. In replies to liftbigweights, fishtank [[./thread#^c17867650|fishtank]] distinguishes aggregate trends from effects on individuals, and nopinsight [[./thread#^c17867658|nopinsight]] argues that expanding education could outweigh pollution-related losses. The disagreement concerns counterfactual performance, which the coexistence of pollution and achievement does not directly measure.
- antt [[./thread#^c17867538|antt]] recommends waiting for extensive replication before changing behaviour. zeveb [[./thread#^c17868711|zeveb]], replying to the precautionary argument, stresses opportunity costs: pollution reduction consumes resources that could be spent elsewhere. This preserves a real disagreement about the evidentiary threshold and acceptable cost of precaution, rather than establishing that exposure reduction is either free or unnecessary.
- rcMgD2BwE72F [[./thread#^c17870516|rcMgD2BwE72F]] prefers reducing emissions at their source and questions pollution generated by purifier production, operation, and disposal. nopinsight [[./thread#^c17873830|nopinsight]] replies that individuals have limited influence over major emitters and need protection meanwhile, asserting that purifier-related pollution is comparatively small. Neither supplies a lifecycle calculation. growlist [[./thread#^c17868497|growlist]] favours eliminating diesel exposure, while JTbane [[./thread#^c17869016|JTbane]] hopes electric vehicles will reduce urban pollution; these are preferences and expectations, not measured comparisons.
- steve\_musk [[./thread#^c17867426|steve\_musk]] and trentmb [[./thread#^c17867682|trentmb]] suggest houseplants based on NASA-related material; steve\_musk explicitly acknowledges doing little research. In the same home-air advice branch, adrianN [[./thread#^c17867725|adrianN]] argues that small particles require HEPA filtration. The supplied discussion contains no room-scale measurements establishing how the proposed plants affect PM2.5, NO2, or CO2.
- alexandercrohde [[./thread#^c17868329|alexandercrohde]] suggests ordinary activated-charcoal exhaust filters would be straightforward. jhpankow [[./thread#^c17868390|jhpankow]] disputes both PM2.5 removal and performance after saturation; alexandercrohde [[./thread#^c17868485|alexandercrohde]] answers with proposed replacement and links. cameldrv [[./thread#^c17869752|cameldrv]] describes existing diesel particulate filters as heat-resistant systems that regenerate by burning accumulated particles. That reply distinguishes engineered particle control from the original simple-charcoal proposal, without resolving its claimed performance.

## Assumptions

- Pollution measures are sometimes treated as interchangeable despite differing evidence: emiliobumachar [[./thread#^c17867412|emiliobumachar]] describes NO2 and SO2 comparisons, nopinsight [[./thread#^c17867616|nopinsight]] quotes a PM10 projection, and geomark [[./thread#^c17867263|geomark]] discusses a separate PM2.5-related childhood study. These comments do not isolate which pollutant caused the cognitive association or justify assigning the same effect size to PM2.5, NO2, and CO2.
- Symmetry [[./thread#^c17869758|Symmetry]], replying within the Salt Lake City cold-weather discussion, proposes that closed windows cause indoor CO2 buildup and that concentrations around 1,000 ppm impair cognition. This is a hypothesised alternative mechanism, with no personal readings or supporting experiment supplied. The additional assertion that hot countries would avoid this problem is also untested here.
- stephengillie [[./thread#^c17867869|stephengillie]] speculates that carbon monoxide and other pollutants entering cars cause road rage, reckless driving, and continuing cognitive effects after a commute. rooam-dev [[./thread#^c17867894|rooam-dev]] similarly labels reduced oxygen, toxins, illness, and stress as educated guesses. Neither provides exposure or physiological measurements; carbon monoxide in this branch must not be confused with Symmetry's separate CO2 hypothesis.
- nopinsight's [[./thread#^c17867616|nopinsight]] estimate that bedroom filtration could remove at least one-third of exposure time assumes substantial time spent there and effective cleaning throughout that period. Predictions of office productivity gains likewise assume that reduced exposure translates into measurable benefits. No purifier sizing, achieved concentration, exposure reduction, or cognitive outcome is supplied.
- dzdt [[./thread#^c17867177|dzdt]] and dunpeal [[./thread#^c17868358|dunpeal]] approach intelligence as relatively stable and distinct from education, which shapes their objections to the headline. emiliobumachar [[./thread#^c17867412|emiliobumachar]] clarifies that the reported outcomes were language and arithmetic scores. Equating those scores with either immutable intelligence or permanent brain damage would therefore require additional assumptions.

## Conditions

- Geographic comparisons depend on the reference point. api [[./thread#^c17867865|api]] regards London as relatively mild compared with Beijing, whereas Reason077 [[./thread#^c17868353|Reason077]] says London is worse than New York and regularly exceeds NOx and particulate limits. contingencies [[./thread#^c17867460|contingencies]] reports a contemporaneous worst-area London AQI of 88 and Chinese city readings commonly around 150–200. These are 2018 assertions with different comparison frames, not a harmonised exposure assessment.
- stochastic\_monk [[./thread#^c17867243|stochastic\_monk]] attributes Salt Lake City's winter pollution episodes to inversions trapping pollutants in a mountain basin. This makes season and local geography central to the account, but the comment supplies neither a personal monitoring record nor comparative measurements supporting its claim that the city becomes North America's most polluted.
- cameldrv [[./thread#^c17869798|cameldrv]] quotes a \$100–200 HEPA purchase price and recommends a HEPA cabin filter for frequent drivers. mike\_h [[./thread#^c17868303|mike\_h]] describes relatively inexpensive PM2.5 filters but identifies practical limits for children: peer reactions to masks, school cooperation, and athletics policies. These are purchase-price and implementation observations from 2018; ongoing costs and measured protection are absent.
- geomark [[./thread#^c17867263|geomark]] frames rural Thailand versus Bangkok as a tradeoff between cleaner surroundings and educational opportunity. nopinsight [[./thread#^c17867442|nopinsight]] proposes local tutoring and using time otherwise lost to congested commuting, conditional on suitable tutors and a regular schedule. geomark [[./thread#^c17867556|geomark]] explains why that may not replace access to better peers, extracurricular activities, and school programmes. Exposure reduction is therefore constrained by family priorities, not merely equipment availability.
- Actual residence and time spent near pollution complicate city-wide exposure assumptions. Reason077 [[./thread#^c17868325|Reason077]] suggests MPs may have better-situated London flats with HVAC and homes elsewhere; tialaramex [[./thread#^c17869307|tialaramex]] personally reports sharing a train with a commuting MP. Neither measures exposure or HVAC effectiveness. Conversely, snarfybarfy [[./thread#^c17868243|snarfybarfy]] repeats hearsay that affluent German neighbourhoods can have worse air because of fireplaces, so presumed wealth-related protection is not established.
- iamben [[./thread#^c17867860|iamben]] assumes relocating an important institution to cleaner surroundings could eventually recreate pollution as a city develops around it. CalRobert [[./thread#^c17868187|CalRobert]] disputes the inevitability of heavily polluted cities. This leaves urban development and exposure as a conditional relationship, rather than making relocation or rural residence a guaranteed solution.

## Lived experiences

- NeedMoreTea [[./thread#^c17868406|NeedMoreTea]], replying to the claim that London is relatively unpolluted, describes seeing and tasting a change near Hendon on the M1, finding clothes and body dirtier, and experiencing air reminiscent of a smoke-filled room. This is a firsthand account of discomfort and perceived pollution, without pollutant identification, concentrations, or a measured health effect.
- geomark [[./thread#^c17867263|geomark]] describes living with family in what they consider a very clean rural mountainous area of Thailand while contemplating Bangkok schooling. In follow-ups, geomark [[./thread#^c17867556|geomark]] [[./thread#^c17867599|geomark]] details school limitations and existing home projects and tutoring. The family decision is firsthand; the predicted lung and cognitive consequences come from reported studies, and no move or resulting health change is reported.
- paraschopra [[./thread#^c17867873|paraschopra]] identifies as an Indian living in India and describes distress about severe pollution and limited action. They report Delhi PM2.5 regularly exceeding 200 and describe less than 50 as good, but supply neither units, a measurement method, nor a supporting standard. This is a local perspective containing numerical claims, not a documented personal sensor experiment.
- trukterious [[./thread#^c17867783|trukterious]] describes rapidly exhaling after noticing concentrated exhaust, walking several paces, and then inhaling again. They acknowledge a detection delay and pollution in surrounding air. This documents a personal coping behaviour, not demonstrated exposure reduction or an established protective technique.
- The firsthand cognitive accounts concern testing rather than pollution. code\_duck [[./thread#^c17867193|code\_duck]] reports learning test-taking skills through schooling; jackpeterfletch [[./thread#^c17867376|jackpeterfletch]] attributes strong pattern-recognition performance during a practice IQ assessment to learned geometry strategies. These anecdotes help explain concern about interpreting test scores, but neither connects a measured cognitive change to air exposure.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Temporary performance impairment versus lasting damage remains unresolved. stochastic\_monk [[./thread#^c17867625|stochastic\_monk]] explicitly characterises the Salt Lake City effect as reversible, and mockingbirdy [[./thread#^c17867681|mockingbirdy]] extends that into an expectation of recovery after leaving polluted air. yorwba [[./thread#^c17868074|yorwba]] responds with stronger associations over longer exposure windows. Longer-window associations do not themselves demonstrate irreversible injury or establish how quickly people recover.
- The meaning and reliability of the cognitive endpoint remain contested. AmericanChopper [[./thread#^c17867346|AmericanChopper]] strongly defends IQ's predictive value, while orwin [[./thread#^c17868261|orwin]] questions interpretation of a single score and describes changing abilities. Symmetry [[./thread#^c17869683|Symmetry]] distinguishes noisy individual results from potentially useful population averages. These general arguments do not resolve how much the pollution study captures temporary performance, accumulated learning, or persistent impairment.
- Sensor reliability is raised but not established experimentally. contingencies [[./thread#^c17867460|contingencies]] repeats a sensor-making friend's claim that many devices saturate below Indian pollution levels, without naming instruments, ranges, or calibration evidence. Combined with paraschopra's [[./thread#^c17867873|paraschopra]] unspecified PM2.5 units, this prevents confident comparison of the quoted numbers. The source contains no firsthand instrument validation or indoor/outdoor PM2.5, NO2, or CO2 time series.
- Opening windows presents an untested tradeoff in this unit. Symmetry's [[./thread#^c17869758|Symmetry]] closed-window CO2 hypothesis suggests a possible benefit from air exchange, while Reason077's [[./thread#^c17868353|Reason077]] description of outdoor NOx and particulate pollution raises a competing exposure concern. Nobody supplies paired open/closed-window measurements or simultaneous indoor CO2 and outdoor pollutant readings, so this discussion cannot establish when opening windows improves overall exposure.
- The effectiveness of the proposed protections remains unquantified. nopinsight [[./thread#^c17867616|nopinsight]] predicts bedroom and office benefits, and cameldrv [[./thread#^c17869798|cameldrv]] asserts that driving exposure is many times higher than home exposure, but neither presents personal measurements before and after filtration. No supplied result shows whether these interventions improve cognition or respiratory symptoms, and the particulate-filter recommendations provide no demonstrated NO2 or CO2 reduction.
- Sleep, cooking, and wildfire exposure are evidence gaps in this source unit. nopinsight's [[./thread#^c17867616|nopinsight]] bedroom proposal concerns exposure duration rather than measured sleep quality; smolder [[./thread#^c17868398|smolder]] discusses poor sleep as a possible influence on cognitive baseline, not an observed consequence of pollution. The supplied comments contain no cooking or wildfire-smoke accounts and no firsthand sleep improvement following ventilation or filtration.
