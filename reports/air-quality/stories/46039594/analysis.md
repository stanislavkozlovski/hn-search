# [I made a quieter air purifier](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46039594)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants discuss a quieter DIY air purifier built with computer fans, comparing airflow, noise, pressure capability, filtration, power use, size, and cost with box fans and commercial purifiers. Several threads explore filter choices, including MERV ratings, HEPA, electrostatic media, pressure drop, loading, replacement costs, and performance against fine particles and tire dust. Users share DIY and commercial alternatives, debate the Corsi–Rosenthal design’s origins and testing, and discuss fan placement and whether fans should push or pull air through filters.
Tags: DIY air purifier, air filtration, computer fans, noise, MERV, HEPA, Corsi–Rosenthal box, CADR

## Relevance

The discussion compares DIY and commercial particle filtration, including airflow, noise, filter efficiency, maintenance, and cost. Firsthand reports describe household fan setups, monitoring particle levels, and filtration during wildfire smoke or near traffic. ## Arguments

- amluto [[./thread#^c46100728|amluto]] argues that useful filtration depends on airflow multiplied by capture efficiency for the relevant particle sizes. Their hypothetical example—1,000 CFM through a filter capturing 60\% yields 600 CFM of clean air—explains why neither fan airflow nor filter grade alone establishes performance.
- dgacmu [[./thread#^c46111528|dgacmu]] recommends MERV-13 for broader protection than tire dust alone, including PM2.5 and cooking-related pollution. amluto [[./thread#^c46100728|amluto]], correcting an earlier recollection of a chart, emphasizes performance at the most penetrating particle size and recommends substantially higher filtration, such as MERV-16 or near-HEPA, for single-pass intake during wildfire smoke or other heavily polluted outdoor conditions. These are recommendations based on referenced material, not supplied test results.
- amluto [[./thread#^c46100728|amluto]] argues that increasing filter area reduces pressure drop at a given airflow, potentially lowering noise and power consumption while extending replacement intervals. dgacmu [[./thread#^c46111528|dgacmu]] similarly says deep, extensively pleated MERV-13 filters can have reasonable resistance. Their practical argument is that filter construction and area matter alongside its rating.
- Implicated [[./thread#^c46113071|Implicated]] argues that PC-fan purifiers can combine useful airflow with tolerable noise, noting that some Arctic fans are designed for resistance from radiators. chr-s [[./thread#^c46098902|chr-s]] also attributes the shift toward PC-fan designs to their performance-to-noise ratio. Neither supplies a numerical comparison of clean-air delivery and sound in this thread.
- LTL\_FTC [[./thread#^c46113686|LTL\_FTC]] argues that recirculating filtration should be accompanied by outdoor-air exchange in tightly sealed homes. Their proposed wildfire arrangement combines large filters, a blower, and filtered outdoor intake through a window or permanent installation; they also suggest that correctly implemented positive pressure can reduce pollutant infiltration. This is a proposed design following personal research, not a documented installation test.
- LTL\_FTC [[./thread#^c46113686|LTL\_FTC]] distinguishes particle filtration from VOC control, claiming meaningful adsorption requires pounds of activated carbon and regular replacement, rather than the thin carbon layer found in many purifiers. Night\_Thastus [[./thread#^c46112991|Night\_Thastus]] likewise identifies carbon exhaustion as a limitation for smoke control. The supplied comments contain no VOC measurements establishing capacity or replacement intervals.
- tehjoker [[./thread#^c46099323|tehjoker]] identifies particulate counts, airflow, energy measurements, and a shroud preventing backflow as useful contributions to DIY purifier design. tehjoker [[./thread#^c46099705|tehjoker]] describes favorable clean-air delivery per dollar, with noise and power penalties. tguvot [[./thread#^c46099495|tguvot]] counters that earlier wildfire-era DIY posts already included particle-reduction charts; the relevant evidentiary issue is what testing establishes, rather than who deserves naming credit.
- tguvot [[./thread#^c46099821|tguvot]] proposes P100 masks as a strong filtration-per-dollar option. tehjoker [[./thread#^c46101481|tehjoker]] emphasizes the distinction between certified masks and uncertified fan/filter assemblies. This introduces personal respiratory protection as an alternative, but the discussion supplies no comparable exposure, wearing-comfort, or cost measurements against whole-room cleaning.

## Counterarguments

- ruralfam [[./thread#^c46100210|ruralfam]] treats the substantial dirt washed from reusable electrostatic MERV-8 filters as evidence of useful fine-dust capture. amluto [[./thread#^c46100903|amluto]] challenges that inference: visibly dirty prefilters can coexist with substantial PM2.5 penetration, while a downstream HEPA filter can look clean. Visible collected material therefore does not establish removal of the particles motivating the freeway-apartment project.
- dpark [[./thread#^c46100233|dpark]] questions whether five small fans can match a box fan and whether the comparison undermines airflow's importance. unregistereddev [[./thread#^c46112749|unregistereddev]] replies that the PC fans were running high while the box fan was running low because of noise. That explanation makes operating speed central to the comparison; it does not establish equal maximum airflow or make airflow irrelevant.
- skhameneh [[./thread#^c46100615|skhameneh]] questions whether the apparent performance depends on unusually large filter area and low resistance, arguing that PC fans generally handle less pressure than purpose-built purifier equipment. LTL\_FTC [[./thread#^c46113686|LTL\_FTC]] similarly favors blowers for higher static pressure. These comments qualify claims that a quiet computer fan can substitute successfully in every filtration or extraction application.
- Implicated [[./thread#^c46113071|Implicated]] claims retail purifiers force a choice between quiet operation and effectiveness. tyre [[./thread#^c46114582|tyre]] disputes this from experience with an automatic commercial unit that is usually quiet and accelerates during cooking or poor air quality. amluto [[./thread#^c46101059|amluto]] separately recommends IKEA units as quieter, more efficient, and cheaper to maintain. None supplies matched sound and clean-air-delivery measurements resolving the competing claims.
- ruralfam [[./thread#^c46112618|ruralfam]] worries that MERV-13 rapidly loses airflow and becomes prohibitively expensive to replace, criticizing the short, new-filter smoke tests they encountered. dgacmu [[./thread#^c46113553|dgacmu]] counters that loading can increase capture efficiency even as airflow falls, so clean-air delivery need not decline as quickly as airflow alone suggests. The relationship is asserted rather than quantified for ruralfam's proposed equipment.

## Assumptions

- For story 46039594, part 0, the health evidence centers on Night\_Thastus's reported allergy relief [[./thread#^c46112991|Night\_Thastus]], while LTL\_FTC discusses stale-air discomfort [[./thread#^c46113686|LTL\_FTC]]. This unit contains no NO2 or CO2 measurements, no measured oxygen depletion, and no reported cognition or sleep outcomes. Quiet operation and improved comfort should not be converted into demonstrated sleep or cognitive benefits.
- amluto [[./thread#^c46100903|amluto]] reports a small particle sensor reading zero after a filtration upgrade, but provides no instrument model, calibration, detection limit, absolute concentrations, or matched before-and-after conditions. The result supports a reported improvement on that instrument; it does not establish literally particle-free air, gas removal, or quantified health protection.
- ruralfam [[./thread#^c46099599|ruralfam]] describes a planned eight-fan purifier as approximately 1,000 CFM. No assembled-system airflow or clean-air-delivery measurement is supplied. Treating that estimate as delivered airflow through the selected filters would assume away their resistance and the effects of the enclosure.
- The underlying studies and linked test datasets are absent from the supplied evidence. amluto [[./thread#^c46100728|amluto]] explicitly corrects a remembered filtration chart, and Implicated [[./thread#^c46112986|Implicated]] cannot locate the filter-loading comparison they recall. Their claims should remain attributed recollections rather than independently established findings.
- ruralfam's wood-stove setup moves existing indoor air between rooms [[./thread#^c46100271|ruralfam]], and scosman describes inter-room circulation [[./thread#^c46098966|scosman]]. Those experiences establish reported heat-distribution and noise benefits, not measured outdoor-air exchange. LTL\_FTC's proposed filtered outdoor intake [[./thread#^c46113686|LTL\_FTC]] addresses a different function.

## Conditions

- ruralfam [[./thread#^c46099599|ruralfam]] is designing for a daughter spending several years in an apartment near a major university and freeway. Tire dust is the motivating concern, but neither outdoor nor indoor exposure is measured. The project is explicitly not low-cost: washable filters, discounted returned units, appearance, and long-term replacement expense all influence the choice.
- The upfront price comparison is contested and model-dependent. notatoad [[./thread#^c46099674|notatoad]] estimates five Arctic fans at about \$100 and argues commercial equipment is competitive; blargey [[./thread#^c46100415|blargey]] quotes five Arctic P12 fans at \$24, while Implicated [[./thread#^c46113071|Implicated]] quotes five P14 Pro fans at \$32. These are prices reported in the discussion, not a complete comparison including filters, enclosure, power supply, energy, and maintenance.
- Implicated [[./thread#^c46112986|Implicated]] recalls Costco sale pricing of roughly \$35 for three Filtrete 2500 MERV-14 filters and recommends stocking up. Replacement economics also depend on loading: dgacmu [[./thread#^c46113553|dgacmu]] describes a home without pets and with continuous HVAC filtration, whereas Implicated's installation serves more than ten cats. Neither establishes a universal monthly replacement requirement.
- Housing infrastructure limits which airflow improvements are available. ortusdux [[./thread#^c46099292|ortusdux]] mentions \$35–40 register boosters and potentially expensive inline duct retrofits; scosman [[./thread#^c46243784|scosman]] points out that radiator-heated houses may lack ducts and registers. tguvot [[./thread#^c46099757|tguvot]] suggests jump ducts where an attic is available, while lozenge [[./thread#^c46099047|lozenge]] identifies continuous extraction and whole-house bidirectional ventilation as other options.
- Space and acoustic context affect practicality. tguvot [[./thread#^c46114666|tguvot]] estimates the linked commercial purifier occupies roughly one-sixth the footprint of the DIY cube and mentions wall-mounted alternatives. LTL\_FTC [[./thread#^c46113686|LTL\_FTC]] proposes furniture-sized equipment to accommodate large media and reduce vibration. tim333 [[./thread#^c46099983|tim333]] suggests a quiet setting might be inaudible near a freeway; background noise masking a purifier provides no evidence of cleaner air.
- Window opening presents a conditional tradeoff in these comments. LTL\_FTC [[./thread#^c46113686|LTL\_FTC]] recommends outdoor-air exchange to address unpleasant indoor conditions, but proposes a filtered window intake; amluto [[./thread#^c46100728|amluto]] recommends high-grade filtration for polluted incoming air. Their reasoning suggests unfiltered opening during smoke could admit particles while easing stuffiness. No open-versus-closed-window experiment, CO2 benefit, or exposure-based opening schedule is provided.

## Lived experiences

- The clearest firsthand PM2.5 measurement account is amluto's [[./thread#^c46100903|amluto]]. They interpreted particle-sensor readings as showing that an older, incompletely specified ISO ePM1 filter missed about half the outdoor PM2.5. After installing a much larger HEPA system with a MERV-8 prefilter and metal louver, the sensor reportedly read zero across its particle-size categories. They also report lower power use and expect lower operating costs, without supplying numerical energy or cost data.
- Night\_Thastus [[./thread#^c46112991|Night\_Thastus]] reports that allergies disappeared and airborne dust and cat hair decreased after using a premade CleanAirKits setup. This is a personal health and cleanliness observation without symptom tracking or a controlled comparison. codemac [[./thread#^c46112426|codemac]] also reports buying and liking several CleanAirKits units, but supplies no health or pollution measurements.
- dgacmu [[./thread#^c46113553|dgacmu]] reports running a four-filter MERV-13 Corsi–Rosenthal box continuously for a year with airflow remaining reasonably good, in a pet-free home also using continuous HVAC filtration. Implicated [[./thread#^c46112986|Implicated]] reports four months of apparently good airflow through dirty MERV-14 filters in a rescue housing more than ten cats. Both observations challenge extremely short assumed lifetimes, but neither measures retained airflow, capture efficiency, or clean-air delivery.
- ruralfam [[./thread#^c46100271|ruralfam]] reports years of quiet, reliable Noctua-fan operation distributing wood-stove heat through a hall and remote rooms, with inexpensive thrift-store power supplies. scosman [[./thread#^c46098966|scosman]] similarly reports satisfaction with computer fans for inter-room movement and describes purpose-built alternatives as three times louder and several times more expensive at the same CFM. Neither provides a sound-test protocol or pollutant measurements.
- Commercial-purifier experiences vary substantially. tguvot [[./thread#^c46101871|tguvot]] describes the linked unit as effectively inaudible from ten feet on low, but an IQAir as airplane-like despite replacement. hasbot [[./thread#^c46112807|hasbot]] finds medium and high settings loud and runs two units continuously on low regardless of air quality. tyre [[./thread#^c46114582|tyre]] instead uses automatic operation and reports speed increases during cooking. That cooking response is an observation of appliance behavior, not a quantified PM2.5 or NO2 measurement.
- tguvot [[./thread#^c46099243|tguvot]] reports building a similar DIY filter during California's 2018 fires and subsequently using it mainly for garage work until recently decommissioning it. turtlebits [[./thread#^c46100825|turtlebits]] reports using a single-filter box fan for wildfire smoke and later realizing its inefficiency. These establish firsthand use during smoke events, without supplying exposure reductions or health outcomes.

## Unresolved disagreements

- Will the proposed washable MERV-8 system adequately reduce freeway-related tire particles? ruralfam [[./thread#^c46099599|ruralfam]] asks that question and points to electrostatic dust capture [[./thread#^c46100210|ruralfam]]; amluto [[./thread#^c46100903|amluto]] questions the inference from visible dirt and the persistence of charge after washing. ruralfam acknowledges the HEPA comparison's value [[./thread#^c46102465|ruralfam]], but provides no completed-project test or final filter decision.
- How does long-term clean-air delivery and total cost compare between MERV-8 and MERV-13 in the proposed design? ruralfam [[./thread#^c46112618|ruralfam]] specifically requests a roughly 30-day comparison. The longer operating anecdotes from dgacmu [[./thread#^c46113553|dgacmu]] and Implicated [[./thread#^c46112986|Implicated]] address feasibility but do not supply that comparison under equivalent loading and airflow conditions.
- Which design offers the best measured combination of noise, airflow, pressure capability, and energy use? dpark [[./thread#^c46100233|dpark]] questions the comparison, skhameneh [[./thread#^c46100615|skhameneh]] requests static-pressure analysis, and DCKP [[./thread#^c46112084|DCKP]] proposes varying fan speeds or using nine slower fans to change the sound. rao-v's larger-is-quieter rule [[./thread#^c46111936|rao-v]] is also not tested here. The supplied comments do not resolve these alternatives with comparable measurements.
- The scope of smoke protection remains unclear. Night\_Thastus [[./thread#^c46112991|Night\_Thastus]] says their setup cannot do anything for wood smoke without carbon, whereas amluto [[./thread#^c46100728|amluto]] recommends high-grade particulate filtration for wildfire-polluted intake and LTL\_FTC [[./thread#^c46113686|LTL\_FTC]] discusses separate VOC adsorption. The comments leave particle reduction, odor reduction, and gas removal insufficiently separated; they do not establish complete smoke protection or NO2 control.
- Fan direction receives conflicting explanations. chr-s [[./thread#^c46098902|chr-s]] recommends pulling through filters to keep fans clean and reduce turbulence; nbf\_1995 [[./thread#^c46102854|nbf\_1995]] suggests blowing inward keeps deposited dust inside the enclosure. brtv [[./thread#^c46098853|brtv]] speculates about overpressure keeping dust out. No supplied test resolves the actual configuration or comparative benefit, and enclosure pressure should not be conflated with pressurizing a home using filtered outdoor air.
- Continuous-use fire safety is disputed without adequate evidence. hollerith [[./thread#^c46111796|hollerith]] cites one linked near-fire account as grounds for calling filtered box fans hazardous. SoftTalker [[./thread#^c46112483|SoftTalker]] questions generalizing from one report, while QuercusMax [[./thread#^c46113393|QuercusMax]] suggests wiring or installation could explain it and offers a separate dishwasher-fire example. Neither the original incident's cause nor comparative box-fan and PC-fan safety is established in this unit.
