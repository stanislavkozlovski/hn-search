# [Show HN: Air Lab – A portable and open air quality measuring device](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44189329)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Participants review a portable, open air-quality monitor and its simulator, discussing the device’s sensors, display, battery, enclosure, firmware, documentation, and crowdfunding price. Feedback focuses on usability, glanceable readings, portability, sensor accuracy and calibration, the lack of built-in particulate measurement, and possible extensions. The thread compares commercial and DIY alternatives, affordability and manufacturing costs, open hardware, Home Assistant and other integrations, connectivity standards, and practical uses for monitoring indoor and outdoor air.
Tags: air quality monitor, hardware design, open source, sensor accuracy, DIY electronics, home automation, product feedback

## Relevance

The thread discusses a monitor for CO2 and NOx, its lack of built-in PM2.5 sensing, sensor accuracy and calibration limits, and how readings can guide ventilation and filtration. Users report applying measurements to sleep, comfort, allergy management, wildfire smoke, and decisions about opening windows. ## Arguments

- Indoor measurements can reveal changes that occupants overlook. samuraijack [[./thread#^c44189847|samuraijack]] argues that people become accustomed to poor air, while hazn [[./thread#^c44189891|hazn]] emphasizes gradual deterioration. joker99 [[./thread#^c44190519|joker99]] describes reminders to ventilate crowded or occupied small rooms. These comments support monitoring as an aid to awareness, without establishing that every home needs continuous monitoring.
- Particle measurement is particularly actionable during wildfire smoke. markasoftware [[./thread#^c44192592|markasoftware]] argues that indoor PM2.5 readings help assess smoke exclusion and whether purification is needed; jvanderbot [[./thread#^c44190835|jvanderbot]] recommends checking indoor and outdoor particles and using HVAC filtration or purifiers. Crucially, 256dpi [[./thread#^c44196091|256dpi]] confirms that Air Lab has no built-in particle sensor; external sensor support and a matching kit were prospective additions.
- Opening windows is presented as a conditional intervention. strogonoff [[./thread#^c44191770|strogonoff]] recommends ventilation for accumulated CO2, closing windows and filtering when outdoor particles are elevated, and briefly ventilating before filtering when both are high. voxlax [[./thread#^c44195369|voxlax]] describes balancing removal of indoor humidity and VOCs against incoming smog. The proposed sequence is a personal strategy, not a demonstrated optimum.
- Monitoring can check whether particle filtration works in a particular home. m4r1k [[./thread#^c44190650|m4r1k]] advocates measuring alongside recirculation and room-level HEPA filters; voxlax [[./thread#^c44195369|voxlax]] uses measurements to control purifiers while minimizing noise. ajolly [[./thread#^c44199360|ajolly]] suggests a computer-fan Corsi–Rosenthal box as a cheaper, quieter alternative, but supplies no comparative performance measurements.
- Measurements may make ventilation automatic and reduce unnecessary operation. bhouston [[./thread#^c44200118|bhouston]] describes using thermostat air-quality signals to drive an HRV on demand and claims substantial energy savings, without reporting energy measurements. westurner [[./thread#^c44196026|westurner]] proposes operating ventilation fans when indoor air quality is worse than outdoors. These are control strategies whose usefulness depends on what the sensors actually measure.
- Readable alerts and learning household patterns may matter more than constant graph inspection. silversmith [[./thread#^c44189618|silversmith]] values readings and warnings visible across a room; wuming2 [[./thread#^c44189964|wuming2]] clarifies that their objection is to watching a display for unpredictable events, favoring notifications. Modified3019 [[./thread#^c44190917|Modified3019]] reports that initial experimentation eventually reduced uncertainty and left mostly seasonal checks.

## Counterarguments

- The incremental benefit of monitoring is disputed. technotarek [[./thread#^c44190425|technotarek]] asks what measurements add when cooking already prompts ventilation and expresses concern about information that creates anxiety without changing action. strogonoff [[./thread#^c44191770|strogonoff]] cautions against optimizing every number and reports being relatively unconcerned after their monitor broke because they had already learned the home's patterns.
- A standalone monitor competes with spending directly on treatment. tigrezno [[./thread#^c44190820|tigrezno]] reports owning a roughly €150 purifier with built-in sensing and questions paying more for measurement alone. snickmy [[./thread#^c44190859|snickmy]] responds that portability serves a different use case. The exchange does not establish that the bundled and standalone sensors have equivalent coverage or accuracy.
- Responsive graphs do not establish absolute accuracy. Responding to enthusiasm about changes after opening windows, Havoc [[./thread#^c44193420|Havoc]] argues that inexpensive sensors can show the same response and that drift and local air movement remain concerns. ajolly [[./thread#^c44197434|ajolly]] makes a narrower defense: their cheap sensors are useful directionally, even when their absolute readings are inaccurate.
- An instrument labeled as reporting CO2 may instead estimate it. sedgjh23 [[./thread#^c44200780|sedgjh23]] reports large discrepancies between an EcoBee and an AirThings View Plus and quotes EcoBee documentation saying CO2 is inferred from VOC measurements. This directly qualifies bhouston's [[./thread#^c44200118|bhouston]] proposed CO2-driven HRV use; neither the discrepancy nor the more expensive instrument establishes the true concentration.
- Claims that reliable monitoring necessarily requires a high purchase price are contested. xvilka [[./thread#^c44190797|xvilka]] rejects sub-\$100 meters, and rspoerri [[./thread#^c44190093|rspoerri]] asserts minimum prices for CO2 and connected devices. Rebelgecko [[./thread#^c44190851|Rebelgecko]] suggests sub-\$100 AirGradient DIY builds, while djrj477dhsnv [[./thread#^c44197149|djrj477dhsnv]] reports a \$45 Qingping with CO2, PM2.5 and Wi-Fi. These alternatives challenge price floors, but their accuracy is not independently demonstrated here.
- The portability-versus-particle-sensing tradeoff may change with hardware. Havoc [[./thread#^c44193127|Havoc]] initially says compact PM2.5 sensing requires a fan; formerly\_proven [[./thread#^c44193341|formerly\_proven]] supplies a USB-key-sized counterexample. Havoc [[./thread#^c44193892|Havoc]] welcomes it because their existing sensor has a whiny fan. This is a product-reference exchange, not a comparison of detection accuracy, airflow sensitivity or noise measurements.

## Assumptions

- Interpreting improved sleep, office performance or allergies as pollutant-specific causal effects requires more than the supplied accounts. joker99 [[./thread#^c44190519|joker99]] reports correlation with high CO2 and improvement after changing ventilation; m4r1k [[./thread#^c44190650|m4r1k]] reports improvement alongside recirculation and HEPA filtration. Neither isolates the responsible exposure or separates the intervention from other environmental changes.
- Fresh-air automatic calibration assumes a suitable reference exposure occurs regularly. vosper [[./thread#^c44192723|vosper]] describes this requirement and worries about invisible drift and unclear calibration status. Aspos [[./thread#^c44193219|Aspos]] places a sensor beside an ERV supply specifically to obtain fresh-air exposure. Neither account supplies an independent check that the resulting calibration is correct.
- Using VOC measurements as a CO2 proxy assumes the relevant sources remain sufficiently correlated. bhouston [[./thread#^c44201864|bhouston]] defends that assumption because their home contains breathing people rather than industrial VOC sources. However, strogonoff [[./thread#^c44191770|strogonoff]] identifies furniture and flooring as possible household VOC sources, explicitly without firsthand VOC measurements. The normal-home assumption is therefore asserted rather than tested in this exchange.
- A reading at one location is assumed to represent the air relevant to occupants. Havoc [[./thread#^c44193420|Havoc]] suspects a bedroom spike on waking reflects mixing and movement rather than a comparable rise in whole-room CO2. radicality's [[./thread#^c44192960|radicality]] five-second sampling provides temporal detail, but by itself does not resolve spatial representativeness or drift.
- Several numbers are operating preferences or cited recommendations rather than measured health boundaries. Modified3019 [[./thread#^c44190917|Modified3019]] aims below roughly 800 ppm CO2; voxlax [[./thread#^c44195369|voxlax]] cites a yearly 5 µg/m³ PM2.5 recommendation and targets indoor levels below 2–3 µg/m³. These have different meanings and averaging contexts; the thread does not validate them as interchangeable thresholds for immediate symptoms.
- Pollutant names and units require caution. jvanderbot [[./thread#^c44191671|jvanderbot]] describes particle counts in the '100s ppm', whereas burnt-resistor [[./thread#^c44199220|burnt-resistor]] reports µg/m³; the former cannot be silently converted into PM2.5 mass concentration or AQI. Similarly, strogonoff [[./thread#^c44191770|strogonoff]] groups CO2 and carbon monoxide in a discussion of sleepiness and invokes increased oxygen, but supplies no CO or oxygen measurements.

## Conditions

- Affordability depends strongly on income and delivery costs. homebrewer [[./thread#^c44190020|homebrewer]], describing severe pollution in an unnamed region, says a \$200-plus monitor could approach \$300 shipped—approximately the local median monthly salary—even though they consider themselves relatively comfortable locally. benchly [[./thread#^c44191734|benchly]] separately identifies unemployment as preventing a purchase. These are specific access constraints, not evidence that exposed households can readily buy instrumentation.
- DIY alternatives differ in coverage, effort and total cost. scottlamb [[./thread#^c44193184|scottlamb]] reports a then-current \$138 AirGradient kit and cheaper historical options; aurelwu [[./thread#^c44190085|aurelwu]] suggests a roughly €70 CO2 development-kit build; Tepix [[./thread#^c44199604|Tepix]] cites a roughly €50 particulate project. crtified [[./thread#^c44205143|crtified]] warns that a practical finished build can take months, whereas ajolly [[./thread#^c44197379|ajolly]] considers ESPHome-based assembly easy.
- Finished-device prices include more than sensor components. 256dpi [[./thread#^c44190496|256dpi]] cites additional hardware, an aluminum enclosure and small-scale purchasing disadvantages. iamflimflam1 [[./thread#^c44190373|iamflimflam1]] explains that CrowdSupply's US fulfillment route can expose overseas projects to tariffs, and 256dpi [[./thread#^c44190872|256dpi]] says this complicated their launch. These explanations concern production and access, not demonstrated measurement superiority.
- Outdoor conditions constrain window-based ventilation. Modified3019 [[./thread#^c44190917|Modified3019]] normally leaves a window slightly open but avoids this during pollen season. voxlax [[./thread#^c44195369|voxlax]] has urban smog and no forced ventilation. strogonoff [[./thread#^c44191770|strogonoff]] reports that a neighbor's cooking can worsen indoor readings through an open window when the wind is unfavorable. Outdoor air is consequently not treated as uniformly cleaner.
- Climate and HVAC conditions reverse humidity needs. rolfus [[./thread#^c44192061|rolfus]] reports winter indoor humidity below 30\%, especially with wood-fired stoves. Modified3019 [[./thread#^c44193601|Modified3019]] describes dry indoor air despite humid Pacific Northwest weather, while dylan604 [[./thread#^c44191825|dylan604]] needs dehumidification in a humid setting. The accounts do not support choosing humidification or dehumidification from outdoor relative humidity alone.
- Claims about wildfire smoke becoming unfamiliar or newly widespread are geographically qualified. dylan604 [[./thread#^c44191428|dylan604]] challenges any implication that Americans previously lacked wildfire exposure, citing California's long history. jvanderbot [[./thread#^c44191671|jvanderbot]] narrows the claim to the upper Midwest and East Coast and contrasts those experiences with having lived in Los Angeles. No historical exposure series is supplied to settle the broader claim.
- Portability does not establish equal performance across environments. Citing external research, 256dpi [[./thread#^c44191164|256dpi]] says the SCD41 and SenseAir S8 compare well indoors but the S8 performs better outdoors; the smaller SCD41 suited Air Lab's design. After aurelwu [[./thread#^c44190033|aurelwu]] asks about elevation changes, 256dpi [[./thread#^c44196261|256dpi]] says pressure compensation is not yet implemented. jdalgetty's [[./thread#^c44199834|jdalgetty]] moisture-resistance question receives no supplied answer.

## Lived experiences

- burnt-resistor [[./thread#^c44199220|burnt-resistor]] built an Arduino/PMS5003 monitor during the 2020 Northern California fires and reports readings above 900 µg/m³, with visibility around 30 metres. They wore a full-face P100 respirator outdoors and bought a BlueAir purifier. They also report fan-bearing failures requiring repair. The concentration is a firsthand DIY-instrument report, without reference validation or a specified particle-size channel in the comment.
- voxlax [[./thread#^c44195369|voxlax]] operates one monitor in a city garden and another indoors, describes local PM2.5 as well above the yearly recommendation they cite, and automates HEPA purifiers through Home Assistant. They manually balance window opening, filter noise and an indoor target below 2–3 µg/m³. That indoor figure is an operating target; the comment supplies no time series showing how consistently it is achieved.
- joker99 [[./thread#^c44190519|joker99]] associates nighttime waking with high bedroom CO2 and reports improved sleep and home-office performance after using monitoring. Their follow-up, joker99 [[./thread#^c44199400|joker99]], specifies opening the window before bed and leaving the bedroom door open, rather than automatically opening windows overnight. No CO2 concentrations, sleep recordings or controlled comparison are provided.
- m4r1k [[./thread#^c44190650|m4r1k]] reports substantially less severe spring and year-round allergy symptoms after maintaining indoor recirculation and HEPA filters in every room. They use monitoring to check filtration. This is a household before-and-after account, without quantified symptom changes, exposure records or isolation of which allergen or pollutant changed.
- Modified3019 [[./thread#^c44193601|Modified3019]] reports painful dry sinuses and nosebleeds when indoor humidity falls below 40\%, with personal comfort around 55–60\%. In contrast, dylan604 [[./thread#^c44191825|dylan604]] reports discomfort at 55\% indoors, an outdoor reading of 81\%, and difficulty keeping indoors below 50\%. These conflicting comfort preferences are explicitly personal and occur in different environments.
- jadbox [[./thread#^c44194281|jadbox]] says an AirGradient revealed deterioration when drying polyester clothes and helped them avoid burning cooking oils by controlling temperatures. They provide no channel or concentration. When sizzle [[./thread#^c44198565|sizzle]] asks whether this means unhealthy CO2, eddyfromtheblok [[./thread#^c44198609|eddyfromtheblok]] only hypothesizes particulates and separately reports lint collecting in a purifier prefilter. That reply does not establish polyester-derived PM2.5 or CO2 emissions.
- Fnoord [[./thread#^c44198090|Fnoord]] reports higher CO2-meter readings when four people share a bed, during cooking and at birthday parties, without numerical values. They usually notice poor air themselves but bought another meter for a mother-in-law with reduced smell. This illustrates perceived usefulness under different sensory circumstances; it does not establish that the CO2 meter identifies the fumes she cannot smell.
- radicality [[./thread#^c44192960|radicality]] reports an ESP32/SCD41 setup showing rapid changes when an oven starts, a window opens or they enter a room after the night. They collect data every five seconds and prefer this setup to their uHoo. Their assessment that it is accurate is based on responsiveness and plausibility, not a supplied reference comparison.
- bethekidyouwant [[./thread#^c44192274|bethekidyouwant]] loses confidence in inexpensive home CO2 and VOC sensors after reviewing months of graphs. Conversely, GeoAtreides [[./thread#^c44196522|GeoAtreides]] reports years of agreement between SCD30 and SCD41 sensors and broad agreement among several VOC sensors, while noting different sensitivities and SCD41 voltage sensitivity. These are conflicting deployment experiences; agreement among instruments alone does not establish absolute accuracy.
- hedora [[./thread#^c44192825|hedora]] finds several years of Ambient Weather PM2.5/PM10/CO2 readings highly plausible. In a calibration-related reply, hedora [[./thread#^c44192851|hedora]] reports living-room readings below 400 ppm, attributes them to temperature-dependent CO2 exchange with new concrete floors, and infers that a simplistic baseline recalibration is not occurring. The low readings are reported observations; the concrete mechanism and calibration inference are not independently demonstrated.
- clvx [[./thread#^c44191104|clvx]] reports persistent, wind-dependent odor around a neighboring marijuana-growing facility and says neighbors within roughly a mile notice it, while the operator disputes producing a smell. The operator is reportedly willing to measure and mitigate. No pollutant identification, concentration, health outcome or completed measurement campaign is supplied.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The health and cognition claims remain observational. joker99 [[./thread#^c44190519|joker99]] reports sleep and office-performance improvements, while strogonoff [[./thread#^c44191770|strogonoff]] attributes sleepiness and reduced mental capacity to elevated gases and describes overnight CO2 as unsafe by an unspecified national standard. Without numerical exposure histories, identified standards or controlled assessments, this unit cannot establish a causal concentration–response relationship.
- NO2 and traffic exposure are largely evidence gaps here. homebrewer [[./thread#^c44190020|homebrewer]] values what they call NO2 measurement, but the advertised VOC/NOx capability is not accompanied by NO2-specific concentrations or validation; 256dpi [[./thread#^c44192268|256dpi]] says laboratory testing remains planned. benchly [[./thread#^c44190387|benchly]] only plans vehicle-mounted monitoring. voxlax's [[./thread#^c44195369|voxlax]] city-smog account does not identify traffic as its source, and radicality's [[./thread#^c44192960|radicality]] oven account does not identify fuel type or NO2.
- Absolute accuracy, drift and device-level uncertainty remain unsettled. Eextra953 [[./thread#^c44192158|Eextra953]] requests reference testing and unit-to-unit characterization; 256dpi [[./thread#^c44192268|256dpi]] says only component datasheet specifications are available pending lab tests. SeasonalEnnui [[./thread#^c44195693|SeasonalEnnui]] asks for SCD30/SCD41 stability comparisons, but 256dpi [[./thread#^c44196052|256dpi]] reports choosing size and planning calibration options rather than providing such data. 256dpi [[./thread#^c44192545|256dpi]] also reports roughly one degree of charging-related warming without proper testing.
- AirGradient reliability reports conflict substantially. cyberax [[./thread#^c44195854|cyberax]] reports frequent lockups, and joker99 [[./thread#^c44190519|joker99]] reports reboots, hung controllers and replacement SenseAir sensors. ted\_dunning [[./thread#^c44196842|ted\_dunning]] reports three devices across generations running for years without hangs, while andyfleming [[./thread#^c44198232|andyfleming]] also reports no lockups. The supplied replies establish neither a common failure cause nor a representative failure rate.
- The practical adequacy of VOC-derived CO2 remains disputed after the measurement method is clarified. sedgjh23 [[./thread#^c44200780|sedgjh23]] reports large EcoBee/AirThings discrepancies; bhouston [[./thread#^c44201864|bhouston]] defends the normal-household correlation and asks how large the differences were and what VOC-producing activities occurred. No substantive supplied reply quantifies the discrepancy or identifies which instrument was closer to the true CO2 concentration.
- The best response when indoor CO2 and outdoor particles are simultaneously high is not demonstrated. strogonoff [[./thread#^c44191770|strogonoff]] proposes ventilating first and filtering afterward, but provides no comparative exposure assessment or optimal duration. Their activated-carbon suggestion for VOCs is explicitly outside their firsthand experience. voxlax [[./thread#^c44195369|voxlax]] describes a workable personal balancing routine, without establishing a universally preferable ventilation or gas-filtration strategy.
