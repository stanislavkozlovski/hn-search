## Description

Participants compare a PC-fan and furnace-filter air purifier build with commercial units, discussing upfront cost, replacement filter expense, repairability, operating costs, noise, durability, and airflow performance. They also mention alternative DIY designs, open-source configuration tools, and use of similar builds for woodworking dust.
Tags: DIY air purifier, Corsi-Rosenthal box, furnace filters, PC fans, particulate matter

## Relevance

The comments compare DIY and commercial air purifiers on filter cost, operating costs, noise, repairability, and reported usefulness for woodworking dust. [[./thread#^c49368637|terribleperson]] [[./thread#^c49371690|Nukit]] [[./thread#^c49371738|Nukit]] [[./thread#^c49383837|spl757]]

## Arguments

- Replying to xnx’s Coway price comparison, terribleperson [[./thread#^c49368637|terribleperson]] argues that furnace-filter replacements are cheaper than most purifier filters. Nukit [[./thread#^c49371738|Nukit]] adds repairability, lower power and consumable costs, and quieter operation for a given clean-air delivery rate (CADR). These are comparative claims; the supplied comments contain no supporting measurements or running-cost calculations.
- Nukit [[./thread#^c49371690|Nukit]] presents an open-source builder that generates fabrication files and calculates configuration performance, and claims DIY and metal PC-fan filter boxes differ in durability rather than performance. Youden [[./thread#^c49371016|Youden]] describes another furnace-filter/PC-fan design and thinks it could cost substantially less than a PC-case build.

## Counterarguments

- xnx [[./thread#^c49368574|xnx]] quotes a build cost of approximately \$300–\$350 CAD and calls it about the same as a Coway. This challenges an automatic purchase-price advantage for this particular DIY design; it does not address the operating-cost and repairability advantages raised in replies.
- Nukit [[./thread#^c49371690|Nukit]] acknowledges durability as a distinction between construction approaches. The claimed performance equivalence therefore does not establish that their practical lifetimes or ownership experiences are equivalent.

## Assumptions

- The ownership-cost case made by terribleperson [[./thread#^c49368637|terribleperson]] and Nukit [[./thread#^c49371738|Nukit]] implicitly depends on replacement-filter and electricity savings being sufficient for the owner’s usage. Neither supplies filter lifetimes, operating hours, electricity prices, or a total-cost comparison.
- Youden [[./thread#^c49371016|Youden]] explicitly frames the alternative design’s lower cost as an expectation. ChiperSoft [[./thread#^c49377016|ChiperSoft]] similarly offers a product idea for PC-case manufacturers during an asserted self-build-market slump; this is speculation about supply, not evidence of an available product or its air-cleaning performance.

## Conditions

- The \$300–\$350 figure quoted by xnx [[./thread#^c49368574|xnx]] is in Canadian dollars and concerns the featured build. It does not establish prices elsewhere, the commenter’s location, or the cost of every DIY configuration.
- terribleperson [[./thread#^c49368637|terribleperson]] identifies Costco and surplus purchasing as opportunities for additional filter savings. The relevance of that suggestion depends on access to suitable filters and local prices, which are not supplied.
- Youden [[./thread#^c49371016|Youden]] says the alternative design’s files require Kickstarter backing and would not arrive until November. Nukit [[./thread#^c49371690|Nukit]] describes an open-source alternative supporting 3D printing, laser cutting, or hand-cut foamcore; these offer different fabrication requirements, without quantified labor or material costs.

## Lived experiences

- spl757 [[./thread#^c49383837|spl757]] reports building a similar box-fan/four-filter unit for woodworking, especially sanding, and says it works pretty well on a budget. This is a firsthand, qualitative usefulness report. It includes no particle measurements, particle-size breakdown, sensor details, or reported health effects.
