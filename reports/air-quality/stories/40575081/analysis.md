# [CO2 helps viruses stay alive longer in the air](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40575081)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion examines research suggesting that elevated CO₂ can help airborne respiratory viruses remain infectious, including a proposed effect on the pH of droplets and how the finding may refine explanations for ventilation’s benefits. Participants debate the headline, study implications, and whether CO₂ should be treated as a direct risk as well as a ventilation indicator. Much of the thread turns to indoor CO₂ monitoring: sensor accuracy, calibration, cost, privacy, ventilation practices, and possible uses for tracking occupancy. Other topics include health effects of indoor CO₂, houseplants and CO₂ removal, air exchangers, and the distinction between outdoor atmospheric CO₂ and indoor air quality.
Tags: air quality, CO₂, ventilation, airborne viruses, indoor monitoring, sensors

## Relevance

The discussion connects CO2 to indoor ventilation and possible viral persistence, and includes firsthand monitoring of CO2 buildup, window-opening time, cross-ventilation, and symptoms. It also contrasts monitors and flags calibration and eCO2 accuracy limits. ## Arguments

- s0rce [[./thread#^c40576170|s0rce]] interprets the reported research as showing increased viral aerosol survival and infectiousness at elevated CO2, while acknowledging not having examined the paper closely. gpm [[./thread#^c40579286|gpm]] explains that the relevant chemistry occurs in water-containing droplets, and quercusa [[./thread#^c40578718|quercusa]] describes CO2 buffering those particles nearer a virus-friendly neutral pH. These are interpretations of reported research, not firsthand infection measurements.
- jefftk [[./thread#^c40576395|jefftk]] distinguishes CO2 as a ventilation proxy from CO2 directly affecting viral persistence: filtration could remove infectious particles while leaving conditions that prolong the activity of particles it misses. SwellJoe [[./thread#^c40576195|SwellJoe]] [[./thread#^c40576810|SwellJoe]] instead emphasizes continuity with existing ventilation advice. Both favor ventilation; they differ over how substantially the proposed mechanism changes its justification.
- viraptor [[./thread#^c40578708|viraptor]] links an article claiming cognitive impairment from elevated indoor CO2. serial\_dev [[./thread#^c40578900|serial\_dev]] lists sleepiness, headaches, and poorer physical and mental performance as reasons to open windows. logifail [[./thread#^c40576453|logifail]] quotes guidance associating 1,000–2,000 ppm with drowsiness and 2,000–5,000 ppm with headaches, stuffiness, and concentration problems. These are claims and quoted guidance, not symptom measurements supplied by those commenters.
- jefftk [[./thread#^c40576454|jefftk]] argues that bringing in outside air, with filtration when polluted, is cheaper than removing CO2 chemically at contemporary outdoor concentrations. unholythree [[./thread#^c40576814|unholythree]] favors particulate filtration and temperature conditioning of incoming air; gpm [[./thread#^c40579385|gpm]] recommends heat exchange to limit heating and cooling losses while ventilating public spaces. No comparative cost or exposure trial accompanies these recommendations.
- SoftTalker [[./thread#^c40576600|SoftTalker]] advocates houseplants as a modest supplement rather than a complete solution, while acknowledging maintenance effort. mistrial9 [[./thread#^c40576624|mistrial9]] argues that calculations about fully offsetting a person miss possible incremental benefits in an already ventilated home, and also mentions benefits people experience from having plants beyond their effect on air.

## Counterarguments

- IvyMike [[./thread#^c40577044|IvyMike]] identifies US Navy experiments as an outlier to the quoted CO2 symptom guidance, but supplies no experimental details in this export. Separately, mgh2 [[./thread#^c40585006|mgh2]] speculates that lower CO2 explains good ideas during walks; burnished [[./thread#^c40586061|burnished]] replies that the walking effect persists in well-ventilated buildings. Neither exchange establishes a causal explanation.
- eternityforest [[./thread#^c40588415|eternityforest]] warns that extremely cheap devices may estimate CO2 from VOCs instead of measuring it with NDIR. drjasonharrison [[./thread#^c40660740|drjasonharrison]] adds that genuine CO2 detection does not guarantee trustworthy displayed values: calibration or data-fitting algorithms can introduce large errors. This challenges treating inexpensive listings as interchangeable with validated instruments, without establishing that every cheap monitor is inaccurate.
- EVa5I7bHFq9mnYK [[./thread#^c40585125|EVa5I7bHFq9mnYK]] challenges dilippkumar's outdoor-baseline accuracy check by asserting that devices periodically assign their lowest reading approximately 420 ppm. If applicable, agreement after opening windows would not independently demonstrate accuracy. The comment's blanket claim about all devices is itself unverified in the supplied discussion.
- Responding to autoexec's question about activated-carbon prefilters [[./thread#^c40576760|autoexec]], TylerE [[./thread#^c40577282|TylerE]] says those filters target volatile organics and do not remove CO2. alexey-salmin [[./thread#^c40577151|alexey-salmin]] also questions the required capture capacity. The discussion therefore does not support treating an ordinary residential air purifier as a CO2 scrubber.
- IvyMike [[./thread#^c40577078|IvyMike]] argues that substantial CO2 uptake requires substantial plant growth. johnsimer [[./thread#^c40576437|johnsimer]] recalls roughly 300 plants per person, while Jarmsy [[./thread#^c40576885|Jarmsy]] links an estimate closer to 700; neither reports a household experiment. mrob [[./thread#^c40577546|mrob]] and Retric [[./thread#^c40576895|Retric]] correct another calculation: approximately 1 kg of exhaled CO2 contains about 0.27 kg of carbon, not 1 kg. The proposed scale remains disputed, and the numerical estimates should not be treated as measured requirements.
- blueflow [[./thread#^c40576156|blueflow]] predicts that rising outdoor CO2 will make ventilation ineffective within three or four decades. gpm [[./thread#^c40579550|gpm]] disputes that implication using an approximate 3 ppm annual increase, yielding around 120 ppm over 40 years. genman [[./thread#^c40579759|genman]] challenges constant-rate extrapolation and emphasizes reduced indoor headroom and higher ventilation energy requirements. This is a disagreement about future personal exposure and ventilation feasibility, not a demonstrated household outcome.

## Assumptions

- Calling CO2 a proxy for general air quality, as dilippkumar [[./thread#^c40577089|dilippkumar]] and SwellJoe [[./thread#^c40576195|SwellJoe]] do, assumes that occupancy and inadequate dilution track the hazards of interest. jefftk's filtration example [[./thread#^c40576395|jefftk]] shows why that relationship can change when pollutants are removed differently. The comments do not establish that a satisfactory CO2 reading also establishes satisfactory PM2.5 or NO2.
- Interpreting one monitor as representative of a room or home requires assumptions about placement and air mixing. taeric [[./thread#^c40584047|taeric]] suspects localized readings and questions comparisons between a large room and a converted closet. This is an unresolved sampling concern, not evidence that identical concentrations necessarily have different physiological effects because room sizes differ.
- OutOfHere [[./thread#^c40581171|OutOfHere]] derives an asserted optimum below 300 ppm from pre-industrial conditions and human evolution, and claims drowsiness can begin at 600–800 ppm. The supplied comment provides no experiment establishing either that optimum or that onset threshold; an evolutionary argument is not itself a measured dose-response relationship.
- The plant proposals require a favorable net balance across growth, respiration, soil processes, ventilation, and time of day. Retric [[./thread#^c40576895|Retric]] and mistrial9 [[./thread#^c40576624|mistrial9]] challenge closed-system assumptions; gpm [[./thread#^c40579179|gpm]] points to nighttime plant respiration, and alexey-salmin [[./thread#^c40576658|alexey-salmin]] to soil microbes producing CO2. somenameforme [[./thread#^c40577953|somenameforme]] instead emphasizes carbon storage in soil. None quantifies the net effect in an occupied room.
- Using submarine or spacecraft CO2 removal as evidence for a practical residential product assumes those technologies transfer economically and operationally to homes. hollerith [[./thread#^c40577224|hollerith]] and gpm [[./thread#^c40579228|gpm]] describe institutional examples, not household installations. Another proposed shortcut—adding nitrogen and oxygen to dilute CO2—is suggested by IncreasePosts [[./thread#^c40577159|IncreasePosts]] and rejected by EVa5I7bHFq9mnYK [[./thread#^c40585306|EVa5I7bHFq9mnYK]] on concentration grounds; no working residential example is supplied.

## Conditions

- Cold weather and building airtightness shape the ventilation problem. dilippkumar [[./thread#^c40577089|dilippkumar]] keeps windows closed much of the year in a cold location. twothamendment [[./thread#^c40578357|twothamendment]], also in a cold climate, considers monitoring especially useful in modern, well-sealed homes. These circumstances limit how broadly either household's ventilation experience can be generalized.
- Opening windows has little equipment cost, which somenameforme [[./thread#^c40577898|somenameforme]] emphasizes, but conditioning replacement air can carry a cost. wcoenen [[./thread#^c40591728|wcoenen]] reports heat escaping through a cracked window with little apparent CO2 improvement. gpm [[./thread#^c40579385|gpm]] recommends heat exchangers to reduce heating and cooling losses; the thread supplies no energy bills or measured savings.
- dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c40583708|dzhiurgis]] estimates an ERV at about \$1,000 plus installation labor, and says in another reply that installing piping can cost more than the equipment [[./thread#^c40583700|dzhiurgis]]. These are commenter estimates without a specified location, building layout, or contractor quote.
- EVa5I7bHFq9mnYK [[./thread#^c40585426|EVa5I7bHFq9mnYK]] contrasts a roughly \$300 monitor with \$10–20 marketplace devices. eternityforest [[./thread#^c40588415|eternityforest]] and drjasonharrison [[./thread#^c40660740|drjasonharrison]] dispute the implied equivalence of their measurements. The practical financial question is therefore price for demonstrated measurement quality, rather than price for a display labeled CO2.
- Outdoor reference values require geographic and temporal context. In this 2024 discussion, elevation [[./thread#^c40576415|elevation]] cites 419.3 ppm, and blueflow [[./thread#^c40576771|blueflow]] acknowledges having misremembered approximately 420 as 480. coldtea [[./thread#^c40576729|coldtea]] asks whether global averages represent populated locations; mike\_hearn [[./thread#^c40578282|mike\_hearn]] describes station and seasonal differences. These comments do not provide street-level exposure measurements for participants' homes.
- A higher outdoor CO2 baseline would leave less room below a chosen indoor target. genman [[./thread#^c40579759|genman]] argues that maintaining that target could require more air exchange and more energy for moving and conditioning air. This is a conditional operational argument; its magnitude depends on the disputed outdoor trajectory and the selected target.

## Lived experiences

- dilippkumar [[./thread#^c40577089|dilippkumar]] reports approximately two years using an Airthings monitor: their apartment needed about 25 minutes of window opening to reach roughly 500 ppm, versus an assumed five minutes, and two additional occupants accelerated accumulation. They acknowledge having no independent calibration equipment; prolonged ventilation brought readings within about 50 ppm of a nonlocal reference [[./thread#^c40577169|dilippkumar]]. This supports usefulness for observing changes more strongly than absolute accuracy.
- wcoenen [[./thread#^c40591728|wcoenen]] reports that merely cracking a window changed monitored CO2 little while losing heat, whereas opening several windows or doors widely for cross-ventilation lowered it within a few minutes. This differs from dilippkumar's 25-minute experience [[./thread#^c40577089|dilippkumar]], but the homes, airflow, starting concentrations, and final targets are not shown to be comparable.
- twothamendment [[./thread#^c40578357|twothamendment]] says Awair readings motivated installation of an air exchanger and helped determine the necessary exchange rate. They also report recognizing children leaving their rooms and the household returning home from CO2 changes, with arrival visible within about a minute [[./thread#^c40593450|twothamendment]]. No before-and-after concentrations or health outcomes are given.
- jauer [[./thread#^c40577309|jauer]] reports that Airthings, Aranet4, and a Vaisala GMP252 probe track closely enough for household use. The Vaisala's quoted ±40 ppm specification is a manufacturer rating, not a demonstrated error bound for all the devices. Agreement among instruments is useful corroboration but does not establish an independently measured absolute error.
- drjasonharrison [[./thread#^c40660740|drjasonharrison]] reports sometimes erratic readings from an inexpensive, battery-powered INKBIRD monitor. They describe corroborating dry-ice and isopropanol tests distinguishing actual CO2 detection from VOC estimation, and substantial overshoot and undershoot relative to better monitors. They attribute the behavior to calibration or fitting algorithms; raw measurements and a definitive software diagnosis are absent.
- fwungy [[./thread#^c40577529|fwungy]] reports feeling better when a cheap Amazon monitor shows lower CO2 and says this increased their appreciation of fresh air. In contrast, taeric [[./thread#^c40579517|taeric]] notices no corresponding change in mood or alertness despite sensor readings; dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c40583592|dzhiurgis]] agrees while still enjoying fresh air. These conflicting subjective accounts neither establish nor exclude a physiological effect.
- dzhiurgis [[./thread#^c40591330|dzhiurgis]] reports that a bedroom becomes stuffy within an hour with windows closed and ventilation off, or within minutes during sexual activity. This is a firsthand comfort observation without accompanying CO2 concentrations, and it does not demonstrate an effect on sleep quality.
- OutOfHere [[./thread#^c40581191|OutOfHere]] recommends an Amazon-listed meter and vouches for its readings rising or falling monotonically with ventilation. That establishes a reported response to changing conditions, not independent calibration, absolute accuracy, or superiority to another model.

## Unresolved disagreements

- The concentration at which CO2 itself produces meaningful symptoms or cognitive impairment remains disputed here. logifail [[./thread#^c40576453|logifail]] quotes relatively higher symptom ranges, OutOfHere [[./thread#^c40581171|OutOfHere]] asserts effects at 600–800 ppm, and IvyMike [[./thread#^c40577044|IvyMike]] points to contradictory Navy research without details. fwungy's positive association [[./thread#^c40577529|fwungy]] and taeric's null experience [[./thread#^c40579517|taeric]] do not separate CO2 from other changes accompanying ventilation. No controlled cognitive scores or sleep-quality measurements are supplied.
- The reported viral mechanism does not establish a universal indoor CO2 target or quantify the relative benefits of ventilation and filtration. rapjr9 [[./thread#^c40578153|rapjr9]] suggests reconsidering 1,000 ppm in favor of 800 ppm or lower, while explicitly calling for replication and acknowledging unknown responses of other viruses. jefftk's residual-virus argument [[./thread#^c40576395|jefftk]] supplies no measured size of the effect and does not show that filtration becomes ineffective.
- Residential CO2 scrubbing remains a disputed proposal. J\_Shelby\_J [[./thread#^c40576330|J\_Shelby\_J]] says residential products are unavailable, while SoftTalker [[./thread#^c40576237|SoftTalker]] considers construction straightforward. OutOfHere [[./thread#^c40581213|OutOfHere]] raises concentration, combustible-gas, and product-handling objections to a proposed chemical approach; somenameforme [[./thread#^c40587073|somenameforme]] calls it safe and suggests other absorbents. No household performance, maintenance, safety, or operating-cost evidence resolves these claims.
- The best affordable monitor is unanswered: amelius [[./thread#^c40577403|amelius]] asks directly about price versus accuracy without receiving a supplied answer. jauer's cross-device agreement [[./thread#^c40577309|jauer]], EVa5I7bHFq9mnYK's calibration assertion [[./thread#^c40585125|EVa5I7bHFq9mnYK]], and drjasonharrison's malfunction account [[./thread#^c40660740|drjasonharrison]] do not establish which models remain accurate under particular calibration settings or occupancy patterns.
- For source unit 40575081, part 0, the window-opening tradeoff involving outdoor pollution remains largely unmeasured. jefftk [[./thread#^c40576454|jefftk]] explicitly qualifies outdoor-air replacement with filtration if it is polluted, and dilippkumar [[./thread#^c40577169|dilippkumar]] mentions traffic only as a possible difference between reference locations. There are no PM2.5 or NO2 readings, firsthand cooking or wildfire-smoke exposure accounts, or measured examples of window opening increasing those pollutants. Lowering CO2 cannot be taken here as proof of lower overall exposure.
- chasil [[./thread#^c40577964|chasil]] asks whether public-space CO2 scrubbing could reduce COVID transmission and whether hospitals already use it. gpm [[./thread#^c40579385|gpm]] responds by advocating outdoor-air exchange and citing ventilation recommendations. The supplied exchange does not establish existing hospital scrubbing practices, a measured change in transmission, or an advantage of scrubbers over ventilation.
