# [Build a do-it-yourself home air purifier for about \$25](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=18445652)

[[./thread|Discussion]]

## Description

Discussion explores building a low-cost air purifier from a box fan and furnace filter, including filter ratings and placement, airflow, noise, motor strain, safety, and whether measured results show meaningful room-wide particle reduction. Participants compare DIY setups with commercial purifiers and discuss air-quality monitors, wildfire smoke, allergies, ventilation, and filter maintenance. A substantial thread turns to household mold, moisture control, dehumidifiers, building practices, tenant protections, and safe cleaning; other branches consider plants, sensor projects, and product claims.
Tags: DIY, air-purifiers, air-filtration, indoor-air-quality, wildfire-smoke, HEPA, MERV, air-quality-sensors, mold, ventilation, dehumidification, product-reviews

## Relevance

The comments cover practical filtration, firsthand wildfire-smoke and traffic-pollution measurements, reported symptom relief, sensor limitations, and tradeoffs involving fan noise, airflow, and filter performance. They also discuss when ventilation or open windows help, and when outdoor pollution or CO2 buildup can make that choice less straightforward. ## Arguments

- Particle filtration has direct, though uncontrolled, support in this discussion. rustychris [[./thread#^c18453178|rustychris]] measured falling indoor PM2.5 during wildfire smoke, while Dave\_Rosenthal [[./thread#^c18446281|Dave\_Rosenthal]] measured substantially lower particle counts with furnace filtration and a bedroom purifier. These observations support the possibility of useful exposure reduction; they do not establish that every fan/filter combination performs similarly.
- Filtration should match the pollutant. leon\_sbt [[./thread#^c18446103|leon\_sbt]] distinguishes particulate capture from gas adsorption, describing prefilters, HEPA and activated carbon as serving different purposes. They recommend pressure-drop monitoring for particle filters and target-gas monitoring for carbon exhaustion. This is a technical recommendation, not a demonstrated NO2, CO2 or VOC reduction in the supplied accounts.
- Opening windows is conditional on outdoor air quality. After initially recommending sealing drafts and briefly airing the house, peferron [[./thread#^c18448106|peferron]] explicitly clarifies that the advice assumed polluted outdoor air, based on living in China and experiencing California wildfires. They favor more ventilation when outside air is cleaner, subject to temperature comfort.
- Indoor activities can dominate particle exposure even when outdoor pollution motivates buying a purifier. peferron [[./thread#^c18447391|peferron]] identifies cooking as the largest contributor in their home monitoring. adamwong246 [[./thread#^c18445877|adamwong246]] reports benefits with pets and recommends running filtration during vacuuming; elektor [[./thread#^c18450716|elektor]] observes automatic fan responses after extinguishing candles and moving around rugs.
- For mold, reducing airborne spores addresses only part of the problem. arendtio [[./thread#^c18449062|arendtio]] argues for finding water intrusion, controlling humidity and removing favorable growth conditions. proee [[./thread#^c18447949|proee]] emphasizes condensation and material susceptibility in Hawaii, while erikpukinskis [[./thread#^c18471267|erikpukinskis]] discusses drainage, ventilation and continuity of building-envelope layers. These comments connect air quality to the building's moisture conditions.
- Filter area and delivered airflow matter alongside filtration ratings. akeck [[./thread#^c18446645|akeck]] describes a three-filter enclosure, johnbrodie [[./thread#^c18446023|johnbrodie]] reports using deep pleats to increase surface area, and linschn [[./thread#^c18448250|linschn]] explains placing a coarse prefilter before the finer filter. The proposed benefits are maintaining flow and delaying loading, rather than simply choosing the highest rating.
- Reported benefits extend beyond instrument readings. clairity [[./thread#^c18446294|clairity]] describes relief from coughing, sneezing and a runny nose; 51lver [[./thread#^c18445934|51lver]] reports that their wife could sleep again during wildfire smoke; dmourati [[./thread#^c18447271|dmourati]] reports improved sleep with a bedroom purifier. These are firsthand perceived outcomes, without controlled attribution or clinical measurements.
- Measurement can make otherwise invisible changes actionable. MarkMc [[./thread#^c18447739|MarkMc]] values checking pollution and purifier performance in Jakarta and Singapore, and pwarner [[./thread#^c18446501|pwarner]] observes readings rising when the furnace fan stops and falling when it restarts. These accounts support monitoring trends, although they do not establish the accuracy of every displayed concentration.

## Counterarguments

- Clean air immediately downstream of a filter is insufficient evidence of whole-room performance. apo [[./thread#^c18446652|apo]] challenges extrapolating a roughly 90\% outlet reduction to room exposure and requests controlled room comparisons. CompelTechnic [[./thread#^c18447119|CompelTechnic]] points to woodshop recordings and gnopgnip [[./thread#^c18446867|gnopgnip]] links a study, but their underlying measurements and methods are not supplied here.
- Some DIY combinations performed poorly. yborg [[./thread#^c18447714|yborg]] reports that a \$15 box fan with MERV 13 drew much of its air back through the front corners rather than through the filter; woogiewonka [[./thread#^c18449768|woogiewonka]] also reports substantial airflow restriction. Their experiences qualify successful DIY accounts without disproving those accounts: the fan, filter area, sealing and operating speed differed.
- Noise can defeat the intended sleep or comfort benefit. rubatuga [[./thread#^c18446854|rubatuga]] could not sleep beside the DIY purifier and eventually ran it only while away, which still bothered a roommate. scurvy [[./thread#^c18513480|scurvy]] describes commercial Vornado units as effective but loud. Effective particle removal and tolerable continuous operation are separate requirements.
- Sealing a room does not address every air-quality concern. joncrocks [[./thread#^c18449282|joncrocks]], replying directly to the sealing recommendation, warns about CO2 accumulation without ventilation. strstr [[./thread#^c18448423|strstr]] adds that an externally vented kitchen extractor can draw replacement air from elsewhere, potentially importing pollution during poor outdoor conditions.
- Natural ventilation is not a universal dehumidifier. In response to Thlom's expectation that houses should dry through airflow, briandear [[./thread#^c18450967|briandear]] recalls severe discomfort with an attic fan during Houston summers and argues that humid outdoor air can make conditions worse. fyfy18 [[./thread#^c18449699|fyfy18]] similarly clarifies that mild, damp weather on the UK south coast differs from genuinely cold winter conditions.
- Plants are disputed as substitutes for filtration. alakin [[./thread#^c18448054|alakin]] distinguishes the cited NASA VOC work from PM2.5 removal and questions practical plant, light and circulation requirements. TeMPOraL [[./thread#^c18448280|TeMPOraL]] emphasizes the sealed experimental setting. The positive park-air impression from pokemongoaway [[./thread#^c18449066|pokemongoaway]] is an outdoor anecdote, not a measurement of indoor plant filtration.
- Plant-based interventions can introduce other concerns. cestith [[./thread#^c18453062|cestith]] identifies orchids and rubber plants as potential allergens; thewhitetulip [[./thread#^c18457305|thewhitetulip]] acknowledges this and accepts commercial purification where suitable plants cannot be used. Their earlier enthusiasm therefore should not be read as an unqualified recommendation for every household.
- A purifier cannot establish that a mold-contaminated home is safe. benbojangles [[./thread#^c18446603|benbojangles]] reports initial relief followed by severe illness and believes mold grew into the paper filter. kijin [[./thread#^c18447921|kijin]] warns about wet filters, while arendtio [[./thread#^c18449062|arendtio]] stresses that an ongoing growth source remains despite airborne filtration. Neither the filter-growth mechanism nor the illness attribution is independently established in this account.
- The suggestion that cleaner air might weaken immunity remains speculative. wenc [[./thread#^c18446697|wenc]] raises a hygiene-hypothesis concern; hourislate [[./thread#^c18446978|hourislate]] counters with continuing urban exhaust and brake-dust exposure, and joelcollinsdc [[./thread#^c18449597|joelcollinsdc]] links particulate-health research. None supplies longitudinal evidence that home filtration harms immunity.
- High prices and advanced technology claims do not settle effectiveness. abalone [[./thread#^c18446551|abalone]] questions Molekule's smoke and VOC claims, and buzzy\_hacker [[./thread#^c18446982|buzzy\_hacker]] quotes a Wirecutter account of testing conditions it would not accept. Conversely, Elien237 [[./thread#^c18449866|Elien237]] argues for professionally developed and tested purifiers. These are competing evaluations, not a supplied independent comparison demonstrating every commercial unit's superiority.
- Mold and odor treatments can themselves create hazardous exposure. endymi0n [[./thread#^c18449241|endymi0n]] and hblanks [[./thread#^c18449602|hblanks]] warn about gases from incompatible cleaners. morganvachon [[./thread#^c18456137|morganvachon]] later clarifies the intended sequence of their earlier cleaning suggestion, but that clarification is not a safety validation. lmilcin [[./thread#^c18448406|lmilcin]] also reports a chemical burn during aggressive remediation.

## Assumptions

- The conclusions apply to story 18445652, part 0, a 2018 discussion containing selected experiences and technical disagreements. Contrasting outcomes—such as rustychris's measured improvement [[./thread#^c18453178|rustychris]] and yborg's poor airflow [[./thread#^c18447714|yborg]]—should remain separate observations, rather than being converted into population-wide HN agreement.
- Dylos particle counts require interpretation before being called PM2.5 mass or AQI. Dave\_Rosenthal [[./thread#^c18446281|Dave\_Rosenthal]] describes channels counting particles larger than 0.5 and 2.5 micrometers and uses an internet-derived approximation of 0.005 times the smaller-threshold count to estimate micrograms per cubic meter. That conversion is a heuristic in this account, not a supplied gravimetric calibration.
- An outdoor comparison may be a spatial proxy rather than a measurement at the home. pwarner [[./thread#^c18446501|pwarner]] uses two PurpleAir sensors about ten miles away in opposite directions, assuming their agreement and shared valley make them representative. That supports contextual comparison but leaves immediate outdoor exposure uncertain.
- Device longevity and sensor agreement are different from absolute accuracy. peferron [[./thread#^c18448076|peferron]] reports years of reliable Dylos operation, while mmagin [[./thread#^c18446045|mmagin]] prefers PurpleAir's two-sensor outdoor model for internal cross-checking. Neither establishes calibration against a reference instrument. misev's projected SDS011 lifetime [[./thread#^c18448408|misev]] is likewise a duty-cycle estimate, not an observed lifespan.
- Claims that repeated passes eventually clean the room assume adequate mixing and removal relative to incoming pollution. giardini [[./thread#^c18447568|giardini]] argues that even partial filter coverage can work over time; LeoPanthera [[./thread#^c18451398|LeoPanthera]] emphasizes repeated passes. Those arguments do not quantify leakage, fresh particle generation, stagnant areas or the time required under continuing wildfire infiltration.
- Smell, visible filter dirt and automatic fan responses are imperfect substitutes for pollutant-specific measurements. LeoPanthera [[./thread#^c18447535|LeoPanthera]] reports loss of smoke odor, valarauca1 [[./thread#^c18446303|valarauca1]] sees a brown filter, and mattlondon [[./thread#^c18449738|mattlondon]] triggers a purifier with coffee breath. These observations show perceived or device responses, not independently measured PM2.5, NO2 or VOC clearance.
- Health improvements are observational and sometimes involve several simultaneous changes. ed\_balls [[./thread#^c18446198|ed\_balls]] combines a purifier, filtered window vents and a robot vacuum before reporting sinus relief. mattlondon [[./thread#^c18449738|mattlondon]] explicitly considers placebo effects. These accounts cannot isolate filtration from cleaning, ventilation, changing outdoor conditions or expectation.
- Linked studies, marketing claims and claimed expertise are evidence of what commenters argued, not independently checked research in this source unit. Examples include stevenwoo's summary of traffic and children's lung-growth research [[./thread#^c18460625|stevenwoo]], nathan\_f77's pollution-related reading [[./thread#^c18449361|nathan\_f77]], and Elien237's account of professional purifier testing [[./thread#^c18449866|Elien237]].
- A favorable dust observation need not establish a breathing-zone health benefit. CarVac [[./thread#^c18449884|CarVac]] reports that a filtered, positive-pressure computer case remained clean for four years; culot [[./thread#^c18446873|culot]] describes washable tracing paper for coarse dust. Neither provides evidence that those arrangements deliver the fine-particle removal or room coverage needed for wildfire exposure.

## Conditions

- The advertised low DIY cost depended on local supplies and existing equipment. valarauca1 [[./thread#^c18446303|valarauca1]] reused a fan and spent about \$17 on a filter plus \$3 on tape. In the UK, okasaki [[./thread#^c18448608|okasaki]] instead found a suitable filter listed at £140 and box fans at £40–50. These are historical prices, not a universally available \$25 solution.
- Wildfire demand and seasonal retail availability constrained access. LeoPanthera [[./thread#^c18446262|LeoPanthera]] describes finding no fans at Home Depot outside summer and possibly buying the last available box fan at a nearby Target. Alternative computer or automotive parts were suggested by Scoundreller [[./thread#^c18447201|Scoundreller]] and semi-extrinsic [[./thread#^c18447887|semi-extrinsic]], but no comparable room-level performance was demonstrated for those substitutions.
- Purchase price, replacement filters and useful service life all affect affordability. ed\_balls [[./thread#^c18446198|ed\_balls]] reports a \$137 imported Xiaomi and about \$55 yearly filters for a broader setup; dmourati [[./thread#^c18447271|dmourati]] reports an approximately \$800 IQAir and one roughly \$100 filter change after 1.5 years. ksec [[./thread#^c18447839|ksec]] explicitly objects to recurring replacement costs.
- Moisture control can impose substantial electricity costs. a012 [[./thread#^c18448130|a012]] reports that maintaining roughly 50\% humidity around the clock in a Southeast Asian studio doubled the monthly electricity bill. patrickbuckley [[./thread#^c18454008|patrickbuckley]] found a roughly \$200 dehumidifier effective after smaller, cheaper units disappointed. Neither supplies a standardized energy-efficiency comparison.
- Climate and season change whether outdoor ventilation helps with moisture. proee [[./thread#^c18447949|proee]] describes Hawaii's small dew-point spread and condensation risk; briandear [[./thread#^c18450967|briandear]] describes hot, humid Houston. Moru [[./thread#^c18449030|Moru]] explains why cold outdoor air can become dry when heated, whereas fyfy18 [[./thread#^c18449699|fyfy18]] reports mild coastal UK conditions of 15°C and 77\% outdoor RH.
- Temperature-based humidity control has comfort limits. kijin [[./thread#^c18450247|kijin]] questions the affordability and tolerability of heating a room to around 25°C. Tharkun [[./thread#^c18448484|Tharkun]] finds temperatures above 20°C uncomfortable, while Foobar8568 [[./thread#^c18448531|Foobar8568]] reports becoming comfortable with a constant 25°C in Switzerland. The discussion does not establish one universally acceptable temperature.
- Housing insecurity and landlord cooperation constrain source control. benbojangles [[./thread#^c18446603|benbojangles]] describes choosing between homelessness and help from a distant relative; nvarsj [[./thread#^c18449283|nvarsj]] reports an expensive London rental where the landlord refused mold repairs. Their legal options are disputed in replies, including Scoundreller [[./thread#^c18447141|Scoundreller]] and distances [[./thread#^c18450893|distances]], so lack of recourse should not be treated as an established rule about subletting.
- Expensive housing does not remove concerns about hidden moisture. clumsysmurf [[./thread#^c18447989|clumsysmurf]] relays a construction friend's account of soaked materials in a luxury Arizona development; rsync [[./thread#^c18450857|rsync]] personally observes rain-soaked framing subsequently enclosed in costly Marin homes. The former is secondhand and the latter does not include later inspection confirming mold inside the walls.
- Room layout and incoming air affect achievable cleanliness. lilbobbytables [[./thread#^c18447270|lilbobbytables]] reports smoke entering through a window air conditioner and plans another purifier for an open floor plan. adventured [[./thread#^c18446249|adventured]] notes the effect of air supplied by the wider building. gdubs [[./thread#^c18450101|gdubs]] relays an auditor's crawl-space infiltration claim, which is not a measurement establishing a universal percentage.
- Exposure circumstances ranged from acute wildfire events to recurrent seasonal pollution. ar\_turnbull [[./thread#^c18446487|ar\_turnbull]] bought a purifier when British Columbia smoke reached Calgary, while nathan\_f77 [[./thread#^c18448697|nathan\_f77]] describes two to three smoky months in Chiang Mai. nathan\_f77 [[./thread#^c18449361|nathan\_f77]] considers leaving seasonally or moving permanently, with pets and house-sitting affecting those choices.
- Monitoring hardware was not equally accessible. MarkMc [[./thread#^c18448727|MarkMc]] used an international forwarding service to obtain a Dylos for Jakarta, describing it as expensive. jly [[./thread#^c18451067|jly]] obtained a Laser Egg through eBay because US availability was difficult, while io\_io [[./thread#^c18445966|io\_io]] found many consumer options expensive or poorly reviewed.

## Lived experiences

- rustychris [[./thread#^c18453178|rustychris]] reports an Oakland installation using a 14×20-inch MERV 13 filter on a box fan, with the remaining opening covered. A PMS5003 showed indoor PM2.5 falling from 25 to 5 μg/m³ and remaining low. A later account [[./thread#^c18456382|rustychris]] explicitly cautions that the hobby sensor's absolute readings may be inaccurate. The accounts differ on two versus three hours and should not be counted as independent replications.
- Dave\_Rosenthal [[./thread#^c18446281|Dave\_Rosenthal]] reports that roughly MERV 11 furnace filters reduced larger particles strongly but lowered small-particle counts at a duct by only about 15–25\% relative to ambient. After several days, house counts were roughly one-third of outdoors. A bedroom BlueAir produced near-zero counts at its outlet and room counts around one-tenth of outdoors; the household also reported better sleep.
- pwarner [[./thread#^c18446024|pwarner]] reports indoor readings around 60 versus roughly 200 outdoors while using a Filtrete MPR 2800 furnace filter. The units are not specified in the comment. They later clarify that the outdoor figures came from remote sensors and describe indoor readings increasing when the furnace fan stopped, then decreasing after restart [[./thread#^c18446501|pwarner]].
- peferron [[./thread#^c18447391|peferron]] describes approximately a year of home particle logging using a Dylos, Raspberry Pi and time-series software, with cooking usually the largest contributor. Their five-year-old Dylos had run in hourly monitoring mode for about 1.5 years without reported failures, although size, fan noise, interface and connectivity were drawbacks [[./thread#^c18448076|peferron]].
- During Bay Area wildfire smoke, valarauca1 [[./thread#^c18446303|valarauca1]] built a purifier after waking with severe coughing and reports easier breathing indoors, continued coughing outdoors without a respirator, and four days of operation without overheating. LeoPanthera [[./thread#^c18447535|LeoPanthera]] separately reports that indoor smoke odor disappeared, but their sore throat persisted while they continued spending time outdoors. Neither supplies particle readings.
- 51lver [[./thread#^c18445934|51lver]] used a four-inch filter on a box fan's intake during Seattle wildfire smoke; it became gray within three days, and their wife could sleep again. jhenkens [[./thread#^c18445887|jhenkens]] reports that a similar intake-filter setup helped during the Thomas Fire despite reduced airflow, but a subsequently purchased commercial purifier worked better by their assessment.
- clairity [[./thread#^c18446294|clairity]] attributes the disappearance of persistent coughing, sneezing and a runny nose to a BlueAir 211+. leetbulb [[./thread#^c18445978|leetbulb]] reports major allergy improvement with Winix units and later says cooking odors clear quickly and indoor year-round allergies have resolved, while explicitly lacking a way to measure effectiveness [[./thread#^c18472093|leetbulb]].
- dmourati [[./thread#^c18447271|dmourati]] initially bought an IQAir to address VOC concerns after floor refinishing before moving in with a small child. They later ran it continuously in a bedroom and reported markedly better sleep. The account does not contain VOC measurements showing that the original safety objective was achieved.
- ed\_balls [[./thread#^c18446198|ed\_balls]] reports that a Xiaomi was quiet enough to sleep beside and that sinus problems stopped after several filtration and cleaning changes. mattlondon [[./thread#^c18449738|mattlondon]] reports less congestion and less morning stuffiness with a Winix, but openly questions whether the perceived improvement is real or placebo. These accounts preserve different levels of confidence in subjective benefits.
- benbojangles [[./thread#^c18446603|benbojangles]] reports breathing problems in a moldy London basement, temporary benefit from a DIY filter, later hospitalization with a lung infection and brain lesion, and continuing impairment two years later. This is a serious firsthand illness account, but the causal contributions of mold, the filter and other factors are not medically established in the supplied discussion.
- Mold exposure did not produce uniformly reported outcomes. the\_clarence [[./thread#^c18447219|the\_clarence]] recalls mold on a mattress and believes no health problems resulted over the following five years. jokoon [[./thread#^c18449925|jokoon]] describes rain-related mold in a converted cave apartment, cleaning it and moving out, with uncertainty about exposure. Neither account establishes that similar environments are safe or predicts another person's outcome.
- arendtio [[./thread#^c18449062|arendtio]] reports no meaningful mold recurrence over three years after addressing environmental conditions, apart from shower spots. patrickbuckley [[./thread#^c18454008|patrickbuckley]] reports months of cold-like symptoms, suspected mold allergy, and resolution of the household mold problem after cleaning, moving furniture away from walls and using a dehumidifier that removed a gallon or more daily. Several interventions changed together.
- nvarsj [[./thread#^c18450976|nvarsj]] reports that at indoor winter temperatures of 18–20°C, closing window vents produced condensation and mold around windows; leaving the vents open eliminated those observations and appeared to keep RH below 50\%. This directly qualifies the preceding suggestion that around 25°C was necessary, but does not establish suitability for a basement or a different outdoor climate.
- zimpenfish [[./thread#^c18449014|zimpenfish]] reports Netatmo readings of 17.8°C and 69\% RH in a bedroom with a slightly open window, versus 14°C and 78\% RH on the balcony; indoor RH had not fallen below 50\% that month. Closing that window did not promptly lower RH, possibly because other windows remained open [[./thread#^c18449802|zimpenfish]]. By contrast, roel\_v [[./thread#^c18448351|roel\_v]] needed continuous humidification to keep their heated home above 30\%.
- majewsky [[./thread#^c18448807|majewsky]] found passive moisture absorbers inadequate for lowering already high humidity and reports that an electric dehumidifier removed more than two liters daily when sufficiently humid. a012 [[./thread#^c18449557|a012]] reports four liters per eight hours from their own unit. Different rooms and conditions prevent treating those amounts as a controlled comparison between machines.
- elektor [[./thread#^c18450716|elektor]] hears Coway units accelerate after a candle is extinguished and sometimes when walking around rugs, attributing the latter to disturbed dust. mattlondon [[./thread#^c18449738|mattlondon]] gets a strong response by breathing coffee-scented breath into a sensor. ghayes [[./thread#^c18446186|ghayes]] reports a purifier indicating improvement within ten minutes. These are observations of automatic responses, without independent concentration validation.
- DIY mechanical experiences conflict. 9wzYQbTYsAIc [[./thread#^c18445844|9wzYQbTYsAIc]] reports blowback with a tightly sealed front filter; nhebb [[./thread#^c18445991|nhebb]] needed two filters forming a triangular intake for adequate airflow. meesterdude [[./thread#^c18450753|meesterdude]] reports a fan dying weeks after adding a front filter, while adventured [[./thread#^c18445847|adventured]] associates past failures with intake restriction. The accounts do not isolate failure mechanisms.
- chaostheory [[./thread#^c18448847|chaostheory]] reports owning a Molekule and finding airflow performance inadequate, high-speed operation very noisy, a defective filter associated with a metallic smell, and smart features unreliable. They explicitly had not tested the particles. Their later concern about a bright, nonreplaceable LED and a two-to-four-year useful life [[./thread#^c18450285|chaostheory]] is a prediction, not a reported completed lifespan.
- lmilcin [[./thread#^c18448406|lmilcin]] reports eliminating severe apartment odors using multiple aggressive treatments and a dehumidifier, but also suffering an immediate skin burn and damaging materials. The account measures success through odor, not residual pollutants or safe re-entry conditions. mnw21cam [[./thread#^c18448897|mnw21cam]] corrects the claimed peroxide decomposition product from hydrogen to oxygen, and lmilcin accepts the correction [[./thread#^c18448923|lmilcin]].

## Unresolved disagreements

- NO2 exposure is essentially unmeasured here. ed\_balls [[./thread#^c18446379|ed\_balls]] mentions NO2 in discussing gases versus particle filtration, and black-tea [[./thread#^c18449949|black-tea]] asks whether the setup helps with nearby diesel traffic. No supplied account measures NO2, compares cooking fuels, or quantifies traffic-related gas removal. stevenwoo's traffic-health argument [[./thread#^c18460625|stevenwoo]] is a summary of linked research, not a household intervention result.
- CO2 and cognition remain largely unanswered. joncrocks [[./thread#^c18449282|joncrocks]] warns about CO2 accumulating in sealed homes, but gives no concentrations or symptom measurements. nathan\_f77 [[./thread#^c18449361|nathan\_f77]] links reporting about air pollution and intelligence while discussing relocation. No supplied account tests cognition, establishes a CO2 threshold, or attributes sleep improvement specifically to lower CO2.
- Consumer sensor accuracy remains unresolved. heybrendan [[./thread#^c18447691|heybrendan]] asks for a Dylos/SDS011 comparison without receiving comparative accuracy data; peepopeep [[./thread#^c18445938|peepopeep]] seeks calibrated 0.3-micrometer measurement. wildfire\_smoke [[./thread#^c18447160|wildfire\_smoke]] reports apparent agreement with outdoor AirNow measurements, while blattimwind [[./thread#^c18446436|blattimwind]] considers smell sufficient. None establishes interchangeable instruments or shows that odor reliably represents fine-particle exposure.
- Particle counts do not settle particle composition or relative harm. amelius [[./thread#^c18449435|amelius]] asks whether a counter distinguishes harmful pollution from coastal salt, later clarifying the concern about useful feedback [[./thread#^c18451550|amelius]]. ajuc [[./thread#^c18451916|ajuc]] offers a salt-resort experience, but no composition measurements or health comparison resolves the question.
- The best intake/outlet arrangement and motor risk are contested. sgc [[./thread#^c18447526|sgc]] argues that restricted airflow can lower motor work while acknowledging reduced cooling; Elien237 [[./thread#^c18449866|Elien237]] warns about resistance, failures and fire risk. joezydeco [[./thread#^c18450691|joezydeco]] favors an HVAC technician's experience over the research suggested by olivierlacan [[./thread#^c18448366|olivierlacan]]. No controlled temperature, power, airflow and lifetime comparison settles these competing explanations.
- Replacement intervals and filter aging are disputed. Elien237 [[./thread#^c18448799|Elien237]] asserts relatively fixed useful lives and declining effectiveness, including for carbon. Scoundreller [[./thread#^c18447201|Scoundreller]] quotes engine-filter efficiency increasing with initial dust loading, and rsync [[./thread#^c18451129|rsync]] distinguishes improving capture from declining flow. Those different applications do not establish one replacement schedule for a home purifier; odor adsorption, particle retention and total airflow require separate consideration.
- No supplied comparison optimizes ventilation, PM2.5, CO2, humidity and comfort together. rsync [[./thread#^c18451038|rsync]] proposes continuous filtered outdoor intake, LeoPanthera [[./thread#^c18451398|LeoPanthera]] emphasizes repeated filtration passes, and ungzd [[./thread#^c18449474|ungzd]] links window-open testing without reproducing results. The effectiveness of a particular window-opening schedule or filtered intake therefore remains conditional rather than settled.
- Claims for UV, ionization and photocatalytic treatment lack measured outcomes here. choot [[./thread#^c18448064|choot]] reports a UV-C lamp but no efficacy test; stamps [[./thread#^c18451106|stamps]] asks about photocatalytic oxidation without a supplied answer; Elien237 [[./thread#^c18449866|Elien237]] recommends filtration plus ionization. chaostheory [[./thread#^c18452165|chaostheory]] and clairity [[./thread#^c18452924|clairity]] also report conflicting California purchase experiences, without resolving the underlying certification or ozone question.
- Some basic filtration descriptions conflict. ed\_balls [[./thread#^c18446379|ed\_balls]] describes HEPA as filtering particles above 2.5 micrometers, whereas Dave\_Rosenthal [[./thread#^c18446281|Dave\_Rosenthal]] discusses 0.3-micrometer performance and observes reductions using a smaller-particle counting channel. The discussion's numerical descriptions should not be accepted as a consistent technical specification, especially when extending particle results to gas removal.
